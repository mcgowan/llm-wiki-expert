# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Simon Mills, a herbal practitioner with roughly 50 years of clinical experience who has held roles including board member of the British Herbal Medicine Association, first chair of the Council for Complementary and Alternative Medicine, leader of a European Commission herbal medicine project spanning over 20 centres, and secretary of the European Scientific Cooperative on Phytotherapy.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w] Mills frames his mission as helping people "build a better boat" — strengthening the body's underlying functions rather than suppressing symptoms — and argues that Western, English-speaking countries are outliers in having abandoned plant medicine, while most of the world (France, Germany, the former Soviet bloc, and especially Asia) still uses it routinely.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]

The conversation centres on the gut and microbiome as the starting point for most chronic illness, on inflammation as a defence mechanism rather than an enemy, and on what Mills sees as the overuse of common drugs: antibiotics (driving resistance he calls one of the most urgent health threats), ibuprofen and other NSAIDs (which suppress a defensive process without asking what caused it), and omeprazole, which he describes as the most widely prescribed drug in the UK and, he believes, the US, with a growing list of serious long-term problems and a notorious rebound effect that makes it hard to stop.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w] Throughout, Mills demonstrates plant remedies live — ginger-and-cinnamon tea, echinacea root extract, rosemary, green tea, cardamom — arguing that most act within minutes to hours, so people can test what suits them individually.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]

# Key points

- Mills says he has treated upwards of 10,000 patients over his career, seeing about 200 actively at any time; roughly a quarter are living with cancer and at least a third with chronic inflammatory and autoimmune conditions such as Crohn's, ulcerative colitis and psoriasis — he treats underlying function (gut, liver, kidney, circulation), not the named disease.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- On antibiotic resistance, the episode cites WHO figures: antibiotic resistance was declared a top global health threat in 2023, responsible for 1.27 million deaths in 2019 and contributing to almost 5 million; US doctors wrote about 236 million antibiotic prescriptions in 2022 (roughly seven per ten people), with about 30% judged unnecessary — Mills adds that hospital-borne resistant infections will make routine operations increasingly risky.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- Mills stresses antibiotics are useless against viruses, and each course selects for resistant bacteria while cutting down the diversity of the gut microbiome — which he says "runs the show", with gut bacteria outnumbering our own cells and carrying about 100 times our genetic capacity.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- His first home remedy demonstration is grated fresh ginger (a thumb-sized knob) with a teaspoon of cinnamon in hot water: heating remedies stimulate pain fibres and trigger reflex hyperemia, opening blood vessels and loosening mucus — appropriate for any "cold" condition that responds to heat, while bitters (wormwood, dandelion, unsweetened espresso) are the classic "cooling" remedies that stimulate digestion and were used to manage fever.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- Garlic — once called "Russian penicillin" and used in WWI — is presented as a powerful raw prebiotic and antiseptic; Mills describes a one-off "garlic intensive" (eight chopped, swallowed cloves over a Friday evening) for low-level gut or lung infections, noting garlic oil exits through the breath strongly enough that one could once kill pathogens on a petri dish by breathing on it.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- Mills reframes inflammation as a defence ("Marines" going in to clean up): ibuprofen and other NSAIDs simply cut that process, so the better question is what upstream burden — usually starting in the gut, where most of the immune system sits — is making the inflammation necessary; for joint pain he recommends traditional counter-irritants like cayenne (capsicum) or mustard plasters and baths that bring blood in without the soreness of inflammation.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- On turmeric, Mills argues the bioavailability debate misses the point: curcumin is only 1-2% absorbed (and is toxic at high doses in the blood), but it belongs in the gut, where the microbiome breaks it into active products — the episode cites a 2016 meta-analysis finding curcumin comparable to ibuprofen for pain relief.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- For brain health he highlights the neurovascular unit (formerly the blood-brain barrier): regular green tea drinking is associated with lower cognitive decline and Alzheimer's risk, rosemary showed pointers to improved cognitive performance in a clinical trial Mills ran in Maryland, and ginkgo is a prescription medicine in parts of Europe.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- Dark chocolate of 75%+ cocoa solids is, in Mills's words, "one of the best medicines around" for long-term brain and cardiovascular health — he tells patients to take 50g a day, citing studies showing beneficial blood-flow changes within an hour of eating cocoa.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- He connects diet, insulin resistance and hormones: keto diets improve insulin sensitivity, and since 80% of women with PCOS have some insulin resistance (a statistic Bartlett cites), reducing sugar can steady disrupted menstrual cycles — Mills claims about 13 "herb babies" from helping women with fertility difficulties by stabilising cycles with traditional women's remedies.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- On omeprazole (PPIs for acid reflux/GORD), Mills argues stomach acid protects us by sterilising food, that long-term use is being linked to cancers and dementias, and that a rebound effect traps users; he weans patients off using the "raft principle" — mucilaginous plants (slippery elm, aloe vera) or seaweed-gum products like Gaviscon that physically float on the stomach contents to block reflux.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
- Dietary advice throughout: "eat the rainbow" — polyphenols (especially purple anthocyanins, largely bred out of modern vegetables) are prebiotics processed by the microbiome into active "postbiotic" compounds; aim for around 30 different plants per week, favour root vegetables and greens, and note that organically grown plants contain more polyphenols because they must mount their own pest defences.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]

# Takeaways

The guest's core argument is that the body is a self-healing system and that plants — as foods and as remedies — support its underlying functions, whereas much of modern prescribing (antibiotics, NSAIDs, PPIs, statins) suppresses defences or symptoms without addressing upstream causes, often at growing long-term cost.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w] Mills does not counsel abandoning drugs — he is explicit that serious infections need antibiotics and that supplements are mostly unnecessary apart from cases like winter vitamin D — but urges people to test cheap, fast-acting kitchen remedies on themselves first and to treat diverse, colourful, plant-rich eating as the foundation of medicine.[^5-natural-medicines-big-pharma-are-hiding-from-y-5Jk5XCLAr6w]
