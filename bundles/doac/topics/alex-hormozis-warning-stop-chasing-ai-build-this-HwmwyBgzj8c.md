# Overview

In this conversation with Steven Bartlett, entrepreneur and investor Alex Hormozi argues that while he is "a big AI advocate", most founders are using AI in the wrong places — doing "a lot of dumb things really fast", building companies the frontier models will swallow, and automating processes that were never the constraint on growth. His test is blunt: are you actually making more money? He cites a business that spent $350,000 building an AI system to replace 11 virtual assistants costing about $11,000 a month, automating a process that wasn't limiting the company at all — its real problem was demand.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c] Hormozi also warns against outsourcing thinking to AI, saying the models can be made to agree with anything and that delegating your hardest thinking makes you "get dumber" — "this is the best asset I've got for now, so I want to keep it as sharp as I can".[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]

What should entrepreneurs build instead? Hormozi's answer centers on things AI cannot replicate: stakes, judgment, liability, and above all reality — "reality is the moat". Brand, reputation, and demonstrable track record (a Warren Buffett quoted verbatim by a teacher in Des Moines still loses to Buffett, because the teacher "forgot to build Berkshire Hathaway") become more valuable as AI floods content and commoditizes production.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c] He pairs this with long-term thinking: the fastest way to build a $10 million business is not the fastest way to build a $100 million one, and focus and patience are "the two enduring competitive advantages because they're so anti-human".[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]

The episode then broadens into Hormozi's compressed business playbook — retention before scale, pricing high, hiring "rhinos plus horses" instead of unicorns, behaviorist incentive design, and the value equation — before turning deeply personal: his father's immigrant story, his wife Leila as "the single best financial decision" of his life, and how he coped when his mother died four weeks after his $106 million book launch, using a self-authored framework of fortitude, tolerance, resilience, and adaptability.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]

# Key points

- Hormozi argues founders should use AI in their business rather than start "AI businesses", and should advertise the customer outcome rather than the AI itself — noting many customers are actually afraid of AI; the only question that matters is whether AI adoption is making you more money.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- He warns against delegating decisions to LLMs: asking the same question to Claude, OpenAI, and other models yields answers "all over the place", and research he references suggests offloading hard thinking makes you cognitively weaker.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- In an AI world, Hormozi locates durable human value in stakes and accountability: someone must own the decision, the liability, the LLC, and the risk — MrBeast's videos work because the $5 million is real, chess thrives despite computers beating humans, and F1 needs Lewis Hamilton in the car.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- Using a block-tower exercise, he shows that foundations depend on the time horizon: entrepreneurs who build for speed hit a plateau and must "take two steps back" to rebuild; thinking in 50-year terms (like Musk building batteries and charging networks, or Bezos making logistics the moat) produces the most durable businesses.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- His retention math: a company that keeps all its customers and adds 100 a year is worth vastly more than one with identical revenue that churns everything, because acquisition costs compound against the churner — solve stickiness before seeking distribution.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- On content, Hormozi predicts AI "slop" will hit entertainers harder than educators, and that credibility scales with the risk of acting on the content — beauty tips need little proof, personal finance needs a Dave Ramsey-level track record, and business advice needs real results.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- His starting-out advice: entrepreneurship begins with four steps (an entity, a bank account, a way to process money, and asking a stranger for money), fear "only exists in the vague, never in the specific", and someone's version of you has to die — he recounts driving halfway across the country before daring to tell his father he had quit his job.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- He champions premium pricing and unscalable offers: charge as high as you can (he describes the Van Westendorp four-question pricing analysis, now runnable in minutes with AI), start with a high-touch premium product Tesla-Roadster-style, and work down-market later.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- Hormozi frames self-improvement through behaviorism: humans, like AI, are trained by reinforcement — his "grandpa's lemonade" story shows you don't persuade people, you arrange conditions so the desired option is the easiest one; a mentor's 5,000-flyer test batches versus his 300 taught him successful people's volume of activity is often 100x greater.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- He tells of an agent-brokerage owner who raised his agent-referral bonus from $500 to $25,000 (against $250,000 gross profit per productive agent) and grew from $10 million to $400 million — an argument that exceptional talent is always a bargain.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- Opportunity areas he names for new entrepreneurs: longevity, med-spa and aesthetics, wealth advisory, insurance, and unglamorous businesses like waste management — "look for things that people would not want to say at parties"; he notes the bottom 50% of Americans hold roughly $2 of every $100 of wealth, so selling to broke customers keeps you broke.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
- After his mother's death four weeks after his $106 million launch — during a quarter with nine open lawsuits and his wife's serious injury — Hormozi says he rejected the idea that "people will judge how much you love someone by how much you choose to suffer", changed as little as possible about his routine, and treated bad days statistically: "my emotional discomfort is not an adequate reason to change what I'm doing".[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]

# Takeaways

Hormozi's closing answer to the previous guest's question — what would you do if superintelligent AI were a few years away — distills the episode: on the business side, build as much real-world proof, track record, brand, trust, and distribution as possible, because reputation and marketplace dynamics won't change; on the personal side, think hard about what it means to be human. Throughout, he presents focus, patience, retention, premium pricing, and betting on what won't change (per Bezos) as the anti-hype strategy that outlasts any AI wave — while cautioning that he sells quantitative outcomes, not happiness, which he says is beyond his scope to promise.[^alex-hormozis-warning-stop-chasing-ai-build-this-HwmwyBgzj8c]
