# Overview

In this episode, California governor Gavin Newsom sits down with Steven Bartlett to counter the "slick trust-fund guy" image he says most people hold of him. He describes growing up with a 19-year-old single mother who worked two or three jobs, rented out her own bedroom, and raised two kids with no wealth, while his elusive father moved in the circles of some of the richest families in the world[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE]. Newsom recounts severe dyslexia he discovered only by accident in his mother's files, being bullied, his mother telling him "it's okay to be average" — a line he says has marked half a century of his life — and later holding her hand through her assisted suicide as she died of breast cancer, which he says was likely illegal at the time and which he later helped make legal in California[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].

The guest walks through his entrepreneurial arc — a wine shop opened in 1992 that grew to roughly 22-24 businesses and about a thousand employees at peak — and his accidental entry into politics when San Francisco mayor Willie Brown made him president of the Parking and Traffic Commission, leading to the board of supervisors, the mayoralty, the 2004 "Winter of Love" same-sex marriages, and the governorship[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].

The political core of the conversation is Newsom's argument that Trump's second term is qualitatively different and darker than ordinary partisan conflict: he describes eliminated oversight, a federalized National Guard in Los Angeles, people "disappearing" from American streets, Texas redistricting designed to "rig the game," and a "Trump 2028" hat sent to him by people close to the president[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE]. He is equally critical of his own party, which he says polled at 27% and has failed young men, entrepreneurs, and authentic communication — failures he is trying to address through his own podcast, where he interviews opponents like Charlie Kirk and Steve Bannon[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].

# Key points

- Newsom says he would consider running for president if "the moment meets you and you meet the moment," noting bookmakers place him among favorites for 2028, and commits to running "the 110 yard dash" through his remaining 18 months as governor[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- He describes hidden dyslexia — a 960 SAT versus his sister's 1380, still being unable to read speeches without a teleprompter — and credits his mother's refusal to tell him about the diagnosis with preventing him from using it as a crutch; he calls dyslexia his "greatest gift" as an entrepreneur[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- His mother left a voicemail announcing her planned assisted suicide ("next Thursday I won't be around"); he held her hand at her death, her last words being "don't forget me," and his family foundation now raises money for cancer research in her name[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- In his businesses he ran a monthly "$500 failure award" (later renamed the magical moment award) — sparked by a night clerk who dumped catfish in ponds to fight mosquitoes — to reward initiative and risk-taking, a philosophy he ties to Tom Peters' book The Pursuit of Wow[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- As new mayor in February 2004 he defied his own party to marry same-sex couples: 4,036 couples from 46 states and eight countries wed in San Francisco's "Winter of Love," provoking fury from Democratic leaders who had told him to "do what you think is right"[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- He calls the crisis of boys and men "code red," praises Richard Reeves and Scott Galloway, cites a coming two-to-one female-to-male graduation ratio in California's UC system, admits the Democratic Party showed a "deep lack of empathy" toward men, and says he has prepared an executive order on recruiting male teachers and caregiving[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- On Trump he argues the second term shows "megalomania" with "no limits": he cites Trump's demand for his arrest, the National Guard federalization the morning after a cordial phone call ending "you need anything, call me," 5,000 troops idle in Los Angeles, and Texas mid-decade redistricting for five extra seats[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- He judges that on the balance of probability Trump will not try to stay in office in 2028 — mainly because he is 79, not 69, and is instead monetizing the presidency to make "billions" — but says people close to Trump sent him a "Trump 2028" hat and "they're not screwing around"[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- Defending Democrats on the economy, he claims that of 52 million US jobs created since 1989, 50 million came under Democratic administrations versus 1.9 million under Republicans, and that the last three Republican presidents all presided over recessions[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- He calls the Democratic Party's disdain for entrepreneurs "the worst part of my party," insists "you cannot be pro job and anti-business," and says he has championed Elon Musk for 20 years while arguing Musk has changed in the last seven or eight and left California mainly to avoid capital gains taxes[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- He confirms the internal Democratic panic after Biden's debate — a "phone tree that lit up that night," White House meetings where governors challenged the president — and admits he was angry to get no heads-up before Biden dropped out, learning of Harris's candidacy from a missed voicemail[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
- On the Epstein files he says flatly "they lied to you then or they're lying to you now," asserts Epstein and Trump were close as "just a fact," and argues attorney general Pam Bondi "doesn't move without Trump," so the decision not to release files came from the president[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].

# Takeaways

The guest presents himself as an entrepreneur-turned-politician whose defining traits — grit, comfort with failure, and an abundance mindset — come from a struggling single mother and a lifelong fight with dyslexia. His prescription for the Democratic Party is essentially the "glass box" approach the host describes: drop sanitized PR, engage opponents directly, reclaim strength, empathy for men, and pro-business aspiration, and answer Trumpism with a congruent story about the American and Californian dream. His warning is that this must happen before the midterms, because in his view American democracy is "on life support" and the window to defend it is closing[^america-is-at-breaking-point-is-gavin-newsom-ame-1NLoTuRR3hE].
