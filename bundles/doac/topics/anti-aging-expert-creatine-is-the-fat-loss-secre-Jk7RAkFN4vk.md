# Overview

Dr. Darren Candow, a Canadian kinesiology professor who says he has published over 120 papers on creatine and run 30 to 40 studies in his own lab, joins Steven Bartlett to make the case that creatine — discovered in 1832 and long dismissed as a bodybuilder's powder — is one of the most broadly beneficial and safest supplements available, with evidence spanning muscle, bone, brain, inflammation, mental health, and healthy aging.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk] He explains that the body makes only 1-3 g of creatine daily in the liver and brain while storing 95% of it in skeletal muscle, where it acts as ATP's "Robin" — the backup that sustains cellular energy during intense effort — and that vegans and vegetarians, who get no dietary creatine from animal flesh, respond to supplementation better than anyone.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]

Candow systematically debunks five myths: kidney damage (elevated creatinine on blood tests is a false positive "99 out of 100 times"), water retention (only from loading phases, and intramuscular water actually stimulates protein synthesis), that creatine is only for men, hair loss (traced to one rugby study that never measured hair), and muscle cramps (creatine super-hydrates muscle and if anything reduces them).[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk] His central framework is a "dosing dilemma": roughly 3-5 g daily suffices for muscle, 8-12 g with exercise for bone, and the healthy brain likely needs none — but a metabolically stressed brain (sleep deprivation, shift work, exam cramming, jet lag) may benefit from 20 g or more, taken acutely.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]

Throughout, Candow positions creatine as one tool in a toolbox in which weight training is the hammer: he argues resistance training two or more days a week is modestly superior to cardio because it delivers cardiovascular benefits plus musculoskeletal ones, and that after age 40 sedentary adults lose about 1% of muscle mass per year — losses creatine, protein, and training together can offset.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]

# Key points

- Candow says creatine is the most researched ergogenic supplement, having overtaken protein and caffeine, and that he can find nobody who can't or shouldn't take it at recommended doses — a 25,000-case safety review found doses above 10 g daily safe over years.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- The biggest myth, per Candow, is kidney damage: creatine's breakdown product creatinine elevates blood markers and lowers estimated GFR, prompting doctors to wrongly blame the supplement; randomized controlled trials show no kidney harm, and he advises always telling your doctor you supplement.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- Muscle gains are real but modest — about 0.86-1.2 kg of lean mass in short trials, only half of which is skeletal muscle; the more robust effect is on performance, training volume, strength, recovery, and functional ability like sit-to-stand in older adults.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- He recommends plain creatine monohydrate over newer marketed forms like hydrochloride, ideally CreaPure-grade and NSF or otherwise third-party certified — noting testing by James Smith found many off-the-shelf products contained no creatine at all.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- Loading phases (20-30 g/day for 5-7 days) saturate muscle fastest but can cause temporary water retention and GI upset; Candow favors microdosing — he personally takes about 10 g daily, split between morning and his workout water bottle, rising to 20-25 g when jet-lagged.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- On bone, his lab's work — including a 2-year trial in postmenopausal women — suggests 8-12 g daily combined with weight training slows bone mineral density loss at the hip (it does not build density), acting somewhat like a bisphosphonate; without exercise, no bone benefit has ever been shown.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- For the stressed brain, he cites a German study in which a single 30 g dose raised brain creatine and offset deficits during 21 hours of sleep deprivation, and a classic 90-minute Stroop-test study in which 20 g improved speed and cognition; benefits show up in task performance rather than felt energy.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- Early Alzheimer's research excites him: single-arm studies (Taylor and Smith) found 20 g daily for 8 weeks raised brain creatine about 11% and improved memory and cognition measures, plus a 1.9 kg hand-grip strength gain — though he cautions these lacked placebo controls.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- On mental health, Candow points to Utah research showing creatine added to SSRIs or cognitive behavioral therapy improved depression outcomes — the host cites a trial where 5 g added to antidepressants doubled remission rates in women over 8 weeks — while stressing creatine has never been shown to work as a standalone treatment.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- His lab found young female athletes taking 5 g on training days slept about an hour longer than placebo — a finding he calls a potential game changer that needs replication in males.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- Fat-loss effects are indirect, he says: meta-analyses show creatine reduces fat mass, likely because added lean tissue raises energy expenditure rather than through direct fat burning.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
- Timing doesn't matter (a just-accepted paper "puts the nail in the coffin"), gummies from validated third-party-tested brands work, children benefit from at least 1 g daily for bone and muscle development per current reviews, and creatine may support women through the estrogen decline of menopause; Candow's own stack also includes protein, vitamin D, magnesium, omega-3s, probiotics, and a multivitamin.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]

# Takeaways

Candow's closing message is that creatine is a genuinely multi-system tool — but only one tool — in a health-promotion toolbox headed by weight training and good nutrition, and he cautions that even he doesn't yet know the single best dose: recommendations remain estimates that scale with body size and stress. His pitch to skeptical loved ones is that creatine may help people stay strong, functional, and cognitively resilient into old age — "get busy living or get busy dying" — though he warns against chronic mega-dosing, whose long-term brain effects remain unstudied.[^anti-aging-expert-creatine-is-the-fat-loss-secre-Jk7RAkFN4vk]
