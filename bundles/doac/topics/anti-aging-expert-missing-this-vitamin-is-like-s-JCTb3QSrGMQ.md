# Overview

Dr. Rhonda Patrick, a biomedical scientist with a PhD spanning aging, cancer, metabolism, and neuroscience research, tells Steven Bartlett that genetics plays only a small role in how we age — she puts 70% or more down to lifestyle — and that simple, poorly known interventions can dramatically change both lifespan and healthspan[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ]. She contrasts hypothetical extremes: someone following her advice could be "out surfing at 90," while the opposite lifestyle costs roughly 14 years of life expectancy and potentially 30-40 years of quality healthspan[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].

The guest calls being sedentary a disease — a stronger predictor of early mortality than type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or smoking — and anchors the exercise case in the Dallas bed rest study, in which three weeks of bed rest damaged five men's cardiorespiratory fitness more than the subsequent 30 years of aging did[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ]. Her core prescription is vigorous, high-intensity interval training — with the Norwegian 4x4 as the gold standard — which she credits with reversing 20 years of age-related cardiac stiffening in sedentary 50-year-olds and growing (rather than shrinking) the hippocampus in older adults[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].

Beyond exercise, Patrick works through the lower-effort levers: vitamin D, omega-3s, magnesium, multivitamins, creatine, ketogenic diets and exogenous ketones, fasting, sauna and heat exposure, and avoiding microplastics and pesticide-contaminated water. Throughout she frames micronutrient deficiencies as insidious, radiation-like damage — invoking her mentor Bruce Ames's "triage theory," under which a scarce mineral is allocated to short-term survival at the expense of DNA repair and long-term cancer prevention[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].

# Key points

- Patrick cites the Dallas bed rest study: three weeks of strict bed rest in the 1960s tanked five men's VO2 max more than 30 years of subsequent aging; she notes high cardiorespiratory fitness predicts about five extra years of life and an 80% lower chance of dying from many causes[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- She recommends vigorous interval training (the Norwegian 4x4: four 4-minute near-max efforts with 4-minute recoveries) at least once weekly, noting that 40% of people doing only moderate-intensity exercise fail to improve their cardiorespiratory fitness; Ben Levine's two-year protocol made 50-year-old sedentary hearts structurally resemble 30-year-old hearts[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- Lactate produced during vigorous exercise is described as a "miracle molecule" and signaling agent that crosses into the brain and activates BDNF — driving neurogenesis, neuroplasticity, and improved mood and focus — which is why she'd swap the 10,000-steps obsession for at least 10 minutes of vigorous exercise[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- In a one-year aerobic training study of adults around 60, the exercise group's hippocampus grew 1-2% while the stretching control group's shrank the expected 1-2%; women with the highest cardiorespiratory fitness were 80% less likely to develop dementia in follow-up[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- On vitamin D: 70% of the US population has insufficient levels; deficiency is linked to an 80% higher dementia risk, while D3 supplementation is associated with 40% lower dementia risk; she recommends blood levels of 40-60 ng/mL, typically achievable with ~4,000 IU/day, and warns that melanin, sunscreen, and northern latitudes compound the problem[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- She reports that a low omega-3 index (≤4%) carries roughly the same mortality risk as smoking, per Bill Harris's research; a high index (≥8%) is associated with five years' greater life expectancy, and 1-2 g/day of fish oil can move someone from low to high — with purified supplements now arguably better than fish because of mercury and microplastic contamination[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- Roughly half the US population lacks adequate magnesium, which over 300 enzymes require; she cites a 24% higher pancreatic cancer incidence per 100 mg drop in daily intake and 40% lower all-cause mortality in people with the highest magnesium levels, explained via Ames's triage theory (energy production is prioritized over DNA repair)[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- Three large randomized controlled trials found a standard multivitamin improved older adults' cognition, equivalent to reducing episodic-memory aging by about five years — reversing, in her telling, the 2013 "Enough is Enough" anti-multivitamin verdict[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- On creatine, Patrick takes 10 g/day (20-25 g when sleep-deprived or under high cognitive load), citing German dosing research showing amounts above the ~5 g muscle-saturating dose reach the brain; studies show 25-30 g can negate — even reverse — the cognitive deficits of 21-hour sleep deprivation, and she dismisses the hair-loss fear as one unreplicated 2009 rugby study contradicted by a 2025 RCT[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- She explains ketosis's brain benefits through beta-hydroxybutyrate: an energy-efficient neuronal fuel and signaling molecule that activates BDNF while freeing glucose to make glutathione, the brain's major antioxidant; APOE4 carriers (25% of the population, 2x Alzheimer's risk; 10x with two copies) should be especially strict about sleep, alcohol, and head trauma[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- On heat: sauna use 4-7 times weekly is associated with roughly 50% lower cardiovascular mortality and 40% lower all-cause mortality versus once weekly; Ashley Mason's infrared heat-bed trial combining hyperthermia with CBT produced a 16-point Hamilton depression-scale improvement, versus 3 points considered clinically significant[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
- On environmental toxins, she warns that heat leaches BPA from plastic-lined cups and cans (55-fold more with heated liquids; ~1,000% higher BPA after canned soup), that "BPA-free" plastics substitute similarly harmful BPS, that living within about a mile of a golf course was linked to 126% higher Parkinson's risk via pesticide-contaminated water (fixable with reverse-osmosis filtration), and that soluble fiber may blunt microplastic absorption in the gut[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].

# Takeaways

Patrick's through-line is that aging is largely modifiable and that prevention beats treatment: exercise is the unmatched "blockbuster drug" you cannot pill up, while cheap, low-effort corrections — vitamin D, omega-3s, magnesium, a multivitamin, creatine, earlier time-restricted eating, and regular deliberate heat exposure — close deficiency gaps whose damage accumulates invisibly for decades before surfacing as dementia, cancer, or cardiovascular disease[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ]. The episode closes on a personal note: answering the previous guest's question, Patrick says her one regret is having only one child after delaying family for her career, and she and Bartlett urge listeners — especially high-performing women — to do family planning early, including egg or embryo freezing, to preserve the option[^anti-aging-expert-missing-this-vitamin-is-like-s-JCTb3QSrGMQ].
