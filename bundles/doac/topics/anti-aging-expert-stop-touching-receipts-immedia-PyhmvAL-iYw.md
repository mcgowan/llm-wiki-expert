# Overview

Biomedical scientist Dr. Rhonda Patrick returns to The Diary Of A CEO to argue that visceral fat — the deep, unpinchable fat surrounding the organs — is an insidious, under-discussed health threat that doubles the risk of early mortality, drives insulin resistance, raises metastatic cancer risk by 44%, and produces the fatigue and brain fog many people live with without knowing the cause.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw] She explains that even lean people can be "metabolically unhealthy" with high visceral fat, that sleep loss and caloric excess build it shockingly fast (healthy young men gained 11% visceral fat after two weeks of four-hour nights without gaining a pound on the scale), and that hormonal shifts — plummeting estrogen in perimenopause, declining testosterone in aging men — accelerate the storage; the good news, she says, is that vigorous aerobic exercise, intermittent fasting, and any weight-loss protocol strip visceral fat first.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]

The episode's signature segment is a walk through Bartlett's kitchen hunting endocrine-disrupting chemicals — BPA, phthalates, and PFAS "forever chemicals". Patrick bins black plastic containers (often made from recycled electronics containing flame retardants), warns against Teflon pans, plastic-wrapped cheese, canned soup (shown to spike BPA levels by 1,000%), plastic blender jugs, and above all thermal receipts, which are coated in BPA — she cites a study in which adolescent boys with the highest BPA levels had 50% lower testosterone, and advises cashiers to wear nitrile gloves.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw] She connects these chemicals to falling sperm quality, earlier menopause, and developmental harms, and recommends glass containers, reverse-osmosis water filtration, and sulforaphane (from broccoli sprouts) to aid BPA excretion.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]

The conversation then covers Patrick's supplement rankings, her case for the new concept of "peak span" (staying within 90% of your peak function rather than merely disease-free), her argument that exercise guidelines dramatically undervalue vigorous exercise, warnings about outsourcing thinking to AI, and a balanced assessment of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]

# Key points

- Patrick says visceral fat is metabolically active, constantly releasing free fatty acids and inflammatory cytokines that block insulin signaling — a vicious cycle in which visceral fat causes insulin resistance and insulin resistance causes more visceral fat, eventually leading toward type 2 diabetes and fatty liver.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Proxies she offers: a waist circumference of 35+ inches for women or 40+ inches for men signals excess visceral fat, with a DEXA scan (ideally under 300 g visceral fat) as the direct measure; 70% of women and 50% of men over 50 carry too much.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- In one study she cites, healthy young men eating roughly 1,200 extra calories a day of ultra-processed food for five days gained visceral fat, showed fatty-liver signs, and developed brain insulin resistance.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Patrick advises stopping eating three hours before bed (late meals keep the sympathetic nervous system active and fragment sleep), and says losing visceral fat requires aerobic, preferably vigorous exercise — resistance training improves glucose handling but "doesn't move the needle" on visceral fat.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- She practices 16:8 intermittent fasting as a low-effort calorie-reduction tool and values the "metabolic switch" into ketosis for sharper cognition, lower anxiety via GABA, and fasted-training mitochondrial adaptations — while warning women doing high-volume training in caloric deficit risk amenorrhea.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- On endocrine disruptors, she cites men with the highest phthalate levels having 20% lower testosterone, roughly 20% of boys now having an undescended testicle, pregnant women with high BPA being six times more likely to have a child with autism spectrum disorder, and PFAS accelerating menopause by one to two years.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Receipts are a major overlooked BPA exposure: thermal paper is coated with it, hand sanitizer or lotion increases dermal absorption roughly a hundredfold, and cashiers show extremely high urinary BPA — Patrick's advice is to opt for e-receipts or simply not touch them.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Her top five supplements, in order: fish oil (a high omega-3 index correlates with five years' greater life expectancy and 66% lower Alzheimer's risk), vitamin D3 (correcting deficiency slowed biological aging by nearly two years in a large study), a multivitamin (the COSMOS trial found Centrum Silver reversed global brain aging by 2.1 years and episodic memory aging by almost five), magnesium, and creatine — she takes 10 g of creatine daily for brain benefits, up to 20-25 g when sleep-deprived.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Newer additions to her stack include urolithin A (a mitophagy inducer that extended mouse lifespan ~20% and improved VO2 max and immune markers in human trials), phytosomal curcumin (lowers the aging-accelerating cytokine TNF-alpha), liposomal glutathione (non-liposomal forms can't enter cells), and glutamine for immune resilience; she cautions supplements are unregulated, so NSF or third-party certification matters, and men should avoid supplemental iron.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- "Peak span", from a recent Duke-led preprint, reframes longevity as staying within 90% of peak capacity; most functions — musculoskeletal, fluid intelligence, cardiorespiratory fitness — peak around 25, while crystallized intelligence peaks around 45, and levers include exercise, omega-3s, sleep, and novel learning (she invokes London taxi drivers' enlarged hippocampi and low Alzheimer's rates).[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- Citing new accelerometer-based research, Patrick argues exercise guidelines undervalue vigorous exercise: one minute of vigorous activity equaled roughly four to ten minutes of moderate activity for reducing mortality and disease risk, and she'd replace "10,000 steps a day" with "10 minutes a day" of elevated heart rate — women doing just 3.5 minutes of vigorous bursts daily had 40% lower cancer risk.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
- On AI, both host and guest worry about "cognitive debt": Bartlett cites a study in which 83% of AI-assisted writers couldn't recall what they wrote and EEG connectivity nearly halved, and Patrick describes her research-type-handwrite protocol for retaining knowledge; on GLP-1 drugs, she calls them life-changing for the obese but warns of muscle and bone loss, rebound hunger "with a vengeance", kidney-cancer signals, and unnecessary use by already-lean people.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]

# Takeaways

Patrick's overall message is that the biggest threats to healthy aging — visceral fat, endocrine-disrupting plastics, sedentary time, and under-dosed exercise — are largely invisible and habitual, and that the counters are equally habitual: vigorous movement (even in one-minute "exercise snacks"), time-restricted eating, prioritized sleep, glass over plastic, a short list of evidence-backed supplements, and continual novel learning to protect the brain. She frames these not as ways to merely avoid disease but as the path to preserving "peak span" — staying near the top of one's physical and cognitive capacity for as long as possible.[^anti-aging-expert-stop-touching-receipts-immedia-PyhmvAL-iYw]
