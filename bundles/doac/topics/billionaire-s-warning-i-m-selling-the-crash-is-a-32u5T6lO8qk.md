# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews Jeremy Grantham, the 87-year-old co-founder of asset manager GMO, who says he has spent roughly 60 years investing and managed as much as $165 billion of other people's money at the firm's peak.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk] Grantham's central argument is that great bubbles always form around genuinely transformative ideas — railroads, the internet — and that AI, while it will "change everything," is now the biggest investment bubble in American history, one whose peak he believes could arrive within days, weeks, or months, and certainly within a few years.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]

His practical advice for ordinary savers is blunt: don't own US stocks, including the S&P 500, and sell large positions in US technology stocks; instead hold a broad non-US equity index (around 60% of a portfolio), 5-10% precious metals, some bonds, and a cash reserve.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk] He argues that investment advisers will never warn clients out of an overpriced market because fighting a bubble destroys their business — his own firm lost half its book of clients by being two and a quarter years early in the late-1990s tech bubble.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]

The conversation then widens into Grantham's other long-run concerns: extreme wealth inequality that history suggests only resets through catastrophe, and a "baby bust" he attributes substantially to endocrine-disrupting chemicals — plastics, pesticides, PFAS — that he says have driven sperm counts down from roughly 100 million units per milliliter in 1970 to about 35 today.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk] Having given away over 90% of his billion-dollar fortune to his environmental foundation, Grantham closes by urging listeners to brace for tougher times, build practical skills, and trust the data over the reassurances of large institutions.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]

# Key points

- Grantham argues bubbles form around the most important ideas, not scams: railroads and the internet both changed the world, yet their stocks collapsed — Amazon rose six or seven times in 1999, fell 92% in the tech crash, then "inherited the retail world."[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He calls AI arguably the biggest investment bubble in American history and says the data would be "compatible with history" for the peak to come very soon; from current levels he says a 70% decline in the high-flying AI stocks "would not be unexpected," noting the Nasdaq fell 82% after 2000.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He cites Japan's 1989 bubble — trading at 65 times earnings versus about 35-40 for the US today — as the biggest in history, followed by roughly 20 lost years and a 35-year wait for the market to recover.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- His portfolio advice for average investors: no US stocks (not even the S&P 500), about 60% in broad non-US equity indices, 5-10% precious metals, some bonds and cash; he notes emerging markets rose 65% in the prior 12 months versus 25% for the S&P.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He contends investment firms never advise clients to exit overpriced markets because it is "lousy business": in a 1999 debate, 400 self-identified stock-market experts agreed a return to 17 times earnings would guarantee a major bear market, and over 99% thought it would happen — yet their firms kept telling clients all was fine.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He calls SpaceX a classic market-top story — a prospectus claiming a quarter of global GDP as its addressable market and asteroid mining — predicting people will tell stories about it "like the South Sea bubble," and says 90% of its theoretical value rests on AI; he'd only buy at 5-10 cents on the dollar.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He argues the Magnificent 7 have shifted from seven comfortable near-monopolies to seven "sharp-elbowed" rivals pouring $100-200 billion each in annual capex into the same AI battle, where they believe only one winner survives.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- On crypto, he owns none and never will, calling Bitcoin "an unnecessary piece of nonsense" useful mainly to criminals and speculators, and predicting it will eventually go to zero.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- On housing, he notes UK house prices rose from 3.4 times family income in 1994 to over 10 times today, and argues that even a 30% fall would leave homes twice as expensive as historic norms — with declining family formation undermining future demand.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- On inequality, he says since about 1975 essentially all US wealth gains have gone to the top 10%, the US Gini ratio now sits alongside Brazil and Mexico, and history shows extreme wealth peaks are broken only by collapse, mass-mobilization war, or revolution — so he advocates gently steeper taxation like the 1935-75 era.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- On fertility, he cites Shanna Swan and Hagai Levine's research showing sperm counts falling about 2.5% a year — from ~118 million units/ml in hunter-gatherer times to ~35 today, against ~45 needed for easy conception — with the WHO saying 17% of young couples now need help, and projections of functional infertility for the median couple within 20-25 years; he blames endocrine disruptors (phthalates, BPA, PFAS, pesticides) and notes the EU has banned ~1,500 cosmetics chemicals versus 12 in the US.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
- He cites Harvard/Mass General fertility-clinic studies in which men eating the least pesticide-laden produce had double the sperm count of the worst quartile, and women eating least badly had 68% live-birth success versus 38% — his top advice being no cosmetics and organic "dirty dozen" produce for pregnant women.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]

# Takeaways

Grantham's through-line is that humans are chronically short-term and optimistic, and that institutions — investment banks, corporations, governments — have no incentive to deliver bad news, whether about overpriced markets, inequality, toxicity, or climate.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk] His closing counsel is that "no help is coming" from large firms: individuals should look at the data themselves, recognize that a bubble "is not hard to see," diversify out of the most dangerous assets now, build cash reserves and practical skills, and plan their lives as if times will not be easy.[^billionaire-s-warning-i-m-selling-the-crash-is-a-32u5T6lO8qk]
