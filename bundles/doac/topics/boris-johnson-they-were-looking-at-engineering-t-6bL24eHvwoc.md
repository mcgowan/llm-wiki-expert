# Overview

Former UK prime minister Boris Johnson sits for a long, frequently combative interview with host Steven Bartlett to promote his memoir *Unleashed*, covering a premiership that spanned Brexit, the pandemic and the war in Ukraine[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc]. The opening third is biographical: a childhood he insists was happy and loving despite 32 house moves in 14 years, his mother Charlotte's obsessive compulsive disorder and eight-month stay in a psychiatric hospital when he was ten, his parents' divorce at 14 — he says he was told the news by a gate near the engine shed in Somerset and responded "so why did you have us then?" — and his conclusion that academic work became his way of rebuilding self-esteem, since "the best therapy for every type of pain, emotional pain, is to try to lose yourself in your work"[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].

The political core is Brexit, and Bartlett presses hard. Johnson recounts David Cameron offering him "a top five job in the cabinet" for backing Remain and threatening to "f*** you up forever" if he backed Leave — an exchange Bartlett repeatedly characterises as bribery and corruption, and which Johnson concedes is a mistake leaders make while suggesting it has been the way of politics "since the dawn of time"[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc]. Confronted with an unpublished article in which he made the case for Remain, Johnson says he wrote it as a deliberate counter-argument exercise after already deciding for Leave. Asked about the absence of a post-referendum plan, he argues that was the government's job, not the campaign's, and rejects a series of economic statistics Bartlett reads out — from the OBR's 4% long-run hit to LSE's finding that Brexit added 6% to food prices — replying "not at all" when asked if he has regrets[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].

On the pandemic Johnson is candid that his own experience with previous zoonotic scares made him initially sceptical, says he now believes Covid "almost certainly was a lab accident" arising from gain-of-function work in Wuhan rather than a weapon, and defends the timing of lockdown as constrained by scientific advice[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc]. The interview's most heated exchange is Partygate, where Johnson disputes the characterisation of the events, denies there was cake, music or dancing at anything he attended, and says his real error was issuing a blanket apology that made every subsequent allegation look validated — while Bartlett tells him plainly that gatherings with alcohol at Number 10 should have been banned[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].

# Key points

- Johnson attributes his formation to relentless sibling competition after his sister Rachel was born, and says the family both competed and mocked the competition — "oh, little baby wants to win"[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- He confirms his mother's OCD, describing her difficulty turning off a tap after washing her hands, says she fully recovered, and pushes back on the idea she was absent, adding that she blamed herself for the eight months away and that he felt strongly that was unfair[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Asked about reports of physical violence in the household, Johnson says the claim was made by one of his biographers, that he has no direct knowledge of it, and that he does not want to be disrespectful to his mother's memory or cause his father pain[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- On his public persona — Bartlett says he initially took Johnson for a *Bo' Selecta!* parody — Johnson denies calculation, describing a family horror of being dull and saying that to interest people in politics "you've got to sugar the pill," comparing the chapters of his book to digestive biscuits[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- He accepts Bartlett's statistic that roughly 20 of 57 prime ministers were educated at Eton and frames his own scholarship there as the source of his central political insight — that "ambition and energy and genius and talent" are evenly distributed across the UK population while opportunity is not[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Rather than a "Pol Pot style" assault on successful institutions, Johnson argues for spreading opportunity American-style, citing gigabit broadband rollout from 7% to about 70% of households in three years, backing HS2 and Northern Powerhouse Rail as equalisers, and pointing to improved PISA rankings and a £30,000 starting salary for new teachers under his government[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Bartlett recounts going undercover in a poorly rated Liverpool school where a teacher taught two classes at once and paid for footballs and pencils herself, arguing league-table funding incentives trap such schools in decline; Johnson does not dispute the account but insists the data show schools improved nationally in that period[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- On his London mayoralty he says he lay awake worrying about teenage knife and gang crime and cites cutting the murder rate by 50% during his eight years as the achievement he is proudest of[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Johnson says he expected to win the referendum when travelling the country and doubted it when back in London, notes 17.4 million Leave votes as the largest number ever cast for a proposition, and describes his eldest child telling him after the Cameron confrontation that he had no choice but to come out for Leave[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- His stated reason for Leave is democratic accountability — that of EU institutions you cannot answer "who put you in authority over me and how can I remove you from office" — and he argues the EU's growth and innovation record compared with America's shows the model is not delivering[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Pressed with Bartlett's parachute and football-manager analogies about having no plan for what followed, Johnson quotes his own book — that he and Michael Gove never discussed a future Leave-based government because it was a referendum, not an election — and calls it "utterly infuriating" that the campaign was blamed for the chaos[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- He inverts the parachute metaphor to argue Brexit regulatory freedom was what let the UK vaccinate faster than any European country — roughly 45% of adults by March 2021 and close to 100% of the over-80s — and exit lockdown in July 2021 with the fastest G7 rebound[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Asked repeatedly to name Brexit's trade-off, Johnson concedes only that some European partners are handling it in an unnecessarily bureaucratic way and that paperwork exists which technology should fix, declining to accept a near-term economic cost and blaming any future drag on Labour's plan to stay aligned as "rules takers"[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- On Covid's origin, Johnson says he called Xi Jinping in February 2020 while the UK was still sending supplies to China and raised the wet markets, but now believes it was "almost certainly a lab accident" from gain-of-function research — a terrible accident rather than a weapon — and suggests the WHO and scientists were hesitant partly because China sponsors so much academic research[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- He quotes his own book on having felt he "knew my SARS and my Ebola," having covered salmonella, BSE and bird flu scares where preparations proved unnecessary, and says the pivotal moment was seeing the Italian health system — which he rates highly from personal experience — overwhelmed[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- On lockdown timing he says acting earlier would have meant anticipating rather than following SAGE advice, and that what was on the table was "imprisoning the whole UK population"; on Matt Hancock's claim that earlier action could have saved 30,000 lives, he says he has no way of knowing[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- He questions how much of the epidemic curve lockdowns actually bent versus what would have happened naturally, and when Bartlett asks whether the benefits outweighed the damage to children's schooling, answers that he thinks they did the right thing while acknowledging many disagree and deferring to the Covid inquiry; he applies the word "bonkers" specifically to the later tier system, whose boundaries proved impossible to draw fairly[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- His mother died in September 2021 while he was still handling the pandemic; he says he grieved her but had to be mindful that many households across the country were losing loved ones too[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- On Partygate, Johnson says calling it a party is "a complete travesty," that he was fined for standing at his desk in the Cabinet Room between meetings, that he saw no cake and attended nothing with music or dancing, and that Sue Gray's initial claims of vomiting and a violent altercation turned out not to be true; his stated regret is not warning staff to be seen obeying the rules, and issuing a blanket apology that appeared to validate every subsequent allegation[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- Bartlett refuses the framing, telling Johnson that seeing the photograph while a friend could not attend a grandmother's funeral is enraging, that leadership is about setting the extreme example, and that all gatherings with alcohol, music and cake should have been banned at Number 10 — a point Johnson partially concedes while resisting the idea of banning alcohol outright[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
- In closing quick-fire questions Johnson confirms he has eight children, denies any relationship with adviser Charlotte Owen beyond her being "a very capable adviser," declines to pick between Trump and Harris on the grounds that ex-prime ministers must work with whoever Americans elect, and — asked which relationship he lost in pursuit of success — names his 2016 rupture with Michael Gove while insisting no rupture is final[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].

# Takeaways

Johnson's account is one of a leader who accepts responsibility in general terms while contesting almost every specific charge — he concedes Britain's opportunity gap is real and that jobs-for-loyalty is a bad way to run a cabinet, but rejects the economic case against Brexit, the case that lockdown came too late, and the popular characterisation of Partygate[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc]. The interview is notable less for agreement than for the friction, with Bartlett repeatedly restating an outsider's view — that a top-five cabinet job offered for a vote is corruption, that leading a campaign implies owning the plan, and that Number 10 needed to be beyond reproach — and Johnson declining to fully accept any of the three[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc]. What he does offer without qualification is the levelling-up thesis he traces to a schoolboy observation at Eton, and a claim that political success ultimately goes to those thick-skinned enough to drive things through and deliver what people want done[^boris-johnson-they-were-looking-at-engineering-t-6bL24eHvwoc].
