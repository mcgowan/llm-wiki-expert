# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett interviews Brian Keating, an astrophysicist and professor at UC San Diego, who says he is paid to chase the question that gripped him as a twelve-year-old in upstate New York: how did we get here?[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Keating frames the origin of the universe as the one event for which no witness existed, and argues it is genuinely unsettled science — the cosmos may have begun once, existed eternally, cycled through creation and destruction, or followed something like a biblical narrative — and that only data can decide.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]

The episode's spine is Keating's account of scientific failure. He led BICEP, a telescope at the South Pole built to capture the earliest possible "baby picture" of the universe in microwave light — a pattern that would exist only if space-time had ruptured in an inflationary Big Bang.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] The 2014 announcement made front pages worldwide and took him, as he puts it, to the brink of a Nobel Prize; roughly six months later, in early 2015, the team had to admit the signal was trillions of tons of galactic dust mimicking the pattern they wanted to see.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] He describes this retraction as the most crushing experience a scientist can have, and says the mathematician Jim Simons responded by proposing a merger, producing the $200 million Simons Observatory now taking data in Chile — which achieved first light a month before Simons died.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]

Much of the conversation is a two-way argument about belief. Keating calls himself a practicing, devout agnostic and a practicing Jew who says you cannot prove God exists or does not exist, and who values religious practice for what it does to behaviour rather than for certainty.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Bartlett, religious until 18 and agnostic since, pushes back repeatedly, and Keating in turn presses him on what an agnostic actually does differently from an atheist.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Along the way they cover simulation theory, alien life, astrology, the scale of the cosmos, and the imposter syndrome Keating says afflicts even Nobel laureates.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]

# Key points

- Keating explains the Big Bang's evidential basis through Edwin Hubble's use of a 100-inch telescope north of Hollywood: red shift, the light analogue of the Doppler effect that makes an ambulance siren drop in pitch, showed every galaxy receding from the Milky Way, overturning roughly 2,000-2,500 years of belief in an eternal universe.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- He brings meteorite specimens to the studio to make the point that heavy elements are forged in stars and scattered by supernovae — the iron in hemoglobin came from an exploded star, and chlorophyll is nearly the same molecule with magnesium at its centre instead of iron.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] His parting gift is a 1.524-gram fragment of Mars, verified by matching its composition to what landers measure on the planet.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- BICEP had to go to the South Pole because water absorbs microwaves — the same physics that makes a microwave oven heat coffee — and Keating admits the team knew the stakes were high enough that they could fool themselves into seeing what they wanted.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- He notes that when the discovery was announced, theologians declared it proof of a creating God ("fiat lux") and militant atheists including Richard Dawkins declared it proof that no God was needed — both camps publishing in major newspapers about the same result — which is why he does not expect any future detection to settle the theological question.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- Keating argues religious practice pays even under uncertainty: he doubts he would give ten percent of pre-tax income to charity without the commandment, says gratitude blessings before and after meals defend against taking beauty for granted, and holds that burying the dead is the highest mitzvah precisely because the dead cannot reciprocate.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Asked what he would change if it were proven God does not exist, he says nothing, because the benefits are too substantive.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- He calls it a form of child abuse to tell a child a rainbow exists because God made it, arguing that the correct move is to chase the causal chain — droplets, chemistry, refraction, wavelength, electromagnetic radiation — and admit God only at the point where the honest answer is "I don't know", which lands you back at the Big Bang.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- Explaining simulation theory from Nick Bostrom, he walks through vision as pure signal transduction — photons to cone and rod cells to optic nerve, the retina being the only part of the brain outside the skull — to argue that sufficiently cheap compute could feed a brain an indistinguishable reality.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] His objections are infinite regress (who simulated the simulators) and irreducible complexity: unlike an Airbus A320, Earth's weather cannot be built from simpler parts, and simulating it properly would need another planet.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] He notes a simulated world would be discretised into voxels rather than continuous, so a fine enough measurement could in principle detect the pixels, with gamma-ray bursts already used for such tests — and says there is currently zero evidence.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- To convey scale, he says that if the table between them were the Milky Way, the entire solar system out to Neptune would be about half a grain of sand, and there are roughly 100 billion such "tables" — more stars in the observable universe than grains of sand on every beach on Earth, and around 10 to the 24th planets.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- Despite that, Keating says possibility is not probability and puts a very high likelihood on humanity being alone.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Voyager, launched in 1977, is only one light-day away after about 50 years while the nearest star is four light-years — 1,200 times farther.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] He offers Antarctica, 12-13% of Earth's land with water, heat and hydrocarbons but essentially zero permanent population, as a lesson in how habitable-looking space can be empty.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- His anthropic argument rests on three ordered bombardments: a planet-sized impactor forming the Moon (giving tides that he says moved ocean chemistry onto land), comets delivering water to about 70% of Earth's surface, and the Yucatán asteroid killing the dinosaurs 65 million years ago and clearing the way for mammals — with Jupiter and the Moon acting as bodyguards absorbing other strikes.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] Six such events at one-in-10,000 odds each, he argues, already yields a number smaller than the count of planets in the universe.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- On imposter syndrome, Keating relays 2017 Nobel laureate Barry Barish saying he felt unworthy signing the laureates' ledger beneath Feynman, Curie and Einstein — then adds that Einstein felt the same about Isaac Newton, and Newton about Jesus Christ, to the point of choosing to die a virgin.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] His conclusion is that imposter syndrome is normal and idols are just people.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
- Asked for the meaning of life, he says it is to do as many things as possible whose removal would devastate you — for him his children — and that before he had kids the meaning was winning a Nobel Prize to outdo his own scientist father, a goal that lost its force once his father died.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]

# Takeaways

Keating's position, as he states it, is that the origin question is now an empirical one, and that he simply wants the truth regardless of which side claims it — while accepting that whatever the Simons Observatory finds, believers and atheists will both read it as vindication.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] His personal settlement is behavioural rather than doctrinal: practise gratitude, community, charity and discipline because of what they demonstrably do to a life, and hold the metaphysics loosely, since he considers proof in either direction impossible.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI] The episode's emotional centre is failure and its aftermath — a retracted discovery that became a larger observatory, a firing that he says led him to his wife, and a chain of Nobel-winning heroes who all felt like frauds.[^brian-keating-im-spending-200-million-to-explore-LiIs5X56JMI]
