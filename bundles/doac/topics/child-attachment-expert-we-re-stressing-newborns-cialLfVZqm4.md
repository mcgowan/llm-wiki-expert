# Overview

Psychoanalyst, social worker, and author Erica Komisar draws on more than 30 years of clinical practice and research in attachment theory, neuroscience, and epigenetics to argue that society is "lying" to parents about children's needs[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4]. Her mission rests on three P's — presence, prioritization, and prevention: children need physically and emotionally present caregivers during two critical windows of brain development (zero to three and adolescence from nine to 25), and she ties the modern child mental health crisis — one in five American children, one in six in the UK, developing serious mental illness — to a cultural turn toward careers, materialism, and "me, me, me" that has left babies' irreducible neurological needs unmet[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].

Komisar makes deliberately controversial claims she frames as "inconvenient truths supported by research": that mothers and fathers are hormonally different and not interchangeable; that daycare in the first three years raises cortisol and drives aggression, anxiety, and attachment disorders; that quality time without quantity is "a narcissistic fantasy"; and that ADHD is largely not a genetic disorder but a fight-or-flight stress response, treated with medication she considers malpractice[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4]. The episode also covers attachment styles in adult relationships, big-T versus little-T trauma, repair through therapy, policy failures on paid leave, the plight of boys and men (including testosterone declines in caregiving fathers), and technology's effect on adolescent brains[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].

# Key points

- The guest cites longitudinal attachment research showing roughly 80% of children insecurely attached at 12 months remain insecurely attached 20 years later, with correlations to mental illness — making attachment security in the first three years, when 85% of the right brain develops, the "emotional core" of future mental health[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- She argues mothers and fathers serve hormonally distinct roles: oxytocin makes mothers sensitive, empathic nurturers who regulate a baby's sadness and fear, while in fathers it drives playful tactile stimulation that regulates excitement and aggression; vasopressin attunes fathers to external threat (citing a UK study where mothers woke to the baby's cry and fathers to rustling outside)[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- Fathers can learn to be primary attachment figures, she says, but only if society admits the sexes differ; she tells same-sex couples one partner must take the "mother" role of sensitive empathic nurturing and the other the "father" role of play and exploration[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- She describes three attachment disorders as coping strategies: avoidant (baby turns away; later linked to depression and inability to commit), ambivalent (clinging; later anxiety), and disorganized (no strategy, cycling through all; correlated with borderline personality disorder, self-harm, and the hardest to treat)[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- On ADHD's roughly 20-fold rise in UK diagnoses (2000-2018) and one-in-nine US children diagnosed, she argues ADHD is mostly a chronic fight-or-flight stress response: early separation, daycare, and sleep training activate the amygdala "precociously," which grows large then burns out while the hippocampus (the stress off-switch) stays small[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- Against heritability claims (twin studies putting ADHD at 74-80% heritable), she counters there is no direct genetic precursor to ADHD, depression, or anxiety (unlike schizophrenia and bipolar disorder) — only a "sensitivity gene" (short serotonin-transporter allele) whose expression she says is neutralized by sensitive, present nurturing in the first year and exacerbated by neglect[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- She calls medicating children "cutting the grass" and pain management, urging parents whose child gets an ADHD diagnosis to seek parent-guidance therapy and identify psychosocial stressors (daycare, divorce, fighting parents, a new sibling, parental mental illness) before psychiatry; the host cites ACE research showing four or more adverse childhood experiences quadruples parent-reported ADHD[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- Daycare, she claims, raises children's salivary cortisol and increases aggression, anxiety, behavioral problems, and attachment disorders; children don't need peer socialization before age three (they only parallel play), so the care hierarchy is primary attachment figure first, then kinship bonds (family or a consistently invested caregiver), with institutional daycare "the least good option"[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- She debunks "quality time": children need you on their time, not yours — "you can be physically present but emotionally checked out, but you can't be emotionally present if you're not physically there enough of the time" — and advises parents to choose flexible, service-oriented careers during the early years, sharing how she cut back to 90 minutes of patient work a day and "did without" materially while her children were small[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- On policy she says "America sucks" as the only country besides Papua New Guinea without paid maternity leave, advocates at least one year of paid leave (noting Hungary, Slovakia, and Estonia offer up to three years and Sweden's 14 months still falls short), and floats letting parents borrow against Social Security to stay home[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- Discipline, in her method, is empathy before structure: acknowledge the child's feeling first ("I can see you really want the sweets"), then hold the limit as a calm "broken record" — an emotionally regulated parent, she says, produces an emotionally regulated child[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- On men and boys she argues schooling favors girls (boys are labeled ADHD "just for being little boys" and would fare better with movement-heavy or single-sex early education), that the feminist movement's gains came with a "vengeful" devaluing of male purpose, and cites a Philippine longitudinal study of 624 men in which new fathers' testosterone fell roughly 30-35%, with heavily caregiving fathers lowest — trends she links to male depression and purposelessness[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
- Healing is relational, she argues: "it takes a relationship to cause the trauma and another relationship to repair it" — favoring long-term psychodynamic therapy over quick fixes, distinguishing big-T trauma from the more common "little T" relational trauma (subtle neglect, a dissociated or depressed parent), and revealing her own mother's loving but dissociative presence as the personal root of her mission[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].

# Takeaways

Komisar's closing charge is that caregiving is the one thing parents cannot delegate: "delegate your accounting, delegate your laundry… but spend time with your children — their mental health depends upon it"[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4]. She urges employers to give primary caregivers maximum leave, flexibility, and part-time options even at the cost of "exact parity" with childless staff, and tells parents that life is long — careers can wait, but the window for building a child's emotional security is short. She accepts that nearly everything she says is controversial, saying she would rather speak inconvenient truths than be liked, because unaddressed early stress produces the broken adults she has treated for three decades[^child-attachment-expert-we-re-stressing-newborns-cialLfVZqm4].
