# Overview

Speaking for the first time at length since his twelve-round war with Conor Benn, boxer Chris Eubank Jr tells Steven Bartlett he expected to "blow him out of the water" but instead found himself in what he calls a historic fight between "two men who were willing to die in that ring" — he claims he and Benn landed roughly 1,500 punches, more than the combined 1,400 landed across three recent headline fights involving Devin Haney, Canelo, and Ryan Garcia[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM]. He repeatedly declines to name the personal and health issues that left him looking diminished in the early rounds, insisting fighters must "fight through the demons" without excuses, but he details the mechanics that drained him: a $1 million fine for being 0.5 lb over the middleweight limit, and a rehydration clause capping him at 10 lb of regain when he would normally regain 14-15 lb — a restriction he accepted only because the Saudi backers paid him an eight-figure purse, at least five times his usual couple of million[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].

The emotional core of the episode is his father. The guest recounts years of estrangement that began when he sent Chris Eubank Sr a letter declaring "I am the boss" of his own career — which "broke" the proud patriarch — compounded by the death of his brother Sebastian, who drowned in Dubai after a suspected cardiac event while swimming[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM]. After two months of his father publicly calling the fight a sham and him a disgrace, Senior texted the night before the fight; when they met at the hotel, he asked "What do you think I want from you?" and then answered "I want nothing" — a moment the guest says gave him "those few extra percentages" and produced the now-iconic joint ring walk that left grown men in tears[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].

Eubank Jr also describes his post-fight hospitalization for severe dehydration — his perspective reset by overhearing a doctor say a neighboring patient would die without immediate surgery — his refusal to shake Benn's hand over the unresolved doping case, his hatred of Billy Joe Saunders and distrust of Eddie Hearn's Matchroom (whose CEO, Frank Smith, is his sister's partner), and his hopes for a Benn rematch, Canelo, and Saunders as his final fights[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].

# Key points

- The guest says he was "technically sound" for seven rounds, but once a headbutt cut left him unable to see, he abandoned boxing for "war mode", telling his corner "It's too late" when they urged him to jab — he claims he threw around 300 punches in the final two rounds on an empty tank because "what you choose to do in these last few rounds is what the people will remember you by"[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- He cites the statistic that he and Benn landed about 1,500 punches, versus roughly 1,400 total across the recent Haney, Canelo, and Garcia fights combined[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- He admits Benn was faster, stronger, tougher, and more mentally willing than expected — "I didn't know he had any dog in him" — and believes the rematch will be easier now that he has "lived with him for 12 rounds", citing his revenge win over Liam Smith as precedent[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- On the estrangement: he wrote his father a letter rather than speak, so it could be read without interruption, declaring independence over his career; Senior's reaction — "you don't want me to be part of your career anymore? Then bye" — plus Sebastian's death and Senior's subsequent marijuana use widened the gap for years[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- The night-before hotel meeting nearly didn't happen — he almost ignored the text — and he was terrified his father would ask for money, which "would have killed me"; instead Senior said "I want nothing", and afterwards slept on a stretcher in the hospital hallway for two days while his son recovered[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- His famous press-conference answer about pain — contrasting the weight cut with his brother buried in Dubai, nephew Raheem asking why his dad can't take him to school, and his idolized father calling him a disgrace — was, he says, the first time his real life broke through his cold persona, and it is why fans wept at his ring walk[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- Raising Raheem has transformed him: he now wants a biological son, saying the love he feels around the boy exceeds anything from poker wins, fights, or travel[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- Financials: an eight-figure purse (versus a usual couple of million), a $1M fine for missing weight by 0.5 lb, pay-per-view bonus tiers in the contract, a rematch clause that pays him more, and his admission that he named a price, Turki Alalshikh said yes immediately, and he undersold — "the Saudis don't have normal money"[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- He refused to shake Benn's hand after the fight because Benn failed two voluntary anti-doping tests for clomifene in 2022 and never owned up; even if the contamination story were true, he argues, an athlete's job is knowing what goes in his body, "so you're still a cheat" — and he calls fighting on PEDs "a form of attempted murder"[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- The £100,000 fine for the egg slap was "worth every penny" — he frames it as "an act of justice" designed to brand Benn's doping case into public memory forever[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- In hospital he was so dehydrated his face swelled and he couldn't urinate for hours despite IV drips; hearing a doctor say the patient in the next bay needed surgery "now, otherwise he's going to die" ended his self-pity instantly[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- Reacting to the old family video of Senior insisting a privately educated boy could never be hungry enough to box, the guest agrees champions usually come from poverty with no other doors — his own path was harder precisely because he always had easy exits, and his father's doubt put a "massive" chip on his shoulder[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
- He says he will never again speak to Frank Smith — Matchroom's CEO and his sister's long-term partner — accusing Matchroom of trying to sabotage his career because he signed with rival promoter Boxxer, and he names Billy Joe Saunders as one of the few people in boxing he genuinely despises and wants to fight to avenge a contested loss[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].

# Takeaways

The guest presents the Benn fight as proof of his lifelong creed: pain tolerance is built in unseen moments — finishing a treadmill run with a cramped foot, returning to sparring after beatings — so that when a fight demands everything, quitting is not an option, because "there's no amount of money that can buy" the pride of having given your all[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM]. But he insists the night's true meaning was the reconciliation — "you only get one father" — with fans telling him afterwards that watching the Eubanks reunite prompted them to call their own estranged fathers[^chris-eubank-jr-i-was-paid-for-the-fight-the-nig-OsAlLgGf9JM].
