# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett interviews Andrew Bustamante, a former CIA officer who spent seven years in the Air Force as a nuclear missile officer and seven years in the agency's clandestine service before founding Everyday Spy, a company that teaches espionage skills as tools for ordinary life.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] Bustamante opens by insisting on precise vocabulary — handlers are the officers who collect secrets, assets are the foreigners who supply them, and "spy" is a loose popular term — and states plainly that anyone listening will learn how to manipulate people, but that the more useful skill is detecting when it is being done to you.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]

A substantial part of the conversation is autobiographical and unusually blunt about why the CIA wanted him. He describes a father killed in a violent crime before he was born, a mother he calls cold who once told him she loved his stepfather more because loving a son is obligatory and loving a husband is a choice, and a childhood in which secrets and lies became normal.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He says the agency told him openly during training that he was recruited because he was, in his words, a little broken — a documented link between childhood trauma and high performance, plus what the service calls moral flexibility.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]

From there Bustamante lays out the frameworks he says he now runs his business on: RICE for core motivations, SADRAT for converting a stranger into an asset or a customer, perception versus perspective, the public/private/secret life model, and stress inoculation as the cure for fear.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] The episode closes on geopolitics — his belief that World War III is already under way as a proxy conflict, that US-China parity is the most dangerous outcome for Americans, and that he intends to move his family out of the United States by 2027.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]

# Key points

- Bustamante says manipulation and motivation are two sides of one coin, and that if you feel bad about manipulating someone you will not do well in intelligence, because moral flexibility replaces good and bad with utility.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] His core motivations model is RICE — reward, ideology, coercion, ego — and he ranks their power in that order of strength as ideology first, then ego, then reward, with coercion weakest.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He says films get this backwards: hold a gun to someone's head and they never trust you again, but appeal to their ideology and they will act for you for years.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He also insists ego is not only for the vain — Mother Teresa, he argues, wanted to be seen sacrificing.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- On detecting lies, he dismisses two popular ideas outright: eye-movement direction and microexpressions are not reliable indicators.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] What works is "time on target" — establishing someone's baseline and watching the delta under pressure — and he uses Bartlett himself as an example, noting he was a different person ten minutes before the cameras started.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] His practical tells: bad liars talk a lot and make statements, good liars talk a little and ask questions — if you left a party fascinated by someone you learned nothing about, he says, you were talking to a very good liar — while unskilled liars cannot stop moving, the origin he suggests of phrases like "hot seat" and "shifty".[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- The advice behind the episode title: do not trust your gut, because perception comes only from your own five senses and is emotional, while perspective gathers objective data points from outside you.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He claims about 90% of people are trapped in perception, and that simply having the vocabulary of trained versus untrained puts a listener ahead.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- SADRAT — spot, assess, develop, recruit, handle, terminate — is the human intelligence pipeline he says he converted directly into his company's sales and marketing process, crediting it with 300% growth every year for three years.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He argues most businesses skip "assess", chasing any customer rather than the cohort with lifetime value; Bartlett confirms the same lesson from his own agency's client-filtering framework.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- On influence, he separates messaging from narrative: messaging is emotional and targeted at an ideology, narrative is the logical structure the emotion delivers, and he says this is how presidents get elected and how nations justify wars — while most businesses still think marketing means brighter packaging on more shelves.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- The public/private/secret life model underpins recruitment: the public life is what you show everyone, the private life is what partners and close friends know, and the secret life holds what you tell nobody.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He says everyone secretly wants someone in their secret life, and that once you are admitted, the person never stops trusting you even if you later betray them.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- His conversational tools include physical mirroring to manufacture subconscious trust, "windows and doors" — offering a partial real vulnerability that the other person can push on rather than inventing a fake one — and the two-in-one combination of two follow-up questions plus one confirming statement, repeated, which he says makes people volunteer information indefinitely.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He maintains that the person asking questions controls a conversation, not the person talking most.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- On his own background, he describes serving 72-hour shifts 100 feet underground with a launch key on a ring, says he would have turned it if a valid order came in, notes crews never know whether an incoming order is a drill, and recounts being recruited via a pop-up while applying to the Peace Corps in 2007 — his ethnicity mattering, he says, because the post-9/11 Commission had criticised the agency's focus on Ivy League Caucasian graduates.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He also says disguises are internally called costumes and come in three levels — light, long-term physical change, and prosthetic — with the prosthetic level rarely used because adhesives fail in heat and cold and because police finding fake ears in your hotel room is far more incriminating than a ball cap.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- He says the CIA actively wants people with anxiety, calling it a superpower because anxious people are naturally paranoid and hyper-observant, and that officers are trained through "stress inoculation" — repeated engineered scares that slow the emotional brain and speed up the rational one.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] His advice to listeners is to inoculate against small, low-stakes fears first rather than the biggest one.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- On operational detail, he says the US and UK do not use coerced sexual espionage while Russia and China do, and that sex creates an untenable power shift because oxytocin undermines handler control.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He recounts believing with high confidence in 2011, after hours running a surveillance detection route, that a foreign team was preparing to apprehend him.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- Despite the episode's title, Bustamante says his target date is 2027, not 2030: he argues the United States is in a national adolescence, unsure whether it wants to be a full democracy or treat everyone equally, and he wants his children to reach their teens somewhere — Europe, the Middle East, Latin America — where they can pursue anything.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He argues the most likely US-China outcome is parity rather than Chinese dominance, and that parity is precisely when it becomes most dangerous to live in either country, because each becomes the other's number one target; he also says World War III is already happening as a proxy war, with Ukraine standing in for the West versus Russia and Taiwan set to do the same for the West versus China.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
- Asked what he has changed his mind about, he says he used to believe people could be equal and now believes equality is not what anyone actually wants — that people publicly demand equality while privately seeking an edge, and that he is now comfortable in an unequal world because he can see through the noise.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]

# Takeaways

Bustamante's framing throughout is that espionage skills are not exotic but simply an accurate model of how people already work, and that the advantage goes to whoever knows the model.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] He is explicit that this is a zero-sum view: when Bartlett notes most people know they should face their fears and still do not, Bustamante answers that he wants them to stay where they are, because an economy needs people trapped in the consumer cycle and their inaction is his edge.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0] His closing counsel is to take any action at all, however wrong, on the grounds that nine in ten people will take none — and to distrust your own self-assessment, since you examine yourself through a magnifying glass while everyone else is standing ten feet away.[^cia-spy-leave-the-usa-before-2030-why-you-should-QVVe2rCHtN0]
