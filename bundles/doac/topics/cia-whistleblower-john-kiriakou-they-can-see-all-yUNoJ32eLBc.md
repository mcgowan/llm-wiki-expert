# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews John Kiriakou, a former CIA analyst and counterterrorism operations officer who spent 15 years at the agency — including serving as chief of CIA counterterrorism operations in Pakistan after 9/11 — and who became famous for blowing the whistle on the CIA's torture program in a nationally televised ABC News interview.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc] Kiriakou recounts being recruited in graduate school by a professor who was a CIA officer undercover, his years writing for the President's Daily Brief on Iraq, and the tradecraft of recruiting spies — the "spot, assess, develop, recruit" cycle — illustrated with stories of turning an Al-Qaeda fighter through simple human kindness and befriending a foreign official at headquarters' direction.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]

His central warning is about surveillance: he argues that spying on Americans, once strictly illegal, now consumes billions of dollars across the NSA, CIA, FBI, and contractors, and that "nothing is secret" — agencies can simply buy citizens' metadata from carriers without a warrant, and the Vault 7 WikiLeaks documents showed the CIA can turn smart TVs into microphones and remotely take control of car computer systems.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc] He describes his own prosecution — charged with espionage after his torture revelations, driven into bankruptcy by $2 million in legal fees, and ultimately serving 23 months on a plea deal — as an example of how the government can ruin anyone it targets, and says he would do it all again.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]

The wide-ranging conversation also covers the CIA's documented history of illegal experimentation (MKUltra, Operation Midnight Climax), the Tuesday-morning "kill list" meetings under Obama, Russian sleeper agents, Israeli intelligence operations from the Hezbollah pager attack to his belief that Jeffrey Epstein was an Israeli "access agent," and his view that China — patient, infrastructure-focused, and quietly letting America spend itself into bankruptcy — is the West's real long-term adversary.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]

# Key points

- Kiriakou blew the whistle on the CIA's torture program because superiors kept repeating that torture worked and saved American lives, which he says was "illegal, immoral, unethical" and simply untrue; he credits his revelations with enabling the McCain-Feinstein anti-torture amendment of December 2014, a point John McCain acknowledged on the Senate floor.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He says CIA director John Brennan pushed Attorney General Eric Holder to "charge him anyway" with espionage despite prosecutors' doubts; facing a 98.2% government win rate (per ProPublica) and bankrupted by $2 million in legal fees, he took a plea to a lesser charge and served 23 months.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- Until 9/11 spying on Americans was against the law; now, he claims, billions are spent on it, and law enforcement no longer needs a warrant — agencies can simply buy metadata from carriers, and per Harvey Silverglate's book "Three Felonies a Day," the average over-regulated American unknowingly commits three felonies daily, giving the state leverage over anyone it dislikes.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- Citing the 2017 Vault 7 WikiLeaks disclosures by a disgruntled CIA software engineer, he says the CIA can turn a smart TV's speaker into a microphone even when the set is off, remotely commandeer a car's computer to crash it, and plant false-flag Cyrillic clues in hacks — and he stresses every major country has similar capabilities.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- Internal CIA studies, he says, found 95% of recruited spies do it for money, with the remainder motivated by love and family, ideology, revenge, or excitement; the agency deliberately hires people with "sociopathic tendencies" (not full sociopaths) who will operate in legal and moral gray areas.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- After 9/11 his budget was effectively unlimited: the CIA paid $10 million to Pakistani intelligence for the analysis that led to the capture of Abu Zubaydah in March 2002, and one individual source received $25 million within 24 hours for a high-ranking terrorist.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He describes the CIA's pre-1975 record — MKUltra dosing its own employees with LSD (some of whom died by suicide), Operation Midnight Climax using prostitutes to lure men to safe houses for drugging, and releasing a virus over San Francisco that sent 11 people to hospital — noting the CIA director destroyed most MKUltra files in 1973 in defiance of the Church Committee.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He states the US still assassinates people: under Obama, John Brennan ran weekly "Tuesday morning kill list" meetings at the White House to select targets said to pose a clear and present danger — a standard Kiriakou calls far vaguer in practice than it sounds.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He estimates 50,000-60,000 spies operate in the United States (American and foreign combined), with perhaps 10,000 foreign spies in Washington alone; Russia, China, and Israel are the biggest collectors against the US, and he says the FBI has identified 187 undeclared Israeli intelligence officers, mostly at defense contractors.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He calls Israeli intelligence the most impressive and most ruthless service — citing the Hezbollah pager operation and the 12-day-war tactic of paying destitute Afghan refugees in Iran $200 a month to locate generals and nuclear scientists via their cell phones — and believes "very strongly" that Jeffrey Epstein was an Israeli access agent whose camera-filled island gathered kompromat on powerful men.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- He identifies China as the West's real adversary: patient 25-year planners who steal technology partly through PhD students ("I'm 1,000% sure" some spy, he says, because they are frequently arrested and traded), buy influence through infrastructure across Africa, and let the US bankrupt itself on a Pentagon budget larger than the next eight countries combined — about $1 trillion a year and rising.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
- His practical advice to ordinary people, quoting Eliot Spitzer: never put anything in a text message, and never say or write anything — even in jest — you would be ashamed to see on the front page of the Washington Post, because it can be taken out of context and used against you.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]

# Takeaways

Kiriakou's through-line is ethics: he argues America is "a country of laws" that stopped obeying its own laws around 2001 — torture that had been a death-penalty offense in 1946 was suddenly declared legal "because we're the good guys" — and that either the US is the shining city on a hill or it is not.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc] For individuals, his message is that digital privacy is an illusion and self-protection means discretion in everything you say and write. He closes on a personal note: he is seeking a presidential pardon, and says the decision that most improved his life was to stop feeling sorry for himself after prison and bankruptcy and rebuild a career as a writer and podcaster.[^cia-whistleblower-john-kiriakou-they-can-see-all-yUNoJ32eLBc]
