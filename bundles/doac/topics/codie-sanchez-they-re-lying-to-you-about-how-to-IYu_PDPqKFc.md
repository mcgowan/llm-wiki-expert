# Overview

Codie Sanchez spent more than fifteen years in investment banking and asset management before leaving finance to write online about buying laundromats, car washes and similar unglamorous companies — a niche she says unexpectedly found a mass audience. Her message, as she puts it to host Steven Bartlett, compresses to a single line: the only way to have freedom is through ownership, and the world does not want to give it to you.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc] She frames the message as landing at a moment of collapsing institutional trust, citing figures she had read on US cable news audiences and the observation that thirty-year-olds now trust social media voices over mainstream media.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]

Asked how she would rebuild wealth from zero with no contacts, Sanchez rejects the tactic-first answer. Her sequence is: attach yourself to someone already successful and relentlessly deliver value to them; treat your twenties as a period of learning rather than earning; leap between jobs whenever learning or earning stalls; and only then move toward ownership, first as an insider with equity, then as a buyer of cash-flowing businesses.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc] She is explicit that she would do zero startup investing until someone is already a millionaire, on the grounds that seed-stage failure rates are punishing.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]

The acquisition thesis rests on demographics. Sanchez argues that retiring baby boomers own most American small businesses, that most have no succession plan, and that if ordinary people do not buy those businesses the wealth either evaporates or is absorbed by large asset managers.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc] Alongside that sit her operating beliefs: speed as a competitive edge, obsession as the real predictor of performance, and a conviction that a bad product — not a marketing shortfall — is what actually kills most companies.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]

# Key points

- Sanchez's core claim is that ownership is the only route to freedom, and that her message is aimed at people tired of others directing their lives; she says it will not resonate with BlackRock, Blackstone or billionaires who already understand it.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- She cites collapsing trust in institutions, pointing to an article claiming a US prime-time cable news show drew roughly 83,000 viewers cumulatively over a week of three-hour segments — a record low — against a high of around 186,000 for its main competitor.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- Her advice for twenty-somethings is that the decade genuinely does suck, that a $65,000 job for two or three years is the normal price of entry, and that people should optimise to "learn rather than earn" and front-load the pain while stamina is high.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- She invokes research on "economic interconnectedness" — poor neighbourhoods that contain a few rich residents produce children who are roughly 35% richer thirty years on than demographically identical neighbourhoods without them — as evidence that proximity to wealth is itself contagious.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- Her paradigm-shift story is discovering that a former Goldman Sachs colleague had effectively bought a US ambassadorship, which she says costs roughly a million dollars donated directly plus a couple million to a PAC — the moment she concluded money is simply power.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- On access, she recommends "concentric circles": approach the richest person on your block or the wealthiest parent at your school rather than famous people, because "go where the water is the shallowest" — the owner of a sprinkler company has no competing inbox.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- She describes a "10x rule" of giving ten times before asking, and a follow-up habit — one-sentence questions, then reporting back on what she implemented — that she says built a relationship with a State Street executive over roughly twenty exchanges across two years.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- Her interview red flags are speaking badly of a previous boss, victim-framing about why things failed, and asking about work-life balance; she deliberately describes the job as worse than it is so nobody joins with inflated expectations.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- On acquisitions, she says a business can be bought with cash, expertise or sweat equity, and that seller financing — the seller paid out of future profits, her example being a $300,000 business paid off over five years from $100,000 annual profit — accounts for around 60% of small business sales.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- She argues more than 50% of US small businesses are boomer-owned, around 65% of those owners have no transition plan, roughly 11 million are for sale, about 70% will never sell, and since some 90% of a small owner's net worth sits inside the business, the celebrated wealth transfer is mostly business equity rather than cash.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- Her "gateway drug businesses" are ones simple enough to explain to a grandmother — laundromats, car washes — while her current interests include senior care homes (helped by grants and demographics, at three to five times normal rent) and low-capital service businesses such as window cleaning, where she calculates $384 a day over five days a week yields a $100,000-a-year business.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- On speed, she credits mentor Bill Perkins with the idea that his only edge is being faster, and describes a 24-hour rule in her companies; on product, she insists there is no leads problem, only a bad-product problem, testable by whether 20–30% of customers refer or review — her "three Rs" being referrals, reviews and retention, illustrated by Scrub Daddy and by Facebook's "seven friends in ten days" activation metric.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- She and Bartlett converge on cross-pollination as the real wealth lever: moving one skill into a scarcer, higher-margin market — Bartlett's example being social media skills moved from fashion e-commerce to pre-IPO biotech, paid in options, and Sanchez's being a copywriter versus a medical writer at roughly $35,000 against $150,000.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
- On prejudice, Sanchez's position is that difference is memorability rather than handicap; she does not deny that people will judge on race or gender, but argues that adopting a victim frame repels the wealthy people you want around you.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]

# Takeaways

Sanchez's argument runs from mindset to mechanics: believe money is a tool rather than a moral stain, buy proximity to people richer than you, learn deal-making before doing a deal, and then take ownership of something small, boring and already profitable. She is candid that none of this is fast or comfortable — her repeated formulation is that upfront pain leads to long-term gain, and that people who refuse to hurt early hurt for the rest of their lives.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc] Throughout, these are her views and her business interests, including the acquisition marketplace and book she promotes in the episode.[^codie-sanchez-they-re-lying-to-you-about-how-to-IYu_PDPqKFc]
