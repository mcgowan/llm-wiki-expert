# Overview

Michael Dowd, the former NYPD officer known as "America's dirtiest cop", walks Steven Bartlett through a decade of crimes committed in uniform during New York's crack epidemic — robbery, extortion, burglary, drug dealing, and ultimately paid protection of major trafficking organizations — crimes he estimates may number in the thousands[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. The guest argues his corruption grew inside a broken incentive system: joining in 1982 at 21 on roughly $18,000 a year, he says officers were effectively told not to make drug arrests because each crack arrest averaged 18 hours of overtime the city couldn't afford — he made only 43 arrests in ten years when he believes around 500 were warranted — leaving the streets "unwieldy" and open to a cop with "entrepreneurial spirit" who couldn't arrest dealers, so taxed them instead[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. He describes an academy culture where an instructor advised recruits to ignore ethics rules and "just cover your ass", and a "blue wall of silence" enforced by the reality that a cop who snitches can't count on backup when he's being beaten in the street[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].

From a first $200 "lobster lunch" shakedown of an unlicensed motorist in 1983, Dowd escalated to stealing from crime scenes with his sergeant's tacit blessing, selling confiscated cocaine, extorting the Dominican street organization La Compañía for $8,000 a week (and facing down its boss in the street after they put a hit on him over a $700 shortfall), and finally protecting kingpin Adam Diaz — who was selling roughly $50 million of cocaine a year — for $24,000 up front plus $8,000 weekly, more, he notes, than the US president earned[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. The guest describes tipping off raids, suppressing a robbery scene so stolen drugs and cash were returned to the dealers, and the guilt after Officer Venable was killed by La Compañía associates — grief he processed by locking himself in his bathroom to cry, which drove him into rehab[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].

Arrested in May 1992 — the day after the Rodney King riots, with cocaine in his pocket at his own drug test — Dowd calls the moment "the biggest relief of my life" because it ended the lies; out on bail he was then lured by his wired-up ex-partner Kenny Eurell into a planned robbery-kidnapping, re-arrested, and sentenced to 14 years, serving 12 years and 5 months[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. His testimony to the Mollen Commission — where he admitted both police and drug dealers were his "employees" — preceded some 200 officer arrests for drug trafficking, and the episode closes with recorded interviews with his elderly parents, including the revelation that his undemonstrative mother went to church every day for the 12 years he was inside[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].

# Key points

- The guest frames his crimes as spanning robbery, extortion, and burglary — "every time you take something from somebody" with a gun on your hip is robbery — including stealing a murdered man's porn collection from a crime scene, and he admits to having sex on duty in his police car[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- He claims arrest incentives drove corruption: one crack arrest averaged 18 hours of time-and-a-half overtime against roughly 150,000 arrests a year in Brooklyn, so the department preferred officers on patrol over arrests — he was reassigned to a desk for arresting too many people — and he made just 43 arrests in ten years[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- Academy "integrity training", he says, was really about not getting caught; his instructor told the class that following internal affairs' rules would prevent a successful career — "just cover your ass"[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- The blue wall of silence, in his telling, is survival logic: the first person to snitch on you will be a cop, but cops avoid it because reporting a partner means risking that no one backs you up on the street[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- His first money crime was soliciting a couple of hundred dollars from a paperless Puerto Rican driver in 1983 by hinting he "liked lobster"; a later turning point came when Sergeant James Otto told him of $600 pocketed at a murder scene: "If I don't see it, it's yours… throw me something later on"[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- His partner discovered that confiscated "trash" was sellable cocaine, and Dowd progressed from fencing seized drugs to buying from dealers to resell — "you become a market maker"; his single biggest score was $40,000-50,000, double his annual salary, though Diaz later chided him for leaving $150,000 behind[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- He drew a line he claims made him trustworthy to civilians: anyone not in the drug business got diligent police service, "but if you were in the drug business, you were mine" — cars, coats, gold, cash[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- When La Compañía shorted his $8,000-a-week protection fee by $700, he blockaded their bodegas with police cars; they put a hit on him, and he says he pulled over the boss and offered a ten-pace shootout — the hit was called off within twenty minutes[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- For Adam Diaz he admits he could actually do "nothing really" but delivered anyway: he once cleared a bodega ("shut it down") about ninety minutes before a 30-40 detective narcotics raid found nothing, and he closed down a robbery scene and made cops return bags of cocaine and cash to Diaz's stash house[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- The murder of Officer Venable by La Compañía associates — the first on-duty police death in East New York, he says — triggered crushing guilt, secret crying sessions in his bathroom, deeper drug and alcohol use, and rehab; afterwards other cops shunned him as a suspected informant, and isolation pulled him back into corruption[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- His 1992 arrest felt like freedom — "I no longer have to live a lie" — but facing a threatened 25-to-life state sentence, he took the bait of a scheme to rob a woman of half a million dollars and 10 kilos to repay his family's $350,000 bail and flee to Nicaragua; Kenny Eurell was wearing a federal wire, which converted him "from a drug dealer to a kidnapper" in prosecutors' framing[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- Sentenced to 168 months, he served 12.5 years believing "every day… the next day I might go home"; he left prison at 44 with nothing, unable to get a job, not knowing his own sons or his nephews' names, and initially wanted to go back inside[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- At the Mollen Commission he refused to name officers, warning "a lot of cops are going to commit suicide", but admitted to hundreds of crimes; around 200 officers were arrested for drug trafficking in its wake, and he claims corruption today has shifted from street-level scores to "massive" systemic corruption at the top of the bureaucracy[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
- Asked what 18-year-old Mike needed to hear, he answers "I'm proud of you and I love you" — tying his choices to a lifelong hunt for his stern mother's approval, and saying he only learned a year ago that she had prayed for him in church daily throughout his imprisonment[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].

# Takeaways

The guest's retrospective argument is that he "became the environment" — a product of perverse incentives, easy money, and a culture of mutual cover — while insisting this explains but never excuses the behavior[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. His counsel now is that honesty is cheaper than lying — "you have to remember the lies every day" — that every act eventually carries a consequence, and that what people ultimately need is love and someone who listens; he says former officers on the edge of suicide contact him because he speaks openly about his disgrace, which he treats as his purpose[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8]. The host closes by reflecting that his own desperation for success could have tipped him toward crime, crediting fear of consequences with steering him into business instead[^corrupt-cop-i-had-sex-with-girls-in-my-police-ca-NSsx6mkiaf8].
