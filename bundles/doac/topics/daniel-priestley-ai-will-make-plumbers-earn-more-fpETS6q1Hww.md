# Overview

In his seventh appearance on The Diary Of A CEO, serial entrepreneur and author Daniel Priestley (whose latest book is "Lifestyle Business Playbooks") tells Steven Bartlett that after 25 years of building companies through the dot-com bust, the global financial crisis, Brexit, and COVID, he has never seen more simultaneous excitement and fear than in the current AI moment, which he compares to the transition from the agricultural to the industrial age.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww] His optimistic frame rests on the Jevons paradox: just as YouTube destroyed tens of thousands of Hollywood jobs but created 500,000-600,000 new ones, AI's collapse of costs will spawn millions of tiny, niche businesses — small SaaS companies with 500 customers and two-person teams, bundled with communities, events, and media — that could never have existed before.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]

His bear case is financial rather than technological. Priestley argues that data centers, unlike railways or roads, have only a 3-4 year lifespan; with roughly $650 billion being spent in the year ahead and history showing that infrastructure build-outs exceeding 3% of GDP have repeatedly bankrupted economies, he predicts a massive financial meltdown around 2029 — a hundred years after the Great Depression — with packaged data-center debt sold to pension funds as private credit amplifying the damage.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]

On work, he contends the pendulum is swinging from devalued blue-collar trades back toward them: as AI guts routine white-collar roles (he resolved a £50,000 legal matter himself using Claude for $20 a month), scarce plumbers, electricians, and bricklayers — a shortage he blames on government-distorted university-loan incentives — may regularly out-earn lawyers.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww] The pair also debate AI content saturation, the exodus of millionaires from the UK, UBI, and what remains "irreplaceably human": lived experience, real-world community, and relationships.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]

# Key points

- Priestley invokes the Jevons paradox against pure doom: disruption transforms rather than eliminates opportunity — journalism jobs fell ~80% in 25 years, but he estimates three to four times more people now earn money doing journalist-like content work; similarly, software companies once needing 50 staff, millions in funding, and 10,000 customers can now thrive with two people and 500 customers.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- Unlike the Industrial Revolution, AI rolls out instantaneously on existing internet infrastructure — the moment an AI learns to be a lawyer or diagnose a disease in one place, it can do so everywhere — and robotics (Tesla's pedal-less Cybercab, humanoid factory robots) is arriving at the same time as the "replacement of the human brain."[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- His central financial warning: data-center GPUs last only 3-4 years versus 100 years for rail and 50+ for roads; ~$650 billion of spend in the coming year (equivalent, he says, to giving every American an iPhone Pro that 95% of them won't pay for, and most payers only $20/month) with capex at hundreds of percent of revenue makes a crash likely — he predicts 2029, and suggests governments may end up bailing out and owning the data centers.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- He argues the personal-brand window is closing like fog rolling into an airport: creators already "above the fog" (with communities, events, books, podcasts) can keep flying, but new entrants face a plateau in human attention (Gen Z's time online has flatlined) colliding with exploding AI-generated content — Bartlett's own data shows algorithms increasingly reward the best individual post over follower counts, the shift from social media to "algorithmic media."[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- What survives commoditization, both argue, is irreplaceably human content and connection: lived experience ("an AI can tell you what happens in menopause; it can't tell you its experience of menopause"), parasocial relationships like streamers build, standing on stage, hosting dinners — informational content becomes a commodity while relationship-building content and real-world ecosystems remain defensible.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- The most important skill set to learn now, he says, is entrepreneurial thinking — a six-step "value creation loop": founder-opportunity fit, validation, product-market fit, go to market, scale, exit; he illustrates validation with two waiting-list experiments (750 vs 4,500 sign-ups) that let him raise about £250,000 from angels within roughly a week on the stronger idea.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- On lawyers: he resolved a legal dispute quoted at £50,000 using Claude for $20/month (coaching, decision trees, documents, negotiation scripts), and notes reports of legacy legal-tech firms losing ~20% of their value in 2026; future lawyers, he argues, become part business coach, part prompt engineer.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- He claims government-subsidized student loans created a market distortion — pushing young people into ~£280 billion of largely unrepayable debt for degrees "no one was asking for" while starving the trades — so the blue ocean is now being a tradesperson, and blue-collar roles may be the most elevated by the AI economy.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- Politically he argues bottom-up markets beat top-down planning: UK government spending at 45-50% of the economy, youth unemployment up over 25%, and record millionaire outflows (citing Henley data: 3,200 net leaving in 2023, 9,500 in 2024, a projected 16,500 in 2025) matter because the top 1% pay ~30% of the bills; he points to New York mayor Mamdani's proposed 9.5% property-tax rise as what happens when high payers leave.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- He warns of an AI-era "Engels' pause" — the industrial-age half-century when wealth flowed to the top before wages caught up — and thinks short-term UBI may be economically feasible given AI deflation, while noting Sam Altman's own funded UBI study found recipients worked fewer hours and earned less; humans, he says, need meaningful struggle.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- Practical advice: build a modest personal brand (2-20,000 people who know what you do), experiment with entrepreneurship at small scale, give AI your hardest problems rather than using it as a search engine, and consider buying baby-boomer businesses — 65% of business wealth is held by over-65s who must sell within 10-20 years and will often finance the purchase.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
- He champions the small "lifestyle business" (2-20 people, $1-5M revenue) as the sweet spot of the new economy — easier than ever to build, while big businesses face constant disruption — and urges generalism over specialization, "pause, reflect, document" thinking with pen and paper, and finding what only you can say from lived experience.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]

# Takeaways

Priestley's closing counter-narrative is hopeful: AI may commoditize standardized work, but it also makes it cheaper than ever to run a small, creative, location-independent business serving a few hundred clients — and society shouldn't grieve the loss of "dehumanizing" jobs if the transition is managed.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww] The conversation ends on mortality: recounting a loved one's stroke and the treasured "it's not lost on me" voice notes he left behind, Priestley argues there are no happy endings, that businesses are poor substitutes for family, and that beneath all the technology talk "the whole game is relationships" — so treasure the happy moments and the people you share the rock with.[^daniel-priestley-ai-will-make-plumbers-earn-more-fpETS6q1Hww]
