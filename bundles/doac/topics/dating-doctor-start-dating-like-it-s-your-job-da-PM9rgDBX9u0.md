# Overview

Dr Orion Taraban is a California-licensed clinical psychologist whose practice focused for years exclusively on men's mental health, and whose book *The Value of Others* frames relationships explicitly as an economic exchange. He opens by telling host Steven Bartlett that the most salient thing happening in the world is a relationship crisis: marriage at historic lows in the United States, half the world's population living in countries below replacement fertility, and — more surprisingly — fewer casual sexual relationships being transacted than in previous decades.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0] Because the culture places sex before commitment, he argues, less casual sex means fewer marriages and children downstream.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]

His diagnosis for men is invisibility. Taraban's blunt formulation is that relationship opportunities do not accrue to good or virtuous people but to people others want something from — which leaves young men, with no money, skills or track record, useless to women seeking long-term partners and to older men seeking capable teammates.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0] For women, the problem he sees most in consultations is the difficulty of converting attention into commitment from the small subset of men they actually want.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]

Much of the conversation is tactical — marketing, seduction, approach anxiety, the transition from attraction to durable relationship — but the through-line is Taraban's insistence that value exchange is the honest description of what relationships are, that love is a separate and non-transactable thing, and that moralising about pornography or OnlyFans will change nothing without understanding what problem those behaviours solve.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]

# Key points

- Taraban cites a 250% increase over ten years in relationships initiated online — from roughly 20% of Americans meeting online to over 50% — alongside a simultaneous collapse in relationships formed, which he calls correlative but strongly suggestive.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He argues dating apps are tools requiring strategy and massive action, and that using them casually produces infuriation and hopelessness, especially for men.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- On the "part women always leave out," he says women genuinely do want kindness, generosity and safety — but only from men they are already attracted to, so pursuing those traits as a route to attraction is a category error.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- His practical attractiveness advice is unromantic: hygiene, fitness, and two or three good outfits costing a thousand or two, on the grounds that being the best-dressed man in the room is a free point where a woman might spend tens of thousands on surgery for the same gain; he adds that the less conventionally attractive you are, the more you must learn seduction.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He distinguishes masculine communication (semantic, confirmed when the content is understood) from feminine communication (emotional resonance, confirmed when a comparable feeling is provoked — his tuning-fork image), and attributes many long-term arguments to men responding to content when their partner is trying to transmit a state.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- Rather than money, he names renown as the most reliable attractor, drawing on his own years as a stage actor in New York where being the name on the marquee made him what he calls the "contextual alpha" — effective for short-term interest, useless for marriage prospects.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He claims most men blow the first date by talking too much and thereby destroying the fantasy that brought the woman there; his prescription is a "slow and gradual disappointment," and he identifies the "crisis of disappointment" as the point at which a relationship actually begins.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- On the disregulated marketplace, he says that when women are empowered to make their own sexual choices they target the top 10% of men — a pattern he compares to elephant seals and wild mustangs, and notes some 85% of human cultures have been polygamous — which leaves both those women unable to secure commitment and average men without options.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He teaches a negotiation exercise from his NYU acting training called "the game of please/no," in which one player may say only "please" and the other starts from "no," to demonstrate that tone, gesture and expression carry the real persuasion — and that the way someone says no contains the clue to how they might say yes.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- Contrary to intuition, he says asking someone for a small favour raises attraction more than doing favours for them, because behaviour generates emotion rather than only following it.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- On women initiating, he argues they historically did so indirectly with plausible deniability — the Victorian dropped handkerchief — and that sustained eye contact remains the minimum viable signal, noting how aggressive the same gesture reads between strange men.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- His pornography argument is about sublimation: civilisation is built on redirected libido, so keeping men in a permanently sexually satisfied state removes the drive that pushes them to take risks, apply for jobs and build things — and he says shame has never worked as an intervention, insight is insufficient, and the only remedy is stopping plus a functional analysis of what boredom or loneliness the habit was solving.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He treats OnlyFans as the mirror-image trap, observing that it may now be easier for the average woman to get one dollar from a million men than a million dollars from one, and compares both to processed food — a close enough substitute that many can no longer justify the cost of the real thing.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- His headline advice is to date like it's your job: wanting a loving, secure, peaceful relationship is as demanding as wanting a job that is high-paying, low-stress and fulfilling, so expect to send the equivalent of 200 résumés, get coached, and compress the search into three or four months rather than years.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]
- He argues against compromise in favour of selection — 90% of successful relationships, in his view — and describes deliberately stripping his own criteria back to sex, sufficient attraction, and a soft femininity unavailable in platonic relationships, after a mentor pointed out he did not need a romantic partner to discuss quantum mechanics.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0]

# Takeaways

Taraban's model is deliberately unsentimental: people move toward those they want something from, so becoming rich in relationships means cultivating things others want and being honest about what you are seeking in return. He allows that the transaction must stay implicit to preserve dignity — the same reason companies talk about mission rather than pay — but insists that meeting a partner's actual needs matters more than any amount of communication technique.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0] He sets love, loyalty and friendship outside the model entirely, as gifts that cannot be bought or earned, and closes on forgiveness as an emotional survival strategy he undertook for his own sake rather than anyone else's.[^dating-doctor-start-dating-like-it-s-your-job-da-PM9rgDBX9u0] These are his professional views and framings as offered in the episode.
