# Overview

Steven Bartlett convenes a three-way debate between journalist Louise Perry, psychoanalyst and parenting author Erica Komisar, and Guilty Feminist podcast host Deborah Frances-White on whether the sexual revolution and modern feminism have served women well[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA]. Perry frames the revolution as two entangled events — an ideological rejection of traditional Christian sexual norms in the 1960s-70s, and material changes (the pill, safe legal abortion, washing machines and domestic technology) that pulled women en masse into the workforce — and argues its promise of freedom conceals real harms, from "rough sex" defenses in women's deaths to a hookup culture that suits average male preferences far better than female ones[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA]. Komisar, a self-described "maternal feminist", contends second-wave feminism damaged children by denigrating motherhood and pushing daycare, citing figures that 72% of young men and 82% of young women report depression and anxiety after casual sexual encounters[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA]. Frances-White, who spent her adolescence inside a patriarchal religious cult, counters that the revolution gave women agency, autonomy, and emotional freedom, that domestic abuse statistics puncture any nostalgia for pre-revolution monogamy, and that blaming feminism for modern unhappiness hands ammunition to the manosphere at a moment when Roe v. Wade has been overturned[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].

The debate ranges across the 2025 Lost Boys report on struggling young men, whether mothers must be home for a child's first three years, government childcare policy that funds only daycare, falling birth rates among progressives, and pornography — the one topic on which Perry and Komisar would "press the button" to abolish the industry while Frances-White resists, arguing regulation rather than erasure of human sexuality[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA]. The guests repeatedly find partial agreement — all three want caregiving valued, all three criticize unregulated capitalism — but split on whether structure and rules protect women or re-imprison them[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].

# Key points

- Perry opens with the "We Can't Consent To This" campaign she co-founded, arguing rising cases of men escaping murder convictions by claiming women consented to fatal "sex games" (usually strangulation) show why sexual freedom must be balanced by partially illiberal laws[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Komisar cites research she wrote up for the Institute for Family Studies claiming 72% of young men and 82% of young women feel depressed and anxious after casual sex, and argues hookup culture turns something sacred into "something irrelevant, unimportant and dangerous"[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- The host notes UK approval of casual sex rose from 10% in 1999 to about 42%, and that average first-marriage ages climbed from roughly 21 (women) and 23 (men) in the 1970s to over 30 for both[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Perry argues campus sex ratios reveal women's true preferences: campuses with more men have more conservative sexual cultures, while female-majority campuses tilt toward hookup culture because scarce men set the terms — evidence, she says, that women engage in hookup culture "because they sort of have to"[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Frances-White, drawing on escaping a cult in her mid-twenties after losing her virginity late, rejects both extremes: chastity-until-engagement atrophied her capacity for intimacy, and she insists "there is no utopia here, there's only options"[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- She counters the monogamy idyll with UK domestic abuse data: a police call every 30 seconds, under 24% of domestic abuse crime reported, one in four women experiencing abuse, and nearly two women killed per week[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Perry's agency argument: sexual liberation assumes everyone is highly agentic, but most people — especially socially attuned, mimetic young women — follow prevailing scripts, so "give everyone choice and say good luck" fails and society must be somewhat prescriptive[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Komisar calls daycare under age three "day orphanages", claiming children need a primary attachment figure (usually the mother) for the first three years for emotional regulation, that 85% of the right brain develops by three, and that rising depression, anxiety, and ADHD are downstream attachment failures; she blames Betty Friedan's "concentration camp with comforts" framing for turning feminism against motherhood[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- On the manosphere, the host cites the 2025 Lost Boys report from the Centre for Social Justice: boys half a grade behind at GCSE, male youth suicide roughly 3.5 times female rates, 15.1% of young men NEET, and 16-24-year-old women now out-earning male peers by nearly 10%[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Perry attributes male malaise to technology and economics — the collapse in the value of male physical strength — rather than feminism, arguing women want a man who "provides excellent insurance" and predicting AI will deepen the disruption; the host adds dating-app data that higher-earning men receive 2.5 times more interest[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Perry attacks UK policy that channels women into work: single-earner families are taxed more heavily, and childcare subsidies flow only to daycare — never nannies or grandparents — which she calls removing choice; she cites figures that about 66% of UK mothers (60% in the US) would prefer to stay home more[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Komisar argues hormonal differences (oxytocin's differing effects in mothers and fathers, vasopressin in men) make maternal and paternal nurturing genuinely different, proposes 50/50 gender quotas in US college admissions to rebalance the marriage market, and defends maternal guilt as a healthy signal of a functioning ego — a claim Frances-White calls piling more guilt on already overburdened women[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- Perry warns of demographically self-defeating feminism: religious conservatives now far out-reproduce progressives, so "if feminism cannot reproduce itself… feminist societies wither and die", making a fertility-friendly, motherhood-respecting feminism a survival imperative[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- On pornography, Perry calls modern porn a "super stimulus" that degrades real sex and an industry built on the "sexual torture of women for profit" that feminism has disgracefully endorsed; Komisar would abolish video porn but spare two-dimensional erotica; Frances-White argues banning it means banning human sexuality and calls instead for regulation[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
- On raising boys: Perry wants specifically masculine virtues channelled through things like combat sports and chivalry revived; Frances-White wants empathy and humanity taught regardless of gender and warns against reimposing provider-protector scripts; Komisar says boys are neurologically more fragile than girls, are mis-educated "like little girls", and may need separate schooling when young[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].

# Takeaways

The closing statements crystallize the three positions: Perry, citing Mary Harrington, sees today's gender turmoil as a renegotiation triggered by the digital revolution, insisting men and women "are going to have to learn to get along" or the species won't reproduce; Komisar defends freedom of choice with the limit that children must come first and no woman's choice be demeaned; Frances-White credits the sexual revolution with agency, autonomy, and emotional freedom, urges women to define their own structure, and warns that dividing feminists against each other only serves a rising far-right that threatens abortion and LGBTQ rights[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA]. The host closes by noting his own opinion shifted during the conversation and praising the guests for debating across genuine disagreement rather than in echo chambers[^debate-feminist-women-vs-non-feminist-women-ZHuZ_8VYCWA].
