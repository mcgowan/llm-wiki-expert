# Overview

In this return appearance on The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett interviews James Sexton, a New York divorce lawyer of 25 years and author of *How to Stay in Love*, whose previous conversation on the show he says reached close to ten million people.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] Sexton's organising idea is "slippage": relationships do not usually collapse from a single blow but from an accumulation of small unremarked choices, which he compares to Hemingway's line about going bankrupt gradually and then all at once, and to the way a suit stops fitting without any one meal being to blame.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]

A large part of the episode is about endings generally rather than divorce specifically. Sexton describes a childhood shadowed by his mother's rare cancer — repeated six-month prognoses beginning when he was six or seven, seven surgeries, and her eventual death after more than a year in hospice — which led him to volunteer at hospice at 21 and to write a master's thesis at NYU on the language people use to avoid naming death.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He argues that the cultural refusal to discuss death and the refusal to discuss divorce are the same refusal, and that neither silence prevents the thing from happening.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]

From there he makes his practical case: that love is loaned rather than gifted, that prenuptial agreements are simply the choice between rules two people write and rules politicians wrote, that performative relationships on social media are actively making people unhappy with real ones, and that his most controversial position is that children should not be a person's greatest accomplishment.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]

# Key points

- The case that haunts him is one he won and believes he should have lost: representing a pimp accused of severely beating the mother of his children, where the state-assigned opposing lawyer — paid roughly $25 an hour against his $750 — could not phrase the question needed to admit a photograph of the woman's injuries into evidence, he objected as his job required, and the judge let it happen.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] His client's remark afterwards was that a good lawyer is better than 20 stickup men.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- Sexton argues acceptance begins with acknowledgement, not justification, and uses the Zen line that if you do not learn to find joy in the snow you will have less joy and precisely the same amount of snow.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He tells clients that roughly 86% of divorced people remarry within five years, and laughs at those who swear they never will.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- On patterns, he says he has looked hard for predictive combinations — same or different religion, culture, race, age gaps, length of engagement — and found none, with one exception: substance use, which either escalates in both partners or splits them when one scales back and the other does not.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] The fastest marriage-to-dissolution he has handled was 72 hours.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- Played a Jordan Peterson clip advising husbands to listen to their wives 90 minutes a week or listen in divorce court, Sexton pushes back that framing it as an endurance quota is the problem — "there was a time when you couldn't wait to hear her" — and equally rejects "happy wife, happy life" as the resigned model that helped produce a 56% divorce rate.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- His alternative is a chapter he calls "hit send now": raise irritations promptly, ideally in writing so it can be edited and so the partner is not cornered into defensiveness, and never open with "we need to talk".[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He praises a friend's weekly walk where each partner names a few things the other did imperfectly, agreed in advance to be heard with love, always closing with three things that made them feel loved.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- On sex, he argues long-term couples deaden it with the best of intentions: you learn what your partner likes and then play "the greatest hits" out of efficiency and kindness, creating a routine in which any deviation now triggers suspicion — where did that come from, are they watching porn, are they cheating.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] His suggested workaround is to raise a desire obliquely, as a dream you had, so either party can retreat without it becoming a confrontation.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- He estimates infidelity is a component in at least 80% of the cases that reach his office, and says technology has changed both the opportunity and the evidence — social media supplies "neutral entry points" for private conversations, while shared Apple IDs mean a lover's text surfacing on a child's iPad is close to a weekly occurrence in divorce practices.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- Sexton says he loves weddings but does not believe in the legal institution of marriage, calling it largely performative and observing that no guest ever asks to inspect the marriage licence.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He calls marriage not a bad idea but a dangerous one, comparing a 56% failure rate unfavourably to skydiving and to an airbag that deploys 30% of the time, and argues it is strange that asking someone "why are you getting married?" is considered rude.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] On prenups, his framing is that everyone already has one — written either by the government or by the two people who chose each other out of eight billion — and that politicians who won a popularity contest and change constantly are poorly placed to author the rules of your marriage.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He recounts that no wedding expo would sell him a booth, however respectfully he offered to word his materials, because the topic shatters the illusion.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- He is scathing about performative relationships, describing celebrity clients in openly fake or purely financial partnerships who give interviews about their happiness while negotiating dissolution, and arguing that comparing a real relationship to a stylised one is like judging your sex life against pornography.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] His summary image is a culture with white teeth and rotting gums.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- His own marriage ended, he says, after a moment at a theme park watching friends walk off holding hands and realising he did not feel that about his wife — and years later, discussing the divorce amicably, she volunteered that she had thought exactly the same thing at exactly the same moment.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] That couple has now been married 28 years, still calls each other boyfriend and girlfriend, survived metastatic breast cancer, and is, he notes, conspicuously unperformative.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- On money, he explains that child support is modifiable roughly every three years on a 30% income change, gives New York percentages of gross income (17% for one child, 25% for two, 29% for three, 31% for four), cites a client receiving $65,000 a month covering tuition and a security detail, and says the recipient parent is not required to produce receipts.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He warns that having a child with someone is riskier than marrying without a prenup, citing a client worth several hundred million who spent about $100,000 in fees arguing over the boundaries of Thanksgiving.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
- Asked for his most controversial opinion, Sexton says children should not be a person's greatest accomplishment — if reproduction is the highest achievement in each generation, that is "the ideology of a virus or a cancer cell" — while stressing he is not an antinatalist and loves his sons.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] He argues that people obsessed with their children stop paying attention to themselves and their partner, that childhood is a temporary state while a spouse is meant to remain, and that a strong marriage is itself a gift to children.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]

# Takeaways

Sexton's single piece of advice, when Bartlett asks how to avoid ever sitting in his consultation room, is to pay attention — specifically to three separate things: the you, the me, and the we.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] Each partner must be allowed to remain the person the other fell in love with, and the relationship itself must be treated as a valuable thing that requires watering rather than as a settled achievement.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ] Underneath the legal detail, his argument is consistently the same one: every relationship ends in death or divorce, love is on loan rather than owned, and the honest conversations people avoid — about prenups, about dissatisfaction, about mortality — are precisely the ones that would have saved them, because the hard thing and the right thing are usually the same thing.[^divorce-expert-slippage-is-tearing-marriages-apa-oAgGirY-GUQ]
