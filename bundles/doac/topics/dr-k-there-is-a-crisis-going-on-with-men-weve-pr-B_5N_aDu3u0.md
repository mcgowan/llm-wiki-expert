# Overview

Dr Alok Kanojia — known online as Dr K — is a Harvard-trained psychiatrist who spent years studying in an Indian ashram before medical school, and who tells host Steven Bartlett that he arrived at psychiatry through his own failure: video game addiction, dropping out of college, and finishing his education at 35 or 36.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0] His central framing is that people assume something outside them will fix something inside them, and that psychiatry is the one branch of medicine losing ground — addiction, depression and suicidality all worsening despite advances in neuroscience.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]

The bulk of the conversation concerns what he calls a crisis among men. He argues it is not new — around 80% of suicides have been male for a century — but that it is worsening, and that its mechanism is not primarily mental illness. Technology, he says, has externalised attention so thoroughly that men no longer have internal signals to consult about who they should be, leaving them dependent on contradictory external messages about masculinity.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0] Compounding this, he claims men are effectively forbidden from articulating suffering, which he compares to a child told to shut up in an abusive household.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]

His proposed remedies are unfashionable: individual introspection rather than societal fixes, curiosity rather than condemnation toward men drawn to online masculinity influencers, and meditation understood properly — as impulse control and focus training that strengthens the frontal lobes addictions prey on.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0] He is careful to say that men's struggles and women's struggles are not a zero-sum pendulum, noting that female suicidality and addiction are also rising.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]

# Key points

- Kanojia's headline statistic is that one study suggests 50% of men who kill themselves have no history or evidence of mental illness — consistent, he says, with his clinical experience that many suicidal men are assessing an objectively unliveable situation rather than suffering a malfunctioning mind.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He identifies "thwarted belongingness" — attempting to join a group and being rejected — as the variable that correlates most strongly with suicide, more than depression.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- His mechanism for the crisis is externalised attention: a thousand years ago a hunter had hours to process an emotional insult, whereas constant podcasts, earbuds and screens leave no processing time, so internal signals atrophy like photoreceptors in a child raised in a dark cave.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He describes anger as men's "umbrella emotion" — the only feeling they are socialised to express — and notes it is also the one emotion society now demonises, so shame, fear and sadness all surface as anger and then as online misogyny.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- On online masculinity influencers, his claim is counterintuitive: the people who hate them drive men toward them, because manosphere figures are the only ones who validate that a man's life is hard rather than telling him he is privileged and should stop complaining.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He advises parents worried about a son consuming that content not to ban it but to ask what he likes about it — noting that in his clinical experience 100% of the incels and toxically masculine men he has worked with began with trauma, while people raised with love and connection do not go down that path.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He cites the double bind men face — roughly 70–80% of women wanting a partner who earns more, while around 60% of college graduates are women — as producing expectations many men cannot meet.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- Kanojia insists the remedy cannot be delegated to society or government, because men have already solved everything solvable alone; what remains — loneliness, touch starvation, sexlessness — requires another person, and "you can't hug yourself."[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- On relationships generally, he argues human bonds were historically built on mutual survival need, and that once groceries, work and even sexual substitutes can be obtained without leaving the house, relationships decay — yet the mind is the only organ that requires another human being to stay healthy.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He defines addiction as two things at once — a source of pleasure and an antidote to pain — and says meditation strengthens the frontal lobes whose weakness makes addiction fertile, while teaching tolerance of pain rather than avoidance.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- On pornography specifically, he names meaninglessness in life as one of the two strongest correlates of addiction, and says use rises with stress because porn suppresses negative emotion rather than merely supplying dopamine — describing patients who keep work on one screen and pornography playing on a second.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- He distinguishes meditation technique (dharana, e.g. trataka candle-gazing) from the state of consciousness it induces (dhyana) and the transcendental states beyond it (samadhi), comparing the confusion to thinking you can "do" sleep rather than create conditions for it; he claims there are 112 techniques and that failure usually means a mismatched technique or a poor teacher — his ADHD patients succeed by speeding the mind up in a cafeteria rather than slowing it down.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- On helping others, he cites a mentor's line that "the extra mile is a mile too far," warns that taking responsibility for someone makes them take less, and recommends motivational interviewing — pushing back gently, since people with ambivalence move opposite to whichever side you take; he adds that his free patients were the most likely to no-show.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]
- His "25% rule" is to halve a goal and halve it again, on the grounds that large goals tank motivation because today's effort never registers as progress — a point Bartlett connects to Dave Brailsford banning Team Sky riders from thinking about podiums.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0]

# Takeaways

Kanojia's argument is that the male mental health crisis is a listening failure before it is a clinical one: men are not primarily ill, they are unheard, and the only groups meeting them where they are happen to be the ones society most wants to silence. His prescription runs inward — introspection, meditation as trained attention, and tolerating rather than fleeing pain — while his practical advice to listeners is that connection cannot be outsourced to institutions.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0] He closes on unusually personal ground, describing a sense of having lost a child in a past life and framing love as the deliberate acceptance of possible loss.[^dr-k-there-is-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-weve-pr-B_5N_aDu3u0] These are his clinical opinions and spiritual convictions as expressed in the episode.
