# Overview

Eric Schmidt joined Google in 2001, when it was doing around $100 million in revenue, and stayed through its growth into a company worth well over a hundred billion.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c] He tells Steven Bartlett that his new book *Genesis* — co-written with Henry Kissinger, who finished the last chapter in his final week of life on his deathbed — argues that the arrival of artificial intelligence is "a question of human survival," because humans have never before had an intellectual challenger of their own ability or better.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c] Schmidt frames his own career as luck of timing: the computer he used in college was, he says, 100 million times slower than a modern phone and served an entire university, and Moore's law defined the wealth, careers and companies of his generation.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]

Roughly half the conversation is business advice drawn from Google. Schmidt's first rule is that you need a truly brilliant person to build a brilliant product, and that he was not that person — his job was to ally with them.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c] He distinguishes "divas" like Steve Jobs, difficult but genuinely trying to solve the problem, from "knaves" acting only for themselves, and argues that innovation almost never happens in the same building as the core business — citing the Macintosh team's separate building with a pirate flag on top.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c] He is skeptical of "founder mode" as a slogan but insists only founders can make company-killing bets, pointing to Mark Zuckerberg burning enormous sums on VR while simultaneously making the AI investments that turned Meta into a genuine AI leader — something he says is almost impossible for a hired CEO.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]

On AI he rejects the popular idea that AGI arrives on a particular day, predicting instead waves of innovation across fields, and says he has spent effort getting governments involved — remarkable, he notes, because in fifty years the tech industry has never asked government for help.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c] His answer to the question of whether this is the end of humanity is no: eliminating all of humanity is harder than people think, and we survived the World Wars, the Holodomor and the Nazis, however horrific the pain.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]

# Key points

- Schmidt's advice to an 18-year-old is analytical critical thinking above all, plus learning Python — the language AI systems write code in — and building something using the models' APIs rather than assuming coding is a dying art.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He defines critical thinking as distinguishing being marketed to (which he equates with being lied to) from being given the argument, and says you have a responsibility to check an assertion before repeating it: "if you can't distinguish between true and false I suggest you keep your mouth shut."[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- On social media he describes recommendation systems as "bandit algorithms" optimising an objective function — attention — and notes that the easiest way to maximise attention is to maximise outrage. He cites Herb Simon's 1971 observation that attention would become the scarce resource, and an article arguing all attention has now been monetised, with young people consuming two and a half hours of video a day.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He dates the harm to roughly 2015, when platforms switched from linear feeds to targeted feeds, and links this algorithmic change to record levels of self-harm and emergency room visits among girls hit at 11 and 12. He says he told platform executives there is "good revenue and bad revenue," and that at Google he arbitrarily split quality improvements 50/50 between more ads and a better product.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He describes Google's 70/20/10 rule — 70% core business, 20% adjacent, 10% new ideas — and says the roughly 10-15 person Google Brain team that came out of it generated "10, 20, 30, 40 billion dollars of extra profits over a decade," which pays for a great many failures.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He credits OpenAI's breakthrough not to the Transformer — invented at Google — but to RLHF, humans doing A/B comparisons at the end so the system learns recursively, and says the ChatGPT launch was an "afterthought" while the team worked on GPT-4, an example of brilliant founders not understanding the power of what they had built.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He notes Anthropic was incorporated as a public benefit corporation specifically because its founders worried a future "evil CEO" would be forced to choose revenue over the world's good — evidence, he argues, that the teams anticipated the scale of impact and were right.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- On five-year forecasting he says scaling laws show no sign of stopping yet, that two or three more turns of the crank could make systems 50 to 100 times more powerful, and that raw unreleased models can already perform zero-day cyberattacks as well as or better than humans because they simply keep trying and never sleep.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- On biological risk he says viruses are relatively easy to make, that one can imagine very bad ones being designed, and that he sits on a commission working to prevent it.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He describes warfare being reinvented in real time in Ukraine, where a $5,000 drone can destroy a $5 million tank, tanks are no longer very useful, and combatants will do harm from a command centre while drinking coffee.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He offers two nonproliferation scenarios: if frontier models stay in a handful of guarded data centres — he draws the analogy to a plutonium facility inside another base, past two rings of machine guns — governments can manage deterrence; if the capability spreads cheaply enough to reach terrorists, the proliferation problem is unsolved.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- His specific "pull the plug" conditions are concrete: if recursive self-improvement reaches a point where you no longer know what the system is learning, and if agents stop communicating in human-understandable language and invent their own. He rejects the claim that you cannot switch them off — "there's a power plug and there's a circuit breaker."[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- On jobs he predicts dislocation but more jobs, not fewer, because developed-world demographics mean fewer young people must support more old people and therefore need better tools. He expects automation of dangerous and repetitive roles like security guards, and says Hollywood's costs will fall through synthetic sets and makeup while the stars and producers keep earning.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- He is a critic of universal basic income, arguing the abundance politics behind it assume humans behave differently than they do — automating legal work will not produce fewer lawyers, just more and more complex law.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
- Asked about remote work, he says he strongly wants people in offices, especially in their 20s, because that is how he learned and got promoted — but explicitly concedes the evidence does not support his preference: the data indicate productivity is slightly higher when work from home is allowed.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]

# Takeaways

Schmidt's stated fear is the inverse of the headline: not that AI destroys us, but that "we're not going to adopt it fast enough to solve the problems that affect everybody" — an AI teacher working alongside human teachers in a child's own language and culture, and an assistant giving every doctor access to the best available treatment given their patient's country, insurance and inventory. Doing only education and healthcare globally, he argues, would establish a level playing field of knowledge and opportunity that has been the dream for decades. His advice for individuals is simpler: life is a series of time-limited opportunities, and the one thing he got right at Google was saying yes.[^ex-google-ceo-ai-can-create-deadly-viruses-if-we-2Zg--ouGl7c]
