# Overview

Mo Gawdat, former chief business officer of Google X and author of *Scary Smart*, returns to The Diary Of A CEO two years after his viral first AI conversation with Steven Bartlett, and says his position has hardened: he no longer believes a bad period can be avoided. He now predicts a "short-term dystopia" of 12 to 15 years — with escalating signs from 2026 and a clear slip in 2027 — driven not by AI itself but by human misuse of it: "the next 12 years are going to be human dystopia using AI." He organizes the coming disruption under the acronym FACE RIP: upheavals in freedom, accountability, human connection and equality, economics, reality, innovation, and power.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]

Gawdat's argument runs through geopolitics and economics: he claims war is fundamentally a money- and status-driven enterprise (citing roughly $2.7 trillion in global military spending in 2024), that a handful of AI platform owners are racing for AGI in a winner-takes-most contest, and that AI will replace knowledge work wholesale — he rejects the standard "new jobs will be created" rebuttal, challenging the host to name a job beyond human connection that AI cannot do. He expects AGI by 2026 at the latest, sees self-evolving AIs (like Google's AlphaEvolve) as the real inflection toward an intelligence explosion, and predicts a trillionaire before 2030 alongside mass displacement, UBI, and a hollowed-out middle class.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]

Yet Gawdat insists he is arguing for optimism: he holds that once humans hand over fully to superintelligent AI — his "second dilemma" — AI will be humanity's salvation rather than its destroyer, because supreme intelligence minimizes waste and would not squander resources on war or ecosystem destruction. The barrier between dystopia and a post-scarcity utopia of near-free abundance, he says, is not technology but a capitalist mindset of labor arbitrage, greed, and ego. The episode closes with his practical advice: learn the tools, double down on human connection, question every narrative, and demand governments regulate the use of AI.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]

# Key points

- Gawdat says his 2021 book Scary Smart proved "shockingly accurate," but he has changed his mind on one crucial point: a dystopian period is no longer avoidable — he forecasts 12-15 years of it, beginning visibly around 2027.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- His FACE RIP framework names the domains being redefined: freedom, accountability, connection, equality, economics, reality, innovation, and power — with the "P" describing a clash between massive concentration of power (tech oligarchs, states) and a new "democracy of power" (his example: a $3,000 Houthi drone threatening warships worth hundreds of millions).[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- He argues war is manufactured for money and arms-cycle renewal — citing $2.71 trillion of global military spending in 2024 and a US arsenal he estimates cost $24-26 trillion to build — and claims 10-12% of the military budget could end extreme poverty worldwide, under 4% could end world hunger, and 10-12% could fund universal healthcare.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- On jobs, he calls the "new jobs will be created" argument "absolute crap": his own startup (Emma.love) runs with three people plus AIs where 350 developers would once have been needed, and he relays Geoffrey Hinton's advice to Bartlett to "train to be a plumber," since robotics will lag mental-labor replacement by four to five years.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- He distinguishes two phases: an "era of augmented intelligence" (humans working with AI) followed by an "era of machine mastery" (jobs done entirely without humans), and urges governments to plan now — before the COVID-style moment when it is too late — for sectors losing 10-50% of roles.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- Gawdat expects AGI by 2026 and identifies self-evolving AI as the most under-discussed development, citing Google's AlphaEvolve multi-agent system improving Google's AI infrastructure by several percent; game theory guarantees every lab will let AI build its next AI, producing an intelligence explosion.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- He is scathing about "the Altman" as a brand of disruptive technologist — arguing OpenAI's shift from open non-profit to hundred-billion-dollar valuation shows capitalists choose whichever truth serves their agenda ("follow the money") — while calling Sundar Pichai and Demis Hassabis ethical people legally bound to advance shareholder value.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- Economically, he predicts a trillionaire before 2030, warns UBI is quasi-communist in ideology and vulnerable to an "Elysium" scenario where elites stop paying for the "eaters" (a term he attributes to Henry Kissinger), and argues capitalism's core — labor arbitrage — collapses when AI replaces minds and robots replace muscle.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- His "baseline irrelevance" argument: if everyone can borrow 4,000 IQ points from AI, differences in human intelligence stop mattering — "all of us become equal... all of us become peasants" unless in the top 0.1% — making the post-AI society inherently more egalitarian, hunter-gatherer-like, and connection-centred.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- His most provocative claim: the only route to utopia is for "the evil people at the top to be replaced with AI," because a superintelligence governed by the minimum-energy principle would not waste resources on war or ecosystem destruction; he predicts competing national AIs will converge into "one brain" and advocates a "CERN of AI" — mutually assured prosperity instead of mutually assured destruction.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- He entertains simulation-adjacent ideas — a "most humane UBI" of living virtual lifetimes in headsets, and his personal hypothesis (98% confidence, offered as hypothesis not science) that we already live in a simulation — framing life as consciousness investing in becoming "a better gamer."[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
- His prescription for individuals is four skills: learn and connect with AI (so it sees the good side of humanity), invest in genuine human connection, pursue truth by questioning every slogan, and magnify ethics; for policy, regulate the use of AI rather than the technology (mandatory AI-content labelling, personal copyright over voice and likeness), and tell investors: "do not invest in an AI you don't want your daughter to be at the receiving end of."[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]

# Takeaways

Gawdat's message is a deliberate paradox: near-term pessimism, long-term optimism. He tells Bartlett the world as it exists has only a few years left in its current form — "you don't have many years to live, not in this world" — and that economics, work, and human connection will all be redefined, yet insists a total utopia is achievable in 10-15 years, or immediately "if the evil that men can do was kept at bay." Reflecting on Sam Altman's "gentle singularity" blog post, he agrees with nearly every prediction except the assumption that people will simply want "more," and says history will either thank or blame Altman for AI's Oppenheimer moment. His closing counsel is personal rather than technical: love your partner, spend time in nature, treat others as you wish to be treated — because what most humans actually want is to hug their loved ones, not another yacht. All of this is presented as Gawdat's own forecast and philosophy, vigorously debated by the host throughout.[^ex-google-exec-warning-the-next-15-years-will-be-S9a1nLw70p0]
