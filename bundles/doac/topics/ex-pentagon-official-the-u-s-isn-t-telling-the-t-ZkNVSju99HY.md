# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Luis Elizondo, a career counterintelligence officer who studied microbiology, immunology and parasitology at the University of Miami, enlisted in the US Army, ran terrorism and espionage investigations across Latin America and the Middle East, and later worked for the National Counterintelligence Executive and the Director of National Intelligence before being recruited into the Pentagon's Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program, which he went on to direct for eight years.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He describes being vetted for the role without being told what it concerned, until rocket scientist Dr James Lacatski asked him over his glasses what he thought about UFOs and warned him not to let his analytic bias get the best of him.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] Elizondo says he arrived a sceptic who had never even liked science fiction and changed his position gradually rather than in a single revelation, as the corroborating data accumulated.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]

His core evidentiary argument is about sensor convergence: the encounters he investigated involved trained military observers whose accounts were backed by gun camera footage, forward-looking infrared, and airborne, ground-based and sea-based radar — five, six or seven independent systems recording the same event at the same time and place.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] In a court of law, he argues, that would be well beyond reasonable doubt, and it is the same sensor suite the US relies on to fight and win wars.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He is careful throughout to describe the objects as a national security issue rather than a threat, on the reasoning that a threat requires both capability and intent, and only capability has been observed.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]

The second half of the episode is about institutional behaviour rather than aerial phenomena. Elizondo says he resigned in protest — out of loyalty rather than disloyalty — because senior leadership refused to brief then-Secretary of Defense Jim Mattis while pilots reported near mid-air collisions and ship captains asked what to do about objects their crews could see.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He traces the secrecy to Cold War triage, to government reluctance to admit a problem it cannot solve, to commissioned studies that concluded the public was not ready, and now to a legal exposure problem in which some officials fear admitting they withheld information from Congress and presidents.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He repeatedly stresses that his book Imminent went through a roughly year-long Department of Defense pre-publication review with visible redactions, because he is not a leaker and will not cross the line into unauthorised disclosure.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]

# Key points

- Elizondo lists five observables the programme used to separate anomalies from conventional aircraft: instantaneous acceleration, hypersonic velocity, low observability, transmedium travel, and the absence of any visible means of propulsion or lift.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- On acceleration, he says a human can withstand about 9 G briefly and an F-16 begins structural failure around 17 G, while the objects observed performed in excess of 2,000 to 3,000 G.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- On speed, he contrasts the SR-71 Blackbird, which needs roughly half the state of Ohio to execute a turn at about Mach 5, with objects clocked in excess of 10,000-13,000 mph performing immediate right-angle turns and 180s.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- He explains the terminology shift from UFO to unidentified aerial phenomena and then to unidentified anomalous phenomena: the stigma of "UFO" suppressed pilot reporting, and the objects are neither flying in the aerodynamic sense nor confined to the air, having been observed underwater and possibly in low Earth orbit.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- The official case count has grown rather than shrunk, he notes, from 143 unresolved incidents in the first government report to roughly 300 and then 800.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- He walks through the 2004 USS Nimitz "Tic Tac" encounters, in which the USS Princeton's SPY-1 radar (capable of detecting a baseball-sized object at 80,000 feet), an E-2 Hawkeye, aircraft radar, eyewitnesses and FLIR footage all registered objects dropping from 80,000 feet to hover at 50 feet and then reappearing 60 miles away at the fighters' next rendezvous point.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- The Gimbal video on his book cover, he says, shows an object at roughly 20,000 feet moving at 120 knots against the wind and rotating to a 90-degree orientation without losing altitude, with pilots audibly discussing "a whole fleet of them" — and he adds that far clearer footage exists but stays classified to protect collection sources and methods.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- Elizondo says the US government possesses exotic material that does not look like it was made by humans — a statement he was contractually forbidden to make until the book review cleared it, and one he declines to extend to any comment on Area 51 or Bob Lazar's claims.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- He claims servicemen and intelligence officials have been physically injured by proximity to these objects and are on full US government medical disability in writing, citing John Burroughs of the 1980 Rendlesham Forest/Bentwaters incident, whose medical files were classified until the late Senator John McCain forced their release.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- He points to the 1977-78 Colares incident in Brazil, investigated by the Brazilian military under General Uchôa, where locals and military personnel reported directed-energy-type tissue injuries, and to the 1976 Tehran F-14 case and Huntsville helicopter tests where aircraft systems were reportedly disabled during encounters.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- The nuclear dimension is why he calls it a security issue: the objects appear able to interfere with nuclear capabilities, and he says a KGB report shared during the post-Berlin-Wall intelligence honeymoon indicated a Russian facility was actually switched on rather than disabled.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- On presidential briefings he says Jimmy Carter and George H. W. Bush were briefed while Bill Clinton wanted to be and was not, and he calls it nonsense that unelected officials decide which commander-in-chief gets to know — citing Trump's statement that he faced fierce resistance to releasing UFO files, and suspecting the influence of the military-industrial complex.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- Pressed to argue against himself, he says the only alternatives are foreign adversarial technology — which would require China or Russia to have hidden a 70-year technological lead, the greatest intelligence failure in US history, and which he likens to finding a working 747 in King Tut's tomb — or mass hallucination among the pilots trusted with live munitions and nuclear weapons, which he says would be a worse problem than UAP.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- He resists the word "alien," arguing from the history of biology — fungi recognised only during the Enlightenment, microorganisms only in the last 120 years despite being the dominant biomass — that these things could come from outer space, inner space, the unmapped 90% of the ocean floor, or outside our narrow band of perceivable reality, and warns against anthropomorphic assumptions about their form or motives.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
- His summary analogy: locking your doors and setting the alarm, then finding size-12 muddy boot prints on the carpet in the morning — nobody is hurt and nothing is missing, but "it could be a threat if it wanted to be, so we should probably figure out how it's getting into the house."[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]

# Takeaways

Elizondo's position is that the debate should be about data rather than belief — he tells Bartlett he does not ask his own daughters to believe in UAP, only in evidence, and that both true believers and true skeptics are equally immovable regardless of what they are shown.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He frames the real scandal as procedural: a reporting culture that told personnel to stay silent about incursions over sensitive installations, oversight committees left unbriefed on billions in spending, and colleagues such as David Grusch whose medical records he says were released illegally within 24 hours of going public.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY] He says he now lives in Wyoming, heavily armed and with six German Shepherds, after repeated threats to his life, but insists on working within the law — "there's a difference between doing things right and doing things right now" — and closes by offering Bartlett the Pale Blue Dot photograph as his answer to cosmic insignificance: you can feel insignificant and special at the same time.[^ex-pentagon-official-the-u-s-isn-t-telling-the-t-ZkNVSju99HY]
