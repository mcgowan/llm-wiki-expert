# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Jason Fung, a nephrologist (kidney disease specialist) and author of The Obesity Code, The Diabetes Code and The PCOS Plan, who is widely credited with bringing intermittent fasting into the modern medical conversation.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] Fung says he was trained in the conventional "calories in, calories out" view of weight, found it did not work for his patients — or even for doctors trying to lose weight themselves — and went to the literature to ask a different question: not how to eat fewer calories, but why the body is being instructed to store energy in the first place.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]

His central claim is that food is both energy and instructions, and that the instruction-giver is hormonal, principally insulin.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] Fung describes the calorie equation as true but shallow, comparing it to saying alcoholism is "alcohol in minus alcohol out" or that the Titanic sank because it hit an iceberg — correct at the surface, useless as a solution, because it never asks why.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] He argues the body defends a "set weight" like a thermostat, using satiety signals such as stomach stretch receptors, peptide YY, cholecystokinin and leptin, and that ultra-processed, fibre-stripped, high-carbohydrate food overrides those brakes while spiking insulin, which locks calories away in fat and leaves the person hungry again.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]

From that premise, Fung reframes several standard weight-loss messages as counterproductive: exercise as a weight-loss lever, breakfast as mandatory, and constant small meals as healthy eating.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] He also uses the framework to push back on the stigma attached to obesity, arguing that a population where roughly 70% of people are overweight or obese has an environment problem rather than seventy million individual willpower problems, and that the same hormonal reasoning makes type 2 diabetes a reversible dietary disease rather than a permanent one.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]

# Key points

- Fung argues insulin, not willpower, drives fat storage: injecting insulin as a type 2 diabetes drug makes almost everybody gain weight, which he presents as direct proof that the body stores energy when it is told to.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He says equal calories give unequal instructions — two slices of white bread and jam spike insulin and send the energy straight to fat, leaving you ravenous by 10:30, whereas an egg produces no such spike and keeps the energy available all day.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- On satiety, he points out that a thousand-calorie soda leaves you wanting chips while a thousand-calorie steak does not, because liquid sugar activates none of the stop-eating hormones — and notes there are no obese antelope or lions in the wild, since body fat levels are too important to survival to be left uncontrolled.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- Fung accepts that twin studies put roughly 70% of an individual's obesity risk down to genetics, but insists this cannot explain the last 40-50 years, because the genetics of the world population have not changed while obesity has risen on every continent.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He says calorie restriction fails because metabolism follows intake: eat 500 fewer calories and the body eventually burns 500 fewer — something he says every study over the last 50 years has shown and medicine has known for at least 80 years — producing the yo-yo effect where returning to 1,800 calories after a 1,500-calorie diet causes weight gain.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- On exercise, Fung says he strongly recommends it for strength, flexibility and general health, but that a half-hour treadmill session burns around 120 calories — "the amount you'd get in a couple of cookies" — and that his book puts 95% of weight loss down to diet.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He cites decades of data on "exercise-induced anorexia" followed by rebound hunger, and a Harvard study measuring children's calorie balance that found watching TV came out at roughly +100 calories per hour and mild exercise at about the same, which he reads as exercise driving compensatory eating.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- Fung links the rise in obesity to the 1977 US advice to eat 55-60% carbohydrates and less fat, and to the eating frequency shift Bartlett cites from a survey of more than 60,000 adults and children: three meals a day in 1977, five to six eating occasions a day by 2003, with snacking becoming institutionalised rather than an indulgence.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- On breakfast he argues the word itself ("break fast") shows a daily fasting period is normal, and Bartlett cites page 132 of The Obesity Code, where breakfast eaters averaged 539 extra calories per day compared with those who skipped it; Fung adds that hunger is hormonally mediated, peaking on average at 8pm and hitting its lowest point at 8am despite that being the longest gap without food, because a 5am surge of growth hormone, cortisol and sympathetic tone has already released stored glucose.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He rejects the "starvation mode" objection to fasting, saying measurements show basal metabolic rate rising rather than falling — from around 2,000 calories on day zero to about 2,200 by day four of a fast — as insulin drops and growth hormone, cortisol and sympathetic tone rise; he compares a hungry wolf, which is activated and dangerous, to a lion that has just eaten.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- Fung presents autophagy — the cellular clear-out whose research won the 2016 Nobel Prize in Medicine — as a further benefit of fasting, estimating it activates somewhere around 20 to 30 hours in and is switched off by eating protein, with growth hormone rising perhaps fivefold over a two-to-three-day fast so old proteins are cleared and new ones built.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- On GLP-1 drugs such as Ozempic, he says he has no objection to them and that they prove his point: the drug burns no calories, it removes hunger, so what was being fixed was never the arithmetic but the hormonal signal — every successful weight drug, he argues, is a hormone.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He describes fasting protocols as having no fixed rules — 12-14 hours is merely the 1970s baseline, 16 hours, a six-hour eating window, one meal a day or multi-day fasts are all options — because 100,000 to 200,000 calories of stored body fat exist precisely to be used.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- On type 2 diabetes, Fung calls it largely a dietary disease that drugs cannot fix, and cites Dr. David Unwin's UK data showing about 50% of patients put into complete drug-free remission by cutting carbohydrates, noting the American Diabetes Association acknowledges low-carbohydrate eating has more scientific evidence than any other diet for the condition.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
- He says he was attacked by doctors and dietitians when he began speaking publicly about fasting around 2013-2014, finds it strange that the same doctors prescribe fasting before surgery and blood work, and blames slow adoption on careers built on the calorie model — while emphasising that fasting is a treatment available to everyone in the world for free.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]

# Takeaways

Fung's argument is that the calorie model is not wrong so much as shallow — correct at the level of accounting, useless at the level of causation — and that the productive question is always one layer down: why is the body hungry, and why is it storing.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] His practical conclusions follow from that: change what the food signals rather than only how much of it there is, extend the daily period of not eating so insulin can fall, and treat the food environment rather than the individual as the thing that has gone wrong.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk] He closes by saying he hopes his lasting contribution is helping people reverse type 2 diabetes and breaking the stigma that frames obesity as a personal failing, insisting that none of what he advocates is new — fasting has run through human history and religion, and science is only now catching up.[^exercise-doesn-t-make-you-lose-weight-doctor-jas-8RuWp3s6Uxk]
