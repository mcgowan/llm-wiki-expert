# Overview

Steven Bartlett hosts a return conversation with Andrew Bustamante, a former CIA field officer and founder of EverydaySpy, recorded shortly after the July 2024 assassination attempt on Donald Trump and days after the death of the grandmother who helped raise him — a loss he says shifted his focus from tripling his business to empowering his team and investing in the people who matter.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] His organising metaphor is the shed: most people spend their lives inside one, looking at a bright forest through hazy glass, sensing there is more but unable to prove it, and he says CIA training did not clean the glass or lead him outside but shattered it.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] He insists the system is not a conspiracy — conspiracy implies malicious intent, whereas a society needs conditioning and predictability to function at all — but argues that because different countries build different sheds, no shed is objectively the best, and the person who benefits most is the one who lives inside one while knowing they can step out.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]

The middle of the episode is a geopolitical detour driven by Bustamante's stated lack of altruism: he says he cares about his family and the people making a difference rather than a universal common good, and wants the United States to remain the sole superpower because reaching parity with China around 2035 means instability, the way a playground with two bullies is more dangerous than one.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] He calls the Ukraine war a full-on proxy war by military doctrine rather than a symptom, describes the real US-China conflict as competition to be the world's net exporter of high technology rather than a trade war, and gives a detailed operational reading of why he believes the Butler, Pennsylvania shooting was a genuine attempt rather than a staged one.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]

The remainder is applied tradecraft for civilians: how to gather perspective rather than defend your own perception, how influence differs from persuasion, how relationships move through avoidance, competition and compliance into trust, and how to win an interview by winning the interviewer.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] Bartlett hands him two cold emails he sent as an 18- and 19-year-old — one that won his first investor, one that got Panasonic to ship him free cameras — and Bustamante annotates them live against the RICE motivation framework.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] Throughout, Bustamante's request is not belief but testing: he says he appreciates it when people believe him, but what he wants is for them to run the framework themselves.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]

# Key points

- Bustamante distinguishes information, knowledge and experience as a flywheel — information becomes knowledge, knowledge is tested through experience, experience yields new information — and argues state and school systems deliberately skip the experience step, which is why CIA teaches by educate, exercise, then experience.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- He says the CIA looks for recruits who question the system enough to be taught a different one, and that he himself had "the right amount of trauma" to be loyal to a chosen authority rather than defiant of all authority.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- Perception is what you believe about the world around you; perspective is what the other person believes about theirs — and combining the two doubles your information, which he demonstrates by reading Bartlett's pre-recording small talk back to him, including inferring that Bartlett's partner was more excited about pregnancy than he was from the phrasing "my partner is thinking about babies."[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- Practical tradecraft he names: the "get quiet" exercise of resting your senses before entering a situation so the brain indexes and observation sharpens, and "windows and doors" — choosing which openings in a conversation to follow in order to collect the perspective you need.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- On risk he cites the Perfection Paradox — endlessly improving a plan without acting — against CIA's "excellence through execution," his example being paramilitary teams planning in roughly 48 hours after 9/11 and figuring the rest out on horseback in the mountains.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- He argues against working through trauma when you can go around it, citing a centimillionaire client who realised his mother's affair set off the chain of events that made him self-sufficient and successful — acceptance and forward motion rather than healing.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- His conspiracy anatomy has four parts: a real fact, a gap in information, speculation filling the gap, and a story that closes the loop, because human brains cannot tolerate open loops — and he applies it to the Trump shooting, noting that a genuinely staged attempt would have shot 35-40 degrees off target rather than at the principal.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- He draws a sharp pre- and post-9/11 line on the agency itself: previously a small, well-funded, barely supervised outfit capable of MK-Ultra and the Bay of Pigs, and since the 2003 9/11 Commission report a large, heavily overseen bureaucracy.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- On the 2024 election he says Trump effectively won it on 13 July in Butler, argues Michelle Obama is the only Democrat who could beat him, and calls both Trump and Biden bad for America — the US bully strategy since 1950 being a game of diminishing returns, with US growth around 1.3% against China's 4.5%.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- Influence versus persuasion: persuasion is active energy spent changing someone's mind — triggering an emotional response and letting them build the rational narrative around it, as in advertising — while influence is passive, what you have when you are not in the room.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- Sense-making is his foundational framework, a cup filled in thirds: avoidance (the default reaction to anyone new), competition (mutual investment of energy, including argument), and compliance (an agreed power dynamic, of which "agree to disagree" is a version) — which feeds the know-like-trust ladder, where his "secret sauce" is that you need only like someone slightly before you fall into trust, and trust is agnostic, meaning predictability rather than approval.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- The Power of Polarity: standing for something creates both enemies and defenders, and defenders invest energy that deepens their trust — which is why he says he wants people to say they dislike him, why Bartlett's friend destroyed his own LinkedIn influence with inconsistent takes on alcohol, and why he endorses Jayne Wallace's line about being willing to upset 80% to win the 20%.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- RICE — reward, ideology, coercion, ego — are the four core motivators, with ideology strongest, then ego, then reward, then coercion; he warns coercion is a cage that burns trust once the door opens, and reframes mentioning rival offers as competition rather than coercion.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- Reading Bartlett's teenage cold emails, he identifies a strong ego play in referencing the investor's story and blog, a reward play in listing the universities and Domino's and Subway backing, ideology in the appeal of giving a brave 18-year-old a chance, and suggests the conspicuous typos may have accidentally triggered reciprocity.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- The four Cs of building influence at work are consideration, consistency, collaboration and control — collaboration meaning a better third outcome rather than a compromise where nobody gets what they want, and control meaning actually spending the "social capital" you have accumulated, the step he says aspiring leaders skip.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
- On interviews he advises asking more questions than the interviewer, because interviewers hire people who are alike rather than merely likeable, and cites a client, Emanuel, who was laid off a month after his first child was born and used the mirroring framework to land a better-paid lab job, and an engineer who won a roughly $32,000 raise and a senior promotion after five years of being refused.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]

# Takeaways

Bustamante's closing position is that all relationships are transactional whether or not people admit it, and that accepting this is what lets him cultivate the transactions worth having rather than pouring years into "money pit" relationships — a view he says sounds cold but frees him to invest fully in his wife and children, whom he does not see as returns on investment.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] He defines courage narrowly as doing the thing you are afraid of, argues leaders are respected and trusted rather than liked, and says the price of leadership is permanent loneliness.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] Asked what he would change if the afterlife he believes in were disproved, he says he would probably never have learned to ride a motorcycle, skydive, free dive or sail, and would never have joined the CIA — an admission that leads into an exchange with Bartlett about how fatherhood reshapes tolerance for risk.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA] He also previews Red Cell, a book about his and his wife's operational history as a clandestine married couple, still awaiting CIA clearance at the time of recording.[^former-cia-spy-reveals-how-theyre-controlling-yo-P_A8XElrAqA]
