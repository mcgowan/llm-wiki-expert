# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Joe Navarro, a Cuban refugee who spent 25 years in the FBI — chiefly in counterintelligence, as a SWAT commander, pilot, and one of six agents selected for the Bureau's once-classified behavioral analysis program — and who has since become a world-renowned body-language author and teacher. Navarro frames his life's work as "teaching": passing on knowledge gained from sitting with spies, terrorists, and "bazooka-yielding enemies," so ordinary people can read others and circumstances faster and gain an advantage at home, at work, and in negotiations.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]

The episode blends espionage war stories with practical instruction. Navarro recounts catching Roderick James Ramsey — whose cigarette shook when a co-conspirator's name was mentioned — unraveling what he calls the most damaging espionage case in US history (the Conrad ring's sale of Army secrets, including compromised nuclear "go codes," to the Soviets via Hungarian intelligence), and identifying a foreign "illegal" simply because he carried a Valentine's bouquet flowers-down like an Eastern European rather than flowers-up like an American.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]

The teaching half of the conversation covers the evolutionary roots of nonverbal behavior (freezing, mouth-covering, neck-touching), the specific tells of the glabella, lips, and suprasternal notch, how to control negotiations through time, seating, and staging, how confidence can be trained, the five traits of exceptional people from his book Be Exceptional, and — per the episode's title — how to recognize and escape malignant narcissists, whom he says you cannot fix, only leave.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]

# Key points

- Navarro argues body language is "supremely important" and evolutionarily hardwired: babies communicate nonverbally from birth, blind-from-birth children still cover their eyes at bad news, and behaviors like freezing, covering the mouth, and shielding the neck are ancient predator-defense heuristics — he notes India recorded 238 big-cat attacks in a single year as evidence these threats shaped us.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- He dismisses demands for perfect accuracy in reading people: evolution works by "approximation for success," and he cites Harvard researcher Nalini Ambady's "thin slice" studies showing humans assess each other about 75% accurately from as little as 3 milliseconds — observers who glimpsed a teacher for seconds rated her the same as students who sat through a whole semester.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- The Ramsey case: a quivering cigarette at the mention of Clyde Lee Conrad's name launched a 10-year investigation, 37 hotel-room interviews (with furniture secretly arranged so Navarro always sat 1-2 inches higher), and multiple arrests; a general testified that had hostilities broken out, "the defeat of the West would have been assured within 3 days," and Ramsey — who had the second-highest IQ the Army had recorded since World War II — got 33 years.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- On spies among us: hostile intelligence officers make up anywhere from 3% to (at one Soviet-era point) 85% of some diplomatic staffs; he estimates at least two dozen "illegals" in the UK and a "constellation" in the US, whose wartime role is sabotage and reporting rather than espionage; he says Chinese intelligence prefers students and scientists, recalling students given small meal allowances but thousands of dollars for photocopying — "we call that a clue in the FBI."[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- Specific tells he teaches: furrowing the glabella signals doubt or dislike; lips are the body's "emotional seismograph," blanching and tightening under stress; touching the suprasternal notch or neck signals insecurity and is "the worst thing you can do in negotiations" because it transmits weakness; fingers spread with confidence and thumbs tuck in with fear; face-touching relieves stress via the short fifth and seventh cranial nerves.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- His core negotiation doctrine is "whoever controls time controls": against aggressive counterparts, slow the pace rather than escalate; plan interviews in exquisite detail (who enters first, who sits where, when breaks happen); offering drinks makes you the archetypal parent figure — the same dynamic, he says, that produced Stockholm syndrome and that operates in domestic abuse.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- An FBI old-timer's advice shaped his interviewing philosophy: "Interviewing isn't about the confession — it's about face time"; keep people talking for hours and they will tell you everything, which is why he prioritizes rapport, mirroring ("synchrony is harmony"), matching the other person's words, and venting negative emotion before transacting.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- He cites a "height dividend" alongside the beauty dividend (roughly 8% higher lifetime earnings for the good-looking): men 6'2" are about 3% of the US population but 39% of Fortune 500 CEOs — an advantage, not evidence taller people are smarter.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- Confidence, he insists, can be trained: start by being confident about one small thing (even stacking papers), master a subject before every meeting, adopt a downward-inflected "command voice" (practice saying "no" so it sounds like a complete sentence), speak in cadence like Churchill and MLK, use the steeple gesture at emphatic moments, and never hold a "still face," which research shows makes you seem threatening and untrustworthy.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- For hiring, he tells executives to ask two neglected questions — "what problems have you solved?" (real solvers relive the emotion of solving; secondhand tellers only convey the story) and "how good are you at observing?" — citing Velcro's invention from one hiker's close observation of burrs as the payoff of observation.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- His five traits of exceptional people: self-mastery (he reads two books a week, aiming for a thousand per decade), observation, nonverbal communication, prompt prosocial action ("movement is equated with caring"), and psychological comfort — which he calls humanity's most powerful strength and the true object of any negotiation, exemplified by Steve Jobs making feared, ugly computers approachable.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
- On narcissists: malignant narcissists are roughly 2% of the population but around 20% of CEOs; they overvalue themselves, devalue others, demand loyalty they never return, and lie while expecting truth; his checklists (from Dangerous Personalities) help identify them, but he says there is no pill and no winning — "the arc of the trajectory does not favor you," so decide how long you will tolerate it and get out, because "the body keeps the score"; combining narcissism with paranoia and psychopathy, as in Hitler, is what makes "three lethal."[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]

# Takeaways

Navarro's closing synthesis is that everyone is "in the people business," and the skills that caught spies — observation, synchrony, controlling time, and above all creating psychological comfort — are the same skills that build trust, relationships, and influence in ordinary life. He reflects candidly on the costs of his career (crime-scene memories the hippocampus never releases, lasting pain over a night on duty when a young woman was abducted) but says most people he encountered were kind, and that mentoring curious young people gives him hope. His practical bottom line: connection is a learnable craft grounded in our evolutionary wiring — and when you find yourself in the orbit of a truly toxic, character-flawed person, the only winning move is to recognize it early and leave.[^former-fbi-agent-if-they-do-this-please-run-narc-ZuiIvevLg40]
