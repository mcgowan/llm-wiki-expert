# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Marc Randolph, co-founder and first CEO of Netflix and author of *That Will Never Work: The Birth of Netflix and the Amazing Life of an Idea*.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] Randolph says he has done seven startups over more than forty years and no longer has the appetite for the "7 by 24 focus" of another one, so his mission now is mentorship — and that he wrote the book because almost everything the general media says about entrepreneurship is wrong, glamorising fast cars and parties when the reality is a very lonely profession.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]

The heart of his argument is a method rather than a story. Randolph rejects the brainstorming rule that there is no such thing as a bad idea, insisting the opposite: every idea is bad, we just do not know why yet — which is why the only thing that matters is how cleverly and cheaply you can collide an idea with a real person.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] He traces Netflix from months of rejected pitches during his commute with Reed Hastings, through the day they turned the car around mid-journey to mail a CD to Hastings' house, to the year and a half of failed tests that finally produced subscription with no due dates and no late fees.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]

He is equally direct about the parts that hurt: the afternoon Hastings arrived with a slideshow about his weaknesses and asked to take over as CEO, his father dying of a brain tumour a week before the dot-com crash, the $50 million hole the company was in when Blockbuster laughed at their asking price, and the near-death moment when Blockbuster's blended model came close to killing Netflix.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] Threaded through all of it is his claim that hard work is a myth as a driver of success, and his defence of a life with room in it for a marriage, children and the outdoors.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]

# Key points

- Randolph credits Netflix to unglamorous prior experience: running the mail order division of a sheet music company, where he learned direct response marketing was really about testing and analytics, and a stint as a magazine circulation director that taught him subscription economics.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- He met Reed Hastings when his company Integrity QA was acquired by Hastings' Pure Atria; they worked together seven or eight months before that company was itself acquired and both faced losing their jobs, at which point they agreed Randolph would found a company with Hastings as angel investor and board chair — all they needed was an idea.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- He describes their partnership as left brain and right brain — Hastings mathematical, methodical and unemotional, himself a marketer who intuits reactions — held together by a shared commitment to honesty and to caring about the right answer rather than whose idea it was.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- The ideas he pitched during their Santa Cruz commute included personalised shampoo formulated from a mailed lock of hair, personalised pet food, custom sporting goods and vitamins; Hastings would sit in silence, then say "that will never work" and explain why, and they would argue the whole way to the office.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Video rental by mail had already been rejected because VHS cassettes were too big, heavy and expensive; the unlock came when Hastings mentioned the DVD, and instead of writing a business plan they turned the car around mid-commute, failed to find a DVD, bought a used music CD and a greeting-card envelope, and posted it to Hastings' house — it arrived unbroken in under 24 hours for the price of a stamp.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- His framework is that the worst thing an entrepreneur can do is fall in love with an idea, and that the goal is not a minimum viable product but something "unviable" — his example is telling a student with a clothes-sharing idea to tape a sign reading "would you like to borrow my clothes, knock" on her dorm door, which would teach her about demand, fit, stains and dry-cleaning costs for the price of paper, tape and a marker.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Netflix was founded on 29 August 1997 and launched in April 1998 into a market with fewer than 250,000 DVD players sold across 130 million US households; the innovation, Randolph says, was one centralised internet store with perfect inventory that could list a film in thirty places at once, serving the whole country with 12 to 15 people against Blockbuster's 9,000 stores and 60,000 employees in an $8 billion US rental market.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- In September 1999 he and Hastings met Jeff Bezos in Seattle at an Amazon that still sold only books, worked at desks made of doors and had pizza boxes and dogs everywhere; Amazon's CFO signalled an offer in the low eight figures, which they read as $10-15 million — "not a bad return for 12 to 18 months work" — but they decided to keep going, a conversation Randolph calls a commitment ceremony.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- He remembers Bezos as unpolished and hugely enthusiastic with a "hyena-like" laugh, and the two swapping startup trivia — both companies had rigged a bell to ring on every order, Netflix had started out named Kibble, and Amazon had nearly been called Kadabra until a lawyer noted it sounded like cadaver.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- In late 1999 Hastings came to his office with a slideshow about his performance; Randolph stopped him — "I am not going to sit here and let you pitch me on how much I suck" — and heard him out as he proposed returning full-time as CEO with Randolph as COO, citing errors of judgement and hiring decisions and telling him he did not appear "tough and candid enough to hold strong people's respect."[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Randolph says he sat in the dark feeling it was unfair before realising he had conflated two dreams — building a big successful company and being its CEO — and that Hastings, who had already taken a company public, scaled one from two to a thousand employees and could raise venture money on his name, improved their odds; stepping aside, he says, was the smartest decision he ever made, and the years that followed were Netflix's renaissance.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- He has fully forgiven the way it was delivered, arguing that trust matters more than style: Hastings had no ulterior motive, and it took courage to walk in and say "I've thought of a way to make the company more successful but it's really going to hurt."[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- On hard work, Randolph says it matters early — like the mass water start of a triathlon, where you sprint 400-600 yards to get clear water — but that treating it as the cause of success is a myth; his image for the rest is running for planes, which he calculated made no difference roughly 49% of the time either way, so he vowed never to run for one again.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- His conclusion from that: "you don't lose the deal at 2 o'clock that morning because you didn't check the fonts, you lost that deal four weeks ago" — being smart about which problems to focus on makes 99% of the difference, and the rest of the effort does not change the outcome.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- The original Netflix model — pay per disc, due dates, late fees — simply did not work; people would not rent, and if they did they never came back, which started an eighteen-month run of hundreds of tests during which Randolph's perfectionism meant each one took weeks to prepare until they compressed the cycle to multiple sloppy tests a day.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- The insight that came out of that speed, he says, is that a bad idea cannot be rescued by a beautifully crafted test, while a good idea shows through even a broken one — customers "perk their head up", raise their hand and phone you, so the skill is building a culture and system for trying lots of bad ideas fast.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Product-market fit arrived when they looked at several hundred thousand idle DVDs in the warehouse and wondered whether customers could store them instead — a monthly subscription with unlimited rentals, no due dates and no late fees, which converted Netflix's greatest impediment into its greatest asset by making the effective wait zero compared with a 20-minute drive to Blockbuster.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Late fees of $3-4 a day were Blockbuster's Achilles heel and were baked into its business model; Bartlett connects this to Kahneman's loss aversion and to the peak-end rule, and Randolph agrees that being charged on the way out was the perfect bad ending to a customer experience.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Asked whether the answer is simply to run more tests rather than debate in a boardroom, Randolph says yes emphatically — customers do not know what they want either, so the only way to find out is to throw things at them; Netflix, he notes, still spends enormous effort testing subscription dynamics more than twenty years on.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Subscriptions nearly destroyed them when the dot-com bubble burst in spring 2000, because a booming subscription business burns cash acquiring customers ahead of revenue — with a first-month-free policy, accumulated losses of about $50 million against $5 million of revenue, most of it lost in the previous twelve months, they were "going to go broke being successful."[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Randolph's father — an investment adviser and self-described anti-entrepreneur who thought the dot-com valuations were hooey — died of a brain tumour a week before the crash proved him right, having just seen his son speak to the DVD manufacturers' association in New York; Randolph says he inherited from him both the belief that you can be true to yourself without chasing trends and a handwritten list of "the Randolph rules of success" that were about decency rather than business: do 10% more than you are asked, be prompt, don't complain, stick to constructive criticism, don't express opinions on things you don't have facts for.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- After months of trying to reach Blockbuster, the call came while Netflix was on a retreat at the Alisal Ranch near Santa Barbara; they chartered a jet to Dallas, arrived in shorts, t-shirts and flip-flops, pitched a blended model and asked $50 million — and Randolph realised the executives were suppressing laughter at the idea that a company $50 million in the hole was worth that much.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Blockbuster later came far closer to winning than is generally told, he says: its blended model letting customers mix mail and store returns was, in his words, a killer they could not compete with, and Netflix survived largely because corporate raiders on Blockbuster's board denied CEO John Antioco his contracted bonus, he quit, and his convenience-retail successor wanted to sell gum and clothing instead of funding digital — a moment Randolph likens to the robot in Spielberg's student film that abandons the chase the instant its cost-benefit calculation breaks even.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- On culture, his central claim is that culture is observational, not aspirational — it is how the founders and executives actually behave, cascading from the first two people to the first ten, the first hundred and the first thousand — so posters in the breakroom are worthless and saying "you're a family" is only legitimate if you mean it.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- Freedom and responsibility, he explains, is the deliberate refusal to build guard rails: no travel policy, no expense policy, no vacation policy, just "use your best judgment", which only works if you hire for judgement and remove the few people who lack it — an insight prompted by watching engineers at a previous employer complain about the company from inside its Olympic-pool-and-hot-tub campus, which told him and Patty McCord that amenities are not what makes people want to work somewhere; being treated like an adult is.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
- His long-standing personal rule is a Tuesday date night with his wife, leaving work at 5pm sharp regardless of what is happening — after a while, he says, crises simply stopped occurring after 5pm on Tuesdays, and modelling it publicly proved that running a startup and having a relationship were compatible.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]

# Takeaways

Randolph frames his whole career as evidence that the scarce skill is not having ideas but disproving them cheaply — the discipline of assuming your idea is bad makes it easy to abandon, and abandoning it quickly is what carries you to the version that works.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] On the day of the 2002 IPO he says he sat in a New York cab with his son, wondering whether he was now different, and concluded he was not: he liked the work, still had problems to solve and went back to it, because entrepreneurs he knows overwhelmingly return to building even when they never need to work again.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] Asked what he was most wrong about, he answers that it took him nearly two years to think of applying his own subscription and circulation experience to Netflix, and that he wishes he could go back and try it sooner.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU] His summary of what he is proudest of is not the IPOs but having done it while staying married to the same woman, having his children grow up knowing and apparently liking him, and still getting out into the mountains.[^former-netflix-ceo-hard-work-does-not-matter-a-2-HSVbD7RhOHU]
