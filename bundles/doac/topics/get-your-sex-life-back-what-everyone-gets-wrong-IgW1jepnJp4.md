# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr Mohit Khera, a board-certified urologist and professor at Baylor College of Medicine who has spent 17 years specializing in male and female sexual dysfunction, testosterone therapy, and infertility. Khera introduces the concept of the "sex span" — how long a person can engage in satisfying sexual activity — and argues that most people want their sex span to match their lifespan, which he says is achievable through four pillars: diet, exercise, sleep, and stress reduction, plus hormone optimization and treating sexual health as a couple's issue rather than an individual one.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]

A central thread of the conversation is Khera's claim that erectile dysfunction is not merely a bedroom problem but the first visible sign of serious underlying disease — cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and depression in particular — and that men's willingness to seek help for ED makes it a "gateway to men's health" that lets doctors catch these conditions years earlier.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4] He also argues that sexual dysfunction remains heavily stigmatized: patients "suffer in silence" despite excellent treatment options, women in particular have almost no FDA-approved therapies, and only a minority of sufferers ever seek care.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]

The discussion ranges across the psychogenic "vicious cycle" of performance anxiety, pornography's role in rising dysfunction, the population-wide decline in testosterone driven by obesity, testosterone therapy for both men and women, premature ejaculation, fertility, regenerative therapies, and penile implants — closing with Khera's personal message, drawn from his father's death, to "enjoy the ride" rather than deferring life's pleasures.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]

# Key points

- Khera defines "sex span" as how long one can engage in satisfying sexual activity, noting US averages of a 77-year lifespan but only a 67-year health span (63 by CDC disability-free measures), and says four pillars — diet, exercise, sleep, and stress reduction — outperform any pill for extending it.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- He claims ED is an early-warning sign: 15% of men presenting with ED will have a heart attack or stroke within seven years, roughly 40% of men at 40 have some ED (rising ~10% per decade), and 66% of men with ED have some degree of depression; he explains this via the "arterial diameter theory" — penile arteries (1-2mm) occlude before coronary (3-4mm) and carotid (6-7mm) arteries.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- He cites a Greek study in which about one in five men presenting with ED had demonstrable coronary occlusion, and a St. Louis study in which 30% of men aged 18-40 screened for ED had pre-diabetes or diabetes on the day of diagnosis — making ED, in his view, "the gateway to men's health."[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- Libido, he argues, is multifactorial: hormones (his "PET T" mnemonic — prolactin, estrogen, thyroid, testosterone), neurotransmitters (dopamine raises desire, serotonin lowers it, which is why antidepressants and finasteride suppress libido), lifestyle factors like fatigue and stress, and psychogenic/relationship factors.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- He describes the psychogenic "vicious cycle" — one failed erection breeds anxiety that causes the next failure — and says daily low-dose (5mg) tadalafil is uniquely useful for breaking it; unlike on-demand Viagra, which he compares to a painkiller masking a broken leg, he argues daily Cialis actually strengthens penile smooth muscle, protects the endothelium, and retains benefit after stopping (citing an Aversa study), with a Kloner-published study showing a 13% reduction in cardiac events and 25% reduction in mortality among daily users.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- On women: 43-48% of US women suffer from female sexual dysfunction (low libido, low arousal, orgasmic dysfunction, or pain), yet only 19% seek therapy, and until 2015 there was not a single FDA-approved drug for it versus roughly 30 for men; only two now exist (flibanserin/Addyi and bremelanotide/Vyleesi), which Khera attributes to a large research-funding gap.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- He argues testosterone is the number-one libido hormone for both sexes — women make more testosterone than any other hormone in their bodies — yet no FDA-approved female testosterone product exists, so he prescribes men's formulations off-label at one-tenth dose; his nonprofit, the Testosterone Project, campaigns for approval and routine testing.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- On the population decline: average male testosterone has fallen from roughly 700 ng/dL in the 1970s Framingham era to the mid-400s by 2015 (~50 ng/dL per decade), which he blames chiefly on obesity — fat cells convert testosterone to estrogen via aromatase and secrete cortisol and leptin that suppress production; losing 10% of body weight can raise testosterone ~85 ng/dL, and 15% about 250 ng/dL (European Male Ageing Study).[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- He co-designed the TRAVERSE trial (5,200 patients, six years), which he says finally showed testosterone therapy does not increase cardiovascular events, prostate cancer risk, or urinary symptoms; his own ~850-patient depression study found severe depression fell from 17% to 2% after a year of testosterone treatment in low-T men.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- On pornography, he takes a middle position: the data are mixed, but excessive use widens the gap between expectation and reality, and men who quit often report improved erections and libido; he cites studies linking 300+ minutes weekly to ED in 40% of viewers aged 35-45, while maintaining that the obesity/diabetes epidemic is a bigger driver of declining sexual activity than porn.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- Lifestyle interventions he cites as evidence-based: the Mediterranean diet (Esposito's trial improved erectile function in obese men with no drugs), 7-8 hours of sleep (under six hours raises dysfunction risk; over nine adds nothing), and 160 minutes per week of vigorous exercise for six months (his own meta-analysis), with a cardiac-rehab study showing a roughly fourfold improvement in women's sexual function scores.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
- Other conditions covered: premature ejaculation affects 30% of men globally (defined as under two minutes with loss of control and bother; average US ejaculatory time is 5.4 minutes versus 13.4 minutes for female orgasm) yet only 9% seek help; Peyronie's disease affects 9% of men; 15% of couples face infertility, with a male factor involved about half the time; and he presents the penile implant (invented 1973) as a last-resort cure with over 92% patient and partner satisfaction.[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]

# Takeaways

Khera's closing message is that sexual dysfunction is normal, common, and treatable, and that the real enemy is silence: only 44% of men developing ED even tell their partners, and most sufferers never raise the issue with a doctor. He urges listeners to treat sexual health as a couple's project built on communication and time together, to use lifestyle change as first-line medicine before pills, and to view sexual symptoms as a valuable early alarm for heart disease, diabetes, and depression. Drawing on his father's late-life illness, he adds a personal coda: don't defer joy to a retirement that may never come — "enjoy the ride."[^get-your-sex-life-back-what-everyone-gets-wrong-IgW1jepnJp4]
