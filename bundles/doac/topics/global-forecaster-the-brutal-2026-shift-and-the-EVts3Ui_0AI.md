# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Ian Bremmer, political scientist and founder of the Eurasia Group, about his firm's annual Top Risks report for 2026.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI] Bremmer's headline claim is that the United States itself has become the biggest driver of geopolitical uncertainty in the world — visible in tariffs, Venezuela, Greenland, and Iran — because Americans are abandoning the free-trade, collective-security, open-borders order they built, leaving a "G-Zero" world with no global leadership where the powerful write the rules and the weak must accept them.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI] He predicts Trump will fail, lose badly in the November midterms, and become a lame duck — while the underlying demand for a US political revolution, from left or right, persists.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]

Bremmer gives a detailed account of why, in his telling, Trump went to war with Iran: emboldened by a casualty-free operation that removed Maduro in Venezuela, convinced by past Iranian restraint that Tehran would fold, and surrounded this term by loyalists who will not push back — despite the military having wargamed for decades how Iran could shut the Strait of Hormuz.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI] His most likely endgame: an extended ceasefire in which Iran compromises on enrichment in exchange for a privileged, toll-collecting position over the strait.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]

The second half turns to China's long-term dominance in critical minerals, batteries, EVs, and clean energy, Europe's post-Cold War complacency, and what Bremmer calls the underappreciated risk of AI — dramatized by an Anthropic model reportedly withheld because it could find and exploit software vulnerabilities at systemic scale — ending with his three-part prescription for AI governance and a defense of human, algorithm-resisting conversation.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]

# Key points

- Bremmer ranks the US political revolution as 2026's most critical risk: America is dismantling the order it created, producing a "G-Zero" world without leadership; he cites democratic socialist Zohran Mamdani's mayoralty of New York — "the capital of global capitalism" — as proof the demand for something radically different persists.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- He predicts Trump will fail and lose big in the November midterms; quoting strategist James Carville, he says Democrats should effectively stay out of the way while their opponent makes mistakes, and dismisses early 2028 polling (Harris 20%, Newsom 19%, Buttigieg 14%, AOC 13%) as meaningless.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- His second major risk, "overpowered," is China's decades-long, at-scale investment in critical minerals, rare-earth reprocessing, EVs, and batteries — positioning Beijing to set global rules and standards while the West optimized for quarterly returns; the biggest danger to the US, he says, "is not China, it's America" getting in its own way.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- He gives three reasons Trump attacked Iran: the "extraordinary" Venezuela operation (Maduro jailed in Brooklyn, a cooperative successor regime) made him overconfident; Iran's history of not retaliating (after the Soleimani killing and the prior 12-day war, when Tehran pre-warned its missile strike on Al Udeid); and a second-term cabinet of loyalists who feed him flattery rather than pushback, unlike first-term figures such as Mattis and Pompeo.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- Iran responded to decapitation with a "mosaic" decentralization to local commanders, yet still demonstrates functioning central control — taking tolls on the strait and fielding a full expert negotiating team for 21 hours of substantive talks in Islamabad, which Bremmer reads as evidence the regime survives and feels it has leverage.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- He argues Trump has "a glass jaw" — democracies cannot absorb economic pain the way autocracies can, as China proved by weaponizing critical-mineral export controls until CEOs forced a climbdown — so the Iranians are incentivized simply to wait him out.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- His most likely scenario: the ceasefire extends and Iran concedes on the nuclear file in exchange for privileged, revenue-generating influence over the Strait of Hormuz (tolls framed as reconstruction money); the worse sub-50% scenario is Trump using roughly 15,000 deployed troops to seize Kharg Island, the lightly defended island handling about 90% of Iranian oil exports.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- He notes knock-on effects: Russia profits from spiking oil, gas, and fertilizer prices and diverted American weapons, making Putin less interested than ever in a Ukraine ceasefire; new Middle East blocs are forming (UAE-Israel-US-India versus a Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey-Egypt "defense quad"), with Pakistan a potential nuclear supplier to Riyadh.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- Europe's decline, in his analysis, stems from post-1989 assumptions that the world would stay peaceful — underinvestment in defense, technology, and nuclear power, plus overregulation and lack of scale across 27+ countries — leaving the Draghi competitiveness plan on paper while the US and China invested in everything.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- On AI, he treats as genuinely serious the report that Anthropic withheld a model capable of finding every exploitable software bug — citing the Fed and Treasury convening bank CEOs and Jamie Dimon treating it as a "five alarm fire" — calling AI a severe but "underappreciated" risk relative to the headlines Iran and China receive.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- He forecasts a populist wave in 2028 driven by educated urban and suburban workers — this time including women with advanced degrees — angry about AI, job displacement, and data centers (a centrist senator tells him constituents have never been angrier about any issue), though he doubts mass AI unemployment arrives as fast as some technologists claim.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
- His three-part AI governance prescription: US-China AI arms-control talks (learning from the post-Cuban-Missile-Crisis hotline), an "AI stability board" of technocrats modeled on financial stability bodies, and funded universal access so AI does not split humanity into empowered "hybrid" individuals and a group treated "like a different species" — warning his deeper fear is not AGI but humans becoming programmed, computer-like creatures of the algorithm.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]

# Takeaways

Bremmer's closing register is guardedly optimistic: the last 50 years' big stories were longer lives, less poverty, and greater human capacity, and he "doesn't see how" a utopian use of today's tools is impossible — but he insists the danger lies in people and politics, not technology, and that a world building superintelligence has "nowhere near adequate governance" for its 8 billion people.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI] He urges independent voices to treat their independence as an obligation — talking across disagreement, resisting the algorithm, doing more live and long-form — and argues that real influence now flows through technology companies and public educators rather than elected office.[^global-forecaster-the-brutal-2026-shift-and-the-EVts3Ui_0AI]
