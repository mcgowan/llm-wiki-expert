# Overview

In this return appearance on The Diary Of A CEO, biochemist and author Jessie Inchauspé — the Glucose Goddess — tells Steven Bartlett that continuous glucose monitors and newer science have made blood sugar legible in a way it never was before, and that the picture is alarming: a billion people worldwide have type 2 diabetes or pre-diabetes, and up to 80% of people with no diagnosed condition still spike every day.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] She describes herself not as a biochemist who is anti-sugar but as one who is "pro knowledge", insisting she eats sugar and carbohydrates herself and only wants people to know how and when to eat them.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]

Her mechanistic case rests on three consequences she says follow every large spike. Mitochondria overwhelmed by too much glucose at once stop making energy efficiently — she compares it to drowning a houseplant — producing chronic fatigue and free radicals that raise inflammation.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] Glycation, the same browning reaction that turns a chicken from pink to brown in the oven, is happening slowly inside us; she says baby cartilage is white and 90-year-old cartilage is brown, and every spike accelerates that cooking.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] And insulin, released to clear the excess into liver, muscle and then fat cells, itself drives insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes — and blocks fat burning while it is present.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]

Much of the episode is practical: ten hacks, of which she names four as essential, plus a 2019 pilot experiment on roughly 2,700 self-reported participants across 110 countries.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] Inchauspé also uses the conversation to argue that GLP-1 drugs are a symptom of a broken food system rather than a triumph — comparing them to inventing a drug that makes you less thirsty instead of fixing toxic tap water — and calls for regulation, education and food access alongside individual habits.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]

# Key points

- Inchauspé says only two food categories raise blood sugar — starches (bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, oats) and sugars — because both are literally chains or pairs of glucose molecules; starches do not taste sweet because glucose itself is not sweet, whereas fruit's sweetness comes from fructose, which raises blood glucose far less.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- She rejects the idea of "good sugar": the sucrose in a fruit smoothie and in chocolate cake is the same molecule, and honey, agave and maple syrup carry marketing health halos without a biochemical difference — so she advises treating all of it as dessert and picking whatever you enjoy most.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Her most surprising CGM foods are honey and grapes; she argues supermarket fruit has been bred for sweetness the way wolves were bred into chihuahuas, and that juicing removes the protective fibre, making orange juice comparable to a can of Coke.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Individual glucose responses vary with muscle mass, hydration, stress, microbiome, genetics, sleep and menstrual cycle phase — she notes people of South Asian descent tend to have a harder time expanding fat cells and so may spike higher — which is why she says comparing your CGM curve to someone else's is meaningless.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Hack one, a savoury breakfast built on protein with healthy fats and optional starch, is the one she says to do if you only do one: a sweet breakfast starts a day-long glucose rollercoaster because dropping glucose activates the brain's craving centre, and a savoury one should keep you full for four hours.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Hack two is a tablespoon of any kitchen vinegar diluted in a big glass of water about ten minutes before the largest meal; she says the acetic acid temporarily inactivates the digestive enzymes that cleave starches, cutting the glucose and insulin spikes by up to 30%, with additional evidence on visceral fat and cholesterol.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Hacks three and four are veggie starters — fibre forming a mesh that slows glucose absorption, a pattern she notes in Italian antipasti, French crudités and Middle Eastern herb plates — and ten minutes of muscle use after eating, including calf raises that engage the soleus muscle.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- The remaining hacks are eating in order (vegetables, then protein and fat, then starches and sugars, which she says cuts a meal's spike by up to 75%), putting "clothes on carbs" by never eating them naked, savoury snacks, sweets only as dessert after a meal, and always eating fruit whole rather than juiced, dried or blended.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- In her four-week pilot — roughly 2,700 people aged 20 to 70 recruited via Instagram, adding one hack per week with no calorie restriction — she reports 90% saw fewer cravings, 90% more energy, and around 40% of those who wanted to lose weight did; she is explicit that it was self-reported, had no control group and was not a randomised placebo-controlled trial.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- On calories, she recounts that they were originally measured by burning food in a box inside an aquarium and recording the water's temperature rise — a measure of heat released, she argues, that says nothing about whether a carrot cake or three avocados will spike you.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- She links glucose to hormones and fertility: insulin resistance is associated with infertility, she says 60% of PCOS cases involve insulin resistance, and excess insulin signals the ovaries to make more testosterone — she reports three women in her second book's programme regaining periods and conceiving within a month of doing the hacks.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- On the brain, she cites a 30-year follow-up finding elevated glucose in one's 40s predicts later Alzheimer's — some scientists call Alzheimer's "type 3 diabetes" — and a 10-year study of 300 initially healthy people in which those who developed insulin resistance had almost a twofold increase in major depression.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- She describes a study of 300 married couples given voodoo dolls of their spouses and told to add a pin whenever annoyed: people with more variable glucose and more post-spike lows had stuck in more pins, which the researchers linked to tyrosine and mood regulation.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- On GLP-1 drugs she says up to 40% of weight lost is muscle, people regain on average 70% of the lost weight after stopping and regain it as fat, so anyone taking them should weight-train three times a week and eat plenty of protein.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- She promotes her Anti-Spike Formula, built from a lemon antioxidant (eriocitrin) that she says boosts natural GLP-1 — requiring about 1.5 litres of lemon juice a day unless concentrated — and mulberry leaf DNJ, which she says blocks up to 40% of a meal's glucose absorption; she stresses the food hacks alone are sufficient.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
- Other claims she makes in passing: fasting is a real stressor that suits men better than women and should be dosed alongside other stressors; menopause raises glucose responses to the same foods, and reducing spikes can ease insomnia and hot flushes; coffee spikes glucose in some people via the stress response, and drinking it after rather than before breakfast when underslept can halve that spike.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]

# Takeaways

Inchauspé's position is that the interventions are deliberately additive rather than restrictive — nothing is removed, four habits are inserted — because she believes the barrier to health is information rather than willpower, and because prevention framing ("this will stop Alzheimer's in forty years") motivates almost nobody compared with same-day gains in energy, cravings, mood and sleep.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] She is careful to say her own Instagram graphs are illustrations of published trials rather than evidence in themselves, and that she draws no conclusions from her personal CGM data.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c] Her broader argument is systemic: she frames the sugar problem as commercial incentives rather than conspiracy, and says individual hacks cannot substitute for regulation, food access and education — her stated goal is to make herself irrelevant.[^glucose-goddess-the-10-glucose-hacks-3esF-pNAM9c]
