# Overview

In this Diary Of A CEO episode, author Graham Hancock — recorded weeks before major heart valve surgery he acknowledges he might not survive — lays out the case he has spent over 30 years building: that humanity is "a species with amnesia" and that a major forgotten episode, a lost civilization roughly 20,000 years old, precedes the conventional 6,000-year story of civilization.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo] Hancock frames the interview as potentially his last word on his life and work, partly because a journalist he says has "very bad blood" toward him has been preparing a hostile story for over two years, and he insists he is not a grifter or hoaxer but a puzzled investigator who does not claim to have proved a lost civilization existed — only that the evidence demands one be considered.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]

His argument rests on several strands: worldwide flood myths he treats as the "memory banks of our species"; the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis, under which fragments of a giant comet struck Earth around 12,800 years ago, triggering megafauna extinction, an anomalous sea level rise, and a 1,200-year deep freeze; hunter-gatherer megalithic sites like Göbekli Tepe (11,600 years old) that upend the assumption that agriculture must precede monument-building; ancient maps such as the 1531 Orontius Finaeus map that he says show Antarctica three centuries before its official 1820 discovery, with accurate longitudes achieved by modern navigators only in the 1750s; and mathematical properties of the Great Pyramid that he argues encode the Earth's dimensions and the precession of the equinoxes.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]

The conversation also ranges into Hancock's personal story — a childhood in India watching his surgeon father perform dissections at age five, a brutal boarding school, lifelong outsiderhood, and roughly 80 ayahuasca experiences he credits with softening his anger — and closes with his warning that today's nuclear-armed, hate-driven civilization "ticks all the mythological boxes" to become the next lost civilization.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]

# Key points

- Hancock argues anatomically modern humans are at least 315,000 years old (citing 196,000-year-old remains from Ethiopia and the Jebel Irhoud find in Morocco), and asks why beings with fully modern brains waited some 300,000 years to build recognizable civilization — unless part of the story is missing.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- He champions the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis: a comet 100–200 km across broke up in an Earth-crossing orbit (comet Encke being its best-known surviving fragment), and around 12,800 years ago Earth passed through a "shotgun blast" of fragments whose airbursts — each comparable to a substantial nuclear blast — triggered wildfires, megafauna extinction, an unexplained sea level pulse, and a sudden return to glacial cold.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- As physical evidence he describes the Younger Dryas boundary layer — a roughly 5-inch black stratum dated to 12,800 years ago containing soot, nanodiamonds, microspherules, platinum, and iridium, which he calls classic signatures of cometary impact — which he examined with comet-research scientist Allen West.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- Göbekli Tepe in Turkey, 11,600 years old, was built by hunter-gatherers using T-shaped megaliths up to 20 tons with precise astronomical alignments — organized labor that archaeologists once said required agriculture, with hundreds more pillars still unexcavated per ground-penetrating radar; Peru's Caral-Supe pyramid-building civilization (~5,500 years old, with earthquake-proof bagged-stone construction) similarly reverses the standard model, he argues.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- The Orontius Finaeus map of 1531 depicts Antarctica — not officially discovered until 1820 — with accurate relative longitudes, though the longitude problem was only solved with Harrison's chronometer around 1750–1760; Hancock takes this as evidence that an ice age seafaring culture mapped the world and its maps were copied down through the centuries.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- He claims the Great Pyramid — about 6 million tons, over 2 million blocks, 750 ft per side, aligned to true north within 3 minutes of arc, sited almost exactly on latitude 30 — encodes Earth's polar radius (via its height) and equatorial circumference (via its base perimeter) at a scale of 1:43,200, a number he ties to the precession of the equinoxes (25,920-year cycle) via Hamlet's Mill by MIT historian of science Giorgio de Santillana, implying precessional knowledge millennia before Hipparchus.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- He suggests knowledge from the lost civilization was transmitted by long-lived orders of sages — the "followers of Horus" in Egypt and the Apkallu of Sumer, said in tradition to have survived the flood and advised the earliest kings — a "secret society" avenue he says he is pursuing for his next book and will abandon if it does not hold up.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- Hancock endorses the claim by Filippo Biondi of huge structures hundreds of feet deep beneath the pyramid of Khafre, calls the mainstream dismissal "shameful," and proposes trialing the radar technology on Turkey's fully-mapped underground cities like Kaymakli as a validation test; he notes the Egyptians themselves called Giza "Rostau," an entrance to the underworld.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- He points to Amazon archaeology — thousands of geoglyphs and 100-km roadways revealed by deforestation and lidar surveys (work with Finnish archaeologist Martti Pärssinen in Acre, Brazil) plus man-made terra preta soils — as proof the "pristine" rainforest once supported millions in city-sized communities, showing how quickly the accepted story can be overturned.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- Hancock describes taking ayahuasca roughly 80 times, emphasizing its "moral dimension" — being forced to feel the pain one's words caused others — and endorses the DMT infusion research at Imperial College London; he argues all civilizations emerged from shamanism and that psychedelics are a serious tool for investigating consciousness, not a trivial recreation.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- The global flood myths, he notes, converge: the Sumerian Atrahasis story — in which the god Enki instructs a man to build a boat and load it with seeds and animals — prefigures Noah's ark almost identically, and he rejects archaeologists' habit of explaining such stories away as exaggerated local river floods.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
- His closing warning: the Taurid meteor stream (crossed by Earth every June and October–November) still holds hundreds of deadly objects, but nuclear war driven by "low consciousness" leaders and nationalism is the likelier cataclysm — he even suggests world leaders should undergo a dozen ayahuasca sessions before taking office.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]

# Takeaways

Host Steven Bartlett frames the episode as mind-expanding rather than an endorsement, and Hancock himself is explicit that he claims puzzlement, not proof.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo] The through-line the guest offers is a plea for independent inquiry — "question everything," including science, which he says has come to occupy the place of religion — paired with a personal reckoning: facing possibly fatal surgery at 75, he reflects on regret, his wife Santha, their blended family of six children, and his hope that humanity wakes up to its oneness before it becomes, as he puts it, the next lost civilization of its own making.[^graham-hancock-they-may-have-secretly-found-anta-Xs94KBeIiAo]
