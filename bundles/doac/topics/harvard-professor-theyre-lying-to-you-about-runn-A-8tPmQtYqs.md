# Overview

Daniel Lieberman, professor of human evolutionary biology at Harvard, returns to the show to argue that the health crisis of the modern West is best understood through the gap between how fast culture changes and how slowly biology can follow.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] His central concept is the "mismatch disease": a condition caused by novel environmental circumstances for which our bodies are inadequately or imperfectly adapted, and he tells Steven Bartlett bluntly that the vast majority of people in the Western world will die from one.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] Heart disease, type 2 diabetes, many cancers, osteoporosis, myopia, back pain, allergies and autoimmune conditions are all placed in this category, and the good news he draws from it is that mismatch diseases are not inevitable.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]

Much of the conversation is Lieberman resisting simple stories, including ones his own field has inspired. He calls the belief that hunter-gatherer life holds all the answers a "Paleo fantasy," pointing out that foragers are just as violent as anyone else and that natural selection optimised our ancestors for reproductive success, not health or happiness — "the equation of life is food in, babies out" — so the fact that our ancestors did something is no proof it is good for us.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] He is similarly sceptical of nasal-breathing enthusiasm, of nasal strips, of keto as a durable weight-loss strategy, and of the wellness and cosmetics industries generally, warning Bartlett that as a self-described seeker he is precisely the target market for the latest big idea.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]

The episode's most pointed argument is about medicine itself. Lieberman describes "dysevolution": because we treat the symptoms of mismatch diseases — glasses for myopia, metformin for diabetes, statins and blood-pressure pills for heart disease — rather than their environmental causes, the causes persist and the diseases keep spreading.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] He is careful to say these treatments should of course be given, but insists the resulting system is expensive, miserable and unjust, since access to exercise, fresh food and unprocessed diets tracks wealth.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]

# Key points

- Lieberman pushes back on the idea that humans are feeble: chimpanzees are only about 30% stronger, and while most quadrupeds outrun us over short distances, he argues we are impressive endurance athletes able to outrun most animals over long distances, and are the ultimate omnivores — Kalahari foragers eat something like 800 different plants, and our livers can convert fat to carbohydrate and back.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- On the vegan-versus-carnivore debate he calls the claim that humans were not meant to eat meat "just nonsense," dating meat-eating to at least 2.5 million years ago (cut-marked bones and stone tools from around 2.5-2.6 million years, clear hunting by 2 million years) — while adding that people can be perfectly healthy without meat, because our ancestors' diet was optimised for reproduction, not health.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- The hunting-and-gathering package — hunting, extractive foraging for tubers, cooking and processing, cooperation and tool use — emerged between three and two million years ago and Lieberman calls it perhaps the most important shift in human evolution, coinciding with bodies like ours (the Turkana Boy, Nariokotome, from West Lake Turkana) and with brain size beginning to climb from the roughly 400-500 gram australopith range towards the modern 1,400 grams.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Brains are metabolically expensive: one in every five breaths at rest pays for the brain, which uses 20% of adult metabolism, and in a newborn it consumes half of all energy — which is why, citing Chris Kuzawa, human babies are born at about 15% body fat, with infant fat stores falling exactly when brain growth is fastest.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- On breathing, Lieberman says the external human nose evolved around 2 million years ago as a humidifier — a Venturi-like narrowing and two right-angle turns make airflow turbulent so it picks up heat and moisture on the way in and recaptures it on the way out — but he is openly sceptical of claims that fixing your breathing prevents a wide range of diseases, notes that "no elite runner on the planet breathes out through their nose," since mouth-breathing dumps heat, and argues sleep apnoea should be treated at its cause rather than with tape on the nose.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Human thermoregulation is presented as an evolutionary superpower: we have roughly ten times the density of eccrine sweat glands of chimpanzees and no fur, effectively turning the whole body into a tongue, which let ancestors run down prey in midday heat until the animal collapsed of heat stroke — though he cautions that skin does not fossilise, so whether sweating preceded hunting is unknown.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Dieting is hard, he argues, because we evolved to store fat and use it, never to shed it: negative energy balance triggers a starvation response in which cortisol rises, driving hunger and sugar cravings and directing fat into visceral, hormone-sensitive belly deposits — and when those adipocytes over-swell they rupture, recruit white blood cells and release adipokines that produce chronic systemic inflammation implicated in diabetes, arterial plaques and Alzheimer's.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Because fat is also an endocrine organ producing leptin and oestrogen, energy availability governs reproduction: very low body fat causes amenorrhoea, and work by his former professor Peter Ellison and by Grazyna Jasienska shows dieting women's luteal-phase progesterone falling by around 50%; conversely sedentary women carry abnormally high oestrogen and progesterone, which he offers as part of why physically active women have 30-50% lower rates of breast cancer.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Cancer is only partly a mismatch — dinosaurs had bone cancer, and cancer is inherent to multicellularity — but Lieberman calls it very much a disease of energy: plot GDP against cancer rates and you get almost a straight line, and Bangladeshi women who move to England, or are born there, see their cancer rates shoot up through diet, physical activity and stress.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- He adds a reproductive-history factor rarely discussed: a modern woman may go through several hundred menstrual cycles in her life (Bartlett cites 350-400 from the book) versus roughly 50 for a hunter-gatherer, who spends most of her adult life pregnant or nursing, each cycle exposing oestrogen- and progesterone-sensitive tissue to hormone surges.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- The evidence he offers that these diseases are environmental: the Tsimane horticulturalist-foragers of the Amazon show essentially no coronary heart disease; 1970s studies found 80-year-old Kalahari hunter-gatherers with the same blood pressure as 20-year-olds while most Londoners of 70 or 80 were hypertensive; diabetes clinics have appeared along the road into Eldoret, Kenya, where he works and where none existed before; and around 12% of Americans now have diabetes, with heart disease killing roughly a third of people in the West.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Myopia is his cleanest illustration of dysevolution: an Alaskan Inuit study found grandparents with perfect vision and roughly 30% of grandchildren needing glasses; an early-1800s study of the royal guards found officers, not foot soldiers, wearing spectacles; and although reading was long blamed, he says careful studies point to time spent indoors when young — but because glasses work, nobody addresses the cause.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- On comfort, Lieberman devotes a book chapter to arguing it is not automatically good for us: US Presidential Fitness Test data across decades shows children are less fit, army recruiters report the same, and osteoporosis is rising because bone is laid down in response to loading — his tennis-player example is a playing-arm humerus up to 40% thicker than the other — with peak bone mass fixed by about 25 to 30, after which everyone only loses.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Chewing softer processed food has shrunk human jaws by about 6%, producing malocclusion and impacted wisdom teeth; he jokes that the ethical test he would love to see is randomising children to years of hard resinous gum against their twins.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Puberty timing is another energy story: French records show menarche around age 16 two hundred years ago versus roughly 12 to 12.5 today, and in the Kenyan population he studies, rural girls reach menarche about two years later than girls in an urban area just 50 km away with more food and more Coca-Cola.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- On sitting, he says the epidemiology distinguishes leisure sitting from work sitting — those who sit all day at work and again all evening carry far more risk — and cites Dave Raichlen's finding that the Hadza sit about 10 hours a day, as much as Westerners, but constantly interrupt it; his advice is to get up every 20 minutes or so, because standing up restarts the body's metabolic machinery "like turning on the car engine."[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- Back pain, which he calls the number one medical complaint, he attributes less to chair design than to back muscles that never work: the best predictor of who gets back pain is back muscle endurance rather than acute strength, and the pencil you bent to pick up is merely the straw that broke the camel's back.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- He treats keto and fasting cautiously: Bartlett's rapid eight-week keto loss was, Lieberman says, mostly water; ketosis is not negative energy balance so does not trigger the same repair pathways; intermittent fasting turns on many of the same genes as exercise but less acutely, and he argues you get more bang for your buck from exercising than from fasting alone.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
- On sanitisation he invokes the hygiene hypothesis with a memorable image: an unchallenged immune system is "like a bunch of teenagers hanging out on the corner with nothing to do," more likely to get into trouble and attack us — which he ties to rising allergies and autoimmune disease, and to an open question about children raised through the pandemic; on mouthwash and cosmetics his advice is simply to be sceptical, since killing your oral microbiome for fresher breath is a trade-off nobody has priced.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]

# Takeaways

Asked for an actionable conclusion, Lieberman gives two. The first is that understanding why a particular mismatch arises converts evolutionary knowledge into action items — get up more often, eat less sugar and saturated fat, load your skeleton while young, prefer more minimal shoes especially for children, reduce psychosocial stress where you can — while explicitly refusing the Paleo fantasy that copying foragers guarantees health.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] The second is the systemic one: he wants people to notice the vicious cycle in which treating symptoms allows causes to flourish, a cultural rather than natural form of evolution that is nonetheless reshaping our bodies, and which he says accounts for the 70-80% of doctor visits that concern preventable disease.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs] Underlying both is his repeated reminder that everything we consider normal — our chairs, shoes, cars, food and hours indoors — is evolutionarily novel, which is not proof that any of it is bad, but is reason enough to pause and ask which parts we could change.[^harvard-professor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-runn-A-8tPmQtYqs]
