# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Sara Szal, a Harvard/MIT-trained physician, bioengineer, and former director of precision medicine at the Marcus Institute in Philadelphia, who describes herself as a healer working with roughly 40,000 patients over her career — from professional athletes to executives to everyday people[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0]. Her central argument is that mainstream medicine is "broken" because it practices "medicine for the average": randomized-trial-driven, pharmaceutical-first care in which 100 to 200 people must be treated with a statin for one to benefit, while roughly 70% of chronic disease is preventable with lifestyle medicine that the system has no profit motive to deliver[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0]. Her alternative is precision medicine: genomics, biomarkers, wearables, and "N-of-1" experiments in which the individual serves as their own control[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].

Szal frames hormones as "text messages" the body sends to keep everything functioning, and singles out cortisol as the hormone to fix first — a "dictator" rather than one voice in a democracy — reporting that around 90% of the people she tests, including professional athletes, have a cortisol problem[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0]. She connects hormone dysregulation to childhood trauma via the ACE (adverse childhood experiences) questionnaire, sharing that her own score is 6 out of 10 and that her trauma showed up in her 30s as depression, a cortisol level of 30 (versus an optimal 10-15 in the morning), pre-diabetes, and stubborn belly fat — which she reversed with lifestyle medicine rather than the Prozac and birth control pill her doctor offered[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].

The conversation ranges across testosterone in men and women, oestrogen and the gut microbiome, the ketogenic diet's different effects on men and women, perimenopause and the women's health gap, and — more personally — Szal's divorce after 20 years of marriage, psychedelic-assisted therapy for trauma, sexual polarity, and the sleep, morning light, and grounding practices she uses to regulate her own nervous system[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].

# Key points

- Szal argues conventional medicine is "imprecision medicine": for statins, 100-200 people must be treated for one to benefit, and while lifestyle prevents about 70% of heart disease and of chronic disease generally, there is no profit motive to prescribe it[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- She says roughly 90% of the ~40,000 people she has tested have a cortisol problem, and speculates 30-50% of the general population do; she calls cortisol a "dictator" among hormones because you cannot live without it and it suppresses testosterone when chronically elevated[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- High cortisol, she claims, is found in about 50% of people with depression (some psychiatrists use it as a suicide marker), grows belly fat via increased cortisol receptors on abdominal fat cells, and — per studies from the University of Texas at San Antonio and Lisa Mosconi at Cornell — shrinks total brain volume in midlife women but not men[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- The ACE questionnaire, validated in the 1990s, links a score of one or higher to increased risk of 45 chronic diseases; Szal's own score is 6/10, which she says manifested as depression, pre-diabetes, cortisol of 30, and low heart rate variability in her 30s[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- She reports receiving only about 30 minutes of nutrition training in medical school — and about the same on perimenopause and menopause — which she cites as evidence of the medical system's blind spots[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- To lower cortisol she recommends bottom-up tools: breathwork (e.g. resonance breathing at roughly six breaths per minute for 10-20 minutes), meditation, rhythmic movement, and supplements such as Cortisol Manager (ashwagandha plus phosphatidylserine) and rhodiola[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- On testosterone: it typically declines from about age 40 in men (she likes 500-1,000 ng/dL); NBA players in the pandemic "bubble" showed low testosterone from isolation stress; in women it is the most abundant hormone and tracks with confidence, agency, and comfort with financial risk, while excess testosterone signals PCOS — the most common female hormone imbalance, with ~70% insulin resistance — which she says can be improved within seven days on a lower-carbohydrate diet[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- She claims the birth control pill lacks true informed consent: she says it raises inflammation two- to three-fold, increases autoimmune disease risk (especially Crohn's), lowers free testosterone, and can shrink the clitoris by up to 20%; she prefers IUDs or barrier methods[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- On keto: men do better than women — around 45% of women on a classic ketogenic diet develop menstrual irregularity or thyroid dysfunction, and women take 18-20 hours of fasting to make ketones versus 14-16 for men; she advises biomarker checks after four weeks and notes ketones aid satiety and act as anti-inflammatory signaling molecules[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- She says 73-75% of women do not get the perimenopause/menopause treatment they deserve despite 100+ symptoms, that women over 40 typically gain ~5 lb of fat and lose ~5 lb of muscle per decade, and that hormone therapy plus heavy weightlifting, cardiovascular fitness, and glucose "disposal" can prevent much of this[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- She attributes the women's health gap to both sex and socially constructed gender differences, citing double the rates of depression, PTSD, and insomnia in women, four times the autoimmune disease, nine times the thyroid dysfunction, ~10% more stress and trauma than men, and a 14-fold higher likelihood of being raped[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
- Other endorsements: continuous glucose monitors as the best behavior-change tool she has seen; 25-35 different plant species weekly to feed an estrogen-recirculating microbiome; vitamin D (deficient in 70-80% of people, with ~400 bodily jobs); sleep as "as close to a panacea as we have" (one bad night raises insulin and cortisol, and only ~2% carry the short-sleep gene); quitting alcohol (which depresses HRV for seven to nine nights); and morning light, where she says benefits begin around 30 minutes rather than the commonly cited 5-10[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].

# Takeaways

The guest's through-line is that hormonal balance — starting with cortisol — is measurable, largely lifestyle-driven, and neglected by an average-based medical system, and that individuals (especially women approaching perimenopause) should establish baseline biomarkers early and run their own N-of-1 experiments with food, breathwork, sleep, strength training, and, where needed, hormone therapy[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0]. These are Szal's clinical views and personal claims as presented in the episode, several of which (supplement effects, birth control harms, morning-light dosing) she offers from her own practice and reading of the literature rather than settled consensus[^hormone-expert-control-your-hormones-control-you-0GQozcTPyO0].
