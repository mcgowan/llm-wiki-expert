# Overview

Steven Bartlett convenes a roundtable of four leading women's health experts with over 80 years of combined experience: exercise physiologist Dr. Stacy Sims, fertility doctor Dr. Natalie Crawford, OB-GYN and menopause specialist Dr. Mary Claire Haver, and orthopedic sports surgeon Dr. Vonda Wright. Their shared thesis is that medicine and research were built around male physiology — women were not even required to be represented in US studies until 1993, and of $450 billion in research spending, less than 1% goes to women over 40 — so women, though 51% of the population and living roughly six years longer than men, spend about 20% more of their lives in poor health with chronic disease or mental health disorders.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]

The panel describe systemic dismissal of female patients — Haver recounts being taught as an intern to label midlife women "WW" for "whiny woman" — and argue this bias persists in everything from "atypical" female heart attacks to endometriosis diagnoses that take 7-10 years. Answering questions submitted by a thousand women in the show's audience, the conversation moves through the mechanics of the menstrual cycle as a vital sign of whole-body health, PCOS and insulin resistance, endometriosis, the trade-offs of hormonal contraception, egg count and fertility decline, pregnancy loss, and perimenopause and menopause — including the guests' own hormone-therapy decisions.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]

Throughout, the experts return to two controllable levers — chronic inflammation and insulin resistance — and to the same lifestyle foundations for every life stage: protein and anti-inflammatory nutrition, resistance training, sleep, and stress reduction, with hormones as one tool among many rather than a cure-all.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]

# Key points

- The panel unanimously say an irregular menstrual cycle is a warning sign: a period should arrive predictably (usually every 25-35 days), and consistent irregularity, missing periods, or changes in bleed pattern are among the earliest red flags of hormonal or systemic disease — Crawford calls the cycle a vital sign far beyond "getting ready to have a baby."[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- The guests stress that having no period (outside hormonal contraception) means harmful low estrogen — bad for bone during the critical building years of roughly 15-25, brain health, mood, and libido — and condemn the persistent sports-culture idea that losing your period means you're training hard enough; Sims recalls a top athletics coach saying he knew athletes were ready "when their periods stop."[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- On PCOS, Crawford explains it as a genetic predisposition (being born with more eggs, which dilutes brain signals and prevents ovulation) that drives ovarian and systemic insulin resistance — nothing the patient caused — while Haver notes she was treated for her own PCOS with birth control pills for 20 years and learned the nutrition side online; management targets insulin resistance and inflammation via fiber-rich whole foods, sleep, stress reduction, and muscle-building exercise (80% of PCOS patients have insulin resistance).[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- Crawford cites startling downstream stakes: infertility is associated with an 80% higher chance of heart attack and 75% higher chance of metabolic syndrome later in life, gestational diabetes leads to type 2 diabetes within 10-15 years in up to 50% of cases, and 50% of "unexplained infertility" patients have endometriosis.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- Endometriosis — illustrated by the 17-year, stage-4 diagnostic odyssey of Bartlett's team member Liv — is described as an inflammatory, autoimmune-like disease diagnosable only surgically, taking 7-10 years on average to identify; teenage pain severe enough to cancel normal life is a highly predictive red flag, and Sims cites pilot data that timed cold-water exposure (around 10-16°C, not ice baths) can dampen inflammation and symptoms.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- On birth control, the panel are nuanced rather than condemnatory: the pill suppresses the brain's FSH/LH signals so the ovaries make no estradiol, progesterone, or testosterone, and Crawford worries especially about high-dose progesterone IUDs silently suppressing ovulation in 18-25-year-olds during critical bone- and brain-building years — while all acknowledge contraception enabled their own careers and that it has legitimate therapeutic uses.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- The guests explain iron and ferritin: menstruating women are commonly iron deficient (WHO estimates ~30% of women 15-49 worldwide are anemic), and they argue lab "normal" ranges have drifted downward with a sicker population — their clinics target ferritin of 60-100 as optimal while women sitting at 20-30 "cannot get help."[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- On fertility math, Crawford's "vault" analogy: women lose most eggs before birth (6-7 million at five months' gestation down to 1-2 million at birth) and ovulate only about 400 in a lifetime; monthly conception odds fall from ~20-25% in the 20s to 10-12% at 35, 5% at 38, and 3% at 40, driven mainly by egg quality (chromosomal and mitochondrial damage), which is why she advises egg freezing by around 32 for women delaying childbearing — insurance coverage raises uptake from under 5% to as much as 50%.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- The panel defend IVF and egg freezing against the "natural is better" narrative — 13 million babies born via IVF — with Wright noting no one attacks technological advances in orthopedics, and Crawford stating 50% of infertility involves male factors; men can improve sperm (a full cycle takes ~90 days) by dropping cannabis, smoking, alcohol, hot tubs, saunas, and prolonged cycling.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- All four guests experienced pregnancy loss and describe returning to work within days because of medical-training culture; they note one in four pregnancies ends in loss, two consecutive losses warrant evaluation, and the post-loss and postpartum hormone crash — the "delta" from the highest estrogen and progesterone levels of a woman's life to near zero — is what the brain struggles with most.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- On perimenopause (typically the 7-10 years before menopause, which averages age 51-52), the guests describe shortening cycles, "I don't feel like myself," a 40% rise in mental health disorders across the transition, and peak female suicide risk between 45 and 55; they attack the "antiquated" requirement of 12 months without a period before treatment, and Haver cites data that early low-dose estrogen can outperform SSRIs for perimenopausal mood symptoms — yet only about 4% of eligible US women were on FDA-approved hormone therapy in 2023.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
- Wright and Haver detail their own regimens (estradiol patch, oral micronized progesterone, low-dose testosterone) while insisting hormones are only one tool alongside lifting, protein, sleep, and stress reduction; Wright warns "you cannot feel your bones crumbling until they're broken," and the panel highlight the genitourinary syndrome of menopause — vaginal atrophy that can make sex "feel like razor blades" — treatable with very low-dose local vaginal estrogen that also prevents the chronic UTIs that "kill old ladies."[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]

# Takeaways

The guests' closing message is agency: women cannot control when PCOS, endometriosis, or perimenopause arrive, but they can understand their own bodies, track their own normal, control inflammation and insulin resistance through food, muscle, sleep, and stress, and demand care with the same persistence they would for a sick child. They frame the conversation as generational repair — Haver contrasts her mother, sedated through perimenopause with a barbiturate "mother's little helper" and now bedbound with Alzheimer's and an osteoporotic hip fracture, with the informed, empowered midlife she wants for every daughter — and as a conversation for men too, since partners, employers, and doctors all shape whether women are heard or dismissed.[^hormone-fertility-experts-we-ve-been-lied-to-abo-kizDk8idpT8]
