# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Pradeep Jamnadas, an Orlando-based interventional cardiologist of roughly 35 years who says he has treated over 30,000 hearts and at least a quarter of a million patients, on why heart disease — the world's number one killer — is striking ever younger people, including a 28-year-old he stented mid-heart-attack[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4]. His central thesis is that heart attacks are not caused by plaque per se but by inflammation cracking a plaque "pimple" that then clots, and that the dominant upstream driver is hyperinsulinemia: years of frequent carbohydrate and processed-food eating keep insulin chronically elevated, producing insulin resistance, fatty liver, and inflammatory visceral fat long before glucose tests flag diabetes — which is why he screens with insulin levels and glucose tolerance tests that standard practice ignores[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].

His signature intervention is fasting, which he distinguishes sharply from calorie restriction: fasting drops insulin, and after about 12 hours the body starts pulling out fat — visceral fat first — while triggering ketone production, brain-derived neurotrophic factor, growth hormone, stem-cell and endothelial progenitor-cell mobilization, and autophagy[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4]. He describes protocols from 12:12 and 18:6 time-restricted eating up to medically supervised extremes — a 72-day fast that resolved one woman's diabetes and hypertension, and a 183-day fast that took a patient from 400 lb to 210 lb — alongside a claim that healthy people should do a 36-hour fast monthly[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].

The rest of the conversation hunts down "hidden" inflammation sources he says explain why apparently healthy people still develop clogged arteries: leaky gut and a bad microbiome, mold toxicity (which he claims affects almost 70% of homes), food sensitivities like undiagnosed celiac disease, heavy metals and pesticides, vegetable seed oils, excess fruit and fructose, arsenic in rice, and poor sleep — one bad night of which, he says, makes you insulin resistant the next day[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4]. He closes with contrarian takes on cholesterol (only small dense oxidized LDL matters), statins, calcium supplements, vagus-nerve "hacks," and a recommendation that every adult get a coronary calcium score and an inflammatory blood panel[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].

# Key points

- Jamnadas explains a heart attack as a three-step failure: plaque formation (an inflammatory "pimple" inside the artery), plaque rupture driven by systemic inflammation, and a blood clot sealing the artery — noting only ~20% of patients with significant blockages get chest pain, and all three processes are worsening today[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- His youngest heart-attack patient was 28 — overweight with visceral fat and fatty liver, prediabetic with normal A1C but very high insulin; Jamnadas argues insulin is "atherogenic" (smooth-muscle proliferation, vasoconstriction, clotting, inflammation) and that by the time diabetes is diagnosed, roughly 10 years of hyperinsulinemia have already produced coronary artery disease[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- He claims glucose is toxic in the bloodstream because it glycates hemoglobin, enzymes, and hormones; frequent eating never lets insulin fall, causing resistance — so the fix is both what you eat and how often[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- Visceral fat, unlike subcutaneous fat, pumps out interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor; ectopic fat also surrounds the liver, pancreas, and coronary arteries, where it stimulates plaque — "if you have a belly, you have a problem"[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- Fasting versus calorie deficit: he argues cutting calories slows metabolism and breaks down muscle, while fasting lowers insulin and withdraws fat "from the bank" — visceral fat first after ~12 hours — and skin retracts, avoiding the loose-skin problem of dieting[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- His protocols run from 12:12 to 18:6, 48-hour weekly fasts, OMAD cycles with 3-day water fasts for diabetics needing major weight loss, and supervised extremes (a 72-day fast, a 183-day fast from 400 lb to 210 lb) using water, black coffee/tea, electrolytes, and MCT oil for cravings; he says most women handle fasting fine except those pregnant or trying to conceive[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- Claimed fasting benefits include ketones as a "cleaner fuel," brain-derived neurotrophic factor, growth hormone (he advises exercising at the peak of the fast), immune-boosting stem cells, endothelial progenitor cells that repair blood vessels, and mitophagy — but he cycles patients out of ketosis rather than keeping them there permanently[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- On exercise, he reports that chronic excessive aerobic exercisers (marathoners, 100-mile cyclists) show more inflammation and coronary disease in his practice than people doing short sprints, resistance work, and HIIT; he recommends only 15-20 minutes of aerobic work plus bodyweight resistance and 30-45-second HIIT intervals with full rests[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- Gut health is central: a dysfunctional microbiome causes leaky gut, letting lipopolysaccharides reach the liver (fatty liver now affects ~25% of the population) and driving coronary disease — he cites a fit, non-smoking 60-something with a calcium score over 1,000 traced to lifelong constipation and gut problems; his prescriptions are varied fiber (30-40 plant types weekly), inulin with FOS, fermented foods like kefir, and 7 hours of sleep[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- He claims almost 70% of homes have some mold toxicity, that mold can colonize the gut and sinuses and re-inoculate the body with low-grade inflammation, and that treating it flattens the progression of coronary artery disease — an area he says medicine has neglected[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- Contrarian food positions: calcium supplements should be stopped (take D3 and K2 instead); rice should be soaked overnight to shed arsenic, then cooled to form resistant starch; excess fruit's fructose causes fatty liver; burned/fried foods create advanced glycation end products; vegetable seed oils skew the omega-6/omega-3 ratio; only sourdough bread gets a partial pass; skip breakfast or eat eggs or kefir with a few berries[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
- On cholesterol and screening: he argues total LDL matters less than small dense oxidized LDL — caused by sugar, omega-6, AGEs, toxins, and leaky gut — that statins carry sarcopenia, diabetes, and cognitive side effects in 20-30% of patients, that "essential hypertension" always has a cause (usually hyperinsulinemia or sleep apnea), and that every adult over 30 should get a coronary calcium score plus an inflammatory panel; he also calls depression "inflammation in the brain"[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].

# Takeaways

Jamnadas's message is that cardiology's focus on stents and LDL numbers misses the disease's real engine — chronic hyperinsulinemia and systemic inflammation from diet, eating frequency, gut dysfunction, and environmental toxins — and that reclaiming the "lost physiology" of fasting and feasting, eating real food, and hunting down individual inflammation sources can prevent or flatten coronary disease, as he says it did for his own father, who outlived a terminal prognosis by 30 years[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4]. These are the guest's clinical observations and views, several of which (extended fasting, mold prevalence, statin skepticism, LDL reframing) run well ahead of mainstream consensus and, as the host notes for the extreme fasts, should not be attempted without medical supervision[^insulin-doctor-the-fastest-way-to-burn-dangerous-gryta3KZKU4].
