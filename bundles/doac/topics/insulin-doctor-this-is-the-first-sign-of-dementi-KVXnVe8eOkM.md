# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Annette Bosworth ("Dr. Boz"), an internist with 25 years of chronic-disease practice, who argues that most of the conditions that bring patients to her clinic — obesity, brain fog, high blood pressure, PCOS, autoimmune problems, depression, and neurodegenerative disease — could be reversed if people routinely made ketones.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM] Her central claim is that chronically elevated insulin is "a chronic disease maker": excess insulin stores energy as fat, drives inflammatory "trash" that accumulates between cells (including in the brain, where she links it to depression, brain fog, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's), and hides the damage for years because standard blood tests check glucose rather than how much insulin it takes to keep glucose looking normal.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]

Bosworth prescribes a "medical grade" ketogenic diet — under 20 total grams of carbohydrates per day, fat-forward eating, no late-night meals, and daily blood measurement of glucose and ketones — organized into a 12-step "keto continuum" that progresses from carb restriction through time-restricted eating (16:8, then 23:1) to prolonged 36–72 hour fasts for the most insulin-resistant patients.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM] She promotes a "Dr. Boz ratio" (blood glucose divided by blood ketones) as a home biomarker, a 3-day sardine-only challenge as a tool for stalled patients, and tells anecdotes she presents as transformative: a Down syndrome patient with an Alzheimer's diagnosis speaking her first three-syllable word after three weeks of keto, a patient ("Jane") who ate only sardines for 100 days, and her own mother, whose cancer markers she says dropped 70% in six weeks on a ketogenic diet when chemotherapy was expected to achieve 30%.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]

The conversation also covers supplements (vitamin D, magnesium, creatine, methylene blue, exogenous ketones), her skepticism that fiber is necessary ("fiber is for farting"), natural alternatives to GLP-1 drugs, and her personal story of being charged with 12 felonies and sentenced to 24 years (suspended) over unwitnessed petition signatures during a US Senate run — convictions she says the Supreme Court and a judge later erased.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM] The host notes his own financial interest as co-owner of the exogenous-ketone brand KetoneIQ, and the episode itself flags that the evidence for keto in Alzheimer's and cancer is early-stage and mixed.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]

# Key points

- Bosworth argues excess insulin — not just high glucose — is the root chronic-disease driver, linked in her account to cancer, high blood pressure, PCOS, autoimmune conditions, and brain "debris" associated with depression, brain fog, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's, and that it goes undetected because doctors test glucose, not insulin.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Physical signs of high insulin she lists include abdominal girth, skin tags in the armpits and groin, acanthosis nigricans ("dirty neck" darkened velvety skin, increasingly seen in teenagers), and loss of hair on the toes ascending up the legs.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- She claims meal timing matters more with age — "one bite of food after 6:00 is worth 10 bites before noon" — and that late-night eating keeps insulin churning for at least 8 hours; she herself stops eating around 3 pm.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Ketones cannot be made until glycogen stores empty and insulin falls; severely insulin-resistant patients on 20 g of carbs a day can take 15 days to produce their first ketone, and Bosworth herself — 60 lb heavier post-third-baby — only produced a urine ketone after fasting a day and walking 22 miles.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- She presents ketones as a "cleaner fuel" that acts as a cellular antioxidant, crosses the blood-brain barrier without insulin-dependent transporters (fueling even insulin-resistant brains), reduces inflammation, speeds muscle repair, and preserves muscle mass during weight loss and chemotherapy.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Citing a study of soldiers, she claims keto-adapted subjects showed little performance change at one month but roughly 20% more power at 6 months and almost 50% more at 18 months versus counterparts — hence her advice not to start keto within 3–4 weeks of a marathon.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Her "Dr. Boz ratio" divides blood glucose by blood ketones (measured on a roughly $60–70 home meter); in-studio, Bartlett measured 86/0.9 (≈95, "burning fat"), Bosworth 83/1.7, and a crew member 88/0.1 (≈880), which she called "a lot of trash being made" and a marker of insulin resistance.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- The 12-step "keto continuum" moves from eating every 2–3 hours, to under 20 total carbs (not net carbs — "fiber is for farting"), to 16:8 and then 23:1 time-restricted eating shifted toward morning, and finally 36–72 hour prolonged fasts to reset pancreatic insulin output.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Her 3-day sardine challenge (unlimited canned sardines in oil, day six of her 21-day course) is pitched as a cheap, high-fat, high-protein way to reach ketosis within 48–72 hours; patient "Jane" ate only sardines for 100 days, during which her vitamin D rose from the 30s–40s to about 108 without supplementation while arsenic and selenium stayed normal.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- On dementia, she tells of a 41-year-old Down syndrome patient with an Alzheimer's diagnosis who, three weeks into a measured ketogenic diet, spoke a three-syllable word for the first time in her life; the episode itself notes on-screen that keto-for-Alzheimer's evidence comes from small, early-stage studies.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Her signature story: in 2015 her mother ("Grandma Rose"), given 6 months to live, refused chemotherapy; on a ketogenic diet her cancer numbers dropped 70% in six weeks versus the 30% expected from chemo, and she lived until COVID attacked the T-cells affected by her cancer — though Bosworth tells current patients keto will not reverse cancer, only help them "deal with it a lot better."[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- Supplement views: vitamin D is a fat-derived hormone that gets "parked" in fat cells when insulin is high; magnesium deficiency is near-universal; creatine is a brain supplement (she takes two scoops daily and says it falsely elevates creatinine-based kidney tests and helps jet lag at 20 g); and methylene blue, she claims, reached brain cells in autopsies and "supercharged" her own cognition.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
- On GLP-1 drugs, she likens using them for vanity weight loss to using chemotherapy to remove leg hair — powerful and costly versus short-term reversible options — and argues ketosis naturally raises GLP-1 and suppresses cravings via butyrate/beta-hydroxybutyrate, with allulose as another booster.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]

# Takeaways

The guest's overarching argument is that decades of silent excess insulin — driven by processed food, constant snacking, and late-night eating — build up metabolic and neurological "trash," and that regularly entering ketosis, verified with blood data rather than guesswork, is the way to "take out the trash" and reclaim brain performance, weight, and healthspan.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM] Listeners should note that many of her strongest claims (keto reversing gray hair, an Alzheimer's diagnosis, or shrinking cancer faster than chemotherapy) rest on personal anecdotes and clinical stories rather than large trials — a caveat the episode itself raises — and that both guest and host disclose commercial interests in exogenous-ketone products.[^insulin-doctor-this-is-the-first-sign-of-dementi-KVXnVe8eOkM]
