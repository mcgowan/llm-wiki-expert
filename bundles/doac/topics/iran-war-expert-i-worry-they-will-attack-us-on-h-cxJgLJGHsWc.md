# Overview

In this third Diary Of A CEO appearance on the ongoing US-Iran war, University of Chicago political scientist Robert Pape — who says he has modeled the bombing of Iran for 20 years and advised every White House from 2001 to 2024 on conflict issues — argues that his predictions have held: despite the US killing Iran's Supreme Leader and roughly 137 top officials in the opening regime-change strikes of late February, the regime survived, consistent with the historical record that air power alone has never toppled a regime since World War I.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc] The war's central result, he contends, is that Iran is now stronger than before it began: within days of the first bombing it seized control of the Strait of Hormuz — the choke point for about 20% of the world's oil — and has held it for five months with mines, drones, and anti-ship missiles that the US cannot eliminate.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]

The guest dissects the failed 14-point memorandum of understanding: he argues Vice President JD Vance treated it as a business deal while Iran read it as a roadmap to regional hegemony, and that President Trump signed it, "owned the loss" for a day or two, then undermined its key articles within 48 hours — on Lebanon, on uncoordinated passage through Hormuz, and on Iran's unfrozen assets — prompting Iran to resume attacks.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc] With strategic petroleum reserves nearly exhausted, the Houthis closing the Red Sea bypass, and Gulf refining capacity offline, Pape places the world on the edge of the "stage three" dilemma of his escalation-trap model: accept Iran as the region's rising hegemon or physically retake the strait with ground forces — a step he puts at roughly 70% probability, possibly within a week.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]

Drawing on his two books on suicide terrorism, the guest warns that ground occupation of territory is the single biggest risk factor for suicide-terror campaigns, and that his greatest fears are a ballistic-missile barrage on US ships in the Gulf of Oman and a mass-casualty attack on American troops or civilians anywhere in the world — potentially inspired rather than directed by Iran's Supreme Leader, whose "unforgettable lessons" rhetoric he compares to ISIS leader Baghdadi's incitement.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]

# Key points

- The guest likens the opening US strikes to a foreign power killing the American president, first family, half the cabinet, the Pentagon and CIA leadership, and a third of Congress — yet Iran's regime stood, as his 20 years of modeling predicted air power alone would fail to topple it.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- Iran controlled the Strait of Hormuz by roughly March 2-3, days after the February 28 regime-change bombing; before the war it had never controlled or charged for passage, but the guest says it "moved from survival to ambition" when it saw the US had no naval presence in place to stop it.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- He reads the signed MOU's Article 5 as ceding fee-setting over Hormuz to Iran and Oman with no US role, and Article 1 (US guarantees of Lebanon's territorial integrity) as anchoring Iran's "security resistance belt" from the Mediterranean to the Red Sea — with Hezbollah in Lebanon and the Houthis in Yemen positioned to threaten pipeline bypasses, protecting a Hormuz asset that would otherwise decline as Saudi and UAE pipelines divert about 7 of 20 million daily barrels.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- Trump's three MOU violations, in the guest's account: Secretary Rubio's Lebanon framework accord locking in Israel's presence, encouraging transponder-off transits of Hormuz's southern passage (bragged about on Truth Social), and conditioning Iran's unfrozen assets on purchases of American grain.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- On why bombing hasn't restored shipping: Iran entered the war with tens of thousands of drones and missiles, was making about 55,000 Shahed-136 drones per year (per an April Pentagon leak), and hides them across a mountainous area 1.5 times the size of California — "like trying to find a truck in California" — while saturation swarms overwhelm US defenses and insurers and crews refuse transits of ships worth roughly $300 million loaded.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- The economic clock: the US released 172 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (part of a 400-million-barrel IEA release across 32 nations), driving the reserve to about 300 million barrels — its lowest since 1983 — matching Trump's G7 admission that reserves would run out in about four weeks; a quarter to a third of world refining capacity is also shut in, compounded by Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- The Houthis closed the Red Sea outlet at Yanbu for Saudi oil hours before the recording, which the guest says bakes in the world economic crisis unless immediately reversed — and notes the spiral is now two-sided, with Iran "in a mood for revenge" and attacking the US base in Jordan.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- He interprets the concentration of roughly 8,000 US ground troops (Marines and 82nd Airborne) with carriers in the Gulf of Oman as preparation to seize a symbolic piece of Iran's sparsely populated southeastern coast — Chabahar or the port of Jask — a "low-probability" play for a victory that might hold weeks, which he explicitly does not advocate.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- Polling he and the host discuss: only 27-34% of Americans support the war versus 53-57% opposed, but 77-85% of MAGA Republicans support it — and the guest argues American deaths tend to harden rather than erode war support in the near term, as leaving would render those deaths meaningless.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- He draws an extended parallel to Lyndon Johnson's taped Vietnam deliberations — escalating despite knowing it would likely fail, to protect a domestic agenda — arguing Trump faces the same incentive structure before the midterms, where a Democratic Congress could defund the war as Congress did in 1974, though Trump's veto and Article 2 claims could trigger a constitutional crisis.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- His suicide-terrorism research — the first complete global dataset, compiled at the University of Chicago after 9/11 — found the key risk factor is military combat presence on territory terrorists prize, not bombing or casualties per se; it predicted the Iraq occupation would spark the largest suicide-terror campaign in modern times, and he calls suicide attacks "the lung cancer of terrorism," deadlier than all other forms combined.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
- On the great powers: he reports Xi Jinping stopped buying 5 million barrels of Iranian oil a day after a summit, warned Trump against a "Thucydides trap" preventive war, and wants neither instability nor a smashed Iran, while Putin sees an opportunity to squeeze an economically strained Europe just as it commits $80 billion more to Ukraine.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]

# Takeaways

Pape's bottom-line prediction is that within months the US will cross into ground operations on Iranian islands or coastline as the economic crisis grows and Trump seeks a "symbolic victory" before the midterms — a move he believes will fail, feed the escalation trap, and raise the risk of the mass-casualty attack he most fears; asked for an off-ramp, he says none remains that avoids the appearance of strategic defeat, the moment for "declare victory and walk away" having passed in early March.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc] His deeper warning, previewing his book "Our Own Worst Enemies," is that America's domestic "era of violent populism" and the international crisis are converging on the most dangerous election of our lifetimes, echoing the chaos of the 1960s — while host Steven Bartlett closes by centering the 90-plus million Iranians living under the bombs, for whom the guest says real hope is now five to ten years away.[^iran-war-expert-i-worry-they-will-attack-us-on-h-cxJgLJGHsWc]
