# Overview

Jimmy Carr returns to the podcast on the day his Netflix special *Natural Born Killer* is released, and opens not with comedy but with gratitude, which he calls "the mother of all virtues."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] His framing device is that Steven Bartlett would hand over everything he materially owns in 25 years' time simply to be the age and health he is now — an observation he pairs with what he calls "life dysmorphia," the Western condition of feeling wretched inside objectively extraordinary circumstances, since happiness in his formulation is "your quality of life minus envy."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] From there he builds a consistently tough-love philosophy: "you can't have an easy life and a great character," it is not the pursuit of happiness but the happiness of the pursuit, and what matters is not the journey or the destination but who you become on the way.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]

The episode's headline argument concerns young men. Carr says the suicide rate among them is horrific and diagnoses the cause as a shortage of agency: helicopter parenting has removed real-world risk, elders have been replaced by YouTube tutorials, and the freedom young men cannot find offline they find online instead.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] He describes video games as a proxy for the career they are not building and pornography as a proxy for sex, and inverts Orwell: power will not be taken by an authoritarian master, it will be given away for cheap dopamine.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] Andrew Tate, he suggests, fills a vacuum — "a 14-year-old boy's idea of what masculinity might look like" — and his own answer to toxic masculinity is deliberately unfashionable and short: be a gentleman, be a mensch.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]

Around this sit Carr's views on craft and character. He argues against portfolio careers and for specialisation, defends imposter syndrome as a signal you are levelling up, treats failure as one of stand-up's great gifts, and describes cancellation as something to be "right-sized."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] The register throughout is a comedian offering his own worked-out philosophy rather than expert advice, and he says so — repeatedly wondering aloud whether he and Bartlett are simply pathologically ambitious.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]

# Key points

- Carr's gratitude practice, which he credits to his friend George Mack, is to stand in a hot shower and remember that nobody you admire from a hundred years ago had that simple pleasure; he notes about 100 billion people have ever lived and that we are in the top percentile for luck, yet "life has never been objectively better and subjectively worse" because human desire is mimetic.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- His practical alternative to long-range goals, developed with Chris Williamson and George Mack, is to ask what you could do today that tomorrow-you would be glad you did — "you won't rise to your goals, you'll fall to your systems" — and he frames work, religion and self-help as versions of the same trade: sacrificing the present for a better future, with God as a proxy for the future.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On identity and self-esteem, Carr says you do not get self-esteem from the six-pack but from being the kind of person who goes to the gym, and that the goal is to give the world — and yourself — "irrefutable proof you are who you say you are"; confidence without that proof, he says, is madness.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He advocates imposter syndrome as a diagnostic: you should feel it roughly every 18 months, and if you have not in the last year or so, push harder — illustrated with a story from Brandon Flowers about Lou Reed, in full leather and mirrored sunglasses, looking in a green-room mirror and saying "I wish I was that guy."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On the young-men crisis, Carr's specific claims are that we no longer learn to shave from our fathers but from YouTube videos, that "14-year-olds used to be babysitters, they now need babysitters," and that children should be given as much freedom as they can cope with — because forbidding risk in the park while permitting anything online is, in his words, a very bad social experiment.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He argues Maslow's hierarchy has been inverted: previous generations lacked food, shelter and warmth but had identity, purpose and connection sorted, whereas we have solved the base and lost the top, worshipping the individual as if a person could survive alone — his image being that "there's no such thing as a baby," only a baby and a mother, because a baby alone is dead.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- Carr says he misses religion despite not believing the stories, calling it two kinds of fool who take it literally and who think it worthless — the ceremony works because people came together, not because God is pleased — and observes that football, environmentalism and politics now serve as proxies, with politics carrying more weight than it can bear.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On grief, which he says is cumulative and comes in waves, he describes learning of Sean Lock's death and "laughing and crying" as friends flooded him with clips, admits to suppressing grief by going to work and putting himself in a joyful place, and argues the metric that matters is not your CV but your eulogy — adding that "if you want to meet someone high agency, meet someone that's got six months to live."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He distinguishes depression from sadness, pushing back on Bartlett's summary of his earlier remarks: depression is a disease and suicide is its symptom, "a permanent solution to a temporary problem," whereas sadness is circumstantial — and if your sadness is because life has not worked out, he argues you are in luck, because that you can change.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- His anxiety theory is that it is the flip side of creativity — the mind that writes the jokes is the mind still whirring at 4am — and that anxiety is an attempt to solve a future problem in a present that contains none; his current management strategy is to give himself more to do, on the grounds that "people don't get depressed when they go to the gym" because a bar on your chest is an immediate problem.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On specialisation he is blunt: the world does not need more people who are mediocre at physics, portfolio careers mean competing at 50% against people doing it 100% of the time, and he tells Bartlett flatly not to turn DJing into a business — "you've got a hobby, not everything's a business." He quotes David Tell's response to comics complaining about their management: "be funnier."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He offers a definition of entitlement — the gap between where you are and where you want to be, if you think closing it is someone else's problem, with ambition being the same gap treated as your own — and cites Nietzsche on resentment: "if you think someone's ruined your life, you're right, it's you."[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On luck he points out an asymmetry: people call Margot Robbie lucky for being born beautiful but never call Oppenheimer lucky for being born with a 170 IQ and a heritable work ethic — success is always talent and effort together, and seeing luck this way, he says, makes you more forgiving.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- His concrete communication tip is to speak at 92 beats per minute, a rhythm he says great public speakers hit regardless of pace; he listens to a playlist of 92bpm songs before going on stage, and readily calls Donald Trump an excellent public speaker.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- On free speech and cancellation, Carr says jokes are magnets that attract and repel, that "respectability is a prison and the gates are open," and that cancel culture is the new book burning — no group of good guys in history has censored things; adversity, he adds, is a filter that shows who your friends are, and the best advice he received while cancelled was to answer one question, "who's Jimmy Carr?", and to right-size it: you told a joke and some people didn't like it.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He argues comedy and podcasts are booming because the gap between public and private discourse has never been wider — self-censorship rather than state censorship — and that the least-filtered person in your life is your best friend, which is why an unfiltered comic on stage feels like intimacy; he applies the same logic to Blue Zones, insisting the longevity effect is the eating together, not the olive oil.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- Carr is unworried by AI, calling it "a covers band" — artificial intelligence, not artificial consciousness — able to recombine but not to produce genuine originality, whether the innate kind or what he calls hyper-accelerated rationality; he compares AI panic to smashing cotton machines and notes that YouTube became the biggest TV channel in the world while the BBC and ITV fought over Saturday-night ratings.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
- He discloses what he calls a low-level eating disorder — acute consciousness of weight and appearance, plus having had work done on his teeth and hair — and relays Chris Williamson's claim that men's body dysmorphia is on course to overtake women's; his drug view is that cannabis is "performance inhibiting" and wrong for men in their twenties whose real asset is raw energy and ambition, while he gave up drinking for 12 years and calls it straight edge.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]

# Takeaways

Carr's closing frame is the gift you give your future self. Asked what Bartlett should want at 51, he grants fitness readily (three gym sessions a week, 80% diet) but refuses the request for a happy family on the grounds that another person's happiness cannot be your responsibility — your locus of control has to sit inside you, and their happiness is a byproduct of yours.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] The mechanism he keeps returning to is small kept promises: Chris Eubank Jr limping the last 8km alone in an empty gym because he said he would, Steve Jobs painting the back of the fence, and Carr's own year in Australia writing new jokes every day until a whole show existed — which is why he thinks New Year's resolutions are a bad idea, since letting yourself down is more damaging than the goal was worth.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68] Asked what he would tell his 20-year-old self, the man who advocates chosen difficulty answers: enjoy yourself more, be more present, worry about the process rather than the results.[^jimmy-carr-there-s-a-crisis-going-on-with-men-uHLAazKUU68]
