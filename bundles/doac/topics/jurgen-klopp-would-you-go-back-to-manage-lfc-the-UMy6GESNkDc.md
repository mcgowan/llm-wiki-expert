# Overview

In this episode, former Liverpool manager Jürgen Klopp traces his path from a 1,200-person German village to Mainz, Borussia Dortmund, and Liverpool, arguing that his leadership style grew out of the mix of a very confident, demanding father and a deeply caring mother.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc] He describes becoming a father at 20 — working two jobs while playing semi-professional football in Germany's third division — as the night he became an adult, and says that knowing he was never the most talented player made him a "warrior" who could only compete by giving everything from first minute to last.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]

The core of Klopp's leadership argument is counterintuitive for business audiences: great leaders do not treat everyone the same. He says he treated players roughly 50% identically (shared rules and standards) and 50% according to what each individual needed, because a squad drawn from Munich, Argentina, and Senegal cannot possibly "tick the same way."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc] He argues a leader's job is to make the destination as clear as the sun so people move toward it automatically, to tell people what they need to hear rather than what they want to hear, and to build a togetherness — down to respecting the kitchen staff and gardeners — that makes a team willing to "walk through fire" together.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]

The conversation also covers the decisions that shaped his career: why he rejected Manchester United's approach after Sir Alex Ferguson retired but said yes to Liverpool's "pure football project," why he left Anfield when he realized he no longer had the energy to be the "energy giver," his admiration for successor Arne Slot, his grief over Diogo Jota's death, and his answer to whether he could ever return to manage Liverpool: "It's possible."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]

# Key points

- Klopp says he would never coach another English club, which means a return could only be to Liverpool — "theoretically it's possible" — but he currently loves his role at Red Bull, does not miss press conferences or standing in the rain, and refuses to rule anything in or out at 58.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He attributes his public-speaking confidence to his competitive salesman father — who raced him on ski slopes and never let him win — and his love of people to his mother, who "loved me more than her own life."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- Becoming a father in December 1988 at age 20, while earning low wages in Germany's third division and holding two other jobs, taught him discipline and made him "more an adult than all the other people in my age group."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- On leadership, he argues you must "lead yourself" first, then understand each person individually: he contrasts educating young Trent Alexander-Arnold with trusting veteran James Milner, and explains he protected stars like Salah and Mané differently than young players — "how can we get the best out of people if you treat them all the same? It's crazy."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He became Mainz manager in 2001 almost by accident — the squad voted no confidence in coach Eckhard Krautzun, the club had no replacement, and Klopp won six of his first seven games; he says the team "changed overnight" when he reinstated Wolfgang Frank's ball-oriented 4-4-2 defending.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He describes confidence as "a little flower" that gets stepped on repeatedly, and says his approach with doubting players was: "If you would believe as much in yourself as I do, that would be a start — but as long as you cannot do that, just trust me."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- Klopp reveals Manchester United approached him in 2013 when Ferguson retired, but he turned them down partly because he was under contract at Dortmund and partly because their pitch — "we get all the players we want" — was about star names rather than a football project; he chose Liverpool in 2015 because he liked the team's "proper engine room" and character.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- On losing: "a defeat is a defeat if you don't learn from it; if you learn from it, it's a very very important information" — he notes he lost more Champions League finals than most people play, and after losing the 2016 Europa League final he pulled his players onto the dance floor and told them it was the first final, not the last.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- His "heavy metal football" label came from a comparison with Arsène Wenger, whose teams he likened to an orchestra; his philosophy was "don't waste time with holding back" — giving everything for 90 minutes is the only thing players truly owe the fans who fund the game.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He argues togetherness matters as much as tactics: respect for kitchen staff and gardeners signals whether a player can respect teammates, and he recounts that Old Trafford hospitality staff summed up the post-Ferguson decline as "the new leadership don't know our names."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He left Liverpool not to rebuild but because his standard for himself was being the "energy giver" at the absolute top of his game; after COVID left him effectively covering sporting-director duties on top of winning matches, he realized "I need a break — and you cannot do that in that business."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
- He praises Arne Slot for having the confidence to change little and win the league "by some distance," and argues Liverpool's uncharacteristic £450m 2025 transfer window was reshaped by the death of Diogo Jota, whom he describes as an irreplaceable family member — "I still cannot speak properly about it."[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]

# Takeaways

Klopp's account presents sustained success at Mainz, Dortmund, and Liverpool as the product of people-first leadership rather than tactical genius: organize the team for stability, then invest everything in relationships, individualized treatment, and a culture where the group's togetherness makes it willing to give maximum effort. He frames himself as "a constant tryer," not a constant winner — someone who treats near misses as information, protects players' confidence, and walked away from the biggest job in his sport the moment he could no longer be its source of energy, while leaving the door to Liverpool ajar.[^jurgen-klopp-would-you-go-back-to-manage-lfc-the-UMy6GESNkDc]
