# Overview

Former Vice President Kamala Harris joins Steven Bartlett to discuss her book "107 Days" and reflect candidly on her truncated 2024 presidential campaign, its aftermath, and the state of the country. She describes the present moment as "a troubling time," says she often tells audiences "it may get worse before it gets better," and admits she thinks constantly about "how different it could have been" had she won.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]

Harris traces her drive to serve to her upbringing: her mother arrived alone from India at 19 and joined the civil rights movement in Berkeley and Oakland, and Harris says every message she received growing up was that she had a duty to participate in the fight for justice and equality. She recounts her legal career — from prosecuting child sexual assault cases that left her crying in courthouse bathrooms, to becoming the first woman and first Black person to serve as San Francisco DA and California attorney general — and shares the tools she developed for walking into rooms where she was the only person who looked like her.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]

The most pointed passages concern her relationship with Joe Biden and his staff. Harris says Biden's inner circle actively let negative narratives about her stand and declined to promote her accomplishments, reasoning that "if I was shining, Joe Biden was dimmed" — behavior she calls shortsighted, zero-sum thinking since "we rose and fell together." She recounts Biden calling her on the morning of her debate with Trump to complain about people saying bad things about him, a call she took as evidence that "his motivation was all about himself." She also discusses election night shock, grief comparable only to her mother's death, and leaves open the question of running again in 2028.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]

# Key points

- Harris says she ran a presidential campaign of only 107 days after Biden withdrew, and while she does not regret running, she deeply regrets not having more time; in hindsight she says her earlier silence about Biden's condition, which she once saw as grace, was "probably reckless."[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- She alleges that Biden's staff suppressed her accomplishments and declined to rebut unfair media attacks on her, citing the political lore that outgoing chiefs of staff tell incoming ones "rule number one: watch the vice president"; she says she heard the fuller stories only after the election.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- On the day of her debate with Trump, Biden called her — she expected encouragement but says he instead complained about Pennsylvanians saying bad things about him; she describes hanging up angry and "deeply disappointed," concluding his motivation was about himself.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- Watching the Biden–Trump debate from the Fairmont Hotel in LA, she says she could tell "something was a little off" and believes Biden "did not want that debate" and was talked into it.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- Her debate strategy against Trump: she wrote a smiley face on her empty notepad and decided "the person who's having fun wins"; her team warned her minutes beforehand about the Haitian-immigrants-eating-pets rumor after spotting its main purveyor on Trump's plane.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- She argues Trump's method is deliberate misdirection — "say the outrageous thing" so press coverage skips his lack of a prices plan — and describes his governing style as gaslighting: convincing Americans "you have less because of people who have even less than you."[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- On Joe Rogan: she says she genuinely wanted to do the show but "a lot of games were being played" over scheduling; the real calculus was return on investment versus time in swing states, and she now definitely regrets that it never happened.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- Election night: her campaign manager called to say they needed 200,000 votes "we can't find"; she describes a state of shock comparable only to the grief of her mother's death, repeating "my God" over and over, and says she and her husband Doug did not discuss that night until she wrote the book months later.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- She frames the current administration as "a high-velocity event" implementing a plan "decades in the making" — citing Project 2025, the Federalist Society, the Heritage Foundation, gerrymandering, and court-packing — and warns Democrats it is "bigger than this one guy."[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- Self-critique of the Democratic agenda: she would have led with family policy (child tax credit, affordable child care, paid family leave) before infrastructure and the CHIPS Act, and says it was a mistake not to invite Elon Musk to the White House electric-vehicle event.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- She proposes lowering the voting age to 16, arguing Gen Z — who face climate anxiety, pandemic-disrupted education, and an expected 10-12 jobs in a lifetime — would force politics to address climate, AI, and affordable housing; she notes one-third of the 2024 electorate didn't vote.[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
- On 2028: she says she is leading in polls but hasn't decided, expects to decide "probably in the next year," and says the case for running is simply "if I can make a difference."[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]

# Takeaways

Harris presents her story as both a personal reckoning and a warning: the guest argues that America's crisis is structural rather than the work of one man, that Democrats lost partly through timidity and poor prioritization, and that leadership must be motivated by something bigger than self — a standard she pointedly suggests Biden's circle, and Biden himself at moments, failed to meet. Her closing counsel is to cultivate a small circle of honest people who will pick you up when you fall, and to refuse to let "your spirit be defeated," because "sometimes the fight takes a while."[^kamala-harris-america-is-at-breaking-point-i-m-d-D3lhrrXb4WI]
