# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr Paul Conti, a psychiatrist of more than 25 years in clinical practice and author of *Trauma: The Invisible Epidemic*, whose book carries a foreword by Lady Gaga and endorsements from figures including Kim Kardashian and Tommy Hilfiger.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] Conti explains he chose those two words because, in his practice, the root of the vast majority of what he treated — depression, addiction, panic — traced back to something that had happened and changed the person's brain, and because trauma's reflexive guilt and shame make sufferers hide it, keeping the epidemic unseen.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] His own entry point was the suicide of his youngest brother Jonathan at 20, when Conti was in his mid-twenties; he left a business career for medical school afterwards, and describes the drinking, unhealthy relationships and sense of being cursed that followed.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]

Conti's working definition of trauma is biological rather than emotional: anything that overwhelms our coping mechanisms and leaves the brain measurably different afterwards.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] On that definition trauma arrives in three forms — acute, chronic and vicarious — all capable of producing the same brain changes, and its consequences are physical as much as psychological: inflammation, altered gene transcription, immune dysfunction, accelerated biological ageing and, through behaviour, elevated mortality.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]

The through-line of his advice is curiosity. Conti repeatedly contrasts high-throughput medicine — which takes an inventory of symptoms and reflexively prescribes — with asking *why* someone is depressed, drinking or not sleeping, and building a life narrative that connects isolated facts about oneself.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] He is emphatic that the book is ultimately hopeful: the brain changes are treatable, the internalised "lessons" of trauma are not truths, and in 25 years of clinical work he has seen people change.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]

# Key points

- Conti estimates roughly one in five people carry trauma directly, but "well over half the population" experience a significant negative life impact either from their own post-trauma changes or through someone they love — impacts he says were largely avoidable or treatable.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- He notes that 50% or more of complaints brought to general medical doctors originate from mental health, and argues the death certificate often misses the real cause: the car crash at 120mph, the heart attack at 62 without other risk factors, all traceable back to trauma.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Bartlett cites a 2021 *BMJ* study finding adults sexually abused by age 16 have 2.6 times the chance of dying in middle age; Conti calls the figure momentous and explains it as a full cascade — altered gene transcription switching healthy genes off, immune activation, worse blood-vessel and cancer-surveillance function, plus higher rates of depression, addiction, impulsivity and risk-taking.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Trauma makes people biologically older than their calendar age — a 40-year-old who is really 46, a 65-year-old who is really 73 — through telomere and cellular ageing mechanisms operating in an inflamed internal environment; his analogy is two identical cars, one garaged in a mild climate and one exposed to ice and heat.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- The three categories are acute (combat, assault, accident — the shell-shock model), chronic (racism, bullying, neglect, being seen as "less than"), and vicarious (trauma absorbed through empathy by clinicians, journalists, families and heavy news consumers). Conti says the field oddly recognises vicarious trauma only in professional contexts, which makes no biological sense.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Susceptibility varies with genetics, early-life experience and the "attunement of the emotional compass"; he cites the multiple-hit hypothesis to reject "what doesn't kill us makes us stronger" — earlier hits often make later, milder events the ones that finally produce brain change.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- On otherness, he recalls research on Somali immigrants in Holland finding higher schizophrenia incidence among those who integrated into the wider community than among those living in immigrant communities — evidence, he argues, that a pervasive sense of being different and "less than" changes susceptibility to an illness that otherwise runs at about 1% of the population.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Trauma is heritable through epigenetics: he points to Stanford psychiatrist Darren Richer's work on generational transmission, the Emory cherry-blossom mouse experiments Bartlett raises, and children of Holocaust survivors, where elevated anxiety was long attributed to anxious parenting but is now understood to include altered genetic expression passed on directly.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- His central image is the abscess: trauma walled off inside a person still spins off symptoms — low-grade fever in the analogy, shorter temper, anxiety, insomnia and impulsivity in reality — and, like an abscess, requires an unpleasant procedure to lance before it stops driving illness.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- "Cognitive blind spots" are lessons of trauma mistaken for lessons of truth — a person assaulted who concludes they are not the kind of person who gets ahead, or a four-year-old whose father leaves and concludes men do not love them. The brain, he notes, never reboots to re-examine these early lessons, so they run as an instruction manual for life.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Repetition compulsion, in his account, is not comfort with the familiar but an attempt at mastery: because the emotion systems "don't care about the clock and the calendar," people choose the inattentive or violent partner again and again trying to solve the past in the present. His challenge to a patient claiming eight failed relationships is that they are describing the same relationship eight times.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- The limbic system always beats logic head-to-head, but Conti insists no part of the brain hates us — the negativity bias that makes one poisoning unforgettable was built for survival and simply misfires in modern conditions, as does shame, which evolved to modify behaviour within a tribe and now attaches itself to assault survivors who did nothing wrong. Shame, he stresses, is aroused first and given a story afterwards, which is why a different story can be built.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- He compares American veterans of World War II, welcomed home as heroes, with Vietnam veterans who were not, and attributes the far higher rates of post-traumatic stress after Vietnam to that reception — evidence that social response, not just the event, determines whether brain changes take hold.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- On soothing, he quotes his own line that "trauma promotes pain, pain increases suffering, and suffering makes us desperate to soothe our pain," and describes distress narrowing perspective to the short term — the elephant on your foot. He calls opiates the most powerful psychoactive medicines on Earth, notes he received no training in them as psychoactive drugs, and blames the "no one should be in pain, we'll give them pills" model for hundreds of thousands of US deaths from prescribed opioids.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Sleep, he argues, is the worst-treated condition in medicine: the problem is usually hyperarousal and vigilance rather than a broken sleep system, so sleeping pills require escalating doses and invite dependence, whereas asking why someone stopped sleeping well four months ago often reveals a trauma problem treatable with a short course calming the vigilance systems.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- Trauma also links to weight through two routes — degraded self-care, and inflammatory markers that make the body hold onto calories — and to subtle modern behaviours like phone and video-game addiction, since addiction machinery is common across cocaine, alcohol, brownies, sex and exercise, and often functions as escape, self-soothing or self-punishment.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
- On recovery, Conti says trauma cannot be taken to zero — the memory stays and can trigger — but the response can be attenuated; change works like a word repeated thousands of times, atrophying only gradually, so the task is to notice the shadow voice, recognise it as untrue and let "the judge" of the whole self rule on it, and not to treat a bad-day flashback as proof the work failed.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]

# Takeaways

Conti's argument is that medicine keeps treating the smoke — the depression diagnosis, the sleeping pill, the drink problem — while the fire is a set of brain changes produced by events nobody asked about.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] His prescription is a shift from symptom inventory to curiosity: build the narrative, ask why now, look at what came before you and what your parents carried, and treat the internalised sense of being "less than" as a claim to be examined rather than a fact about yourself.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ] He frames the book as a warning that is nonetheless optimistic — trauma does not have to control us, and he has watched people who believed they could never change do so — and closes by naming his own regret: that he did not look around himself more when he was younger, at Jonathan and at others, when doing so might have kept several people alive.[^leading-childhood-trauma-doctor-10-lies-they-tol-USE89i0kiiQ]
