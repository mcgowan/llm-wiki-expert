# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Michael Franzese, a former capo in New York's Colombo crime family who became one of the highest-earning mafia figures of his era and now works as a speaker and author, most recently of *Mafia Democracy* — whose foreword, he notes with some irony, was written by Rudy Giuliani, the prosecutor who once sought to bury him.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] Franzese opens by recounting Jordan Peterson asking how he did things he was uncomfortable with; his answer was that he stepped out of himself, took care of business and stepped back in — to which Peterson asked which man was the real one.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]

He describes a childhood shaped by his father Sonny Franzese, a Colombo underboss under permanent surveillance by six or seven agencies, and says he was a pre-med student intending to become a doctor when his father received a 50-year sentence for a string of bank robberies Franzese maintains to this day were a government frame-up.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] He left school to raise legal money and hunt down witnesses, told his father he could kill if the circumstances required it, served a two-and-a-half-year probation period and took the blood oath on Halloween night 1975.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]

The middle of the episode is part organisational history — Prohibition, Lucky Luciano, the Commission, the family hierarchy — and part business case study, as Franzese explains the gasoline excise-tax fraud he ran for seven years through shell companies.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] The end is confessional: a friend's mutilated body in an open casket, a made man who killed his own father, a partner who informed, 29 months in solitary, and the night his own father failed to defend him in a room he thought he might not leave alive.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] Franzese, now a Christian and married 39 years, frames the whole account around the claim that "you're not defined by your past."[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]

# Key points

- Franzese distinguishes two roles in the life: the gangster, who does the violent work, and the racketeer, who earns. Of about 115 made men in the Colombo family, he says roughly 20 were genuinely earning — and a racketeer still has to be a gangster when called upon.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- On origins, he argues the US government made the American mafia strong through Prohibition — New York State alone had 36,000 speakeasies, all mafia-controlled — and that Lucky Luciano, with Meyer Lansky (barred from membership as a Jew), created the Commission in the 1940s, splitting the organisation into nine families nationwide. He compares the Commission to the United Nations: it sets policy, but an autonomous boss can ignore it.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- The New York structure he confirms runs boss, underboss, consigliere, capos, soldiers (the first made rank), and associates who never take the oath; across the five families in his era there were roughly 750 made men, with the Gambino and Genovese families around 250 each and the Colombos among the smaller.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- Disputes were resolved by "sit-downs" chaired by a boss whose ruling was final and unappealable, with strict etiquette — you cannot call another made man a liar even when he is lying, or you lose the argument for the disrespect. Franzese notes his publisher HarperCollins was so taken with the idea after his business book *I'll Make You an Offer You Can't Refuse* that they renamed their meetings sit-downs.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- His negotiation principles: know the other person's personality and your fallback position before walking in, be the last to speak, and never let the room know whether you are the smartest person in it or not — techniques he says were essential because you could be entirely right and still be trapped by skilled old-timers, and lose everything you had.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- The gasoline scheme, which he calls probably the biggest scam since Prohibition, exploited a law making licensed wholesalers responsible for collecting fuel tax: he ran 18 licensed Panamanian bearer-share shell companies, collected the tax for up to a year while the government's quarterly reporting lagged, then closed the office and reopened under a new licence — running it for seven years.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- With 40 cents a gallon of margin to play with, he would buy one station on a four-station corner and tell the neighbours he would stay two cents cheaper and could undercut them into oblivion — so they bought their fuel from him instead. He owned or operated over 300 stations, sold around half a billion gallons a month and took in $7-10 million a week, paying his boss $2 million a week in exchange for a guarantee that he won every sit-down.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- He describes the peak trappings — a jet, a helicopter, Mazda and Chevrolet dealerships, a leasing company, restaurants, a video shop and roughly 30 horror and exploitation films — and says the largest amount of cash he ever saw at once was $40-50 million in a room, mostly small bills that smelled so strongly of gasoline he kept them in a refrigerator with baking soda.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- The induction ceremony he recounts: cut finger and blood on the floor, a burning image of a saint in his cupped hands, and the boss telling him he was born again into Cosa Nostra and would die and burn in hell if he betrayed his brothers. He says he felt exhilarated rather than frightened, and that the books had been closed nationwide for around 20-25 years before the mid-1970s, breeding resentment from men who had waited decades.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- Franzese repeatedly calls it an evil life because of what it does to families, offering his own as the case: his mother spent 33 years without a husband and died estranged from him, one sister died at 27 of an overdose, his brother was a drug addict for 25 years, another sister died at 40, and his father served 40 of the 50 years.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- He declines to discuss killing directly — noting he never received immunity, unlike cooperators who now speak freely on YouTube — but says he was among those following orders rather than those who enjoyed it, citing Roy DeMeo as a man who "would have been a serial killer if he wasn't in the mafia." He argues everyone carries the capacity for violence, pointing to how the military trains ordinary people to kill.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- The costs he does describe: viewing the unrecognisable body of Arty "the animal," a father figure, in an opened casket at 19 or 20 — an image that still surfaces in his sleep, where his wife says he fights; a friend he tried and failed to save from a sit-down verdict; a made man who was given the contract on his own father and had not been alone in his house in over 20 years because of the ghost; and a soldier named Tony who shot himself in a phone booth rather than be walked into a room.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- The turning point was betrayal: after a newspaper falsely reported he was strong enough to break away and start his own family, he was summoned to a room where his father had been questioned first and, as the driver Jimmy told him afterwards, "didn't help you one bit — he hurt you in there tonight." Franzese says the bond had to break before he could leave, and that meeting his wife two years later gave him the reason.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- He was arrested 18 times, faced seven indictments and won five trials before his partner became an informant; he took a plea deliberately as strategy, negotiating from a proposed 25 years and $100 million down to 10 years, $14.7 million in restitution and $5 million in forfeitures after surrendering the second half of an Austrian numbered account holding about $33 million — which the informant, who received $2.5 million back, had been unable to access alone.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- He served 29 months and seven days in solitary as retaliation for refusing to testify against a friend, describing men banging their heads on bars and injuring themselves to escape the hole, calling solitary a form of torture he opposes for young people, and crediting a Sony Walkman, the Bible, pastor Greg Laurie's radio broadcasts and above all phone calls and visits with his wife for getting him through.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
- His father's prison advice — "please, thank you, excuse me" — worked, he says, because men serving life have nothing to lose and respond badly to anyone throwing their weight around, as John Gotti discovered. On business today he offers "do what I do best, delegate the rest," insists micromanaging steals a leader from their highest contribution, and argues a business will end up a reflection of the owner's personal life.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]

# Takeaways

Franzese's account holds two claims in tension: that the life gave him genuine skills — negotiation, discipline, punctuality, reading people, taking responsibility rather than assigning blame — and that it is fundamentally evil, because he knows of no member's family that escaped devastation, including his own.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] He credits a half-hour conversation with the pastor who married him, Dr Myron Taylor, with planting the possibility that he could be forgiven and change course, alongside his father's proposal into the life as the two defining moments of his story.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A] What audiences actually ask him about, he tells Bartlett, is almost never the mob: it is how his marriage survived, how his children reacted, how he adjusted — which is why his closing message is that people are not always defined or defeated by their past.[^mafia-boss-i-was-making-1-4-million-a-day-michae-S1JXKT7mf9A]
