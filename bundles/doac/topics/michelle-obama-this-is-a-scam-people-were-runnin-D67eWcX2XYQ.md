# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews former First Lady Michelle Obama and her brother Craig Robinson, co-hosts of the podcast IMO, about the upbringing that shaped them and the realities of life before, during, and after the White House. The siblings describe growing up in a roughly 700-square-foot apartment above their aunt Robbie's bungalow at 7436 South Euclid on Chicago's South Side, where their father — a city worker with multiple sclerosis who "never took a sick day" — and their stay-at-home mother modeled decency, unconditional love, and the rule that what was said around their own table mattered more than what anyone outside it thought.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]

Race runs through the conversation: the guests recount "white flight" emptying their South Shore neighborhood of white families, and Michelle Obama describes arriving at Princeton as an "affirmative action kid" only to earn straight A's her first semester and conclude that the message that she didn't belong was "a scam" — a racket designed to keep underestimated kids from competing. She traces her later pivot from a lucrative corporate law career at Sidley Austin, prompted by the sudden deaths of her father and her close friend Suzanne and by meeting Barack Obama, an "ultimate swerver" who encouraged her not to settle for a partner-track life she had chosen only by "checking boxes".[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]

The interview also covers the strains of marriage and parenthood — including her openness about IVF, miscarriage, and marriage counseling — the unbudgeted costs and security complications of raising young daughters in the White House, the relentless and racialized public scrutiny she faced as the first Black first lady, the recent loss of their mother Marian Robinson, and her decision to skip Trump's 2025 inauguration, which she frames not as marital trouble but as finally giving herself permission to choose for herself at 61.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]

# Key points

- The Robinson household's foundational values were modeled rather than lectured: a father who mentored the whole neighborhood's kids despite progressive MS, a mother whose "philanthropic gene" ran on time rather than money, and parents who treated their children as voices worth hearing — anger only surfaced if the kids let outside opinions override "what you hear around this table".[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- Michelle Obama credits her mother's advocacy with rescuing her from a chaotic second-grade classroom — a month of listening, a visit to the school, and testing that let her skip the grade — and says the kids "left behind" in that class still drive her advocacy for underestimated children.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- The siblings describe white flight firsthand: a once-mixed neighborhood of Korean, Mexican, and white neighbors emptied "without a trace", teaching them as children that "these people are running from us"; their parents' response was to frame racism as "a dumb manifestation of ignorant people" and to keep moving through it armed with empathy — which, Michelle notes, was deliberately instilled because anger in a young Black boy was dangerous.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- At Princeton, despite weak standardized test scores, she earned straight A's and realized the campus was full of "affirmative action they never talk about" — wealth, legacy, and athletic admissions — concluding the belonging myth is "a scam... a racket" meant to keep underestimated students from competing.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- Her late-20s identity crisis came while earning more as a first-year Sidley Austin associate than her parents combined; the sudden deaths of her father and her friend Suzanne (diagnosed with lymphoma in December, dead by May) made her question a life of box-checking, and journaling on "what brought me joy" pointed her to mentoring and public service — the pivot captured in Valerie Jarrett's letter recounting hiring her on the spot at 27.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- She initially refused to date Barack Obama — she was his assigned mentor at the firm and thought two Black Harvard associates dating would look "tacky" — until a full-day first date (Art Institute, a Michigan Avenue stroll, dinner at Lake Point Tower, and Spike Lee's Do the Right Thing) changed her mind; Craig recalls the family predicting "he may last two months" and their mother's verdict, "at least he's tall".[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- Her relationship advice to Bartlett: two independent careers work fine until children — the first joint project you cannot do separately — and resentment builds when one partner's sacrifices go unnegotiated; she urges couples to define "your one life together" early and argues people give up on marriage too quickly because friction is built in.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- On fertility, she speaks openly about the biological clock ("we are born with a finite set of eggs"), her miscarriage, and IVF — noting women carry the physical and emotional burden of shots, appointments, and self-blame while insurance didn't cover treatment — and says the silence around women's health left her feeling "jipped".[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- Her first reaction to Barack running for president was "don't do this" — fearing death threats against the first Black president, the disruption to their young daughters, and costs few know about: first families pay for their own food and even the children's travel on the first lady's plane, while campaigning two years without income.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- She says she negotiated only that Barack quit smoking and that they keep a financial nest egg; in hindsight she should have demanded his team genuinely value his family, since the presidency "doesn't recognize families" — she fought alone for accommodations like keeping two consistent Secret Service detail leaders with her daughters rather than rotating strangers with guns.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- Reading from her own writing, she recounts being "held up as the most powerful woman in the world and taken down as an angry black woman", mocked by a sitting congressman, and questioned "right down to whether I'm a woman or man"; both siblings say empathy — asking what happened to make detractors so afraid — plus the reminder that "this is still the country that elected Barack Obama twice" kept them from bitterness.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
- On skipping Trump's inauguration, she says it was a deliberate act of taking back control after a lifetime of doing the right thing for others — "I think I've done enough of that... let me start now" — and she dismisses divorce rumors with the joke that if she had problems with her husband, "everybody would know about it"; she frames the IMO podcast as the siblings stepping up as the family's next "line of elder wisdom" after their mother's death in 2024.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]

# Takeaways

The episode's arc is from foundation to permission: the guests argue that unconditional love, empathy, and refusing to internalize outsiders' judgments equipped them to move through a racist society without bitterness, and that the "you don't belong" message aimed at underestimated kids is a scam worth naming. Michelle Obama presents her current chapter — the podcast, the skipped inauguration, coaching her daughters on boundaries — as a 61-year-old finally owning her wisdom and practicing the "no" she spent decades unable to say, observing that "your no doesn't usually change anyone else's life." Bartlett closes by crediting the family's grace and example with shaping his own life as a young Black man; the perspectives throughout are the guests' own reflections on their singular experience of race, marriage, and power in America.[^michelle-obama-this-is-a-scam-people-were-runnin-D67eWcX2XYQ]
