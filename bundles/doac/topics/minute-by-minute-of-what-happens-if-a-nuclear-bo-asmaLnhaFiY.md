# Overview

Investigative journalist Annie Jacobsen tells Steven Bartlett that the question behind her book *Nuclear War: A Scenario*, published in March 2024, came from a lifetime of interviewing military and intelligence sources who told her with pride that they had dedicated their careers to preventing nuclear World War III — which left her asking what happens if deterrence fails.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] Her answer, drawn from former secretaries of defense, a former STRATCOM commander, a former nuclear submarine commander, a former FEMA director and Manhattan Project veterans, is that a strategic exchange would not escalate over days or weeks but would run to the end of civilisation in 72 minutes.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]

The mechanism she describes is architectural rather than malicious. Because an intercontinental ballistic missile crosses continents in roughly 30 minutes — and a submarine-launched missile from off the coast in under ten — the United States gives the president sole authority to launch, with a six-minute decision window and a pre-prepared list of strike options that one source likened to a Denny's menu.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] Jacobsen argues that means the president in practice has little idea what he is striking or how many millions will die, and that most presidents are poorly briefed on the role because, as her sources told her, most do not want to know.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]

Jacobsen insists she is a hopeful person writing a warning rather than a prophecy: a man-made threat, unlike an asteroid, admits a man-made solution.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] Her model is Ronald Reagan watching the 1983 television film *The Day After*, changing his position, and driving the arms reductions that took the world from a peak of 70,000 warheads to roughly 12,500.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] The episode closes on the human weight of the reporting — a Nagasaki survivor she met in Brussels, and the source who wired that bomb.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]

# Key points

- Jacobsen says sole presidential authority is genuinely real despite widespread online insistence otherwise, and that Congress issued a report during the Trump administration confirming the president need not ask the secretary of defense, the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, or Congress.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- She describes the "football" or emergency satchel carried by the military aide as containing authentication instrumentation linking the president to the National Military Command Center beneath the Pentagon, plus the "black book" — named, she was told, because it involves so much death — of pre-prepared strike options.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- The US triad, on her account, comprises 400 ICBMs in Midwestern and Western silos, ballistic-missile submarines that a former nuclear force commander, Admiral Connor, told her are harder to find than a grapefruit-sized object in space, and bombers; nine nations are nuclear-armed — the US, Russia, China, the UK, France, India, Pakistan, Israel and North Korea — with Iran a destabilising potential tenth.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Detection runs through the SBIRS satellite constellation, which she says measures hot rocket exhaust from 22,000 miles up in geosynchronous orbit, letting analysts establish a missile's trajectory in about 100 seconds — but she stresses a submarine launch gives no reliable attribution at all, a gap she deliberately builds into her scenario.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- On near-misses, she recounts former Secretary of Defense Bill Perry telling her first-hand that on his night watch during the Carter administration he was told a mass of Soviet ICBMs and sub-launched missiles were inbound, confirmed by both the Pentagon bunker and STRATCOM at Offutt — the cause was a simulated war-game tape mistakenly inserted into a machine, and, as Perry put it, it looked real because it was meant to look real.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- She cites Proud Prophet, the rare declassified 1983 nuclear war game ordered by Reagan, whose civilian participant Paul Bracken concluded publicly that no matter how nuclear war begins — with or without NATO, with or without China — it ends in Armageddon, and that everyone left the exercise deeply depressed.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Her scenario opens with North Korea, chosen after Richard Garwin — who designed the first thermonuclear bomb for Edward Teller at 24 and has advised every president since Eisenhower — spoke to her of the "mad king" and the phrase *après moi le déluge*; she notes North Korea launched more than 100 ballistic missiles in an 18-month period from 2022 without giving the customary notification.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- The scenario's fatal turn is miscommunication rather than malice: the US counterattack of 82 weapons at North Korea must overfly Russia because American ICBMs lack the range to reach it otherwise, Russia reads the launch as an attack on itself, and — following launch-on-warning doctrine rather than "mad king logic" — fires everything.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Jacobsen calls missile defence a fallacy of arithmetic: the US has 44 long-range interceptors, four at Vandenberg and 40 at Fort Greely, against roughly 1,770 US and 1,674 Russian deployed weapons, with a curated-test success rate of 40-55%; a Pentagon spokesperson described interception to her as shooting a bullet with a bullet, one travelling at 14,000 mph and the interceptor at 20,000 mph, 500 miles up — and MIRVed warheads release multiple warheads plus decoys.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Detailing the Secret Service side, she says the Counter Assault Team would evacuate the president rather than keep him on comms for the military, that Marine One is retrofitted against but not proven against the electromagnetic pulse inherent in a nuclear detonation, and that the backup plan is a tandem parachute jump — complicated by the parachutes being kept in the White House office rather than aboard the helicopter.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- She reports that if the president orders 82 weapons the system cannot fire an 83rd without fresh authorisation, which is why a "universal unlock code" exists that the president can release to the STRATCOM commander to continue launching after his own death.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- On effects, Jacobsen describes a one-megaton thermonuclear detonation over the Pentagon producing a flash of 180 million degrees that ignites everything within a nine-mile diameter, blast winds that collapse structures, radiation deaths over minutes to weeks, and fires that merge into megafires of 100 square miles or more; at minute 72 of her scenario a thousand Russian weapons land on the United States.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Craig Fugate, Obama's FEMA director for eight years, told her there is no population protection planning for nuclear war because everyone will be dead, and that his role from the Mount Weather bunker would be continuity of government — which Jacobsen calls a form of fantasy, noting that even secret bunkers run only as long as the diesel lasts.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- For the aftermath she draws on Professor Brian Toon, a co-author of the original 1983 nuclear winter paper with Carl Sagan, whose 2022 *Nature* work puts the death toll at five billion; mid-latitude farmland such as Iowa and Ukraine would be under snow for a decade, ozone destruction would force survivors underground, and only New Zealand and Australia could sustain agriculture — which Jacobsen pairs with Khrushchev's line that the survivors would envy the dead.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- Asked about AI seizing nuclear command, Jacobsen pushes back that ChatGPT is partly channelling *The Terminator*, and offers as counterevidence how analog the systems remain — submarine-launched ballistic missiles, she says, navigate primarily by star sighting — while granting that other nations' systems may be far less robust and that AGI is itself an argument for driving arsenals toward zero.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
- On the hopeful precedent, she recounts that roughly 100 million Americans watched *The Day After* in 1983, that Reagan watched a private screening at Camp David against his advisers' counsel and recorded in his journal that he became greatly depressed, and that the path from there through Reykjavik to the joint Reagan-Gorbachev statement that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" cut the global stockpile from a 1986 peak of 70,000 warheads to about 12,500.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]

# Takeaways

Jacobsen's stated purpose is to make the consequences legible fast enough that readers finish the book and act, on the argument that passivity is not the answer and that Eisenhower's less-quoted warning — that a knowledgeable and alert citizenry is what balances peace against defence — is the actual lever.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] She declines to forecast whether nuclear war will happen and defers the disarmament question to specialists, but leaves the reporting's emotional core as the argument: meeting a woman who was 22 months old when Nagasaki was bombed, having spent years with the source who wired that weapon, she frames the whole subject as cause and effect with real, innocent people on both ends.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY] Her closing anecdote — casting a general as a villain because he ignored her emails, only to learn his wife had cancer — is offered as the same lesson at human scale: the figure coming toward you on the path may be a teammate rather than an enemy.[^minute-by-minute-of-what-happens-if-a-nuclear-bo-asmaLnhaFiY]
