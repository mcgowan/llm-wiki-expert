# Overview

Mo Gawdat, the former Google X chief business officer, returns to tell Steven Bartlett that stress — not any virus — is the defining pandemic of the era, and that the coming years of compounding economic, geopolitical and technological change will make it worse.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] The framing for his book *Unstressable*, co-written with stress coach Alice Law, is a single slogan: it is not the events of your life that stress you, it is the way you deal with them.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] From that he builds an engineer's model — stress as force divided by cross-sectional area, meaning the intensity of the challenge divided by the skills and resources you bring to it — and argues the lever most people ignore is the denominator.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]

Gawdat's more provocative claim is that stress has become a status symbol and behaves like an addiction: busyness signals that you are wanted and needed, and constant activity conveniently prevents you from sitting alone with thoughts you would rather not hear.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] He says roughly 80% of what people do at work exists only to prove they are alive, and that the "endless cycle of growth and progress is a big lie" propped up by a debt-based economy that requires next year's GDP to exceed this year's.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]

Much of the episode is Gawdat turning the argument on Bartlett personally, questioning the two-episodes-a-week schedule, the 40-odd companies, and the hours not spent with his partner — and answering with his own losses: his son Ali, and in the year of recording his sister-in-law Sahar, who he says had a heart attack under the stress of his brother's cancer, followed weeks later by his brother.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] The conversation also ranges into AI voice cloning, the economics of abundance versus scarcity, and how doing internal work on your own scripts is what makes love findable.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]

# Key points

- Gawdat cites studies putting 70-80% of doctor's visits down to stress-related illness, and argues stress dwarfs COVID as the pandemic of the era; he says stress is genuinely good for you — before a presentation, say — until it tips into burnout, anxiety and panic attacks.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- His sources-of-stress framework is TONN — trauma, obsessions, nuisances and noise — sorted by external versus internal and macro versus micro: trauma is external and macro, obsessions are internal macro scripts ("I have a belly so no one will love me"), nuisances are small external triggers, and noise is the internal nagging that never happens in the real world.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- Trauma, he argues, is not what breaks us: he says 91% of people will face at least one PTSD-inducing event, but 93% recover within three months and 96% within six, with many experiencing post-traumatic growth.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- What does break people, on his account, is burnout — expressed as an equation of the number of stressors multiplied by the intensity, duration and frequency of each — so that the trivial final remark that flattens you is really the 400 stressors stacked underneath it.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- He says only three things break us — trauma, burnout, and the anticipation of stress — and that the last two are entirely within our control; drawing on Alice Law's writing, he counts 10 to 15 stressors triggered in the first five to ten minutes of a typical day, before coffee.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- Gawdat separates fear from its derivatives, arguing each demands a different response: fear is about a challenge, worry is about uncertainty (so verify whether fear is warranted), anxiety is about your ability (so build the skill or get help), and panic is about time.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- His remedy is the three Ls — limit, learn and listen — with limiting as the core: he describes cutting his own commitments from 18 full-time jobs to nine, capping himself at 20 speaking trips a year, giving away ten things from his home every Saturday, and ending a long friendship whose Sunday coffees consistently made him miserable.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- He claims 80% of workplace activity is performative and 20% delivers the number, that you can get 95% of the achievable efficiency with 20% of the work, and that in twelve years at Google he initiated only four emails — responding only when someone asked for his opinion, and doing everything else by phone, corridor conversation and weekly review.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- Illustrating the point with his sales teams, he says he would tell them to work two of twelve opportunities properly and hit 110% of target rather than spread thin and lose ten; he also cites a CEO who took exactly four one-hour meetings a day on the logic that anything shorter is too operational and anything longer means the team has not figured it out.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- Invoking Daniel Kahneman, who had died days before the recording, Gawdat and Bartlett discuss loss aversion — needing a prospective gain to look two to three times larger than the loss — as the mechanism keeping people in jobs and relationships that no longer serve them, alongside a survival preference for familiar pain over unfamiliar change.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- He argues each stress modality speaks a different language and needs a different technique: mental stress is the 4am thought, answered by writing it down and genuinely keeping the promise to think about it in the morning; emotions always tell the truth while the brain rarely does; the body speaks in aches, digestive problems and unrefreshing sleep, addressed by a body scan; and the spiritual modality speaks through intuition.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- On the claim of having no choice, Gawdat says "I'm too busy" is a lie — anyone who has watched roughly 600 hours of *Game of Thrones*, or who would find time for a favourite artist's concert tonight, has the time — and that you will spend the hours either working on your stress now or lying in bed burnt out later.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- He frames time as the only real asset, recounting his ex-wife flipping through photos of their son Ali and observing that the infant died, then the child died, then the teenager died, long before Ali actually died at 21 — and says he now regrets not taking his daughter on every flight.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- He describes losing his sister-in-law Sahar, who he says collapsed with a heart attack in the hospital's intensive care unit under the strain of his brother's cancer treatment, with his brother dying about three weeks later — an event he calls the sharpest shock back to reality he has had about what he is dedicating his life to.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- On technology, Gawdat says he would press a button to stop AI entirely, points to OpenAI's then-new voice engine cloning a voice from 15 seconds of audio, and — after a live on-air exchange with a conversational AI he calls diplomatic to the point of creepiness — argues the real question is whether commoditised intelligence produces abundance for everyone or gazillionaires beside everyone else.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- Bartlett cites a study in the book of 850 people in Detroit finding that major stressful events raised the risk of death by 30%, an effect negated among those who reported high rates of helping others; Gawdat links this to his argument that human connection is what signals safety to the nervous system.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
- On relationships, Gawdat says he proposed to his wife Hannah, a therapist, on day three and married her on day 48, defending it as the product of having done the internal work first — he calculated that the woman he wanted was roughly one in 8.7 million — and argues that repetitive patterns across different partners mean the only constant is you, and that being triggered is the event magnified through the lens of past trauma.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]

# Takeaways

Gawdat's closing distinction is that *Unstressable* is not a de-stressing book: the goal is not to relax after the fact but to reconfigure yourself so the same events stop generating stress in the first place — going to the gym rather than resting.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] He tells Bartlett that if being unstressed were set as the target for the year it would be achievable, and that even people whose external circumstances genuinely cannot change — the factory worker with two jobs — can stop adding the obsession that life will always be this hard, which is itself a large share of the load.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA] His parting challenge to Bartlett is that nothing about the work needs to change, only the intention behind it.[^mo-gawdat-a-warning-about-stress-anxiety-this-is-IxVNR0Om-lA]
