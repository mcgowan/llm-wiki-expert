# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Jill Bolte Taylor, a Harvard-trained cellular neuroanatomist who experienced a massive hemorrhagic stroke in the left hemisphere of her brain in December 1996, at age 37, while teaching and performing research at Harvard Medical School. She recounts the morning of the stroke in detail — the pounding pain behind her left eye, her arm going paralysed, losing language and even her sense of self — and describes spending eight years rebuilding the skills of her left hemisphere using what remained in her right.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]

The guest's central argument is that the brain comprises four anatomically distinct modules — left thinking, left emotional, right emotional, and right thinking — which manifest as four predictable "characters" or personalities in every person. She contends that modern society is dangerously skewed toward the left thinking brain, the ego-centred, analytical part focused on "me and mine," while the right hemisphere — which lives entirely in the present moment and experiences connection, peace and possibility — is undervalued and treated by traditional medicine as unconscious.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]

During the conversation the guest brings a real dissected human brain and spinal cord into the studio, walks the host through its anatomy, and has him test special glasses that block half the visual field to preferentially stimulate one hemisphere. Her practical message is that once people learn to recognise their four characters, they can deliberately choose who and how they want to be in any moment — what she calls "whole brain living" — and thereby manifest their own mental health.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]

# Key points

- The guest describes four anatomically structured parts of the brain that produce four distinct personalities: character 1 (left thinking — analytical, organised, ego-centred, the "A-type" that goes to work; she names hers "Helen, hell on wheels"), character 2 (left emotional — stores pain from the past and knee-jerk reactivity), character 3 (right emotional — playful, experiential, present-moment), and character 4 (right thinking — wisdom, gratitude, connection to the whole).[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- She argues every relationship therefore contains "eight of us" — four characters in each person — and that knowing your own four is the first step to choosing between them on purpose rather than running on automatic.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- On 10 December 1996 she suffered a hemorrhagic stroke from an arteriovenous malformation: a blood vessel exploded, blood spilled into the extracellular matrix (blood is "essentially poison to cellular communication"), and over four hours her left hemisphere progressively went offline, taking with it language, numbers and her identity — 911 "didn't exist" for her, and it took 45 minutes matching squiggles on a business card to dial her office.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- Two and a half weeks later surgeons removed a golf-ball-sized blood clot from her left hemisphere; recovery took eight years, including four years just to understand what the number one was, and she describes becoming "an infant in a woman's body" at 37.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- She claims trauma, craving and addiction live in the left hemisphere's emotional system (naming the insular cortex as the seat of craving), and that healing trauma means not getting rid of it but acknowledging it, letting character 4 self-soothe and "hold" it, and redirecting energy rather than turning trauma into a lifestyle.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- Her "90-second rule": an emotion triggered by a thought physiologically loops through the body in under 90 seconds; staying angry longer means you are re-thinking the thought and re-stimulating the loop. She says she enjoys even anger and grief as evidence of being wired for full human experience.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- The host tests glasses that occlude half of each visual field: opening the right lateral field (stimulating the left hemisphere) made him feel focused and analytical, while opening the other side made him feel deeply relaxed; the guest attributes this to visual fibres crossing to the opposite hemisphere and cites fMRI work at Harvard by Marty Teicher and psychiatrist Frederick Schiffer, who used such glasses with psychiatric patients.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- She frames human existence in cellular terms — roughly 50 trillion "molecular geniuses," with the embryo multiplying at about 250,000 new cells per second across gestation — and tells the story of the egg cell forming in the fifth week of the grandmother's pregnancy to argue people should feel awe at the odds they beat just to exist.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- Her brain-health advice at the cellular level: sleep above all (so glial cells can clear waste), avoid preservatives, pesticides and sugar, move your body, stay hydrated, keep learning, and avoid alcohol, which she says dehydrates and eventually destroys cells.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- She argues society's individualism, zero-sum leadership and "spiritual crisis" reflect collective over-reliance on the left thinking brain, and that the next step in human evolution is "waking up the whole brain" so war, hate and division are no longer acceptable.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- As a playfulness intervention, she had physicians draw a hopscotch in chalk outside an emergency room to restore moments of glee — mentioning that physicians currently have very high suicide rates because society expects them to be in left-thinking mode all the time.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
- Her closing message: "Your life is worth 30 seconds" — physically pause, slow down, and take a breath rather than rushing, a phrase she says changed her own life.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]

# Takeaways

The guest presents her stroke not as tragedy but liberation — "it set me free" from living by others' expectations — and argues that everyone, without losing half their brain, can access the same lesson: learn the four characters, practise recognising which one "has the microphone," and deliberately shift between them to balance productivity with presence, and self-protection with connection. Steven Bartlett closes by noting her remarkable gratitude and the way the conversation itself shifted him from his working, factual mind into the present moment.[^no-1-brain-scientist-your-brain-is-lying-to-you-hQaN5w3YwtM]
