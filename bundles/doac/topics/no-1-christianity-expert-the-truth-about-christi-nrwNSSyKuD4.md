# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett interviews Wes Huff, a Christian apologist, historian, and theologian with Apologetics Canada who studies ancient biblical manuscripts, papyrology, and paleography. Framed against what both men describe as a surprising religious revival — Bartlett cites data that 63% of US adults identify as Christian, Bible sales hit a 21-year high in 2025 with 19 million units sold, and weekly Bible reading rose to 42% of US adults — Huff argues that the decline of "new atheism" (Dawkins, Harris, Hitchens, Dennett) left a vacuum of meaning that Christianity is uniquely positioned to fill.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]

Huff makes a historian's case for the reliability of the New Testament: he argues the Gospels are first-century Greco-Roman biographies written within the lifetimes of eyewitnesses, that Paul is the earliest source, and that comparative source analysis favors Jesus over even the Roman emperor Tiberius. He contends that "embarrassing" details — such as women being the first witnesses to the empty tomb in a culture that discounted female testimony — and the disciples' willingness to endure persecution ("liars make poor martyrs") point to the resurrection being genuinely believed and, in his view, true.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]

Bartlett presses back throughout with classic skeptical objections — the "Chinese whispers" problem of oral transmission, the problem of evil, the geography-of-birth argument, evolution, simulation theory, and the fairness of hell — positioning himself as a former new-atheist reader who is now "open-minded and curious." Huff responds that salvation is "received, not achieved," that everyone is heading to hell apart from Christ's substitutionary sacrifice, and that in an age of AI-driven job displacement and rising meaninglessness, Christianity offers not just an antidote but "the antidote" to the identity crisis he sees coming.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]

# Key points

- Huff and Bartlett open with data suggesting a religious resurgence: 63% of US adults identify as Christian (~160 million people), 2025 Bible sales hit a 21-year high at 19 million units, weekly Bible reading rose to 42% of US adults (up 12% since 2024), and Christian/gospel music streams grew roughly 20% in 2024.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Huff argues new atheism "worked better in print than in real life," failing to answer identity and meaning questions, and that removing God from Western societies has correlated with rising anxiety, depression, and what sociologists call "expressive individualism."[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- His comparative-sources argument: Jesus has four first-century biographical accounts (the Gospels) plus Paul as an even earlier source, whereas the emperor Tiberius's four biographers (Velleius Paterculus, Cassius Dio, Suetonius, Tacitus) mostly wrote in the second century — and the earliest, Paterculus, was a "paid propagandist."[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Huff claims roughly 99% of historians and biblical scholars agree the 27 New Testament books were written in the first century, within about 40-60 years of Jesus's death — closer to events than most ancient biography.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Against the "Chinese whispers" objection, he argues oral cultures transmitted stories in large groups with living witnesses who could correct errors, that the disciples preached the resurrection back in Jerusalem ("the scene of the crime"), and that memorable events imprint like 9/11 memories.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- He treats the women discovering the empty tomb as a "criterion of embarrassment" argument: women were not considered credible witnesses in Greco-Roman or Jewish society, and the later apocryphal Gospel of Peter even tried to rewrite the scene — evidence, he says, the original account wasn't invented.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- "Liars make poor martyrs": Huff argues the disciples gained no money, sex, or power — only persecution — so their transformed boldness after the crucifixion is best explained by their genuine belief that Jesus rose, unlike other messianic movements (e.g., Simon bar Giora) that died with their leaders.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- On the problem of evil, Huff argues that calling anything evil "with a capital E" presupposes objective good and therefore a moral law-giver, citing C.S. Lewis's crooked-line argument, and claims altruism toward strangers is an inherited Judeo-Christian ethic rather than a purely evolutionary product.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Huff rejects neo-Darwinian evolution in favor of intelligent design (citing Stephen Meyer and others), accepts an earth billions of years old and adaptation within species, and believes in a historical Adam and Eve — while saying belief in evolution wouldn't undermine Christianity anyway.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- On hell, Huff states that "everybody is going to hell" by default because "no one is good but God alone"; salvation is "received, not achieved" through Jesus's substitutionary sacrifice — heaven is "full of people who understand they are not good enough," and those who reject God get separation from his goodness.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Bartlett cites the meaning crisis: three in five American adults aged 18-25 say their life lacks meaning, US depression prevalence is up 60% over a decade per the CDC, suicide is the third leading cause of death among 15-29-year-olds globally, and about 60% of Americans worry AI will take away what gives them meaning; Huff argues Christianity is "the antidote" because identity is not "the sum of our actions."[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
- Huff shares his personal story: at 11 he was paralyzed from the waist down by acute transverse myelitis and told he would likely never walk again, then woke up walking one month later — doctors used the word "miracle" — an experience that shaped, but he says did not by itself settle, his faith, which he later tested by reading the Quran, the Book of Mormon, the Bhagavad Gita, and the new atheists.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]

# Takeaways

The episode presents Huff's case that Christianity is both historically defensible and existentially necessary: he claims the manuscript evidence, the "embarrassing" details, and the martyrdom-shaped behavior of the first disciples put the resurrection beyond reasonable doubt, while the failures of new atheism and the coming AI-driven identity crisis make the search for transcendent meaning urgent. Bartlett remains a sympathetic skeptic — granting that religion demonstrably helps people like his converted friend, while questioning whether transformation is evidence of truth. Huff's closing counsel to the lost, as he frames it, is that they have God-given purpose and should start by reading the Gospels of Matthew or John to investigate who Jesus is.[^no-1-christianity-expert-the-truth-about-christi-nrwNSSyKuD4]
