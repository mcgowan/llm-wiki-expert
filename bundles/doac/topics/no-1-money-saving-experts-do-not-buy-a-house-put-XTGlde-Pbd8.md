# Overview

In this roundtable episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett hosts three finance educators with sharply different philosophies: Humphrey Yang (a YouTuber and former financial adviser who favours a conservative ~90% index-fund allocation), Raoul Pal (ex-Goldman Sachs macro investor and Real Vision founder who is effectively all-in on crypto), and Jaspreet Singh (Minority Mindset/Market Briefs founder who preaches diversification across real estate, stocks, speculative assets and gold). The conversation is framed as a "money-making masterclass" covering income, investing, debt, housing, pensions and the psychology of money.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]

The guests share substantial common ground: that income is the primary lever early on and sales the single most valuable life skill; that most people avoid their finances out of fear and jargon; that emotion — not information — is the main destroyer of returns; and that leaving money in a bank account is "a guaranteed loss" once inflation outpaces near-zero interest. They also converge on the episode's most contrarian claim: none of them would recommend buying a primary home as a wealth-creation strategy, with Pal calling "get a job, get money, then get a mortgage" one of the worst pieces of advice you can give.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]

The central tension is between Pal's thesis that currency debasement makes conventional saving futile and crypto the only rational escape for a generation priced out of assets, and Singh's counter that Bitcoin's volatility, 70% drawdowns and lack of cash flow make full concentration reckless for ordinary people — with Yang steering a middle course built on index funds, expense awareness and "Coast FIRE."[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]

# Key points

- Singh's passive-investing maths: $1,000 a month in the S&P 500 (historically ~10% a year) grows to about $1.9M over 30 years; a good adviser beating the market at 11% but charging 1.5% leaves ~$1.8M after ~$600k in fees; an active investor earning 13% would reach ~$3.5M — but he says 98% of Americans should stay passive, and the host cites data that over 90% of actively managed funds underperform the S&P 500 after fees over 20 years.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Pal's debasement argument: printing excess money creates an "optical illusion" that assets are rising when currency is falling — salaries grow 2-3% a year while houses, gold and the S&P rise ~12-13% — so young people's future selves are "automatically poorer," which he says makes their high-risk behaviour logical; he notes the share of married 30-year-olds with a mortgage fell from 52% in 1950 to 12%.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Pal claims Bitcoin has returned roughly 145% a year since 2012 (about 90 million percent since 2010, "the highest performing asset of all time") despite three ~70% drawdowns, that there are now 650 million crypto brokerage accounts — more than all stock brokerage accounts combined — and that only the NASDAQ (~18%/year) and crypto outpace currency debasement; his own liquid net worth is mostly in Sui, plus one Bitcoin and Ethereum-based digital art.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Singh's rebuttals: Bitcoin fell 50% in 2020 and 60% in 2022 while stocks fell less; government policy, quantum computing or fading interest are real risks; unrealised gains are "invisible money" versus dividend and rental cash flow; and even Warren Buffett's ~19% a year should make people suspicious of promised 100% returns. He describes selling some Bitcoin (bought at $3,000) around $70k to buy rental properties that pay monthly income.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- On housing, the panel is unanimous that a primary residence is an expense, not an investment: Singh walks through how a $500k house at 6.5% with 20% down means over half of each payment goes to interest for the first 20 years (with refinancing resetting amortisation), plus rising property taxes and insurance; Yang rents by choice and cites a New York Times report that millionaire renters tripled between 2019 and 2023; Pal notes his first London flat cost 3.5x income while the equivalent today is 12x.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Money-psychology statistics discussed: money is Americans' number-one stressor; 82% avoid thinking about their finances; 67% of Gen Z avoid checking their bank account; 65% of Americans don't know what they spent last month — the panel's first prescription is tracking expenses for 30-90 days (Singh discovered he was spending $2,800/month when he thought $1,500).[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- On a $1,000 windfall, Yang and Singh would invest it in skills and income first (Yang's $150 AdWords course launched his consulting; they frame AI today as the same "knowledge arbitrage" that social media was), while Pal would buy the NASDAQ 100 and crypto; Singh notes $1,000 left in the S&P 500 since 1971 would be worth about $330,000 today.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- All three endorse dollar-cost averaging and systems that remove emotion — Pal calls buying drawdowns "a secret hack that makes people fortunes," Singh's acronym is POOP ("panic leads to overselling leads to opportunity leads to profit"), and they note the best-performing brokerage accounts belong to dead people.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Responding to a real message from Bartlett's friend £40,000 deep in personal loans and credit cards, the panel advises ranking debts by interest rate (avalanche method), extreme expense cuts, extra work, possibly selling the house — and treats bankruptcy as a legitimate option, citing research that those who file often recover faster than those who limp along avoiding it.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Pal calls the "passive income industrialization complex" a social-media fantasy — "there is no such thing as returns without effort," and property is "the least passive income you can imagine" — while Singh argues real estate can become genuinely passive only after a painful learning curve building the right property-management team, targeting 7% cash-on-cash returns.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- On pensions: Singh avoids 401(k)s (limited investment options, fees 92% of Americans don't know, locked until 59½) and describes US Social Security as pay-as-you-go rather than a personal piggy bank — sustained ultimately by money-printing that erodes the cheque's purchasing power; Pal ties the "retirement crisis" (average boomer 401(k) ~$100-200k) to aging demographics; Yang defends 401(k)s as valuable forced savings with employer match, and explains "Coast FIRE" — e.g., $150k invested by 35 compounds at 8% to roughly $1.5M by 65 without further contributions.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
- Pal forecasts an "economic singularity" past 2030 in which AI agents and robots break the population-productivity-debt growth formula, argues agent-to-agent payments will run on crypto rails, and — asked about geography — dismisses the UK as an "economic backwater" that "dropped the ball" on finance, AI and crypto through regulation and demographics, while the US retains unmatched capital and intellectual density.[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]

# Takeaways

The closing positions crystallise the three philosophies: Yang says personal finance reduces to income minus expenses, consistency and avoiding traps like the average $745/month American car payment; Pal says educate yourself, invest above saving, build a generous network — and buy Bitcoin; Singh warns against internet fantasies of easy passive income and prescribes a "decade of sacrifice" of hard work and disciplined investing. A recurring secondary theme, illustrated by stories of Harry Stebbings and Iconiq's founder, is that networks and relationships — giving into them rather than extracting — are among the most underrated engines of wealth. All three are optimistic about AI. Throughout, the guests stress these are their personal strategies and that "personal finance is personal."[^no-1-money-saving-experts-do-not-buy-a-house-put-XTGlde-Pbd8]
