# Overview

Dr Yvonne Burkart, a PhD toxicologist who studied reproductive toxicity and cancer in the laboratory, tells Steven Bartlett that her warning fits in one sentence: do not believe what is printed on the front of a product, read the ingredients.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s] Her argument is that in the United States and many other regions chemicals and products are not required to be tested for safety before going on sale, which she says makes consumers participants in a mass human experiment nobody consented to.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]

The episode is framed by Burkart's own history. She says she stopped menstruating for nine months and at 32 was told IVF or adoption were her only options, with no explanation of cause; she then went outside conventional medicine to integrative and functional practitioners, cleaned up her diet, stripped her cosmetics and fragrances, and had her mercury amalgam fillings removed — after which her period returned within about three months and she conceived without assistance within nine.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s] The experience forced her to unlearn a foundational principle of her training, she says, because endocrine disruptors show non-monotonic dose responses in which very low doses can have greater activity than high ones.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]

Much of the conversation is a live audit of products in the studio — aerosol deodorant, roll-ons, non-stick pans, plastic spatulas and containers, takeaway coffee cups, tampons, clothing, laundry products, candles and incense — with Burkart explaining what she would keep and what she would discard.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s] She closes by insisting the goal is empowerment rather than fear: small, cheap, incremental swaps rather than throwing everything away.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]

# Key points

- Burkart says fragrance is the single most useful red flag on a label, because the words "fragrance" or "parfum" are legally protected as trade secrets in both the US and Europe and can conceal known carcinogens, endocrine-disrupting phthalates, and the most potent known allergens; phthalates are used as film formers so a scent lingers, which she says is why a smell hours later signals exposure.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- She calls aerosol deodorant one of the worst products a person can use, because propellants are petroleum-derived and thousands of products were recalled in recent years over benzene contamination — benzene being a known human carcinogen linked to leukaemia — with the spray itself creating an inhalation risk; Bartlett bins his on air.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On generational effects, Burkart explains that a pregnant woman's exposures reach her daughter's developing ovaries and therefore the eggs that become her grandchildren — multigenerational toxicity — and that epigenetic changes can extend to great-grandchildren, meaning someone living a clean life may already be predisposed.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- She says children are the most vulnerable because a child's detoxification capacity is not fully developed until around age 10, while dust-bound fragrance chemicals settle at floor level where crawling babies inhale and ingest them; she links exposures to childhood leukaemia, cognitive delays, behavioural problems and rising obesity.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On puberty, she says observational studies find children who use more beauty and personal care products carry more phthalates in blood and urine and begin menstruating earlier, which shifts the whole reproductive window forward and brings earlier menopause with downstream risks such as bone fracture.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- She cites a 50% worldwide decline in sperm count over 50 years and argues falling birth numbers may reflect inability rather than choice, while stopping short of predicting extinction.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- Burkart describes a study in which women with no history of breast cancer removed specified ingredients — chiefly phthalates in fragrance — from skincare, deodorant and shampoo for 28 days, after which breast tissue samples showed reduced breast cancer gene expression, with no other lifestyle change.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On PFAS, she describes a class of roughly 15,000 "forever chemicals" including PTFE (Teflon), notes they have travelled up the food chain as far as polar bear fat, and cites findings that a single surface scratch on non-stick cookware can release 9,000 particles into food while a crack releases 2.3 billion; she links PFAS to kidney and testicular cancer, thyroid disorders, miscarriage, pre-term birth, pre-eclampsia, obesity, endometriosis and PCOS.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- Bartlett cites a 2023 study finding higher PFAS blood levels in women in Singapore associated with a 30-40% lower chance of pregnancy and live birth within a year, and a study of blood samples in which participants gained weight regardless of diet if PFAS exposure was elevated — which Burkart attributes to these chemicals acting as "obesogens".[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On microplastics she says they have been found in lungs, blood, gut, heart, brain, placenta, penis and in newborns' first stool, that a 2024 study found brains contained 10-20 times more than other organs at roughly 0.5% plastic by weight because plastic is fat-soluble, and that they cause inflammation and oxidative stress she likens to a million small fires the body cannot extinguish.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- Her product verdicts include: never microwave plastic containers, since even seconds can release billions of particles from a square centimetre and "BPA free" does not change that; disposable coffee cups are plastic-lined and shed around 25,000 microplastic particles within 15 minutes of hot liquid contact plus heavy metals; plastic spatulas shed fragments while wooden utensils, stainless steel, cast iron, glass and ceramic are fine.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On antiperspirants versus deodorants, she says antiperspirants use aluminium salts that block sweat ducts, that aluminium acts like an oestrogen in the body, and that the study she finds most compelling links underarm antiperspirant use in young girls to increased breast cancer risk.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- She argues menstrual products are especially high-risk because the vaginal canal is occluded, warm and subject to friction, and because absorption there bypasses liver detoxification; she cites a study finding higher heavy metal levels in tampon users and recommends organic cotton, noting cotton is among the most heavily glyphosate-sprayed crops.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On environmental injustice, Burkart says products marketed to Black women contain some of the worst ingredients she has seen, including formaldehyde releasers; Bartlett cites Harvard Health figures that about 50% of products advertised to Black women contain endocrine disruptors versus roughly 7% of those advertised to white women, and Burkart notes Black women have the highest risk of breast cancer under 40 and calls the pattern environmental racism.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- She gives five reasons to avoid conventional paraffin candles — carcinogens including benzene, toluene and formaldehyde; ultrafine particles under 0.1 microns that reach the brain and cross into the bloodstream at the alveoli; volatile organic compounds; undisclosed fragrance; carcinogenic dyes — noting the Consumer Product Safety Commission regulates the vessel and wick rather than the wax, and says incense is worse, producing more ultrafine particles than cigarette smoke with roughly threefold increased cancer risk in children whose parents burn it at home.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- On air, she cites the EPA finding that indoor air can be up to five times worse than outdoor air, recommends the German practice of *lüften* — opening windows even in winter — plus removing shoes at the door, regular dusting and vacuuming, and controlling humidity to limit mould; Bartlett cites WHO figures of 3.2 million annual deaths from household air pollution and 6.7 million from combined indoor and outdoor pollution.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
- Her defence mechanism of choice is glutathione, the antioxidant made from glutamate, cysteine and glycine that is concentrated in the liver, kidneys, testes and ovaries; she says it is depleted by non-stick cookware, fragranced products, ultra-processed food, poor sleep, cigarettes and alcohol, and built by exercise, sleep, sulphur-rich foods such as cruciferous vegetables, eggs and dairy, whey protein, and matcha.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]

# Takeaways

Asked for the cheapest high-impact changes, Burkart's ranked answer is: filter your water, stop buying fragranced products (which saves money), and cook at home — with unprocessed single-ingredient foods and stainless steel or cast iron where possible, but without discarding what you already own if you cannot replace it.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s] She is explicit that she does not want the message to be fear, since chemicals are unavoidable and not all of them are harmful; her framing is a marathon of small swaps that compound, and she says being a living example persuades more effectively than warning people at dinner parties.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s] Her view is that waiting for regulators is futile — US cosmetic rules went roughly a century without meaningful update — so the leverage sits with informed consumers, and the emerging area she says most urgently needs research is the disease burden of microplastics.[^no-1-toxicologist-these-products-were-making-me-rObAX1r8r0s]
