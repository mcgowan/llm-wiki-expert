# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Chris Koerner, known as "the king of side hustles," a serial entrepreneur who says he has launched at least 80 businesses generating cumulatively low hundreds of millions in revenue and low tens of millions in profit — while acknowledging most of them fizzled, failed or were abandoned, calling it "a numbers game." His entrepreneurial origin story starts at age nine, selling golf balls retrieved from ditches across from a Utah golf course ("three for a dollar") to save for a red Schwinn bicycle.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]

The guest's central thesis is that starting a business has never been more accessible — around 90% of the ideas he discusses can be launched with $500 or less, on nights and weekends, without quitting a job or raising money — and that the two things stopping people are fear of what others think and failure to connect readily available tools (Facebook Marketplace, AI image generators, vibe-coding apps) to their ideas. He advocates unapologetically copying business models that already work, since existing competitors have stress-tested pricing and operations, and only layering in your own "twists" later.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]

The format includes a suitcase game in which Bartlett hands Koerner budgets of $500, $1,000 and $5,000 and asks what businesses he would start, producing detailed playbooks for an AI implementation agency, drop-servicing home services, directory websites, vending machines, wedding rentals, local email newsletters and small RV parks. The episode closes with a candid discussion of the trade-offs of entrepreneurship — Koerner argues it is often "more of a prison than their 9-to-5" and that a generation has been oversold independence.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]

# Key points

- The guest's foundational mindset rule: "the pain of your problem needs to be greater than how much you care about what people think about you" — he calls caring about others' opinions the single biggest roadblock to entrepreneurial success.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- On copying: seeing a competitor already exists is validation, not discouragement — he uses the Web Archive and SimilarWeb to reverse-engineer how successful businesses evolved, and says his broken-iPhone-screen refurbishing business (copied from a cold caller offering $3 per screen) did $2M, then $5M, then $9M in successive years before an exit.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- His viral Buc-ee's story: noticing the beloved 50-location Texas gas-station chain had no online shop, he bought one of everything, photographed it, launched an unofficial online store, got press coverage that drove hundreds of thousands of dollars in the first 30 days, received the brand's informal blessing after adding disclaimers, and still owns the profitable site five years later.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- His idea filter: if he is still thinking about an idea two weeks later, that's the signal to act; more broadly he urges people to shrink the time between having an idea and doing something about it, building a compounding "bias for action muscle."[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- Validation method: use Meta's free, low-friction tools first — e.g., for a women's creatine brand, generate AI product mock-ups, post gummy vs. powder listings on Facebook Marketplace with identical copy, track views/clicks/messages in a spreadsheet, mine Facebook groups, then graduate to $10 boosts and Facebook ads; he calls Facebook ads "the infinite money glitch" and a skill everyone should have.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- On passion: "follow the profit until you can afford to follow your passion" — the odds that what you love and what makes money overlap at the start are near zero, so fall in love with commerce itself first; he cites his own hated-but-profitable concierge car-buying business as "sweaty, ugly income" he eventually shut down.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- $500 ideas: an AI implementation agency for small businesses (400 million small businesses worldwide; he claims 77% of surveyed owners say AI would be transformational but only about 5% meaningfully use it — charge $500-$5,000 upfront plus ~20% ongoing, e.g., building an after-hours AI voice agent for a gutter-cleaning company); drop-servicing home services (marketing arm with subcontracted fulfillment); SEO-driven directory websites; and vending machines sourced on Facebook Marketplace and stocked at Costco.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- $1,000 ideas: wedding rentals (a Reddit-sourced example of a ~$300 DIY wedding arch renting for ~$1,000 per wedding, sold through wedding planners scraped from The Knot and Zola) and hyper-local email newsletters (acquire subscribers for ~$0.50-$1 each via Meta ads, monetise at ~$0.50 per subscriber per month through local sponsors, then copy the template to new cities).[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- $5,000 idea: small RV parks of 3-10 pads bought with seller financing — he claims they are 3-10x more profitable than single-family rental homes, that roughly 2-3 of every 100 parks called are good deals, and that his RV/mobile-home park business (about 35 parks over seven years) generates $3-4M a year in revenue and has netted him $400-600k.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- Against co-founders: he says failure rates are significantly higher for co-founded companies than solo ones, that people put "99% less thought" into partnerships than marriages, and that 50/50 splits require implausibly symmetrical, permanent commitment — instead, define equity only after hitting a pre-agreed metric like $10,000 in revenue; after ~15 partnerships he now prefers paying operators from quarterly profits rather than equity, which "most of the time goes to zero."[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- He classifies entrepreneurs as starters, maintainers and finishers — he is purely a starter — and argues only someone who is all three (naming Zuckerberg, Musk and Jensen Huang as the rare zero-to-a-trillion founders) needs obsessive focus; for ordinary millionaires, "focus is overrated and momentum is underrated," and distraction can be a signal worth heeding.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
- Both men develop the "mirage opportunity" concept — obvious-seeming markets where everyone fails (group-organising apps, campus what's-on apps, password replacements, podcast tooling) — as a filter for what to avoid, reinforcing the value of copying what already works; Koerner's macro themes are implementing AI into old businesses and the 10,000 baby boomers retiring daily whose businesses can be bought or modernised.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]

# Takeaways

The guest's closing message tempers the hype: entrepreneurship is a trade-off, not a solution — he quotes "there are no solutions, only trade-offs" — and people should test it cheaply to learn whether they love the actual grind rather than the idea of it, with no shame in concluding a 9-to-5 suits them better. He recounts rejection-proofing himself through two years of door-knocking as a missionary in Hungary, and keeps a 2009 psychiatric report calling his ambitions "delusions of grandeur" in his desk drawer as motivation. Bartlett closes by noting that Koerner's path — many small, unsexy, copied businesses run on momentum rather than focus — is a liberating counter-example to conventional startup wisdom.[^passive-income-expert-how-to-make-10k-per-month-4QLWlcneJig]
