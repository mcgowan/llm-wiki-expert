# Overview

Steven Bartlett hosts a return appearance from Paul Carrick Brunson, a former investment-banking researcher who left finance for relationship science, worked for Oprah Winfrey, built and sold what he describes as the largest matchmaking business in the United States in 2017, and now serves as head of global insights at Tinder — a role that gives him access to first-party dating data he says few people in the world can see.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI] His framing device throughout is Eli Finkel's history of marriage in three eras: a pragmatic phase lasting from prehistory to roughly the 1800s in which partner choice was about surviving the week, a romantic phase to 1960 in which the spouse became a companion, and the current age of self-expression, opened by the contraceptive pill and the washing machine, in which we expect a partner to help us self-actualise.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]

That escalation of expectations is, in Brunson's account, why dating is harder than it has ever been: he cites Finkel's finding that 80% of current marriages are more dissatisfied than ever while 20% are more satisfied than ever, because couples now want a partner to be best friend, lover, therapist, spiritual leader and co-CEO at once — a demand only the couples doing deliberate work can meet.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI] His headline claim is that the leading cause of breakups is neither finance nor infidelity but bad partner selection, or failing to make a selection at all — a pattern he says he sees especially among heterosexual women who "allow themselves to be selected" — and he treats this as a life-or-death decision given the health, longevity and domestic-violence consequences.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]

The conversation ranges across evolutionary-psychology claims about attractiveness and mate value, Tinder data on ethnicity and Gen Z, the crisis he sees among men, attachment theory and its Western bias, the dark tetrad, and finally John Gottman's Four Horsemen — with Brunson repeatedly flagging which of his positions are contested and inviting pushback rather than presenting them as settled.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]

# Key points

- Brunson argues arranged marriages on average last longer and produce higher reported satisfaction for both partners — not as an endorsement (he notes many are entered without consent and calls those amoral) but because a village of people who know you assesses behaviour you cannot see, whereas most people choosing for themselves are in a blind state of limerence.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- He replicated that village effect commercially: his matchmaking agency interviewed friends, family, colleagues, direct reports and former partners before matching anyone, on the reasoning that cognitive dissonance makes self-report unreliable — his test for real values is where a person spends discretionary time and money, not what they list.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- His strongest social claim is that when every friend says a partner is wrong, it will not work out "100% of the time," and he treats consensus among the people who love you as truth.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- Against the pop-culture belief that shared values or good sex predict a strong relationship, Brunson says the research points instead to pre-existing individual satisfaction: the level of flourishing you bring in determines the satisfaction you get out, which is why he champions Carol Ryff's six dimensions of psychological well-being — personal growth, purpose, autonomy, environmental mastery, positive relationships and self-acceptance — as work to do with or without a partner.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- The "golden mean" he describes is a waist-to-hip ratio of about 70% for women, which he says holds across eras and across collectivist and individualist cultures as a proxy for fertility (Twiggy hit it despite being thin; he estimates Kim Kardashian nearer 50%), and a waist no more than 70% of shoulder width for men, read evolutionarily as capacity to protect — he notes he used to put male clients in wide-shouldered blazers before dates.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- "Mate value" is his term for the total bundle a person brings to the market — looks and status but also kindness, behaviour and confidence — and he stresses it is context-dependent, illustrated by the "premium effect" a British accent would confer in Richmond, Virginia, or the way children raise or lower perceived value depending on what the other person wants.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- His scarcity strategy came from finding that Black women got fewer matches on mainstream apps — which he attributes to ethnicity being the first filter for millennials and older generations and to small pools of Black men in that age range, not to desirability — so he placed clients on JDate instead and saw, alongside crude and fetishising responses, enough genuine curiosity to produce what he calls phenomenal success.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- On Tinder data he reports that 80% of Gen Z users have dated someone outside their ethnicity, which he reads as the arrival of an "inter-everything" generation, and predicts Gen Z will have the fewest marriages of any generation but the strongest ones, because those who marry will have chosen consciously and will have the tools.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- He says the number one reason men report for not finding a partner is fear of rejection — a finding he expects people to laugh at — and connects the wider male crisis (suicide, loneliness, unemployment, educational attainment) to Richard Reeves's *Of Boys and Men* and to what Reeves calls sedation through drugs and pornography; his historical framing is that low-status men were the ones sent to die in wars, and modern warfare no longer absorbs them.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- Sex ratio shapes behaviour at scale, in his account: the male surplus in rural China left by the one-child policy has given women there far more negotiating power than 40 years ago, while female-majority university campuses produce the opposite dynamic — a small pool of men with leverage and, in his words, more casual sex.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- The largest cohort of new daters is 18-year-olds, with the second wave around 45 and up after empty-nest breakups; Brunson says this older group performs surprisingly well precisely because they cannot build a polished "representative" — unfiltered photos and profiles that state fears and vulnerabilities read as authentic, which the data says wins.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- He defends hypergamy (women selecting partners of equal or greater resources, visible in college-degree swipe patterns) as the residue of a structure that governed hundreds of thousands of years of survival and of a patriarchy that has not been dismantled — women in wealthy Western democracies having had real choice only since about 1960.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- On attachment theory he traces Mary Ainsworth's strange-situation work with John Bowlby and Amir Levine's *Attached*, but adds a caveat he says he has never heard anyone make: the categories are culturally Western, and studies in Japan and Uganda classify as secure the same distressed behaviour that would read as anxious in the US, because caregivers there are never expected to leave.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- He cites research linking attachment style to orgasm rates by context — secure and anxious people orgasm substantially less in one-night stands and casual sex, while avoidant people orgasm far more, and the pattern reverses in friends-with-benefits arrangements where avoidants must surrender some emotional intimacy.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- His five fundamentals of a partner, drawn from ten years of research, are emotional fitness (with low neuroticism — staying the same person through highs and lows — as the underrated component), courageous vision, resilient resourcefulness, open-minded curiosity (which he calls one of the sexiest traits), and compassionate support; a partner need only be on the path toward these rather than scoring highly on all of them.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- The dark tetrad — narcissism, psychopathy, Machiavellianism, sadism — covers less than 15% of the population by his estimate, and he notes speed-dating research finding narcissists and psychopaths are the most successful, because they present as self-assured and are willing to say whatever gets them what they want; he says a clinically diagnosed narcissist does not change, only learns to manage the behaviour.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- He declines to treat past infidelity or a criminal record as red flags, holding that behaviour can change, and names only two genuine red flags: the dark tetrad, and no effort — a partner who refuses therapy, exercises, or any work on the relationship.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
- On Gottman's Four Horsemen — criticism, defensiveness, stonewalling and contempt — Brunson says contempt is the killer, carrying a 99% likelihood of breakup, because the eye-roll and the look-away communicate that a partner is beneath you; he adds that couples who date about two years before marrying show a substantially lower divorce rate, around 22%.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]

# Takeaways

Brunson's argument reduces to a sequence: flourish first, choose deliberately using the five fundamentals rather than a checklist of height, income and race that mathematically eliminates almost everyone, and then treat the relationship as a skill practice rather than a finish line — "great love looks boring but it feels great," and a partnership is always either growing or dying, never static.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI] He frames most breakups not as single catastrophes but as hundreds of hairline fractures from unresolved conflict, which makes conflict resolution the practical lever and self-esteem the upstream one — since low self-esteem both drives people to date for public validation and makes them easy prey for the dark tetrad.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI] His closing case for taking any of this seriously is that Sheryl Sandberg called partner choice the most important career decision a person makes and Warren Buffett called it the most important decision full stop, and that at the two deathbeds he has sat beside, nobody talked about their career.[^paul-brunson-the-70-30-body-shape-is-scientifica-i2sHBL8BjWI]
