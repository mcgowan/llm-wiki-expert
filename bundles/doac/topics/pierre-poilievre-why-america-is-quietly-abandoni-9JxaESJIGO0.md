# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Pierre Poilievre, leader of Canada's Conservative opposition and a potential future prime minister. Poilievre argues that America's adversarial turn — tariffs on Canada and Trump's "51st state" talk, which he says will "never happen" — is "a very big strategic mistake": Canada holds the world's fourth-largest oil reserves and is the only reliable US supplier among the top five, and he wants Canada to leverage its resource superpower status to win tariff-free trade for steel, aluminum, lumber, and automobiles while building alliances with the UK, Australia, and New Zealand. On Iran, he backs the US strikes on nuclear sites as "definitely necessary," calling Tehran the leading state sponsor of terrorism, recalling flight PS752's downing that killed 55 Canadians, and arguing the theocratic regime is less deterrable than North Korea.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]

The domestic core of his argument is that Western governments practice "socialism for the very rich": monetary expansion (Canada's money supply doubled from $1.4 trillion to $2.8 trillion in a decade while housing stock grew 13%) enriches the financially connected first via the Cantillon effect, while regulations, permits, and taxes make Canada — with the most land per person in the G7 — the G7's most expensive housing market. His remedies: fastest permits in the world, tax-free home building, unblocked oil and resource development, capped immigration numbers tied to housing and job growth, and licensing reform for the 20,000 immigrant doctors and 32,000 nurses barred from practicing.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]

The interview also covers his personal story — adopted at birth by two schoolteachers after his 16-year-old biological mother gave him up, meeting her at 21, his father later coming out as gay — his intellectual debts to Adam Smith and Stoicism, his narrow 2025 election loss which he attributes partly to Trump's tariffs and annexation talk shifting the conversation, his critique of "wokeism" as illiberal, and his life as father of a non-verbal autistic daughter, Valentina.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]

# Key points

- Poilievre argues the US going it alone is a strategic mistake: Canada has the fourth-biggest oil reserves, sells to US refineries at a steep discount, and — unlike Venezuela, Saudi Arabia, Iran, and Iraq — is a reliable friend; he proposes a Canadian strategic oil reserve of a couple hundred million barrels as insurance against events like a Strait of Hormuz closure.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- He wants Canada to use resources as leverage: tariff-free trade for steel, aluminum, lumber, and autos in exchange for more oil at better prices, plus strategic minerals — while refusing to criticize Prime Minister Carney abroad during trade negotiations because "I have to put my country above myself."[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- On Iran, he says the regime killed 55 Canadian citizens and 30 permanent residents by shooting down flight PS752, harasses Jewish and Persian communities in Canada, and enriches uranium only for weapons purposes; he supported the strikes, distinguishes Iran's "theocratic dream" from North Korea's survival-driven deterrence logic, and hopes the Iranian people overthrow the regime.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- His biography: given up for adoption by his 16-year-old biological mother and raised by schoolteachers Marlene and Donald, who divorced when he was about 12; Donald later came out as gay; Poilievre met his biological mother at 21-22 with his adoptive mother's blessing, and says the experience taught him not to judge people and to "be yourself — scars are the trophies of survival."[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- His economic philosophy rests on Adam Smith — pairing The Wealth of Nations with The Theory of Moral Sentiments to argue self-interest and altruism intersect — and he charges socialism with a contradiction: treating people as greedy in markets but angels in government, when "absolute power corrupts absolutely."[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- "Socialism for the very rich": he claims government actively redistributes wealth upward — housing regulations enrich mansion owners while locking out the young, and money printing (via the Cantillon effect) delivers new cash to the financially connected first while destroying working-class wages, which he calls the root of working-class anger across the West.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- Housing numbers he cites: Canada has 10 times the land per person of the next G7 country but the fewest homes per capita and the most expensive housing; more of a new home's cost goes to bureaucrats than to the tradespeople who build it; Canada needs roughly 450,000 new homes a year until 2035 but builds about 240,000, while 100,000 construction workers sit unemployed; he says there is "zero" case for slow permits.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- Canada's decline as he frames it: from 5th to 25th on the world happiness ranking since 2015, the worst food-price inflation in the G7 (blamed on carbon taxes, fuel taxes, and the single-use plastics ban), and a flat GDP per capita; he holds up Switzerland and Lee Kuan Yew's Singapore as small-government, free-enterprise counterexamples.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- On immigration, he says Canada ran the world's most successful points-based system for 200 years until an "inexplicable" 2021-24 surge outran housing, healthcare, and jobs — benefiting only wealthy landlords and wage-cutting multinationals — and proposes capping numbers so population never grows faster than housing and jobs, while fixing licensing so immigrant doctors and nurses can practice.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- On AI, Bartlett describes agent-proficient hires being worth "50 team members" and cites Anthropic data on entry-level job disruption; Poilievre concedes this disruption may be "fundamentally different" in speed from past technological revolutions and offers principles rather than a plan: AI should empower people with more agency and meaning, and cost savings should lower the cost of living, not be inflated away.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- On the 2025 election loss: his party won its highest vote share since 1988, but Trump's tariffs and annexation talk shifted the campaign onto external issues, collapsing other parties' support behind the Liberals; he refuses to blame Trump for the result, invoking Stoicism, Marcus Aurelius, and Invictus — "focus on what you can control."[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
- He calls "wokeism" a "deeply illiberal ideology" that divides people by race and gender, arguing for colorblind meritocracy; pressed by Bartlett on systemic discrimination (e.g., black mortgage applicants denied up to 200% more often at similar financial profiles), he attributes barriers mainly to government policies — anti-housing rules, occupational licensing, and soft-on-crime policies — rather than to a case for DEI; he also discusses his autistic daughter Valentina, saying she has deepened his compassion and his commitment to work opportunities for people with disabilities.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]

# Takeaways

Poilievre's through-line, as he presents it, is that the anger of the Western working class stems not from free markets but from governments that print money, block building, over-tax initiative, and gatekeep opportunity — and that the fix is "small government with big people": fast permits, low taxes, unlocked resources, controlled immigration, and a colorblind meritocracy. Geopolitically, he wants Canada to arm itself with resource leverage and a bigger military so it can be "masters in our own home" while remaining America's friend, warning that his greatest fear for Canada is becoming the frog in slowly boiling water, and for the West, abandoning the Magna Carta principles of servant government and free citizens. Bartlett closes by noting Poilievre's team placed no questions off limits — a transparency he wishes more politicians would follow.[^pierre-poilievre-why-america-is-quietly-abandoni-9JxaESJIGO0]
