# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews actress and comedian Rebel Wilson around the publication of her memoir *Rebel Rising*, and the conversation traces a line from a shy suburban Australian childhood to a Hollywood career built, by her own account, on being "the fat funny girl".[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] Wilson describes a family that sold pet products out of a yellow caravan at dog shows, a mother who taught kindergarten, and a father whose own father died suddenly when he was 18 — grief she says he never processed, which surfaced as violent rages at the children, emotional abuse of her mother ("fat lazy cow, no one will ever love you"), and financial abuse through gambling.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] Watching that marriage, she says, is why she resolved never to be in a relationship, and she did not date through her teens and twenties.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]

The turning points she identifies are strikingly specific: reading in a school library at 13 or 14 that your personality at 15 is your personality for life, which panicked her into deliberately changing; finding her father's motivational cassettes, including *How to Win Friends and Influence People*, and following its advice to talk to five new people a day; being sent to South Africa on a Rotary youth ambassador programme, contracting a severe strain of malaria, and hallucinating in hospital that she won an Academy Award and delivered a rap instead of a speech.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] She qualified as a lawyer anyway — a five-year double degree that took her ten because of filming — while building a comedy career on the observation, reinforced by a university subject called "comedy and power", that audiences laugh harder at people with a visible physical irregularity.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]

The second half is about unwinding that bargain. Wilson describes a fertility doctor who looked her up and down and said she was not healthy — a blunt remark from a stranger outside her fan base that landed where years of praise had not — and how her team warned that losing weight would destroy the "multi-million dollar pigeon hole" she had built.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] She made 2020 her "year of health", worked with a doctor introduced by Anne Hathaway who focused on emotional processing rather than diet alone, and says the emotional work was the real mechanism.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] She also discusses her memoir's redacted chapter about Sacha Baron Cohen, her partner Ramona, her daughter born via IVF, and her continuing suspicion that she remains a workaholic.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]

# Key points

- Wilson says her father's unprocessed grief over losing his own father at 18 manifested as sudden rages — she recounts him beating her and her sister at around eight years old after mistaking a mattress they had poured water on to cool down for bed-wetting — and that trauma "finds other ways to manifest itself" if not addressed.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- She attributes her lifelong avoidance of relationships to watching her parents' marriage, which took seven years to unwind and left her mother with nothing because "all the money went to the lawyers"; she had one boyfriend at 16 who cheated on her, then did not date again for two decades.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- The article she read at 13 or 14 claiming personality is fixed by 15 triggered a deliberate campaign against her own shyness: talking to five new people a day, joining sports teams and clubs, and — following a strategy of being "naughty to get attention" — behaving outrageously at school to build a reputation.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- Her mother physically dragged her to drama classes at a community centre to build self-confidence, not to make her an actor; Wilson says playing characters worked precisely because "it's not really me", and the confidence gradually transferred to her real self.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- She was diagnosed with PCOS around age 21–22, gained roughly 30 kilos rapidly, and noticed while staging a play she had written that a larger cast member got far more laughs from evenly written roles — an observation she says she chose to exploit rather than resist.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- Her account of the "comedy and power" theory: audiences laugh at people they are not attracted to, and a distinctive physical irregularity (being bigger, very tall, very short, a large nose) makes people want to be your friend rather than your lover — which she treated as "a good tool in my comedy toolbox".[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- Early roles institutionalised this: at 23 she played what she calls "the whale character" on the Australian show *Fat Pizza*, the obese girlfriend a man tried to hide, and describes a fan at a post office repeating the show's insults to her in real life as genuinely hurtful in a way the acting was not.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- She was paid $3,500 for *Bridesmaids*, her first American job, all of which went to joining the union, so she lost money after buying a premiere dress and lived on $60 a week in Los Angeles for the year before release; the film's success then brought six movies including *Pitch Perfect*, which she calls her "golden ticket".[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- Wilson frames her success as an athlete-style trade-off — "I excelled in one area but now there's others that I'm quite lacking in" — noting she had never properly dated, lost her virginity at 35, and describes herself as stunted socially and in health while being the most successful person from her high school.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- The catalyst for change was a fertility doctor at 39 who said she would have a much better chance of a baby if she were healthy; she says the disdain in his voice made it a criticism she could not ignore, unlike the congratulations she got in Hollywood.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- Her team's reaction, she says, was to warn her off: losing weight meant throwing away the multi-million-dollar niche she had created, effectively posing the choice as career versus health and a child; she chose the latter believing her career could be over.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- She credits the emotional work — fortnightly calls with a doctor introduced by Anne Hathaway, processing her father's death and childhood suppression — as the decisive factor over the two-to-two-and-a-half-hour daily workouts and high-protein cooking, saying the weight had functioned as a barrier to intimacy; she cites Johann Hari's account of obese women regaining weight after being hit on as a pattern she recognises in herself.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- She notes acting contracts, including *Pitch Perfect*, commonly bar drastic appearance changes because of reshoots and sequels; that she dated around 50 people in a 2019 "year of love" experiment before her "year of health"; that she briefly used Ozempic for weight management after losing about 35 kilos and would have used it in 2020 had she known; and that she has since regained around 10 kilos after having a baby and directing a film.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- On losing weight she describes a jarring shift in how strangers treated her — doors opened, groceries carried — after years of feeling "invisible attractiveness wise", and interprets backlash to her change as people resenting a sibling-like figure who "dared to rise above"; she notes her father died of a heart attack with diabetes complications and believes she was heading the same way.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
- On the memoir chapter redacted by her UK publisher for defamation reasons, she describes working with Sacha Baron Cohen on *Grimsby* a decade ago as the worst professional experience of her career, one that left her feeling humiliated and degraded; she says he has denied it, that her aim is not to cancel anyone, and that her shame came from going along with it because her self-worth was too low to walk out.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]

# Takeaways

Wilson's account is of someone who converted a liability into a business model with deliberate strategic clarity, then discovered that the model had a cost she was no longer willing to pay — and that unwinding it required emotional processing rather than another diet.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] Her closing advice to isolated young people is that change is available if you actively pursue it, particularly through creative expression of any kind, and that being a "late bloomer" carries no shame — the reason she says she included the story of losing her virginity at 35 despite finding it embarrassing.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs] She remains ambivalent about her own drive, still working seven-day weeks and still chasing the Oscar from her malarial hallucination, while saying her daughter makes her want to work less.[^rebel-wilson-i-used-my-weight-to-my-advantage--xjneW1XZBs]
