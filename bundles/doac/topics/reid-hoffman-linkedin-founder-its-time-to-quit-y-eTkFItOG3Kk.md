# Overview

In this long-form conversation, Steven Bartlett interviews Reid Hoffman — co-founder of LinkedIn, early PayPal executive, Greylock partner, and investor in Airbnb, Facebook and OpenAI — recorded shortly after the 2024 US election.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] Hoffman traces his own formation to science fiction, tabletop role-playing games (he redlined a draft of *Borderlands* for Chaosium at 12 and was paid roughly $160 for work still in the book), and the accident of being born at Stanford Hospital — which he insists is luck rather than manifest destiny, arguing that networks amplify productivity and that "people go 'I'm a genius' — it's like, no, I'm in Silicon Valley".[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]

His central strategic claim is that there are two kinds of business ideas: ones most people agree are good, which are validatable with customers but attract heavy competition, and ones smart people think are crazy, which cannot be validated but buy years of unchallenged runway.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] He says he deliberately builds and funds the second kind, illustrating with LinkedIn — which everyone in 2003 told him would fail because it was individual-focused — and Airbnb, which his own partner David Sze offered him as "the deal you're going to fail on".[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] Around this he layers frameworks: Marines take the beach, army takes the country, police govern the country; ABZ planning rather than a single plan B; and the "infinite learner" disposition he probes for when meeting founders.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]

The conversation also covers his blunt defence of startup work intensity, his views on Elon Musk's treatment of employees, his expectation of retaliation from the incoming Trump administration for having supported Kamala Harris, the distinction between freedom of speech and freedom of reach, and an extended case for AI as "amplification intelligence" — the subject of his forthcoming book *Superagency*.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] He closes on friendship, marriage counselling as a proactive tool, and his decision not to have children.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]

# Key points

- Hoffman says he told early LinkedIn recruits the company had at most a 20–25% chance of success, and argues that anyone claiming 100% is "lying to themselves or lying to you"; he frames that risk window as the opportunity itself, because it is precisely why competitors stay away.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- Contrarian ideas only count, he argues, when it is smart people who think you are wrong — not people who don't understand the domain; when starting a company he goes to his smartest friends and asks specifically why it will fail, citing a breakfast where Mozilla's John Lilly told him LinkedIn's network could never bootstrap because early users see no value.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- His answer to that cold-start problem was that of the first thousand joiners, 10 to 100 would be curious enough to invite others; he also notes LinkedIn's original design as a public CV with search meant a dormant profile still added value to the network — "a virtue, not a bug".[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- He attributes the "PayPal Mafia" to three things: high density of risk-taking contrarian talent, PayPal going public in a technology winter (one of only two tech IPOs that year) and being bought by eBay, leaving a cash-rich network that still believed in the consumer internet while Silicon Valley VCs had moved to cleantech and enterprise.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On hiring, he says a founder not spending a third of their time on it is underdelivering; that references beat interviews ten times out of ten; and that he asks referees for a weakness with the framing that giving none implies the candidate should not be hired — then uses that answer as leverage with the next referee.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- He says he brought from Social Net to PayPal a preference for "two years of experience and an insane learning curve" over the business-school standard of ten-plus years doing the same job — which he ties directly to the calibre of people PayPal ended up with.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On pitching, he warns against reality distortion and says all pitches are dialogues: pitch the huge vision but volunteer the risks and how you will navigate them, because that establishes credibility and trust; and start conversations with investors, hires and partners long before you need anything from them.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On work-life balance he is unapologetic: startups are "default dead", the game is intense, and people who call it toxic "don't understand the startup game and they're just wrong" — though he stresses it is a voluntary choice that should be stated honestly at onboarding, and describes LinkedIn's early norm of six-and-a-half-day weeks with family dinner followed by reopening laptops.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- He contrasts leadership styles: Elon Musk, whom he calls one of the world's great entrepreneurs and who once pitched him SpaceX as sending a turtle to Mars, treats people as disposable parts relevant only to the mission and leaves burnt-out people in his wake; Hoffman says he by contrast is referenceable by every entrepreneur he has worked with, including ones he has fired as CEO.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- He stepped back as LinkedIn CEO after four years on self-awareness grounds — saying he is as good as anyone at running 150 people or fewer, but that beyond 500–1,000 the job becomes governing a community — and kept his office next to Jeff Weiner's, describing his ego as "attached to LinkedIn succeeding".[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On politics, Hoffman says he puts a greater than 50% chance on suffering repercussions "from a misdirection and corruption of the institutions of state" for having tried to help Harris get elected, hopes it stays in the "soft" range of IRS audits and pressure calls, has no plans to leave the US, and says other billionaires privately applauded him while calculating a minimax that staying quiet was safer.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- He nonetheless expects Trump to be broadly good for entrepreneurship through deregulation — he has invested in fission and fusion companies held back by regulation — while calling the environmental consequences real damage and immigration restriction a loss, and criticising David Sacks's focus on "anti-woke AI" as not in the top 100 AI issues.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On speech, he distinguishes freedom of speech from freedom of reach: people should be able to post that the world is flat or that vaccines are a plot, but platforms should attach expert context (as pre-Musk Twitter did) rather than amplify it, and LinkedIn's standard is civility tied to real identity rather than scientific truth — while he explicitly defends having allowed lab-leak discussion.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- His AI framing: it is "amplification intelligence" and a general-purpose technology comparable to the printing press (which he notes preceded a century of religious war) — transitions are painful, pausing is futile because no one group sets the clock, and today's models are savant co-pilots rather than Terminators; he demonstrates with a prompt comparing mixture-of-experts, economic game theory and oceanography that no human could answer.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- His practical AI advice is to use it on something in your own domain of expertise, and specifically to assign it roles — "argue against me as a critic", "how would a historian of technology criticise this argument" — citing Ethan Mollick's line that today's AI is the worst you will ever use.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On careers, he calls "follow your passion" bad advice on its own and urges young people to build "soft assets" — knowledge, skills, network — over salary, saying a 30% pay rise is not what predicts the 300% outcome; take big risks early, establish a platform, and only then go volunteer in Bhutan.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On quitting: you quit when your new plan A is materially worse than your old plan A (that is "plan Z"); a founder effectively cannot quit before the business is a strong going concern, while an employee signs up for a "tour of duty" — he cites telling Matt Cohler to take the Facebook job three years into LinkedIn.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- On blitzscaling, he stresses it is a strategic response to global competition and the need for critical mass (he cites payments businesses being irrelevant below about $1bn annual volume in 2002), not an end in itself — "if you can get away with not blitzscaling it's much better" — and says UK and European founders must reckon with global rather than local competitors.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
- Personally: he says he avoids the "billionaire" identity, worked from a Starbucks before the interview, defines happiness as going through life with people he loves while building things, has taken notes for 30 years toward an unwritten book on friendship, and says he and his partner Michelle see marriage counsellors proactively rather than because they are fighting.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]

# Takeaways

Hoffman's through-line is that success is engineered by choosing games where your view of the risk differs from everyone else's, then assembling a team, capital base and learning cadence capable of surviving the gap.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] He repeatedly pushes self-awareness — about competitive landscape, about your own strengths and weaknesses, and about the trade-offs behind the founders people admire, which he says the brain conveniently edits out.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk] His closing advice, echoing the thesis of *Superagency*, is that you cannot reach an optimistic future by trying to avoid failure — you get there by envisioning it and heading towards it, whether the subject is a startup, a career, or society's handling of AI.[^reid-hoffman-linkedin-founder-its-time-to-quit-y-eTkFItOG3Kk]
