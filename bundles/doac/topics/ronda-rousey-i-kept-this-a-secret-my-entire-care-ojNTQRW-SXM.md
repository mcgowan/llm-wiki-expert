# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Ronda Rousey, the Olympic judo medallist who became the UFC's first female champion and was voted the best female athlete of all time in a 2015 ESPN fan poll, on the release of her book covering the years after her career ended.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] She traces her formation to a set of early ruptures: she was born with the umbilical cord around her neck, scored zero on the Apgar scale, and developed apraxia, a motor speech disorder that left her unable to speak in full intelligible sentences until around age five and pushed her toward sport as a way to connect with other children without talking.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] Her father, whose spine was disintegrating from an untreatable blood-clotting disorder after a sledding accident, died by suicide when she was eight — a loss she says left her permanently braced for the ball to drop even when life is good, and which she later learned made her a second generation of suicide in the family.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]

The disclosure that gives the episode its title is that Rousey fought her entire MMA career while managing an accumulating brain injury she told nobody about. She describes dozens of concussions during a decade of judo in which coaches told her to stop complaining and keep training, to the point that she was symptomatic more often than not, and says the celebrated speed of her UFC finishes was not a stylistic flourish but a survival strategy — she had to end fights before anyone could hit her.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] She kept it from her coaches, from Dana White and from herself, she says, because admitting it would have taken the decision to stop out of her hands, and she stayed silent for years afterwards rather than paint a target on her head or hand a talking point to a wrestling industry already dealing with CTE controversy.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]

The conversation's second thread is what she now thinks winning was worth. Rousey argues that she spent her life hoarding achievements on the theory that they would eventually add up to permanent happiness, forgot most of them, and only found what she was looking for after losing everything — in her marriage to Travis Browne, her children, a regenerative-agriculture ranch in Oregon and a new writing career.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] Along the way she is blunt about abusive coaching cultures, the WWE under Vince McMahon, and the rarely discussed grind of miscarriage and IVF.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]

# Key points

- Rousey says her father hid his diagnosis and chronic pain from the family entirely, explaining that he did not want their last memories of him to be of him in a bed with tubes running in and out of him, and that her mother — a PhD in educational psychology — told her the facts directly when she was eight; for a long time she coped by telling herself he was away on a business trip.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Her mother, AnnMaria, scored a perfect SAT at 16, graduated college at 19, and became the first American to win a judo world championship while working as an engineer, raising children as a single mother and completing a doctorate; Rousey calls the armbar a family heirloom, since her mother rebuilt her game on the ground after blowing out both knees at 17.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- She describes her defining trait not as perfectionism but as a compulsion toward mastery — as a child she drew the same picture of a bunny thousands of times trying to make it match the image in her head, and as a teenager she could train a single technique for hours without noticing time pass, a fixation she says cuts both ways since it also traps her in rumination.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- She dropped out of school at 16 and left home to train with the Pedros in Massachusetts, one of the few high-level judo environments in the US, and developed bulimia in the same period from repeated weight cuts to a weight her growing body could not healthily hold — a pattern she traces to a coach pressuring her into a chocolate shake she then made herself throw up.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Rousey says she was mocked as "Miss Man" at school for being muscular in the early 2000s and wore a zip-up hoodie regardless of heat to hide her arms, which is part of why pushing back against the idea that muscle is masculine later became important to her.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Becoming the first American woman to win an Olympic judo medal, in Beijing, paid roughly $6,000 after tax; she complained about the medal tax publicly enough during her MMA years that it was eventually removed.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- On the mechanics of the hidden injury, she explains that each concussion makes the next easier to get, that the damage came less from fights than from sparring in 14-ounce gloves and headgear that protect cosmetically while making concussion more likely, and that by the end even a light tap — including a scripted slap from Stephanie McMahon, a non-fighter — could concuss her, which she took as proof she should not be fighting.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Going into the 2015 loss to Holly Holm at UFC 193 she had knocked herself out falling down the stairs at home about a week earlier, had a bad weight cut, and wore a mouthguard lacking protection behind the bottom teeth; she says the first exchange knocked her teeth loose and left her "out on her feet" with no depth perception, so she kept walking forward blind rather than reveal she was hurt — and then had to listen to the world critique a game plan that was never a game plan.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- She had suicidal ideation immediately on returning backstage after that loss, and says what stopped her was not wanting to hand her pain to Travis Browne — her understanding of suicide being that it relieves your own pain by distributing it to everyone else, and that her father, who was dying regardless and in constant pain, had a justification she did not.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- On coaching, Rousey says she cannot name a single coach relationship that was healthy: a childhood coach dislocated her jaw for throwing him in front of others, another grabbed her by the throat to prove women cannot defend themselves, and Edmond Tarverdyan came from the same school of thought — she frames it as a systemic problem, noting that teachers are observed and graded while coaches operate as unaccountable kingdoms over athletes who have no alternative.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Her fix was boundaries rather than a new coach — banning FaceTime so she could not be tracked at all times, and confining the relationship to the job — after which she says both the training and the relationship improved; she notes her own mother deliberately refused to be her coach because being parent and coach at once is inevitably unhealthy, and quotes the *Kill Bill* line about a man who lost his father early and collected father figures.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Against her reputation for steeliness, Rousey says she was extremely emotional as a fighter and cried in practice for years — including through an entire double-elimination tournament she went on to win — and was yelled at for crying, which made her cry more.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- She fought as often as she did, sometimes three times in nine months and always on the least attractive cards, because she had promised Dana White she would be available whenever the company needed her and never said no; asked what advice she would give herself on signing day, she says she would change nothing.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- After the Amanda Nunes fight at UFC 207 she spent roughly a year in what she calls depression — undiagnosed, "sad and high and playing video games and eating crepes" — describing exhaustion that a long sleep cannot fix, and credits Browne with dragging her out of what she calls her Gollum cave; she has tried therapy a few times and never found anyone she clicked with.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- On the WWE she is scathing about the organisation while praising the women's locker room and saying Stephanie McMahon and Triple H were doing their best: scripts rewritten at the last minute make moves unrehearsable and cause avoidable injuries, performers are contractors who pay for their own healthcare, and they were deliberately made to feel expendable so they would do as they were told.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- She states that Vince McMahon "created a fundamentally sick environment," that after his formal resignation over misconduct allegations he continued running the company informally through lieutenants — she singles out Bruce Prichard as "Vince's avatar" — and that she believes he still was at the time of recording, arguing Ari Emanuel will have to clean house entirely to make the business function.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Rousey discusses two miscarriages — the first of which she blamed herself for after doing stunt work while pregnant, though her doctor told her the embryos were genetically not conducive to life — followed by four IVF cycles to bank eight embryos, the first of which became her daughter; she reveals on the podcast that she had learned the day before that her latest cycle had failed, and argues the process is far more common than the silence around it suggests.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
- Her account of what actually produced satisfaction is deliberately unglamorous: she says nobody reminded her she had flown with the Thunderbirds until years later, and that restoring degraded ranch land in Oregon through regenerative practices, writing screenplays, and interning in a talent agency's story department reading scripts are more rewarding than "a whole morning of positive comments" ever was.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]

# Takeaways

Rousey's central reframe is that the loss which ended her aura was, in the long run, the thing that freed her: it revealed which friendships were real, broke her dependence on external validation, and forced her back to the standard her mother set — that it only mattered whether she knew she was the best, not whether anyone else did.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] She also makes a case, without asking for sympathy, that she gave the sport everything her nervous system had: she believes she built the most efficient fighting style that has existed, and accepts that losing was necessary for the women who came after her to be taken seriously rather than dismissed against an undefeated ghost.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM] What she says she chases now is the same flow state that judo, fighting and pro wrestling gave her — total absorption in a task, which she calls her happy place and her defence against self-destructive rumination — only pointed at land, writing and her family instead.[^ronda-rousey-i-kept-this-a-secret-my-entire-care-ojNTQRW-SXM]
