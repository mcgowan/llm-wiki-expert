# Overview

In this return appearance on The Diary Of A CEO, NYU professor and Prof G host Scott Galloway tells Steven Bartlett that the two greatest brand collapses of the past 18 months are the United States abroad — for the first time more people see China as a force for good than the US — and AI itself, whose approval now correlates directly with wealth: only people earning over $200,000 rate it positively.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc] He dismisses CEO catastrophizing about total job replacement (Musk's "AI and robots will replace all jobs") as "thinly veiled" fundraising: the valuations require either a trillion dollars of incremental AI revenue or massive labor destruction, and since the data shows neither — US unemployment at 4.5%, radiologist and coder job listings actually up — he believes AI will ultimately create more jobs than it destroys, while conceding a severe "V" that hit 20% unemployment could trigger civil unrest.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]

Yet Galloway also expects a major market correction: infrastructure spending above 2-3% of GDP has always preceded a crash (railroads, electrification, the dot-com era), 40% of the S&P is now tied to the AI bet, and he gives a one-in-three chance that AI proves as world-changing as vaccines or jet travel while capturing no concentrated shareholder value — with Chinese "AI dumping" of cheap open-weight models a plausible crash trigger.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc] The episode's title theme is his account of elite nihilism: he estimates one in three US billionaires has a "go bag" plan — Gulfstreams to New Zealand bunkers — and argues the 0.1% have dissociated from America's well-being because they are sequestered from its schools, healthcare, and streets.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]

The conversation ranges across Sam Altman's fall from grace and why tech CEOs shouldn't be trusted but regulated, the strategically incompetent US war in Iran, career and investing advice for the young, and — closing on his book Notes on Being a Man — rejection tolerance, fatherhood, and purpose.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]

# Key points

- Galloway calls AI job-apocalypse rhetoric "dressed up fundraising": to justify current valuations there must be either ~$1 trillion in incremental revenue from AI-licensed products or massive labor-cost destruction within three to five years — otherwise valuations need to fall 50-70%.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- The employment data shows no meteor: US unemployment is 4.5% (youth 8.8%, slightly below historical average), new business formation per capita has doubled in a decade, radiologist listings are up in 2026, and coder listings are up 11% year on year — though he'd "hate to be a truck driver," the biggest job for non-high-school-graduate American men.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- "AI is not going to take your job — someone who understands AI is going to take your job": he advises keeping a permanent second screen of LLMs, and the host adds that one AI-enabled person takes five jobs, citing his analyst Molly replacing a planned five-analyst team; Galloway now saves a third of his six-figure legal bills by having Claude or ChatGPT redline contracts.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- He sees humanoid-robot promises as "jazz hands" — the CEO's job has become overpromising to access cheap capital — while the real value is industrial robotics: Amazon (his 2026 stock pick) has a million industrial robots, 2.5 times the rest of the US combined, and plans to double retail without an incremental hire by 2032; Tesla he calls a great car company that should trade at a fifth of its valuation.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- On Sam Altman and tech "heroes": technology is the closest thing modern America has to religion, tech CEOs are its successive messiahs who each follow "the journey from Anakin Skywalker to Darth Vader" — they're not evil, they're doing their job of maximizing shareholder value, and citizens are failing at theirs: electing officials who impose guardrails.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- Elite nihilism: he estimates one in three billionaires has a doomsday "go plan" (private jets, New Zealand bunkers) and recounts telling one, "You don't think your pilots are going to kill you?"; roughly 300 US billionaires supply about 20% of political donations, while the 0.1% — with private schools spending $75,000 per child versus $10,000-15,000 in public schools, concierge medicine, and private security — are no longer invested in America.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- The host relays a secondhand account of an AI CEO who reportedly accepts a roughly 7-10% chance of catastrophic outcome but considers summoning the new intelligence more consequential — corroborating, in Galloway's view, the nihilist vein in big tech.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- Bubble history: infrastructure spending above 2-3% of GDP has always preceded a crash; every most-valuable company has suffered a 40-97% single-period drawdown (Amazon fell ~94-97% in 1999-2001); the majority of recent US GDP growth has come from AI capex, so a slowdown means immediate recession — and he gives one-in-three odds AI ends up like jet travel, PCs, or vaccines: transformative for stakeholders, break-even for shareholders, with "AI dumping" by China the trigger he'd fear most.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- He argues GLP-1 drugs, not AI, are the more important technology of 2025-26 for real-world impact and shareholder value, and warns AI's biggest danger is not weapons but loneliness — 42% of American men 18-24 have never asked a woman out in person — though he finds it encouraging that heavy AI use appears to moderate political views rather than polarize them.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- On the Iran war: "operational excellence and strategic incompetence" — a justified strike concept ruined by failing to enlist allies, brief Congress, or game out the Strait of Hormuz, effectively handing Iran a weapon "more powerful than nuclear" (fertilizer-linked mass-starvation risk), gutting US credibility while a gutted diplomatic corps leaves America "flying blind"; his optimistic scenario is a multinational force reopening the strait plus economic pressure on IRGC-controlled oil exports.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- Enduring skills: storytelling above all (he cites Bezos's 1997 letter, Jensen Huang's stadium keynotes), relationships as technology converges, and — his signature mentoring theme — the ability to endure rejection: "the goal is no," since comfort with rejection is what all self-made entrepreneurs share.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
- Money advice: get rich "slowly" — automatic saving into low-cost index funds, diversification (he caps any position at 3% of net worth and is diversifying out of the US), invest in yourself and in relationships, which compound; he also argues constant bailouts (e.g., a mooted $500 million Spirit Airlines loan) rob the young of the cheap-asset opportunities recessions historically provided.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]

# Takeaways

Galloway's throughline is a double claim: be optimistic about the technology and skeptical of the people and prices attached to it — AI will likely enrich stakeholders and create jobs over the medium term, but its valuations, its messianic CEOs, and the political system's failure to regulate them are set up for a correction whose pain will fall on those least sequestered from it.[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc] His personal counsel to the young is behavioral rather than predictive: learn the tools, seek out rejection until "no" loses its sting, save relentlessly and diversify, stay humble because both success and failure are largely luck, and invest in relationships and purpose — for him, fatherhood, the thing you give to without expecting a return — because "nothing's ever as good or as bad as it seems."[^scott-galloway-the-rich-are-quietly-preparing-fo-NdU6UdUKaYc]
