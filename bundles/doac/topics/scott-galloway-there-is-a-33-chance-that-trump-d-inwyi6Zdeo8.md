# Overview

Recorded on the eve of the 2024 US presidential election, Steven Bartlett's conversation with NYU Stern marketing professor Scott Galloway is an attempt at objective analysis from a man who states up front that he has publicly endorsed Vice President Harris while conceding "perfect's not on the menu."[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] Galloway's framing is that America is coming apart from the inside despite being, on his account, in extraordinary economic shape: political parties, he argues, have stopped being membership organisations competing on policy and become "quasi-religions that attempt to sanctify your beliefs," producing what feels to him like a holy war.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]

The analytical core of the episode is what Galloway calls the dissonance between economic reality and perception — a "vibe-cession." He walks through data showing the US outperforming every peer nation while arguing that the prosperity is concentrated at the very top, that a 30-year-old is no longer doing as well as their parents were at 30, and that this generational break "creates rage and shame" in young people and their families alike.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] From there he builds his central electoral thesis: that the deciding factor is not abortion — those voters, he argues, are already decided — but which party offers young men a more credible vision of being a provider, protector and procreator.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]

The back half broadens into Galloway's recurring themes: identity politics versus class-based affirmative action, social media algorithms as a deliberately exploited vector for foreign polarisation, the failures of the Democratic Party's handling of Biden's age, and the crisis among young men that he says his next book will address.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] Bartlett pushes back at several points, including on his own position that he sees "no great option," and the two find common ground on moving affirmative action from race to class.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]

# Key points

- Galloway's case that America is objectively thriving: the US economy has grown 12.5% since 2019, roughly double any G7 nation; US stocks have gone from a third of global market capitalisation in 2009 to half; Nvidia is worth more than the entire German stock market; average household income in Mississippi, the poorest state, exceeds that of the UK, Germany or Japan; and unemployment is at its lowest since 1968. He says 189 of the world's 190 sovereign nations would trade places with the US.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- Against that, he cites a study finding that if you remove the top 1% of American earners, French household income has grown faster than American; 1% of America owns 90% of the stocks, making the Dow a metric of how the rich are doing; the average 70-year-old is 72% wealthier than 40 years ago while the average person under 40 is 24% less wealthy; one in three men under 25 and one in five at 30 live with their parents; only one in three men under 30 has a girlfriend; and the share of 30-year-olds with at least one child has fallen from 60% forty years ago to 27%.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- He argues the incentive structure has replaced legislators with performers: incumbents are re-elected 92% of the time despite Congress's 8% approval, gerrymandering makes the primary rather than the general the real contest, and small-dollar fundraising rewards incendiary TikTok-friendly statements — so some of the most famous members of Congress have never passed a bill.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- On Trump's political skill, Galloway's marketing read is that he zagged while everyone zigged: after decades of PG-13 politicians afraid of offending anyone, Trump's raw, off-colour speech read as authentic and unafraid, and he treats attention itself as the currency, invoking Umberto Eco's point that the new economy is about being famous regardless of what for.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- He calls 2024 "the podcast election," noting cable TV is down 22% and podcasts up 30% since the last presidential race, that Trump's Joe Rogan appearance reached 11 million live and 40 million on YouTube against roughly half a million for a prime-time cable show, and that Harris's "Call Her Daddy" appearance dominated a week of conversation in a way a Face the Nation interview never would. His one piece of advice to the Harris campaign was to get on a plane to Austin and do Rogan.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- Galloway argues young men are not moving toward the Republican Party so much as being pushed out of the Democratic one: the DNC website's "who we serve" page lists 16 demographic groups totalling roughly 76% of the population, which he says by definition discriminates against the remaining 24%, and the convention was "a parade of demographic groups" with no mention of the cohort that has fallen furthest — young men. Bartlett cites Wall Street Journal figures showing Republican support among young men rising from 35% in 2016 to 48% in 2023.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- His statistics on that cohort: three out of four homeless people and three out of four opioid deaths are men; men are three times as likely to kill themselves and twelve times as likely to be incarcerated; four in five US suicides are men; and a man's suicide risk rises eightfold after divorce. He borrows Chris Williamson's "high heels effect" to argue women are rising economically while men fall, shrinking the pool of partners women will date, and that men without romantic prospects "come off the rails" into vaping, video games and porn.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- The masculinity argument is his headline electoral claim: the far right offers a vision he characterises as "coarse and cruel," the far left's is "be more like a woman," and neither resonates — but the right's version resonates more. He argues the Democrats failed to explain to young men that losing bodily autonomy affects them too, and praises Michelle Obama's speech for making protection a man's default operating system: "real men break up fights at bars, they don't start them."[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- On abortion restrictions across 21 states, Galloway calls it "not a war on women, it's a war on poor women," since wealthy families can travel or access medication while a poor pregnant teenager cannot.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- He advocates replacing race-based affirmative action with class-based, citing the University of California's 1997 ban and its adversity score as the model, noting that 70% of Harvard's non-white students come from upper-income two-parent homes, that Black and Latino household net worth averages $20-25k against $150-160k for white households (so most current beneficiaries would still qualify), and that a child from a top-1% household is 77 times more likely to attend an elite school. He discloses he is himself a beneficiary of Pell Grants, raised by a single immigrant mother on under $40,000.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- On the Madison Square Garden "island of garbage" joke about Puerto Rico, Galloway agrees comedians deserve a wide berth but argues the failure was that it was not funny — "if you're going to be offensive you better be funny" — and that tactically, with 400,000 Puerto Ricans in Pennsylvania, a risk-adjusted campaign should have cut it from the teleprompter. He also criticises his own party for becoming "humorless."[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- Galloway says he is betting $358,000 on Harris on Polymarket at 62-38 odds, reasoning that prediction-market users skew young and male and therefore pro-Trump while every A+ poll shows a toss-up — making a payout of roughly 2.8-to-1 on a near coin flip a free risk-adjusted return, worth about a million dollars if it lands.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] He predicts Harris wins, while acknowledging this is confirmation bias and that a statistician forced to pick would pick Trump.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- The title claim: Galloway says that on age and body mass index alone, if he were an insurance agent quoting Trump $300,000 of life cover he would charge $100,000, because there is a one-in-three chance Trump "leaves feet first from the White House."[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] He is scathing about Biden, arguing he was not persuaded to withdraw but "booted out" by Nancy Pelosi and has not spoken to her since, and that Democrats' fear of appearing ageist cost them a competitive primary — "the primary process is combat," and Harris was never battle-tested.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- On platforms, Galloway argues China cannot beat the US militarily or economically so is weaponising social media to make Americans hate each other, citing 52 pro-Hamas TikTok videos for every pro-Israel one and noting no American media company operates in China. He claims Meta discovered that rage outperforms sex as an engagement driver, and speculates in a scene for his forthcoming Netflix series that platforms escape regulation because they offer intelligence agencies targeting help.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
- Asked about AI, Galloway names loneliness as its biggest threat — not sentience or misinformation — arguing that one in seven men has no friends at all and one in four cannot name a best friend, and that lifelike AI companions will offer "reasonable facsimiles" of relationships that erode the will to endure real rejection. His advice to young men is to modulate porn and get in the arena: "be the guy you'd want to have sex with"; his advice to young people generally is to drink more, be around strangers, and accept that "anything wonderful in your life is really hard."[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]

# Takeaways

Galloway's throughline is that the visible political conflict is downstream of an economic and social one: a country doing historically well in aggregate while a specific cohort — young people generally and young men in particular — falls behind, gets told by an algorithm 210 times a day how well everyone else is doing, and finds only one party willing to say it sees them.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] He is unusually explicit that his own read is coloured by confirmation bias and that deficits under either candidate amount to a deferred tax on the young, a point he says the left has failed to make because it is "wonky and boring" next to whatever outrageous thing dominated the news cycle.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8] His hope is less that a particular candidate wins than that someone wins decisively enough to end the contestation, and his stated project — the book he is writing on men — is to articulate a modern code of masculinity for a group in which "useless" and "worthless" are the two words that appear most often in suicide notes.[^scott-galloway-there-is-a-33-chance-that-trump-d-inwyi6Zdeo8]
