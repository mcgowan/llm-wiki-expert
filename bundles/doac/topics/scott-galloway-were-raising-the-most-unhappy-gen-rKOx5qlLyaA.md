# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews NYU marketing professor and author Scott Galloway about his book *The Algebra of Wealth*, which Galloway describes as "a memo to my 25-year-old self".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] He frames the project around a personal record of having been rich three times and lost it twice — broke at 35 after the dot-com crash and broke again at 42 or 43 in 2008, when he had borrowed against stock in his own e-commerce company Red Envelope to buy more of it and went from a paper net worth of $10–12 million to negative $2 million.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] The lesson he draws is diversification: he now caps any single investment at 3% of his net worth, and says a $5 million write-off the previous week ruined only an hour of his day.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]

His "algebra" has four components — focus, stoicism, time and diversification — and much of the conversation is spent unpacking them into concrete advice: find your talent rather than your passion, choose an industry with a 90%-plus employment rate, get credentialed, move to one of about 20 "super cities" while you are young enough to be geographically flexible, and set up forced-savings mechanisms because "98% of us will spend everything we get our hands on".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] His investment recommendation is deliberately unglamorous: low-cost index funds and ETFs such as SPY, with at most 30% ring-fenced for stock-picking as a life lesson in humility.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]

Running underneath is a social argument. Galloway credits luck — being born a white heterosexual male in 1960s California with access to a UCLA that admitted 76% of applicants — attributes his own risk tolerance to atheism and an acute sense of mortality, and repeatedly returns to young men he says are isolated, screen-bound and struggling, citing Jonathan Haidt's *The Anxious Generation*.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] The episode ends with an unusually blunt tour of how wealthy Americans minimise tax, which Galloway both practises and criticises.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]

# Key points

- Galloway argues money is taboo by design: bosses know every salary while telling employees not to discuss theirs, and "asymmetry of information will always benefit the person that has" it — so he advocates talking openly about salaries, mortgage rates and losses from a young age.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He credits his own start to his mother's boyfriend Terry, who at 13 gave him two $100 bills and told him to buy stock or return the money; he bought 14 shares of Columbia Pictures at Dean Witter and phoned the broker daily from a school payphone for three years.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- His core behavioural claim is that outsized returns go to people willing to endure rejection and public shaming — he lost four consecutive school election campaigns — and says atheism unlocked this for him because "everyone whose opinion you are worried about is going to be dead" in 30 or 40 years.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On failure modes: success is best, fast failure is second best, and slow failure is worst — a business giving just enough green signals to keep absorbing time and money, as Red Envelope did over 11 years; he urges ring-fencing any venture after your 30s to no more than 10–20% of your capital.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He distinguishes rich (owning things to impress friends) from wealth (passive income exceeding your burn rate), and advocates setting a number by working backwards — his own moved from $1m at graduation to $10m in his thirties to $100m, derived from wanting $3m a year at a 4% return.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On creative careers he refuses to crush dreams but insists on economics: acting and music have "a 99% unemployment rate", and 83% of SAG-AFTRA members — among the 180,000 most credentialed creatives in the world — earned under $23,000 and had no health insurance the prior year.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- His counter-example is the "soapstone guy", an Iraqi immigrant high-school dropout who he says made £1.3 million last year: passion follows mastery rather than preceding it, and young people "mistake their hobbies for their passions".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On geography, he says two-thirds of economic growth over the next 30 years will occur in about 20 cities, compares being mediocre in a great economy to surfing perfect Hawaiian waves, and notes Nvidia is worth more than the entire UK stock market with perhaps 10–15,000 of its 30,000 employees now worth $10m or more.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He is sharply critical of meritocracy as an ideology because it makes failure feel like personal fault, insists there is dignity in all work, and says a hard-working employee acting like an owner at a fast-food chain could be managing the store within two years on $60–80,000.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- His advice on mentorship is not to ask for a mentor — too big an ask — but to request specific advice, keep it brief, make a personal connection, state the ask crisply, and follow up; he also urges assembling a "kitchen cabinet" of three or four honest advisers, saying "it is really hard to read the label from inside of the bottle".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He cites his own failure to consult anyone when he turned down $55 million for Prophet Brand Strategy at 33 — roughly 18 times revenue for a services firm — because he believed the internet would make it worth a billion.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On entrepreneurship he says most people should not attempt it: 70% of entrepreneurs are immigrants who lacked access to corporate employment, and he tells students with Google or JP Morgan offers to take them, calling the American corporation the best risk-adjusted wealth platform available.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He names storytelling as the single most durable skill — preferable, he says, to swapping civics for computer science — and sets students the task of reaching the top 1% of any medium; his own chosen niche is being a heterosexual man in his fifties who talks openly about emotions and failure.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On marriage he argues the team beats the individual, says the widely quoted divorce rate is misleading because marriage is becoming a luxury good (marriage rates among poor Americans have fallen below half), advises a prenup where there are premarital assets, and warns that splitting costs roughly 70% of net worth plus a forced house sale at the worst possible moment.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He rejects the trope that wealthy people are ruthless, arguing that self-made wealth depends on accumulating allies through small acts of generosity over decades, and cites a Google finding that 80% of the time an offer goes to a candidate with an internal advocate.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On the anxious generation, he cites his NYU colleague Jonathan Haidt to argue that despite prosperity the US is raising "the most anxious, depressed, obese, and addicted generation in history", reminded 200-plus times a day of others' curated success, with only one in three men under 30 having a girlfriend.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- His investing prescription: a low-cost diversified ETF such as SPY, since 80–95% of day traders lose money and the aggregate returns of hedge funds, private equity and mutual funds trail the S&P by roughly the amount of their fees; he calls belief in expert stock-pickers "one of the greatest grifts in the modern economy".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- He illustrates compounding with a bucket-of-sand demonstration — a glass representing £1,000 a month invested for a year at 25 versus a beach-sized volume by 65 — and answers the "I'll invest once I have a million" objection with "the way you get a million pounds is by investing that 500".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On property he says Case-Shiller data shows real estate has not outperformed other asset classes once maintenance is counted, but he likes it for US tax advantages, 4:1 leverage via a 20% deposit, deductible interest, capital-gains exclusions and its function as forced savings — with the caveats of keeping housing under 40% of income and holding for at least seven years; he notes the average US home rose from $290,000 to $420,000 through the pandemic, pushing typical mortgage payments from $1,100 to $2,300 and affordability from two-thirds of Americans to one-third.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
- On tax, he says the 25 wealthiest Americans pay an effective rate of 6–8% while high-salary "super earners" (his example: a lawyer and chiropractor couple) pay 45–52%, so the goal is to become an owner rather than an earner; he describes buy-borrow-die, state arbitrage (naming Bezos's move to Florida and Musk's to Texas), and his own use of the US qualified small business (Section 1202) exemption, under which the first $10m from his analytics company L2 and $25m from another investment were tax-free — adding that the tax code's growth from 400 to 4,000 pages exists "to turn rich people into super rich people" and that he would vote for an alternative minimum tax.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]

# Takeaways

Galloway's argument is that wealth is a "whole person project" assembled from unglamorous, repeatable behaviours — diversify, automate saving, compound early, invest in relationships, get good tax advice — rather than from a single heroic bet, and that the go-all-in mythology sold to young men systematically omits that the wealthy diversify the moment they have capital.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] He is explicit that economic security is a means rather than an end: what it buys him is the ability to care for his 93-year-old father at roughly $250,000 a year without stress, and to be present with his sons.[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA] He is equally explicit that luck did much of the work in his own case, and that the tax strategies he both uses and describes amount, in his words, to how "we really screw over the little guy".[^scott-galloway-were-raising-the-most-unhappy-gen-rKOx5qlLyaA]
