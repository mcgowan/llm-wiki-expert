# Overview

Evy Poumpouras spent roughly twelve years in the US Secret Service after starting as a New York City police officer, protecting presidents including Bill Clinton, George W. Bush and Barack Obama, working undercover, and serving about eight years as a polygraph examiner and interrogator; she tells Steven Bartlett that this environment — not talent — is what shaped her, and that she is "always becoming," the idea behind her book *Becoming Bulletproof*.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] Her central claim about influence inverts the popular intuition: the person doing most of the talking is not in control, because talking reveals your values while listening reveals theirs.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] Understanding what she calls a person's "motivational mindset" — their values and beliefs — is, she argues, how you get anyone to act, and she illustrates it with an undercover case in which a Russian fraudster was lured from the Dominican Republic to a takedown at the Brooklyn Bridge purely by appealing to his greed.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]

On reading people, Poumpouras is deliberately unglamorous about lie detection: she describes establishing a behavioural baseline and then noticing deviations, treating them as prompts for better follow-up questions rather than verdicts.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] She warns that the tips-and-tricks framing of confidence does not work — Bartlett's own account of failing with women until he genuinely believed he was "good enough" prompts her agreement that change is compounding layers of small actions with no shortcut.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]

The conversation's second half is about identity. A 9/11 survivor who was in the World Trade Center, Poumpouras argues that trauma is something that happened to her, not who she is, and she describes colleagues whose lives collapsed around the event they refused to leave behind.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] She extends the same logic to prejudice and discrimination: she does not deny they are real, but declines to spend finite energy fighting every person who misreads her.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]

# Key points

- Poumpouras says the biggest mistake in any negotiation or interaction is talking too much: "there's a myth that people think if I do most of the talking I have control — it's garbage," because silence lets the other person reveal their values, purpose and motivators.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- She rejects the goal of being liked, calling it "a track for disaster," and substitutes competence plus warmth — following through, arriving early ("if you're on time you're late"), being open, approachable and non-judgemental while keeping authority and boundaries; she cites research by Susan Fiske and Chris Malone that being overly nice gets you "rolled."[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- On disrespect, her first move is to turn the question back on herself — what standard did I set that made people think this was acceptable? — and she relays advice from former CIA chief of station John Ferrari that it is far easier to start authoritative and soften than to be everyone's friend and then impose boundaries.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- Lie detection, as she describes it, is baseline-and-deviation work: she watches a person's normal posture, gestures and eye contact, notes the shift when a question lands badly, and treats it as a cue to ask better follow-up questions — most people, she says, register the shift and then let it go.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- She flags "divine intervention" — suspects swearing "God is my witness," or arriving with Bibles and rosary beads — as a pattern she repeatedly saw in guilty people, while stressing it is a red flag rather than proof.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- The best liar she met was an ATM skimming suspect who turned up to the interview wearing the same New York Knicks hat as in the surveillance photo, looked at the photo and said "it looks like me but no it's not me," and never broke.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- On selection, she says the Secret Service reviews only about 1% of applications — she calls it easier to get into Harvard — with essays, invasive background checks (an agent was sent to Rome to interview her college professor), a polygraph and the "TEA" written exam; the traits they screen hardest for are trustworthiness and integrity, and past drug use is a fast disqualifier.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- Watching presidents up close taught her emotional detachment in decision-making: she says she never once heard a US president yell, and that they weighed pros, cons and facts while pundits insulted them on live television minutes before they walked on stage.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- Her most frightening moment was not a threat to a protectee but a foot pursuit into a Manhattan parking garage where she drew her weapon on a suspect hiding under a car whose hands she could not see; she chose not to fire, and he turned out to be a 16-year-old who had stolen a gold cross — "I almost killed an innocent person for nothing."[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- She describes an undercover operation against an Albanian organised-crime ring selling authentic passports and birth certificates to terrorists, in which she posed as a sex-trafficking victim with an Eastern European accent, carried no wire and no gun to survive a possible search, paid $5,000 for a genuine Polish passport, and then introduced more undercover "girls" to build enough transactions for prosecution.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- On being heard at work she points to paralinguistics — tone, pitch, projection and how you end a sentence — over content alone, and pushes back on the advice that women must always speak up: "do you have something beneficial to say?" Bartlett adds his own framing of a "contribution score" that colleagues keep on you.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- She identifies "identity" language — repeated I, I, I, high emotionality, chronic complaint — as a marker of people stuck in victimhood, arguing that reliving drama delivers cortisol and adrenaline spikes people become addicted to, and that trauma has become "a badge of honour" and a competition.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- As a parent to an 18-month-old daughter (born when she was 46), she says she has never put an iPhone in front of her and keeps no working television in the house, arguing that if you would not drop your child in the middle of Times Square to talk to strangers, you should take the phone out of their hands.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
- She rejects the term imposter syndrome outright, and recounts discovering mid-training that the Secret Service had separate male and female physical standards — after an agent told her she had received "special treatment," she trained morning and night to meet the male standard.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]

# Takeaways

Poumpouras's through-line is sovereignty: the point at which you stop chasing approval, stop taking inventory of everyone's opinion, and become, in her words, a magnet.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] She frames the practical skills — listening over talking, baselines over gut feeling, warmth paired with boundaries, paralinguistics over word choice — as layers that compound rather than tricks that work overnight, and she is blunt that mind and body are not separate systems, crediting running at night with discharging accumulated stress.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0] Her most contested position is on trauma and discrimination: both are real, she says, but making either your identity forecloses who you might still become.[^secret-agent-how-to-detect-a-lie-instantly-evy-p-iz_SJ5TpLJ0]
