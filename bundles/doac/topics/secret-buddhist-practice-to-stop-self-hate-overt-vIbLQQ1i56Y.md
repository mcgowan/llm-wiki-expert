# Overview

Gelong Thubten, a Tibetan Buddhist monk of some 30 years who has taught meditation to Hollywood stars, CEOs, and corporations, tells Steven Bartlett that modern life leaves people "at the mercy of our own minds" — bombarded by messages that something is always missing and that happiness depends on external events, so that we live "at the receiving end of life."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y] His own path was forged through suffering: sexual abuse at 14 by an older bandmate, his parents' sudden split at 17, expulsion from Oxford amid depression, and a party-fueled attempt at an acting career that ended at 21 with a burnout so severe he was diagnosed with a heart condition. His childhood friend Tara all but carried him to the Samye Ling monastery in Scotland for what was meant to be one year as a monk — thirty years later, he is still there.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]

The episode's centerpiece is Thubten's account of a four-year retreat (2005-2009) on a remote Scottish island, where two years of horrific depression, anxiety, and panic attacks culminated in him literally jumping over the retreat wall to escape. Readmitted after a week's reflection in a caravan, he transformed his practice: instead of analyzing the story behind his pain or pushing it away, he made the pain itself — "a knife twisting in my heart" — the object of meditation, flooding the feeling with compassion "as if I had found a frightened rabbit or a bird with a broken wing."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]

The guest argues meditation is not about clearing the mind but about changing one's relationship to thoughts, and closes by walking Bartlett through a simple 10-minute daily practice — no "spiritual tat" required.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]

# Key points

- Thubten argues the West is "materially more comfortable than ever and emotionally more uncomfortable," citing the US falling from 11th (2011) to 24th (2025) in global happiness rankings, the UK dropping to 23rd, and UK suicide at its highest level in decades — which he attributes to a consumer culture that must constantly tell people they lack something.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- On desire, he says dopamine falls away just before you get what you want, so the chase always outshines the getting; what people actually seek in every acquisition is momentary "freedom from wanting" — and meditation reveals that freedom was available inside all along.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- He describes Buddhism as "a science of awareness" rather than a religion: no creator, nothing to worship, hell and heaven treated as states of mind, and "the deepest addiction we all have is the addiction to our own thoughts."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- He initially hated meditation because he thought it meant clearing the mind — which only amplified his inner "devil voice" of self-disgust; real meditation, he says, is three things: being with the breath, noticing you got lost, and returning — "waking up inside your thoughts... that is the definition of meditation," and each return is a rep that strengthens the mind like a gym workout.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- The observer insight: the part of the mind that watches anger cannot itself be angry — Buddhism's sky-and-clouds metaphor, where awareness is the sky, always bigger than the storm of thoughts and feelings.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- His core healing practice is "dropping the story and looking at the feeling": locate suffering as a physical sensation, bypass the judgments and the why-questions, move toward it, and send love into it — he argues pushing pain away generates cortisol while accepting it releases endorphins, and that this rests on the Buddhist view that the mind is naturally compassionate.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- When his teacher and closest friend, Akong Rinpoche, was murdered in China eleven years earlier — stabbed by a former monk of their own monastery who shared Thubten's name — the same practice of "sending love into the flames" carried him through grief and enabled forgiveness, which he calls his teacher's "last gift."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- On grudges, he inverts the logic of holding on: a grudge is "holding a hot coal in your hand" — the wrongdoer wins every moment you keep suffering — and forgiveness is a strength that frees you, not a concession to the other person; he adds that he has forgiven the feelings his abuse and family trauma gave rise to, which matters more to him than forgiving the people, and quotes his teacher: "suffering is like compost."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- Addressing the CEO worried meditation would make his team "too relaxed," Thubten insists meditation is about precision and presence, not tranquilized calm — it lets you work harder and tire less; Bartlett adds testimonials from Ray Dalio, Marc Benioff, Jack Dorsey, Oprah Winfrey, and Steve Jobs crediting meditation for their effectiveness.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- Emerging from his retreat in 2009 into a world suddenly full of smartphones, he was struck by how systematically fear is used — in clickbait news, politics, and "hurry while stocks last" marketing — and prescribes "microscopic moments of meditation" in queues and traffic jams to rewire reactivity, finding the gap between impulse and action where response replaces reaction.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- His starter plan: bin the spiritual paraphernalia, meditate 10 minutes every morning immediately on waking (when cortisol spikes), begin and end with an intention of compassion for yourself and all beings, rest attention on hands, shoulders, then the natural breath — and he claims brain scans show visible changes after just four days of 10 minutes daily.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
- Two warnings: don't judge sessions or chase blissful sensations — his teacher once told him "you're a junkie, you're using your meditation like a drug" — and don't mistake insight for completion: after 30 years he says "I'm still a mess, but I'm okay with being a mess," which he calls the real happiness.[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]

# Takeaways

The guest's message is that everything people chase — coffee, drugs, sex, success — is a proxy for a freedom that only exists in the mind, and that the missing link is not knowledge but daily practice: ten unglamorous minutes each morning, plus micro-moments of awareness through the day. You cannot outrun what torments you — "you can run to the end of the earth and that thing that has been tormenting you is part of you" — but by moving toward pain with compassion instead of suppressing it, Thubten argues, suffering becomes compost for transformation, self-hatred can dissolve, and you become "the CEO of your own mind."[^secret-buddhist-practice-to-stop-self-hate-overt-vIbLQQ1i56Y]
