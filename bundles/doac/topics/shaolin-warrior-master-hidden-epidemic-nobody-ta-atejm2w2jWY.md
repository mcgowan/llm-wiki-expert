# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett speaks with Shaolin warrior master Shi Heng Yi, who has spent decades training in Shaolin kung fu and now teaches mind-body practices for self-mastery. The guest argues that the defining struggles of modern life are choice overload — a world so full of options that people cannot decide which direction to take — and a fundamental misuse of the body: people sit all day and invest nearly all their energy in mental work, creating an imbalance he says translates into anxiety, dissatisfaction, and a perpetual, never-satisfied searching.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]

The guest frames much of modern suffering as a battle between "doing and being." He argues that ambitious goal-setting usually comes from a feeling of lack and from the urge to nourish an identity, and that since everything a person attains lasts at most a lifetime, the deeper skill is learning to let go — of possessions, patterns, and the illusion of the self. He illustrates this with his own story: raised by Vietnamese-Laotian refugee parents in the poorest area of Kaiserslautern, Germany, trained in Shaolin kung fu from age four, and driven by a constant feeling of never being enough, he pursued academic and martial achievement for his father's recognition — recognition that never came before his father died of cancer around 2012.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]

The conversation covers the five hindrances from his TED talk — the mental states that pull people off their goals — the four-step RAIN method for working with them, the fourteen Shaolin virtues, daily routines for presence and energy, and how to break lifelong behavioral patterns. It culminates in a demonstration in which the guest breaks two bricks with his hand, which he attributes not to raw strength but to years of conditioning plus the ability to read the structure of an object and focus energy into a single moment.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]

# Key points

- The guest says people's number-one struggle today is diversity and overload of choice — an inability to make clear decisions — and that the answer starts with learning who you actually are, because a wrong self-perspective misdirects every decision built on it.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- He identifies a "hidden epidemic" in not using the body as intended: excessive sitting and all-mental work create unbalanced energies that he says show up as anxiety, dissatisfaction, and endless searching, and blind people to how precious the present moment is.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- The five hindrances — sensual desire, ill will (aversion), dullness, restlessness, and skeptical doubt — are five mental states that make it hard to keep a goal in mind; his 2020 TEDx talk on them has drawn tens of millions of views.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- His guiding image for the hindrances is "don't touch the water": don't pull pleasurable things toward you and don't push disliked things away, because both ripple your surroundings and drain energy from the goal.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- To overcome the hindrances he teaches the four-step RAIN method: Recognize the mental state you are in, Accept/acknowledge it, Investigate what led to it like a detective, and practice Non-identification — not equating your being with body, mind, or the states you observed.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- He argues goal-setting typically comes from a space of lack and identity-building rather than connection to "the source," and that attachment — not possession itself — is the problem: "It's not about what you possess, it's about which of these possessions are possessing you."[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- On breaking patterns, he says you must first see the pattern, then replace it by training new behaviors with the same daily consistency used to build physical skill — treating virtues and habits as things to be trained, not merely chosen.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- Awareness must precede discipline: he argues discipline is useless if you cannot feel the moment a trigger "lights the small flame," and that breathwork, yoga, and body-sensitivity practices train people to catch emotional fires before the whole house is burning.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- The Shaolin tradition describes 14 virtues — four "to be expected" before entering training (such as self-control, discipline, benevolence), five virtues of action (how to behave: loyalty, respect, courage), and five virtues of the mind (patience, persistence) that make the virtues of action possible; he notes character, not power, determines whether skill serves or harms humanity.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- His daily practice: waking "empty" without yesterday's thoughts, sitting alone breathing and body-scanning for the first hour, plus push-ups, sit-ups, and 15-minute standing postures (such as the "squatting monkey") that build energy by demanding willpower — because willpower and energy development go hand in hand.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- On the brick break, he explains he reads the stone's density the way he reads a body's tension, combines that with knowledge of how his energy will come down, and strikes — a one-second act built on years of conditioning through wood, then stone, then metal; he estimates Bartlett could learn it in about three months of dedicated training.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- Reflecting on worry, he says roughly 80-90% of his worries of recent months never happened, and for the 10% that did, worrying could do nothing — a realization that helped him let go of the idea that life should be one-directionally positive.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
- Discussing struggling young men and his three-month-old son, he says he will never make his son feel "not enough": his job is only to provide a safe framework for trial and error, since failing and getting up is how a secure, self-sufficient individual learns his own value.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]

# Takeaways

The guest's closing counsel is that self-mastery means refusing to outsource your well-being to anything you cannot influence — governments, colleagues, critics — and grounding it instead in the only things you control: your body and your mind. His practical starting point is to identify the single most disturbing emotion or blockage from your last week or month, investigate its trigger, and pour your energy into resolving that one thing, because an unaddressed pattern simply compounds year after year. He says he seeks peace rather than happiness — since whatever can rise must fall — and that the master people search for in gurus and mentors "is already sitting inside each individual"; it only needs nourishing.[^shaolin-warrior-master-hidden-epidemic-nobody-ta-atejm2w2jWY]
