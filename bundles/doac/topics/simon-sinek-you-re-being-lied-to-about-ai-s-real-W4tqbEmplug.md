# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, author and speaker Simon Sinek returns to discuss AI with host Steven Bartlett — though, as Sinek puts it, he is "not in the AI business, but in the humanity business."[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug] His central argument is that public debate about AI fixates on the destination — its ability to write the book, paint the painting, solve the problem — while ignoring the value of the journey. Sinek contends that struggle itself is what builds intelligence, resourcefulness, and confidence: he says he is smarter not because a book exists with his ideas in it, but because he endured the excruciating process of writing it.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]

Sinek warns that outsourcing effort to AI risks the quiet disappearance of core human skills — listening, holding space, resolving conflict, giving and receiving feedback, taking accountability, and expressing empathy — losses he says the internet and social media have already begun and AI will exaggerate. He compares this to giving up phone-number memory to smartphones, but argues these skills are different because losing them damages relationships and the ability to cope with stress: "AI will provide boats for everyone, except for the time there's a storm and you don't know how to swim."[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]

The conversation ranges across the irony of knowledge workers now demanding the protections once denied factory workers, the honest answer that nobody — not even AI leaders — knows how it plays out, the coming premium on imperfect, human-made things, and Sinek's forthcoming book on friendship, which he calls "the ultimate biohack."[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]

# Key points

- Sinek argues society is results-obsessed and views AI only through output, forgetting that personal growth comes from doing the work yourself — writing your own book, building your own business — "not for the output, for your personal growth."[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- He notes the irony that when robots displaced factory workers in the 70s and 80s, elites told them to "reskill," but now that AI threatens knowledge workers — coders and finance people, while plumbers and baggage handlers are unbothered — the same classes call for universal basic income, citing Sam Altman's Worldcoin as an example.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- On whether AI fears are overblown, Sinek's honest answer is "I don't know — and neither does anybody else"; Bartlett agrees, noting that Mustafa Suleyman, Google leadership, and Reid Hoffman all gave him different predictions. Sinek adds a warning to watch the messenger: no one who owns an AI company will voice doomsday scenarios, comparing them to cigarette executives who didn't smoke.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- Bartlett recounts a chilling secondhand story: a billionaire friend told him a major AI-company CEO privately believes something "horrific" is coming and is "totally cool with it," while publicly presenting as a nuanced optimist.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- Sinek argues the AI race is so reckless because internet-era tech breaks his "infinite game" theory — unlike cars or fast food, tech categories produce single winners (Amazon, Google), so companies race for dominance without controls; he supports regulation, likening it to seat belts and speed limits, and notes America suffers more than Europe or China for lacking internet controls.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- An AI friend trained to affirm you like the best psychologist means "you'll feel like you have a friend, but you're not learning to be a friend" — and he illustrates the danger with a ChatGPT-scripted apology to a girlfriend: even done "right," it removes the humanity, whereas bumbling through a fight is what brings couples closer.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- Drawing on the Japanese concept of wabi-sabi and a firefighter story he attributes to James Surowiecki's The Wisdom of Crowds, Sinek argues imperfection and experience-born wisdom are what make humans valuable — artists are now signing affidavits that AI didn't make their work, and human-vs-human chess draws bigger audiences than perfect computer play precisely because of human error.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- He predicts a rebellion in which handmade becomes more valuable and expensive — citing Ferrari's 39-month handmade waiting list, the Mona Lisa's theft-driven fame, and Apple's art-gallery store staging — because people buy the story and the human touch, not just the product.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- On jobs, Sinek disputes mass idleness: he cites the IRS digitization, which saved zero money because fired accountants were replaced by hired IT staff, and argues AI's enormous energy needs mean careers like nuclear engineering will boom — "the workforce looks different, but it didn't get smaller."[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- His advice to a hypothetical 10-year-old: learn human skills (parents should remove phones at playdates, make kids apologize in person, teach conflict resolution and accountability) and learn a "real skill" by doing something difficult — prompting doesn't count.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- On money and meaning, Sinek cites the roughly $70,000-a-year threshold beyond which income buys no discernible happiness increase, argues small companies out-innovate big ones because their ambitions exceed their resources, and describes a gratitude practice forged during the LA fires when he packed a go-bag and said goodbye to a beloved painting he couldn't fit in his car.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
- Sinek defines community as "a group of people who agree to grow together," calls loneliness a self-preservation trap that makes people more selfish and harder to befriend, and prescribes service — helping someone else who is lonely — as the way out; his forthcoming book tackles friendship because industries exist for leadership and romance but almost nothing teaches how to be a friend.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]

# Takeaways

The guest's throughline is that the journey, not the destination, is where humans are made — and AI, by delivering destinations instantly, tempts people to skip the very struggle that builds skill, confidence, and connection. Sinek urges listeners to keep writing, building, and fighting through conflict themselves, to invest deliberately in friendships (he schedules friend time like business meetings and refuses to cancel it), and to expect a growing premium on authentically human, imperfect work. His closing mantra, taken from the Air Force Weapons School coin he helped create: build, teach, lead.[^simon-sinek-you-re-being-lied-to-about-ai-s-real-W4tqbEmplug]
