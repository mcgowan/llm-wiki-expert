# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Michael Breus, a clinical psychologist and sleep specialist with 26 years of practice who is one of a small number of people to have passed the sleep medical boards without attending medical school. Breus explains sleep through two systems — sleep drive (adenosine accumulation) and sleep rhythm (the circadian clock) — and argues that most sleep problems are behavioral and psychological rather than pharmaceutical: in his estimate, roughly 75% of insomnia is driven by anxiety, fear, or depression.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]

A centerpiece of the conversation is Breus's chronotype framework — lions (early birds), bears (the middle majority), wolves (night owls), and the dolphin, the anxious light-sleeper type he says he identified — which he claims is genetically set via the PER3 gene and determines the ideal timing for work, exercise, coffee, alcohol, and even sex (which he argues is hormonally best in the morning, not at 10:30pm). He walks through his clinical protocol for the common 3AM wake-up: don't get up to pee unless you must, don't look at your phone or the clock, use 4-7-8 breathing or other distraction techniques to keep heart rate below 60, and stay positive rather than catastrophizing.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]

The episode also covers sleep apnea (which Breus himself has) and its links to Alzheimer's disease, a skeptical deep-dive on melatonin supplementation and unregulated dosing, supplements like magnesium and valerian, jet lag as a solvable "math problem" of light timing, dream therapy and nightmare re-scripting, and practical gear advice on pillows, sleep position, and temperature.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]

# Key points

- Breus explains sleep via two systems: sleep drive (adenosine builds up as cells burn glucose, making you sleepy) and sleep rhythm (circadian timing); his "nap-a-latte" trick — drink cooled black coffee fast, then nap 25 minutes — burns off adenosine while caffeine, one molecule different, blocks the receptors for about four hours of alertness.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- He says chronotype is genetic (a PER3 single-nucleotide polymorphism): lions are 10-15% of the population, bears 50-55%, wolves are the creative night owls, and dolphins are intelligent, detail-obsessed light sleepers with anxiety and low sleep drive; chronotype also shifts across life — children are lions/bears, adolescents become wolves, and melatonin production declines from around age 50.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- On school timing, he cites a University of Minnesota study in which starting first period one hour later improved student grades by a full letter, and a 2020 "synchrony effect" study of nearly 800 students showing performance improves when class schedules match chronotype.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- His number-one sleep tip is waking at the same time seven days a week: morning light hits melanopsin cells that switch off melatonin and set a roughly 14-hour timer (the melatonin phase response curve), so a late Saturday lie-in pushes the whole night's sleepiness later.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- For the 1-3AM wake-up, he explains everyone's core body temperature cycle produces a warming phase — and an awakening — in that window; his protocol: check (lying on your back ~25 seconds) whether you really need to pee, avoid phone and clock, use Dr. Andrew Weil's 4-7-8 breathing for about 20 cycles or count backwards from 300 by threes, and replace the brain's automatic negative "second thought" with permission to simply rest, since a heart rate of about 60 or below is required to fall asleep.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- He advises stopping food, fluids, and alcohol three hours before bed and caffeine by about 2pm (half-life 6-8 hours); delay morning caffeine about 90 minutes after waking and hydrate first, since sleep costs nearly a liter of water and cortisol and adrenaline already have you stimulated; his alcohol rule is one glass of water per drink and a three-hour gap before bed, because alcohol destroys stage 3/4 deep sleep where the glymphatic system clears Alzheimer's-linked proteins.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- On sleep apnea, the episode cites roughly 936 million to 1 billion adults worldwide affected (about one in seven, comparable to diabetes), 80-90% undiagnosed; Breus himself stops breathing about 26 times an hour and uses a CPAP, and alternatives include oral appliances, tongue-stimulation devices, surgery, and pills now in development; the Queensland Brain Institute found untreated apnea carries a 45% higher Alzheimer's risk.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- He warns against over-the-counter "PM" sleep aids containing diphenhydramine, saying data links daily use to Alzheimer's, and calls alcohol the most common self-treatment for insomnia; the proper treatment for insomnia in his view is CBT-I, which reschedules sleep and dismantles catastrophic thinking.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- On melatonin, he stresses it is a hormone and a sleep regulator, not a sleep initiator: prescription-only in most countries outside the US, appropriate at only 0.5-1.5mg (while shops sell 10-20mg gummies), interacting with SSRIs, birth control, blood pressure and diabetes medication; a 2024 analysis found children's melatonin gummies contained 0% to 667% of the labeled dose, child overdoses have risen nearly 600%, and he opposes giving it to anyone under 18 except children on the autism spectrum — reserving it mainly for jet lag, shift work, and age-related deficiency.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- On supplements generally, he recommends fixing blood-work deficiencies first (vitamin D, magnesium, iron — which he says resolves sleep problems for about 15% of his patients), prefers single-ingredient supplements at studied doses, cites valerian-plus-hops and studied magnesium forms like magnesium threonate, and demonstrates his "banana tea" ritual — boiling a halved banana with the peel to extract magnesium about 30-40 minutes before bed.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- He treats jet lag as a math problem: correctly timed bright light (~10,000 lux) can shift the circadian clock by up to eight hours, the basis of the Timeshifter app, which grew out of NASA work on the International Space Station (where astronauts see a sunrise every ~2 hours) and was used by the Mercedes Formula 1 team.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
- On dreams, he calls them "emotional metabolism" — nightly processing of the day's emotions — and describes dream therapy and imagery re-scripting: writing down a recurring nightmare, changing the ending, and re-reading it before bed for 7-10 days to stop the nightmare loop; practical bedroom advice includes sleeping on the left side (right-side sleeping worsens reflux), a pillow that keeps the nose aligned with the sternum, a cool room, air purifiers, and flexibility about TV or podcasts if they work as distraction for that individual.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]

# Takeaways

Breus's core argument is that sleep is trainable behavior governed by heart rate, temperature, light, and individual chronobiology — not a problem to be solved with pills, which he says most people don't need. His prescription: learn your chronotype and schedule life around it, wake at the same time every day, protect a wind-down "runway" before bed, keep the middle-of-the-night response calm and screen-free, and get tested if apnea symptoms are present, since untreated sleep disorders quietly erode cardiovascular and brain health. The episode repeatedly frames sleep as upstream of mood, relationships, work, and longevity — in Breus's words, when you change someone's sleep, you change their life.[^sleep-doctor-if-you-wake-up-at-3am-do-not-do-thi-pXlMKzcZlwM]
