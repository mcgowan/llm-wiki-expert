# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Dr. Roman Yampolskiy — associate professor of computer science, author, and the researcher who says he coined the term "AI safety" some 15 years ago — argues that after starting out convinced safe AI was achievable, he concluded the opposite: every component of the control problem is not just difficult but impossible, and indefinite control of superintelligence belongs to a class of unsolvable problems.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI] While AI capability progress is exponential, he says, safety progress is linear or constant — labs merely "patch" behavior that smarter systems route around — so the gap between what systems can do and what we can predict, explain or control keeps widening.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]

His timeline, drawing on prediction markets and lab CEOs: AGI around 2027, delivering "drop-in" free cognitive labor that makes hiring humans irrational for most computer-based work; capable humanoid robots by roughly 2030, extending automation to physical labor including plumbing; and by 2045 — Kurzweil's singularity date — a pace of automated invention no human can follow.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI] The result he foresees is not 10% but 99% unemployment, with no "plan B" occupation to retrain into (he notes "learn to code" and "prompt engineering" have already been consumed), leaving only jobs where people specifically prefer a human. He argues the deeper crises are meaning and governance rather than economics, since free labor creates abundance but governments have no programs for mass joblessness.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]

The conversation ranges across his criticisms of Sam Altman and the racing labs — whom he accuses of "gambling 8 billion lives" in unconsented, unethical experimentation — his advocacy of narrow AI over general superintelligence, his near-certainty that we live in a simulation, and his views on longevity and Bitcoin. Host Steven Bartlett presses him on rebuttals, hope, and what listeners should actually do.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]

# Key points

- Yampolskiy claims we don't know how to make advanced AI safe and never will: there is no seminal solved result in AI safety, only patches that get jailbroken, and he argues perpetual control of a self-improving superintelligence is an impossible problem, not merely a hard one — his aim in proving impossibility being to stop people selling billion-dollar "solutions."[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- He predicts AGI by about 2027 (citing prediction markets and top-lab CEOs), humanoid robots able to compete with humans in all physical domains by about 2030, and a 2045 singularity beyond which technological iteration is unpredictable "by definition" — likening the human-superintelligence cognitive gap to a French bulldog trying to understand a podcast.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- On jobs, he forecasts levels of unemployment "we've never seen before — not 10% but 99%": with free cognitive and physical labor, retraining is no longer a plan because all jobs can be automated, leaving only work where a human is preferred for its own sake, which he compares to a niche "almost fetish" market like hand-made goods.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- He dismisses the "just unplug it" argument: superintelligence would be a distributed system like Bitcoin or a virus, smarter than us, with backups, that would "turn you off before you can turn them off" — and unlike nuclear weapons it is an agent making its own decisions, not a tool awaiting a human order.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- Against the Industrial Revolution analogy, he argues intelligence is a "meta invention": previous inventions were tools, but inventing an inventor means there is no new job the displaced can flee to — "it's the last invention we ever have to make."[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- The extinction pathway he says he can predict is AI-enabled synthetic biology — a psychopath, terrorist or doomsday cult using advanced tools to build a novel virus — but he stresses the real danger is that superintelligence can invent attack vectors as far beyond our imagination as human methods are beyond a dog's.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- He emphasizes that modern AI is "grown" not engineered — a black box whose own creators must run experiments for months to discover its capabilities, still finding new ones in old models — making prediction and consent impossible; since subjects can't comprehend what they're consenting to, he calls the current race unethical human experimentation on 8 billion people without permission.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- On Sam Altman, he says a decade-old published set of guardrails was violated "every single one," describes Altman as a perfect public interface whom insiders consider the wrong person to control such a project, putting safety second to winning the race, and speculates that Worldcoin (biometrics plus a UBI-style currency) fits an ambition to "control the light cone of the universe."[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- His proposed strategy is appeal to self-interest rather than law (which he deems unenforceable across jurisdictions): convince builders that racing to superintelligence is a "suicide mission" even for them, a mutually-assured-destruction logic that applies to the US and China alike, and instead build narrow superintelligent tools — "my company cures breast cancer, we make billions, everyone's happy" — noting he claims ~60% of jobs could already be replaced with existing models, so decades of economic growth need no new general superintelligence.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- He cites Hinton, Bengio and thousands of signatories of the statement on AI dangers as agreeing with the risk, notes safety teams historically fail and dissolve (OpenAI's superalignment team was canceled within about half a year), and supports Pause AI / Stop AI protests while conceding an average person can do little beyond joining such movements and living each day well.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- Yampolskiy says he is "very close to certainty" that we live in a simulation: once human-level AI and indistinguishable virtual worlds are affordable, billions of simulations of any moment will be run, making the odds of being in the base reality one in a billion; he reads world religions as folk descriptions of the same theory, and says the belief changes little — "pain still hurts, love is still love" — beyond his curiosity about what lies outside.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
- On longevity and money, he calls aging "just a disease" one breakthrough away from a fix — endorsing longevity escape velocity — predicts a 2100 world either free of humans or incomprehensible to us, and holds Bitcoin as the only truly scarce resource; asked about the button that would shut down all AI, he would keep narrow AI (turning it all off would kill millions who depend on it) but stop AGI and superintelligence development.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]

# Takeaways

Yampolskiy's closing message is that humanity should stay in charge by building only beneficial, narrow, controllable AI tools, demanding that anyone racing to superintelligence publish peer-reviewed explanations of how they will control it, and insisting that those making species-level decisions have moral standards and ask permission from the people affected.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI] He sleeps well, he says, through the same psychological machinery that lets mortals enjoy life — and his personal advice is correspondingly simple: live each day as if it's your last, do interesting and impactful work, and don't spend too long doing things you hate.[^the-ai-safety-expert-these-are-the-only-5-jobs-t-UclrVWafRAI]
