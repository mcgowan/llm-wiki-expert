# Overview

Dr. Bessel van der Kolk — author of *The Body Keeps the Score*, described by the Financial Times as perhaps the most influential psychiatrist of the 21st century — tells Steven Bartlett that the word trauma has swung from being an alien subject when he began at Harvard to a total explanatory mode today, and that his own interest has shifted from trauma itself to what he now sees underneath it: a breakdown of connection and synchrony between human beings.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] His core claim is that trauma is not a story or even a memory but a perception lodged in the nervous system: the brain and perceptual system get rewired so the past is experienced as the present.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] He describes the first neuroimaging study of people reliving trauma, in which the cognitive left side of the brain goes quiet, the emotional right temporoparietal region fires, and the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex — what he calls the brain's timekeeper — goes offline, so the person loses any sense that this belongs to yesterday.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]

From this he draws a methodological conclusion that has repeatedly put him at odds with mainstream psychiatry: you cannot reason someone out of an unreasonable state.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] He is dismissive of cognitive restructuring and prolonged-exposure desensitisation as answers, and instead advocates bottom-up approaches — EMDR, yoga, neurofeedback, psychodrama, martial arts, bodywork, and more recently MDMA- and psilocybin-assisted therapy — that give the body experiences contradicting helplessness.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] He is candid that most of these were mocked when he introduced them, and blames a profit-driven system: the field, he says, funds what is cheap and productive rather than what helps, and one insurance executive told him plainly that his interest was in subscriber numbers, not cures.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]

The conversation is unusually personal. Van der Kolk, born in 1943 in Nazi-occupied Netherlands to a "frozen" mother and a father detained by the Germans, links his lifelong quest to understand trauma to his own history — a connection he says he only really grasped after age 70, and partly through his own psychedelic experiences.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] He demonstrates EMDR on Bartlett live, and Bartlett reports that within a minute he feels calm and finds it hard to recall why the memory bothered him.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]

# Key points

- Van der Kolk defines trauma as an overwhelming experience of complete helplessness with nothing in you that knows how to respond, and insists what matters is perception rather than the event — the same incident can devastate one person and barely register for another.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He dislikes the small-t/big-T framing but affirms that relational trauma — not being seen, heard, or acknowledged; being told you are the reason a parent drinks — is what actually brings most people into his practice, and he estimates about 90% of his patients' adult dysfunction traces to early childhood experience while acknowledging his practice is a filtered sample.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- Citing the CDC's 25,000-person adverse childhood experiences research by Vincent Felitti, he says eradicating child abuse in America would cut depression by more than half, alcoholism by two thirds, and suicide, drug use and domestic violence by three quarters.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- On the brain, he points to the periaqueductal grey — what he nicknames the cockroach centre — firing constantly in traumatised people, producing subliminal dread below the level of anxiety; a hypersensitive amygdala acting as an over-tuned smoke detector; and a shut-down insula that severs the link between bodily sensation and self-knowledge, leaving people numb and agitated at once.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- His NIH-funded EMDR research took 15 years to fund; it found that moving the eyes back and forth while recalling a traumatic experience activates pathways between the temporoparietal junction and the insula that let the brain register the event as past — and that 78% of people with adult-onset trauma such as assault or rape were completely cured, though early childhood trauma proves far more stubborn.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He deliberately does not ask what the memory is during EMDR, saying he is suspicious of language because people filter what is embarrassing, and the aim is to circumvent verbal meaning-making altogether.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- Van der Kolk says he ran the Boston arm of the large MDMA-assisted therapy trial and calls the results stunning — Bartlett cites the phase 3 finding that 67% of MDMA-assisted participants no longer met PTSD criteria versus roughly 30% on placebo — but van der Kolk argues the more important, less-quoted result was the secondary data showing people developed self-compassion instead of self-hatred, and sometimes compassion for the perpetrator.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- His own MDMA session was not blissful: he lay in agony for eight hours as the accumulated pain of decades of patients' stories surfaced, and says it left him "a much sadder but somewhat wiser man," more humble and more compassionate.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He was the first to study yoga for PTSD and was told he had gone off the deep end; the study found it effective, and he stresses it does not treat trauma directly but repairs the relationship to the body — noting that happy baby pose reliably triggers sexual abuse survivors.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- Neurofeedback, in his account, treats brain organisation rather than trauma: traumatised brains often produce fast, agitated waves at the back of the head when the eyes close, where slow, peaceful waves should appear, and training can reorganise that.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He calls psychodrama his favourite clinical activity — placing real people in three-dimensional space as your father, then as the father you wished you had — because a therapist's kindness cannot substitute for the imprint of what being cared for at age three would have felt like, but a physical enactment can approximate it.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He is scathing about diagnostic categories, calling ADHD and PTSD dimensional rather than binary and the DSM — which he helped create in a minor role — an artifact of people making up lists in a room 40 years ago with no scientific validity; virtually every traumatised child he has seen, he says, meets ADHD criteria.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He notes that children from low-income families are around four times more likely than the privately insured to receive antipsychotics, and warns that altering brain chemistry during development may prevent the brain from learning the adaptations his own neuro-atypical son was able to make.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- Asked how not to raise a traumatised child, he answers: do not raise them alone — a child needs multiple adults so they can see their parent as safe but flawed, and the more nuclear the family, the harder it is to keep your own pathology out of your child's life.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
- He describes a pivotal moment in his own life when, prompted by his mother, he found himself walking towards his three-year-old daughter to punish her, recognised he was about to re-enact his own upbringing, and stopped — an act he frames as liberation, but also as separation from his tribe.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]

# Takeaways

Van der Kolk's prescription is less a protocol than an orientation: start by asking who a person is, what works and what gets in the way, rather than cataloguing how sick they are, and then find the practice — movement, rhythm, synchrony, touch, group creation — that restores the connection trauma severed.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] He believes essentially anyone can be helped given the resources, but is pessimistic about the systems meant to deliver that help, arguing that the effective methods remain unconventional precisely because the funding incentives reward cheapness over recovery.[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo] Answering the closing question of where people should go for help, he concedes the honest answer is uncomfortable: everyone he knows who got better did so as an accidental explorer, and the hardest skill is telling the difference between "this treatment isn't working" and "something is wrong with me."[^the-body-trauma-expert-this-eye-movement-trick-c-Qx5J5nwDBTo]
