# Overview

Thomas Seyfried, a professor of biology, genetics and biochemistry at Boston College, tells Steven Bartlett that the dominant framing of cancer as a genetic disease is wrong, and that when the field eventually accepts what he says the data show, the misdirection will be recognised as the greatest tragedy in the history of medicine.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] In his account, every cancer — regardless of the tissue it arises in or how different the cells look under a microscope — shares one pathophysiological feature: a dependence on fermentation, meaning energy generated without oxygen, following chronic damage to the mitochondria.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] He traces the idea to Otto Warburg's 1920s work showing tumour slices consuming far more glucose than normal tissue while taking in less oxygen and dumping out lactic acid, and says he only revisited Warburg after learning of Linda Nebeling's ketogenic-diet work with two children with brain cancer at Case Western Reserve.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]

The therapeutic proposal that follows, which he calls metabolic therapy, is to simultaneously restrict the two fermentable fuels he says tumours require — the sugar glucose and the amino acid glutamine — while transitioning the rest of the body onto fatty acids and ketone bodies, which he argues healthy mitochondria burn as a "super fuel" but defective tumour mitochondria cannot use.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] To make this measurable he and his students built the glucose ketone index (GKI), dividing blood glucose in millimolar by blood ketones in millimolar, and he says a value of 2.0 or below places a person in what he calls the Paleolithic zone.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] He presents this as both prevention and treatment, differing mainly in that treatment adds drugs targeting glutamine.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]

Seyfried is careful in the conversation to say he is a scientist rather than a physician, declines to tell listeners what to eat, and does not tell patients to refuse chemotherapy — he argues instead that existing tools including surgery, radiation, low-dose chemotherapy and immunotherapy would work better on a patient already in nutritional ketosis.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] He frames the field's resistance as a Kuhnian paradigm problem comparable to the Copernican revolution or germ theory, and admits he himself eats donuts, drinks whiskey and lives inside the same food environment as everyone else.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]

# Key points

- Seyfried cites American Cancer Society figures of nearly 2 million new US cases a year and about 612,000 deaths in 2024 — roughly 1,700 people a day or 70 an hour — plus around 8,000 deaths a day in China, and says the picture is projected to be worse by 2050.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He argues the widely cited 31-32% reduction in US cancer death rates is a prevention effect from 1990s anti-smoking campaigns measured against a counterfactual trajectory, not evidence that treatment has improved.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- His core claim is that all cancers, however different genetically or morphologically, depend on fermentation of glucose (producing lactic acid) and glutamine (producing succinic acid), because the mitochondrion — which he describes as a second living organism inside the cell — has been chronically damaged.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He says he published early work showing that blood sugar level predicts tumour growth rate in mice, and claims the relationship has since been replicated across human cancers.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- Against the somatic mutation theory he offers three arguments: some cancers grow out of control with no mutations; healthy people carry thousands of so-called driver mutations in normal tissue without tumours; and nuclear transfer experiments show that a tumour nucleus in normal cytoplasm gives regulated growth, while a normal nucleus in tumour cytoplasm gives dysregulated growth.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He argues the mutations seen in tumour nuclei are largely downstream of mitochondrial dysfunction, which throws out mutagenic reactive oxygen species (ROS).[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- As evidence that lifestyle drives the disease he cites Albert Schweitzer's observation that cancer was extremely rare among Africans living traditionally, its rarity in Arctic and aboriginal populations before modern diets arrived, the absence of any documented breast cancer in a female chimpanzee, and his own dog research noting that wild wolves rarely die of cancer while it is the leading killer of domestic dogs.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- The mitochondrial damage he blames is cumulative and multi-causal: processed carbohydrates, lack of exercise, chronic emotional stress, poor sleep, and carcinogens including asbestos, chemical agents, microplastics and talcum powder — the last, he says, forming foci in the urogenital tract that provoke an inflammatory, growth-factor response.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He distinguishes progression-free survival from overall survival, arguing drug approvals now lean on the former; he singles out Avastin (bevacizumab) in brain cancer as, in his words, an immoral drug that makes scans look better while dispersing tumour cells through the brain.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He says irradiating a brain tumour frees up glucose and glutamine in the microenvironment, and that glioblastoma survival curves are grimly consistent across hospitals worldwide under standard care.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- The GKI calculator, originally built for brain cancer patients and now used by healthy people, was prompted by a patient in Nice whose blood sugar spiked during an argument over a parking space; he recommends measuring with a blood meter such as the Keto Mojo rather than breath or urine proxies.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- His headline case is Pablo Kelly from Devon, diagnosed with an inoperable glioblastoma in 2014 at around 22, who refused radiation and chemotherapy, held his GKI near 2.0, underwent three debulking surgeries over the following decade, and died in August 2024 of a cerebral haemorrhage after the third operation rather than of the cancer.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He also describes a pit bull with a mast cell tumour whose owner used a raw-food, calorie-restricted protocol until the tumour disappeared, the dog living to 15 and a half and dying of heart disease — while insisting metabolic therapy is a non-toxic management strategy rather than a cure.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He points to combining a ketogenic diet with hyperbaric oxygen, which he says increased average survival in mice by roughly 80%, on the logic that removing glucose and glutamine strips tumour cells of the antioxidant protection they use against internally generated ROS.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- He says colleagues in Istanbul have shown chemotherapy can be more effective at much lower doses when a patient is at a GKI of 2.0 or below, and he explicitly does not tell patients to refuse conventional treatment.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
- On practice, he suggests visiting rather than permanently living in a restricted state — intermittent and occasional water-only fasting, an initial 10-14 day zero-carbohydrate period, vigorous exercise, low-glycaemic foods — and notes that the National Cancer Institute's roughly $7 billion a year mostly funds gene-focused work while his own lab runs on philanthropy through Boston College and Travis Christofferson's foundation.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]

# Takeaways

Seyfried's position is that cancer is far more preventable than the public is told, because in his framing the disease is a chronic failure of cellular energy metabolism that diet, exercise, sleep and stress all feed into — and that the same logic yields a management strategy patients can partly execute themselves by tracking a single number.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk] He acknowledges the burden this places on the individual, saying success with metabolic therapy rides on the patient's own shoulders rather than a clinician's, and concedes he cannot promise it will work for everyone; his stated hope is simply that the paradigm shifts, which he insists is a matter of time rather than of belief.[^the-cancer-doctor-this-common-food-is-making-can-VaVC3PAWqLk]
