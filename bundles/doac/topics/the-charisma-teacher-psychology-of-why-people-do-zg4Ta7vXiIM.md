# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Charlie Houpert, co-founder of the Charisma on Command YouTube channel (about 6.8 million subscribers) and the Charisma University course. Houpert argues that charisma and confidence are "perhaps the most underrated piece of success" — citing Donald Trump talking his way into the presidency and his own off-cycle promotion in consulting, won not by being the best analyst but by building closer connections with the company president. He rejects the myth that charisma is innate: he grew up so shy his class of 500 voted him "most likely to break out of a shell in college," and only transformed at 19 while studying abroad in Costa Rica, running deliberate social experiments and adopting the mindset that every failed interaction was something he could have done differently.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]

The conversation is a tour of his practical frameworks: the "one more sentence" habit for everyday interactions; establishing fun, trust, and respect in the first 60 seconds before expressing interest; leaving conversational "hooks" in answers to routine questions; "prey versus predator" movement and the body-language feedback loop; and his six charismatic mindsets, from "no matter what I will be okay" to "I go first in humanizing the interaction." He also breaks down five charisma types (high conviction, authentic, funny, empathetic, energetic), analyzes Trump's "Teflon" primary-debate performances, and details tactics for interviews, raises, dating, and public speaking.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]

The episode ends with an unusually raw personal disclosure: Houpert describes being sexually abused at around age 10, internalizing shame that surfaced as perfectionism and approval-seeking, and using roughly quarterly psychedelic journeys in recent years to reconnect with himself — reframing charisma from "something you do" to "the essence of who you are."[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]

# Key points

- The guest's easiest starter tip is "one more sentence": in the dozens of daily micro-interactions (elevators, Uber drivers, counters), add one sentence beyond the prescribed script — connections compound from there. Changing your environment (as he did abroad) is one of the most powerful ways to change social habits, because friends and family resist shifts in how you socialize.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- On first impressions he argues "be interested in them" is incomplete because it ignores status: first communicate fun (be "better than good" when asked how you are), trust (nonverbals, not overqualifying or name-dropping), and respect — which can take under 60 seconds — and only then express interest.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- Habitual answers to stock questions ("what do you do", "where are you from") kill conversations; instead reverse-engineer answers that leave three "crumbs" or hooks tied to topics you actually want to discuss, giving people opt-in points every three to five sentences rather than a monologue.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- "Prey versus predator" movement: darting eyes and jerky gestures signal unsafety and low status, while slower, calmer movement and being comfortable "caught looking" signal safety and status. Body language runs a two-way loop — opening posture and exposing vulnerable areas (neck, palms, inner elbows) genuinely changes how grounded you feel, akin to Tony Robbins's pre-stage incantations.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- He frames interactions as "two storms of belief" colliding, where the higher-conviction belief usually defines the relationship — and notes people spend their lives undershooting socially: we have thousands of undershoots and fewer than a dozen genuine overshoots, so the rational risk is to overshoot (illustrated by his brother writing "Dragon" on a networking name tag and becoming the hit of the event).[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- The six charismatic mindsets: (1) no matter what, I will be okay; (2) I care more about my character than my reputation; (3) I have impeccable honesty and integrity — even small lies ("I'm on the way") erode your capacity to speak with conviction, and friends noticing you lie to others stop trusting what you tell them; (4) I don't need to convince anyone of anything — invitation beats convincing, and filtering beats persuading; (5) I proactively share my purpose — illustrated by The Buried Life team talking their way into playing basketball with Obama; (6) I go first in humanizing the interaction — first joke, first compliment, first vulnerability, because "whoever goes first becomes the leader."[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- His five charisma types are high conviction (Conor McGregor, Steve Jobs, Trump), authentic (Joe Rogan), funny (comedians), empathetic (Oprah), and energetic (early Will Smith, Jack Black); conviction and empathy tend to trade off, and he calls Obama a "charisma freak" who combines several — evidence, he argues, that elections are popularity contests more than merit contests.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- Analyzing Trump's 2016 primary debates, he says Trump never cracks or plays defense — always offense — and that doubling down repeatedly pulls audiences along, while rivals who trailed off mid-sentence looked weak; his advice to shy people is to always finish your sentence rather than trail off when interrupted.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- For job interviews: the interview starts when you leave your house (warm up on the Uber driver, security guard, secretary), prepare three to five up-down-up career stories that can be slotted into any question, and close by asking "if a year from now you look back and hiring me was a great decision, what would I have had to do?" — a question he says repeatedly won people jobs, and which generalizes to raises, personal training, and agency work.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- On attraction, he says conviction through the first awkward 5–15 seconds matters, boring openers fail, and the biggest miss for men is lacking (and failing to communicate) standards beyond beauty; he recounts holding a boundary with a chronically canceling date — declining to rearrange his plans — which immediately flipped the power dynamic, while cautioning that this must be true, not a game.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- His "five habits that make people instantly dislike you" analysis (via Brie Larson's press tour) includes needing to win every banter exchange and interpreting ambiguous comments negatively; the fix is charitable interpretation (Ted Lasso-style) and "tagging" jokes at your own expense, which signals security better than defending yourself.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
- Speaking like a leader: replace filler words with silence ("silence is a vacuum" that pulls attention), learn story beats, and fill your own space fully — elbows off your sides, full-wingspan gestures — without invading anyone else's; and remember "there are no superiors": you are always dealing with people, not roles.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]

# Takeaways

The guest's through-line is that charisma is a trainable stack — bodily calm, conversational hooks, going first, invitation over convincing — but that its deepest layer is internal alignment: honesty that lets you speak with conviction, character over reputation, and self-acceptance. His closing account of healing from childhood sexual abuse, and the self-love he says he found through that work, reframes the whole subject: the tips create connection, but the durable version of charisma, he argues, comes from no longer being at war with yourself — from listening to yourself the way he once needed an adult simply to sit and listen to him.[^the-charisma-teacher-psychology-of-why-people-do-zg4Ta7vXiIM]
