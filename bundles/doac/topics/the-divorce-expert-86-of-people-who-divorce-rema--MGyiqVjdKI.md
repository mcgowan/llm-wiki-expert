# Overview

James Sexton is a New York divorce lawyer who has spent roughly 25 years representing people in contested divorce and custody proceedings, including billionaires, athletes and celebrities.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI] His central claim to host Steven Bartlett is that marriage is treated as an assumption rather than a decision: people can articulate what a mug or a coaster is for, but stall when asked what problem marriage is designed to solve, even though he considers it the most legally significant thing most people do apart from dying.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI] He is careful to separate the legal status of marriage — a government-defined bundle of rights and obligations that can change under you mid-marriage — from the religious, social and emotional meanings people load onto the same word.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]

Much of the conversation is about prevention rather than litigation. Sexton frames prenuptial agreements as two people deciding they are better placed to write the rules of their own economics than a legislature is, and argues the same logic applies to emotional maintenance: the best time to agree how you will fight is when you are not fighting.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI] He describes a "hit send now" technique from his book — saying the difficult, honest thing quickly and flagging it as a technique while goodwill still exists — and small daily gestures such as leaving a note, as the relational equivalent of maintaining a healthy weight rather than trying to lose a hundred pounds later.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]

The episode turns unexpectedly toward grief and love. Sexton, a former hospice volunteer, argues that everything we love is loaned rather than gifted, that loving anything is "insane" precisely because loss is guaranteed, and that this impermanence is what makes love worth the risk.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI] He says his work has made him a romantic rather than a cynic, and that he is a fan of marriage only to the extent it facilitates love — arguing you can have the connection, the public declaration and the accountability without the legal contract.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]

# Key points

- Sexton says roughly 56% of marriages end in divorce, and estimates another 20% or so stay together miserably for children or finances — an activity that on his reckoning fails 70–75% of the time.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He finds the 86% remarriage-within-five-years figure more revealing than the divorce rate itself, taking it as evidence of how powerfully humans are drawn to the institution despite their record at it.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He describes drafting a prenup for a client who had just endured a four-to-five-year brutal divorce and was remarrying a woman 30 years younger whom he had known four months, insisting "this one's different."[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- The most shocking enforceable prenup he has seen awarded the wife around $70,000 a month in alimony but deducted $10,000 a month for every 10 lb she gained after the marriage; a court called the provision disgusting but upheld it as a valid contract.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He explains that a prenup is only voidable if it was unconscionable when made — not if it merely produces a shockingly unfair result later — absent fraud, duress, undue influence or intoxication at signing.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He calls fidelity clauses a terrible idea legally, because defining cheating is hard and infidelity already carries enormous built-in penalties; adding a financial one, he argues, deters as poorly as criminalising heroin deters an addict.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He reports a generational shift: people in their mid-20s to mid-30s are getting prenups at roughly five times the rate of 10–15 years ago, alongside a more pragmatic view of relationships.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- On sex, he calls it the thing that distinguishes a romantic relationship from a platonic one and "a great canary in the coal mine" — nearly every client he speaks to reports some change in sex when the marriage began to sink, though he stresses cause and effect are hard to untangle.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He observes that when men learn of a partner's affair they ask "did you sleep with them?", while women ask "are you in love with her?" — and says men in his practice are generally more dissatisfied with sexual frequency while women want higher-quality sex.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- On money, he says the issue is transparency and power rather than poverty; he sees a husband losing his job as a major precipitant of divorce in heterosexual marriages, while a wife losing hers rarely destabilises the marriage in his experience.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He notes that divorce splits assets net of liabilities, and that many apparently wealthy clients — celebrities especially — are heavily leveraged; he also recounts a lottery winner who was told his wife was entitled to exactly half, after which she immediately left.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- He attacks the soulmate myth as good for his business but bad for people, preferring to see relationships as chapters — a relationship that ends in something other than death can still have been successful — and warns against comparing real partners to imagined ideals or to performative social-media relationships.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- The fastest marriage-to-divorce he has seen was 48 hours, typically handled as an annulment; he contrasts the absence of any waiting period for marriage with waiting periods for firearms.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
- In 25 years he has had one client whose spouse tried to kill her — stabbing her and running her over multiple times; she survived and he is serving life — and says client and opposing-party suicides have occurred several times.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]

# Takeaways

Sexton's summary of his own practice is that all marital problems reduce to two things: not knowing what you want, and not knowing how to express it.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI] From that he draws a practical prescription — pay attention, say the uncomfortable thing early and with love, and treat honest conversation about sex, money and dissatisfaction as preventative maintenance — and a philosophical one, that love is loaned rather than permanently gifted, which for him is a reason to hold it more deliberately rather than less.[^the-divorce-expert-86-of-people-who-divorce-rema--MGyiqVjdKI]
