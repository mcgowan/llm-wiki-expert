# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews nutrition researcher Alan Aragon, who describes over 30 years in the field — a decade of personal training, a decade of nutritional counseling, and 13 years in research and education, with around 30 publications including systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and randomized controlled trials. Aragon says he has coached high-profile clients including Stone Cold Steve Austin, Derek Fisher, and Pete Sampras, and that the most common goal people bring him is improving body composition — losing fat and/or gaining muscle.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]

Aragon's central argument is that people have the hierarchy of protein priorities backwards: the total daily protein amount is "the cake," while meal distribution and timing are only a thin layer of icing. He walks through evidence-based targets (1.6–2.2 g of protein per kilogram of goal body weight for those pushing muscle gain), debunks fears about high protein harming kidneys, liver, or bone in healthy people, and explains metabolic adaptation — arguing that when dieters say their metabolism has slowed, the major component is a drop in non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT) of roughly 200–300 calories, not a mysteriously broken metabolism.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]

The conversation also covers rapid weight loss, GLP-1 drug rebound, menopause and PCOS, ketogenic and carnivore diets, fasting and autophagy, creatine, artificial sweeteners, and sugar. Aragon closes with a personal account of overcoming a six-year alcohol addiction in his 40s, and argues that the real difference between people who transform their bodies and those who struggle is not metabolism but whether the physical goal becomes their top priority.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]

# Key points

- Aragon argues the biggest protein myth is a scrambled hierarchy: total daily protein matters most, distribution across meals is secondary, and timing around workouts rarely matters. He cites a recent study in resistance-trained subjects comparing three versus five protein feedings a day (both at roughly 1 g per pound) that found no significant differences in muscle size or strength gains.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- His protein prescription: 1.2–1.6 g per kilogram of body weight for the general population, 1.6–2.2 g/kg of goal body weight for those maximizing muscle gain — for Bartlett's 90 kg target, up to 198 g per day. Women should almost always start at the lower end because of higher body-fat proportion.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- He cites Jose Antonio's trials of very high protein intakes (3.3–4.4 g/kg) in resistance-trained, free-living subjects, arguing high protein facilitates fat loss, and says harm from high protein is rare — essentially limited to pre-existing chronic kidney disease — with studies showing virtually zero threat to kidney, liver, or bone health in healthy people.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- On animal versus plant protein, he says animal proteins are generally more anabolic gram for gram (with mycoprotein a notable exception that outperformed milk protein), but two controlled studies show vegan and omnivore groups gain equal muscle when total protein is optimized at 1.6 g/kg/day.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- On "slow metabolism": he explains that dieting triggers a drop in NEAT of about 200–300 calories plus adaptive thermoreduction of another 50–100 calories, and cites a Levine study where subjects overfed by 1,000 calories per day increased NEAT by an average of 336 calories (one subject nearly 700) — the same mechanism, he argues, that explains "hard gainers" who can't put on weight.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- For fast weight loss, he says the only real option is an aggressive 20–40% caloric deficit with protein kept very high — but warns against exceeding roughly 0.5–1% of body weight lost per week, because faster loss sacrifices muscle and triggers "collateral fattening," where the body ramps up hunger to rebuild lost lean tissue, driving rebound.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- His best-diet answer in one sentence: the diet with enough protein, enough total calories, comprised predominantly of healthy food choices, that fits the individual's preferences and tolerances. Belly fat can't be spot-reduced, though he says diets lower in saturated fat (swapping fatty land-animal meats for avocado, nuts, olive oil) are more conducive to reducing visceral fat.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- On menopause, he pushes back on claims that women are "doomed" to gain fat and lose muscle, citing the SWAN study: average fat gain across the entire menopausal transition was 1.6 kg and average muscle loss just 0.2 kg — statistically significant but not insurmountable. His main adjustment is halving the expected rate of fat loss, not rearranging the program.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- He treats plateaus as "maintenance practice" rather than failure — the body achieving homeostasis — and recommends diet breaks ("non-YOLO maintenance") of a week off every 4–8 weeks or every 5–10 lb lost, as a tactic for long-term adherence.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- On keto, he says it works for fat loss by cutting hyperpalatable junk and raising protein, but that in long-term studies most keto dieters creep carbohydrate intake back up (from an assigned 50 g to around 150 g by 12 months), and that high-carb groups have an edge in muscle-size gains; strength gains are similar when protein and calories are matched.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- He calls creatine "king creatine" — over a thousand studies, roughly 20% versus 12% lifting-capacity gains against non-users in typical 8–12 week studies, plus emerging benefits for glucose control, memory, and joint health — though when forced to pick three supplements he chose multivitamin, fish oil, and vitamin D3.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
- On fasting and autophagy, he argues autophagy ramps up in any caloric deficit and through both resistance and aerobic exercise, so chasing it through prolonged fasting is a double-edged sword; artificial sweeteners other than saccharin he calls "a nothing burger," and he defends fresh fruit while capping added sugar at about 10% of calories.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]

# Takeaways

The guest's through-line is that body composition change is governed by a few boring fundamentals — total daily protein, a sensible caloric deficit, resistance training, and a controlled rate of loss — while the details people obsess over (meal timing, named diets, autophagy, gut-microbiome fixes) rarely move the needle. He argues that physique competitors always hit their goals not because of special metabolisms but because the goal is their top priority, and his own recovery from alcohol addiction — seven years sober by visualizing the full consequences of a drink — illustrates the same premise: lasting change comes from priorities and habits, not hacks.[^the-fat-burning-expert-the-real-reason-youre-not-3C185Gkgg0U]
