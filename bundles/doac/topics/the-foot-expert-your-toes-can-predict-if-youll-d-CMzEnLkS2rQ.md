# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Courtney Conley, a chiropractor and foot specialist who has spent 20 years treating foot pain and now campaigns to make people rethink their feet and footwear. Her core argument is that the foot — with its four layers of muscles, recoiling arch, and thousands of sensory receptors — is a foundation the whole body is built on, and that modern shoes systematically weaken it: narrow, tapered toe boxes reshape the foot, elevated heels shorten the muscles of the back of the leg, and thick cushioning cuts off the sensory information the foot needs for balance. She says one in three people (especially over 45) will experience foot pain, a prevalence rivaled only by low back pain, and that losing the ability to walk cascades into physical, emotional, and mental decline.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]

The guest's mission is personal: as a former ballet dancer and triathlete she suffered bunions, neuromas, and heel pain at a time when movement was her "mode of survival" through personal struggles, which she says took her to dark places and set her on a quest to fix her own feet and her patients'. She makes the case for walking as "the most underrated, underutilized, easily accessible activity," citing research that small step-count increases meaningfully cut mortality, depression, and dementia risk, and illustrates it with a 27-year-old chronic heel-pain patient who had been told to cap his day at 2,500 steps and was rebuilt through five-minute "micro walks."[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]

The conversation is also highly practical: the guest reviews a table of common shoes (trainers, dress shoes, high heels, the Nike Alphafly "super shoe"), defines her three criteria for functional footwear, examines Bartlett's own feet (finding early arthritis of the big toe and a curled pinky toe), and demonstrates a home "foot gym" of toe spacers, toe strengtheners, bands, and a ball — while insisting that people transition gradually because "you have to earn your right" to minimal shoes.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]

# Key points

- The guest cites step-count research: adding 500 steps to a 2,500-step baseline cuts cardiovascular mortality risk by 7%; a 1,000-step increase cuts all-cause mortality risk by 15%; 5,000 steps a day reduces symptoms of depression and 7,500 reduces the prevalence of depression diagnoses; 9,800 steps reduces dementia risk, with about 3,800-4,000 steps already delivering 50% of that maximal benefit.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- She says most people globally average only about 4,500-4,900 steps a day, and recommends "micro walks" (a 5-minute walk is roughly 500 steps, 10 minutes roughly 1,000) as digestible movement snacks throughout the day.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- The foot is described as the only place in the body where abnormal loads are visible from the outside — bunions, hammer toes, and neuromas are windows into dysfunction elsewhere in the kinetic chain (she notes tilting the pelvis forward drops the arches, tucking it lifts them), and bunions and hammer toes increase fall risk and decrease balance.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- Around 70% of children (particularly girls) wear shoes that are too narrow; the guest argues that if children started in proper footwear "I'd be out of a job," because tapered toe boxes change foot structure the way a decade in a sling would waste an arm — invoking Daniel Lieberman's thought experiment of a child raised in two-inch-thick gloves.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- Her three criteria for a functional shoe: a wide toe box that lets the toes splay (her non-negotiable), heel and toes in the same plane (zero drop), and a thin, flexible sole; she warns a "wide shoe" is not the same as a wide toe box, since width is often added at the midfoot while the toes stay tapered.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- On cushioning: the more material between foot and ground, the less sensory acuity — a problem for balance and for ankle-sprain patients, whom she keeps close to the ground; heel-to-toe drop acts like a "mini high heel," shifting pressure forward and shortening the posterior chain up through calf, hamstring, and back.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- She criticizes carbon-plated super shoes like the Alphafly for training use: toe-spring rocker technology yields a 2-4% running-economy gain on race day, but research shows habitually offloading the foot this way weakens its intrinsic muscles — so train in the "workhorse" shoe and save the super shoe for racing; plyometric training can deliver a comparable 2-4% capacity gain.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- On plantar fasciitis/fasciopathy, she says the trigger is almost always load added "too fast, too soon" to a weak foot (which handles four to six times body weight in walking), weakness of flexor digitorum brevis strongly correlates with the painful side, and orthotics should be a temporary offload paired with strengthening and an "exit strategy" — not a permanent fix.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- Her patient story: a 27-year-old quadruplet, two years into chronic heel pain, capped at 2,500 steps and living depressed in his father's basement, was rebuilt through progressive micro walks, foot strengthening, footwear changes, and weekly "three good things" reflections — he now averages 5,000-6,000 steps a day and reports he cannot remember the last time he cried.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- Home assessments and exercises she demonstrates include: 40-45 degrees of big-toe extension needed for walking, a credit-card pull test under the toes for flexor strength, "toe yoga" (lift, spread, reach the toes while keeping the foot's tripod), banded toe curls (research supports four sets of 12 reps for plantar heel pain), and rolling a ball under the foot 60-90 seconds to wake up its receptors.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- She flags the soleus as an underappreciated powerhouse that can put eight times body weight through the forefoot; a return-to-run benchmark she cites is six single-leg seated calf-raise reps at 1.5 times body weight — yet the seated calf raise machine is "always open" at the gym.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
- Common running errors she highlights are overstriding (landing far ahead of the center of mass, masked by heavy cushioning) and crossover gait; she dismisses towel-scrunch exercises as non-functional beyond early rehab, and argues hiking boots offer a false "neurological hug" that restricts ankle dorsiflexion and transfers load to the knee.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]

# Takeaways

The guest's closing advice is that the lowest-hanging fruit is simply wearing a shoe that fits the actual shape of the foot — wide toe box, flat, flexible — because research shows this alone begins strengthening the foot, and she recounts listeners like a 65-year-old whose foot, ankle, knee, and hip pain resolved within months of switching to zero-drop, wide-toe-box shoes. But she stresses the transition must be earned gradually along a "shoe spectrum" rather than by burning all your cushioned shoes overnight, and that stiff sport-specific footwear (cleats, ski boots, cycling shoes) is fine so long as you do foot care before and after. Her big picture: pay attention to the foundation you walk on, because you cannot "build a jet engine on a paper airplane," and strong feet protect not just mobility but emotional and mental well-being.[^the-foot-expert-your-toes-can-predict-if-youll-d-CMzEnLkS2rQ]
