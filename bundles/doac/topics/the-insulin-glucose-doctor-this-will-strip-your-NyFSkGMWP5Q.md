# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Benjamin Bikman, metabolic scientist, BYU professor, and author of "Why We Get Sick." Bikman's central claim is that most chronic diseases — type 2 diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, fatty liver disease, Alzheimer's, and common forms of infertility including erectile dysfunction and PCOS — are branches of one tree whose root is insulin resistance, a condition he says affects some 88% of US adults to some degree. Conventional medicine, he argues, prunes the branches with drugs while never addressing the root.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]

He explains insulin resistance as a two-sided problem — cells becoming "deaf" to insulin while blood insulin levels climb — and describes two roads to it: "fast" insulin resistance (from stress hormones, inflammation, or excess insulin itself, inducible in hours and just as quickly reversible) and "slow" insulin resistance, driven by fat cells swelling to their limits under chronically elevated insulin from constant carbohydrate-heavy eating. He emphasizes that fat-cell size, not fat mass, determines metabolic harm — explaining ethnic differences via the "personal fat threshold," why type 1 diabetics cannot gain fat without insulin, and why liposuction worsens metabolic health.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]

The conversation covers Alzheimer's as "insulin resistance of the brain," ketones as a superior brain fuel, his defense of cholesterol and salt, critiques of the calorie-centric model of obesity and of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, and his four pillars: control carbohydrates, prioritize protein, don't fear fat, and frequently fast.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]

# Key points

- The guest describes three "fast lane" causes of insulin resistance — stress (cortisol and adrenaline, raised by sleep deprivation and excess caffeine), inflammation (infections, autoimmune flares, even cigarette and vape exposure), and too much insulin itself — any of which, he says, could make a person measurably insulin resistant within about 6 hours in a lab, and which reverse just as quickly when removed.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- "Slow" insulin resistance develops as fat cells hypertrophy: with roughly 70% of global calories coming from carbohydrates and six-times-a-day eating, the average person spends every waking moment with elevated insulin. Fat cells near bursting resist insulin's "keep growing" signal, and that resistance spreads.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- Fat gain requires both a signal (insulin) and substrate (calories): type 1 diabetics who skip insulin cannot gain fat no matter how much they eat — the basis of the eating disorder diabulimia — and fat cells in his lab's Petri dishes stay small amid abundant calories until insulin is added.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- Fat-cell number is largely fixed after puberty; what changes is size, and small fat cells are anti-inflammatory and insulin sensitive. Ethnic differences in fat-cell number (Caucasians and Africans more, East Asians fewer, Hispanics in between) explain why a thin East Asian can develop type 2 diabetes from weight gain that leaves a Caucasian metabolically healthy — the "personal fat threshold" concept. Visceral fat is far more harmful than subcutaneous fat: transplanting visceral (but not subcutaneous) fat between animals induces insulin resistance.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- Insulin resistance can be physiological and purposeful in the "two P's" — puberty and pregnancy — but gestational diabetes is that process gone too far; he cites a roughly 40% higher chance of obesity and metabolic complications in the children of mothers with gestational diabetes.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- On Alzheimer's, he argues the amyloid-plaque theory failed — plaque-clearing drugs didn't restore cognition, plaques appear in cognitively healthy brains, and foundational plaque papers were exposed as fabricated — and that the disease is better understood as brain insulin resistance producing "brain glucose hypometabolism," an energy gap that ketones can fill; he cites case reports of Alzheimer's patients drawing clock faces and tying shoelaces only when in ketosis, and studies finding insulin resistance in 40–80% of Alzheimer's patients.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- He urges people to measure fasting insulin, not just glucose (warning thresholds of about 10 microunits/mL US or 40 pmol/L UK), noting many insulin-resistant people — including lean people and lean women with PCOS — have normal glucose, and cynically suggests medicine measures glucose and LDL because drugs exist for them, while triglycerides and insulin are more predictive but unmedicated.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- On longevity, he dismisses "longevity gurus" for relying on correlational and insect data, and argues the longest-lived humans are the most insulin sensitive — citing a recent Swedish study finding good glucose control, high cholesterol, and low uric acid among the longest lived — and that autophagy is best controlled by keeping insulin low (fasting or ketogenic diets), not by expensive supplements.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- Ketones, he argues, are underappreciated: the brain takes the majority of its energy from ketones even at modest levels, his lab found belly-fat metabolic rate tripled in ketosis, ketones protect muscle from injury and are anti-inflammatory, and low-carb isocaloric diets raise metabolic rate by nearly 300 calories a day while ketones are exhaled and urinated out as lost calories. Downsides he concedes: temporarily reduced metabolic flexibility, and the need for extra salt, water, and enough dietary fat and calories to preserve muscle.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- He attacks "eat less, exercise more" as a recipe for hunger, citing The Biggest Loser contestants whose metabolic rates stayed suppressed even after regaining weight, and David Ludwig's finding that insulin-spiking meals leave people hungrier; the first step of fat loss, he says, should be lowering insulin so the body can burn its own fat and hunger falls naturally.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- On GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic/Wegovy), he cites the STEP 5 trial finding roughly 40% of weight lost is lean mass (muscle and bone), notes about 69% of UK users quit within two years — regaining fat but not the lost muscle and bone — and cites reports of doubled suicidal-thought risk and tripled major-depression risk on starting; his preferred use is the lowest effective dose as a temporary aid to break carbohydrate addiction while learning to eat properly and lift weights.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
- His four pillars: control carbohydrates (whole fruits and vegetables, "eat them, don't drink them," nothing from "bags and boxes with barcodes"), prioritize protein (especially animal-sourced), don't fear the fat that comes with protein, and frequently fast — plus resistance exercise, since contracting muscle pulls in glucose without insulin and muscle strength predicts longevity better than aerobic fitness. Contrarian side claims include salt restriction causing insulin resistance, aspartame and stevia being insulin-inert (he avoids sucralose because it crosses the blood-brain barrier), vaping being chemically worse than smoking for airway damage and insulin resistance, diesel exhaust enlarging fat cells in his animal studies, and skepticism that reduced gut-microbiome diversity on carnivore/keto diets is proven harmful.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]

# Takeaways

The guest's overarching argument is that modern medicine and nutrition policy watch the wrong dials: calories and glucose instead of insulin. In his telling, chronic disease, stubborn body fat, brain fog, and even accelerated brain aging trace back to chronically elevated insulin, and the remedy is behavioral rather than pharmaceutical — restrict refined carbohydrates, build meals around protein and fat, add strategic fasting and muscle-building exercise, and let ketones fuel the brain. He presents keto and carnivore approaches, dietary cholesterol, and salt far more favorably than mainstream guidance does, and these are his contested positions rather than consensus; his consistent refrain is that the simple plan is not the easy plan, because carbohydrate is the one macronutrient humans show addiction to.[^the-insulin-glucose-doctor-this-will-strip-your-NyFSkGMWP5Q]
