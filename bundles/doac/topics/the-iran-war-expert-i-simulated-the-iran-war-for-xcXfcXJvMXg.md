# Overview

Professor Robert Pape, a University of Chicago scholar who says he has studied political violence for 40 years, built the US Air Force's air-power curriculum, and advised every White House from 2001 to 2024, joins Steven Bartlett to analyze the unfolding US–Iran war. Pape says his class has run war-game simulations of bombing Iran for 20 years, most recently just before the June strikes on Natanz, Fordow, and Esfahan, and that events are now playing out as those simulations predicted: near-perfect tactical success paired with strategic failure, because the US does not know where Iran's enriched uranium is.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]

His central framework is the "escalation trap" (or "smart bomb trap"), which he says unfolds in three stages: stage one is precision bombing that hits targets but misses the political point; stage two is regime change, which he argues produces a harder, more aggressive successor regime rather than collapse; and stage three is limited ground deployments that invite retaliation reaching the attacker's homeland. Pape argues the US entered stage two when the supreme leader was killed, and he assigns a 75% probability that Washington will send ground forces into Iran to hunt for dispersed nuclear material — with the remaining 25% resting on a negotiated deal.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]

Beyond Iran, Pape contends the war is eroding American primacy: he argues the conflict is "manna from heaven" for China, which he says is racing ahead in AI and advanced manufacturing while the US is distracted, and he warns that the normalization of political violence inside the United States — the subject of his forthcoming book — may be a greater danger than Iran itself.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]

# Key points

- Pape says his 20-year-running simulations show B-2 strikes destroy Iranian nuclear facilities over 90% of the time, but that tactical success is hollow because the goal is the enriched uranium, not buildings — and after the simulated strikes "we didn't know where a single ounce was."[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- He states that as of the previous May, Iran had 60%-enriched uranium sufficient for 16 nuclear bombs, and that satellite imagery showed trucks moving material out of Fordow two days before it was bombed, suggesting the material was dispersed before the strikes.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- Pape argues the assassinated supreme leader was actually a guardrail against weaponization, having issued two fatwas against nuclear weapons; his son and successor has issued no fatwa, is described as far more aggressive, and is backed by the 150,000–200,000-strong Revolutionary Guard within Iran's million-man military.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- He rejects the "Jenga" model of decapitation strikes — the idea that removing one node collapses a regime — arguing revolutionary regimes are adaptive matrices, and cites Gaddafi's 1986 retaliation (Pan Am 103, 271 civilians killed) and Milosevic's 1999 expulsion of a million Kosovars as the pattern of leaders lashing back after attacks.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- Iran's current phase, per Pape's Foreign Affairs piece published the day of recording, is "horizontal escalation": precision drone attacks on Gulf-state tourism and economic nodes (5–10% of those countries' GDP) designed to drive wedges between Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and the US and get American bases expelled.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- He says Russia is providing targeting intelligence to Iran's drones — mirroring US support for Ukraine — and speculates Trump's calls with Putin may aim at a swap: cutting off intelligence to Ukraine in exchange for Russia dropping support for Iran.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- Pape argues Iran is following the "North Korea plan": building multiple bombs simultaneously so it can conduct mountain tests and deter regime change, noting North Korea now has roughly 60 working nuclear weapons and that Ukraine's surrender of its arsenal is widely regretted there.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- He emphasizes the US had a working solution in the Obama deal — nearly all enriched material shipped out, 24/7 camera monitoring, on-site inspections — which Trump unilaterally abandoned in 2018, after which Iran went "pedal to the metal" on enrichment; he also says a better-than-Obama deal was on the table the day before the February bombing began.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- On US vulnerability, Pape argues America's soft underbelly is political, not military: adversaries win long wars of choice, as in Vietnam (58,000 dead without losing a battle) and Afghanistan, and he warns Trump faces "Lyndon Johnson territory" with midterms approaching and no "golden off-ramp."[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- He describes stage three as limited ground deployments (for example, the 82nd Airborne securing a site like Esfahan for weeks of searching), which his research suggests is the most likely trigger for Iranian-directed terrorism reaching Western homelands — citing how Russia's 1996 assassination of Chechen leader Dudayev produced Basayev's far more vicious "Operation Jihad."[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- Pape says the conflict benefits China, which buys 90% of Iran's oil but would happily sacrifice Middle East oil access to pin the US in another quagmire; he describes touring Wuhan, Shenzhen, and BYD factories and argues China's AI-driven industrial clusters are "uplifting" millions while US primacy erodes under $40 trillion in debt.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
- His advice to Trump: take the deal, get as much 60%- and 20%-enriched uranium out of Iran as possible, and accept that "freezing" the problem for 20 years beats chasing the "myth of 100% security," which he says is how big powers lose wars to small countries.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]

# Takeaways

Pape's closing argument is that bombs change politics more than they change facts on the ground: the US has destroyed targets but strengthened Iran's incentive to build a bomb, empowered a harder regime, and trapped itself between accepting a modest political loss now or a far larger one later. He predicts, with stated 75% confidence, an escalation to ground forces, and warns that the deeper threats are longer-term — China's rise while America is distracted, and the normalization of political violence within the United States, the subject of his book "Our Own Worst Enemies," which he calls a greater danger than Iran.[^the-iran-war-expert-i-simulated-the-iran-war-for-xcXfcXJvMXg]
