# Overview

In this Diary Of A CEO episode, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Georgia Ede, a Harvard-trained psychiatrist and pioneer of "metabolic psychiatry" — the study of how food and brain metabolism drive mental health.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0] Ede argues that the real root causes of mental illness are not mysterious chemical imbalances but brain inflammation, oxidative stress, and insulin resistance — the last of which she claims now affects more than 90% of Americans — and that a ketogenic diet addresses all three by "changing the brain's operating system" from a glucose-based to a fat-and-ketone-based fuel supply.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]

Her conviction is personal: after conventional low-fat, high-fiber eating failed her during a health collapse in her early 40s (chronic fatigue, IBS, fibromyalgia, migraines, anxiety), six months of trial-and-error led her to a mostly-meat, low-carbohydrate, high-fat diet that she says resolved every symptom — "backwards from what we're told is healthy" — prompting years of independent study of nutrition science.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0] She distills brain nutrition into three principles — nourish (all essential nutrients, which she claims cannot be met from whole foods without some animal foods), protect (subtract inflammatory ingredients like refined carbohydrates and vegetable oils rather than adding "antioxidant" superfoods), and energize (get glucose and insulin into healthy ranges).[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]

The centerpiece evidence discussed is a retrospective study of 31 treatment-resistant psychiatric inpatients in France, plus case studies from Ede's own practice, alongside frank discussion of the diet's difficulty, the "keto flu," carnivore diets, fiber myths, and the limits of the evidence base.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]

# Key points

- The guest describes a study with her French colleague in Toulouse: 31 hospitalized adults with treatment-resistant bipolar disorder, major depression, or schizophrenia (ill an average of 10 years, on an average of five psychiatric medications) tried a mildly ketogenic whole-foods diet; 28 stayed on it beyond two weeks, every one improved, 43% achieved clinical remission, and 64% left on less medication — results she says are unheard of in conventional psychiatric care.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- She concedes the study's confounds when the host pushes (hospital supervision, removal of ultra-processed food), noting randomized trials are still needed — the host cites a 2023 Cambridge systematic review calling for RCTs and a 2024 Stanford pilot in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder showing improved energy, sleep, mood, and quality of life.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- The ketogenic diet originated in 1921 to mimic fasting for children with severe epilepsy; she reports a greater-than-50% response rate for seizures and 10–20% becoming seizure-free, and defines a ketogenic diet not by a food list but as any way of eating that lowers insulin to the fat-burning point — achievable within vegan, vegetarian, omnivore, or carnivore patterns.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- Ketosis is measured as blood beta-hydroxybutyrate of 0.5 mmol or higher; she argues everyone needs at least intermittent ketosis because the body's "healing pathways" — repair, recycling, cleanup — are largely inactive outside it, comparing a never-paused metabolism to a factory that runs 24/7 without maintenance.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- She claims insulin is a master metabolic hormone, not just a blood-sugar regulator: a glucose-insulin roller coaster destabilizes stress, satiety, and reproductive hormones — citing a study in which teenage boys' adrenaline quadrupled 4–5 hours after a sugar-sweetened cola while the sugar-free group stayed flat.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- On weight loss, her one-line biology lesson is "you cannot burn fat if your insulin levels are too high" — ketones on a meter are a mirror image of insulin — and she recounts her 91-year-old mother losing 50 lb on a ketogenic diet.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- She says the most predictable benefits practitioners see are reduced anxiety (often within 3 days to 3 weeks) and mental clarity, and that patients describe ketosis as a "buffer" giving space between stimulus and reaction — including a bipolar-II patient whose suicidal ideation during marital arguments reliably vanished whenever he was in ketosis.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- Her book's opening case, "Carl," a man in his mid-60s with lifelong depression and anxiety who had abandoned medications after they induced mania, scored zero on all depression and anxiety scales by week six of a carnivore diet (3–4 lb of fatty meat daily), later reintroducing about 100 g of whole-food carbohydrate on training days to regain lost weight.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- On ADHD, she cites European "few foods" (oligoantigenic) elimination-diet studies from the 1980s–2000s reporting 62–82% response rates and up to 70% remission in children within weeks, while acknowledging (when the host cites the American Academy of Pediatrics and NICE) that dietary modification is not standard care; she also defends stimulants as among psychiatry's most effective medicines for those they help.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- Two ketogenic-diet-for-ADHD trials she is involved with were slated to begin in 2025 at Oxford University and the University of Michigan, motivated by metabolic clues such as children with obesity and adults with type 2 diabetes each being twice as likely to have ADHD.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- She challenges fiber orthodoxy — fiber is indigestible by definition, there is no evidence it "sweeps the colon clean," and the butyrate argument is moot because the ketone beta-hydroxybutyrate feeds intestinal cells equally well — and argues most nutrition belief rests on questionnaire-based epidemiology, "untested theories, wild guesses, and wishful thinking."[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
- Practical cautions she stresses: medications must be monitored during the transition (diabetes drugs may need halving on day one to avoid dangerous hypoglycemia), ease in over a week or two with electrolyte supplementation to avoid "keto flu," aim for consistent daily ketosis for at least six weeks, and ideally work with a team — prescriber, keto-trained dietitian, and coach or therapist.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]

# Takeaways

Ede's core message is that mental and physical diseases alike are downstream of cellular metabolic malfunction, and that the power of dietary change lies in subtraction — removing refined carbohydrates and vegetable oils — rather than adding superfoods or supplements.[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0] She describes herself as "nutritionally pro-choice," personalizing whichever dietary pattern a patient prefers around the fixed principles of nourish, protect, and energize, and insists that unlike medications, which "cover up" symptoms, properly designed nutrition strategies can address root causes — offering people who think they have tried everything the message that "hope is on the menu."[^the-keto-psychiatrist-what-keto-is-really-doing-B7tnfSPySb0]
