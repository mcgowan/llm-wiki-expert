# Overview

Soviet-born British commentator Konstantin Kisin, co-host of the Trigonometry podcast and author of "An Immigrant's Love Letter to the West," joins Steven Bartlett to explain what he sees as the connecting thread behind Greenland, Venezuela, Iran, Ukraine, and Taiwan: the final collapse of the post-WWII, post-1991 "rules-based order." Kisin argues international law was always a shared myth with no enforcer beyond the most powerful state, and that Trump is simply acting on that reality — pursuing raw American interest (the Monroe Doctrine revived) in a world where "we are not going to play by the fake rules anymore that no one else is playing by anyway."[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]

In his telling, the West invited this moment by growing weak and comfortable after 1991: Russia's invasion of Ukraine, October 7th, and China's open talk of taking Taiwan were all tests of a West that had lost its focus, moral authority (eroded by Iraq and Afghanistan), and military strength. He is especially scathing about Europe and Britain — citing Europe's outsized welfare spending, Germany's abandoned nuclear power, Britain's record peacetime tax burden and de-industrialization — and argues Britain has become "irrelevant," no longer consulted on major decisions like the strikes on Iran.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]

The conversation ranges into the domestic consequences: the rise of socialism among young people priced out of housing, AI and robotics as an accelerant of job losses and extremism, the flight of entrepreneurs from the UK, and Kisin's prescription — economic growth, controlled immigration and full integration, military rebuilding, alliance with the US, and more children — delivered with his self-described "accelerationist" belief that things must get visibly worse before the culture changes.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]

# Key points

- Kisin frames history as three phases — Cold War bipolarity (1945–1991), the American unipolar moment, and now a disintegrating framework — arguing the West "lost its focus and sense of purpose" after defeating the Soviet Union and took its eye off the ball.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- He argues international law, like money in Harari's "Sapiens," is a shared fiction with no enforcement mechanism beyond the strongest power, so Trump's actions in Venezuela (removing Maduro), Greenland, and Iran are recognition of an "every man for himself" world rather than its cause.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- On nuclear weapons, both host and guest note the pattern that superpowers only target non-nuclear states; Kisin warns that failing to properly back Ukraine teaches smaller countries that nuclear weapons are "the only guarantee of security," creating a serious proliferation danger.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- His indictment of Europe: 12% of the world's population, 25% of its GDP, and 60% of its welfare spending — comfort that enabled "economic suicide" via net zero, Germany's reliance on Russian gas, and Britain's highest industrial electricity prices in the developed world.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- On Britain specifically, he says debt interest payments run 1.5 times the defense budget, debt-to-GDP exceeds 100%, GDP per capita is lower than in 2006, and the country holds its highest peacetime tax burden in history — making it "irrelevant" to American decision-making.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- He links the rise of socialism (exemplified by Mamdani's New York win) to housing unaffordability, "elite overproduction" of graduates without matching jobs, and AI anxiety — and admits that in a world where robots create all wealth for 50 owners, "I'm like 100% on board with communism," since redistribution would happen "voluntarily or at the end of bayonets."[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- The pair discuss Elon Musk's claim that Optimus robots will outperform the best human surgeons within about three years at scale; Kisin discounts the timeline for salesmanship but believes the disruption arrives within ten years, making rigid career training obsolete in favor of adaptability.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- On Iran's uprising (with death-toll estimates the host cites at 2,000–18,000), Kisin sympathizes with protesters but warns against encouraging them without full Western backing, fearing a repeat of Ukraine-style "rah-rah" rhetoric followed by abandonment, and stresses the unsolved "what comes after" problem seen in Libya, Iraq, Egypt, and Syria.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- On the UK exodus of wealth creators, the host calculates that Revolut founder Nik Storonsky's departure represents roughly £3 billion in lost capital-gains tax — equal to the annual income tax of about 430,000–450,000 average UK taxpayers — while Kisin notes the top 10% already pay about 60% of income tax and the top 1% about 30%.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- Kisin's fix for Britain: abandon net zero, cut business taxes, reform an "unsustainable but uncuttable" welfare system, control immigration and fully integrate arrivals, rebuild the military, anchor the US alliance, and raise birth rates — arguing mass immigration has masked per-capita decline the way adding non-earning in-laws inflates household income.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- Reviewing historical multipolar eras (ancient Greece, 19th-century Europe, Warring States China), he endorses the pattern of weakening rules, regional wars, arms races, and eventually a new hegemon — while hoping nuclear deterrence and the self-preservation instinct break the cycle this time.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
- He warns the right against its own "woke right" — an identitarian victimhood movement exemplified in his view by figures like Nick Fuentes — arguing that tolerating extremists would discredit conservatives with moderates exactly as woke excess discredited the left.[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]

# Takeaways

Kisin's core message is that the world has reverted to its historical norm of great-power competition, and the West's choice is between rebuilding real strength — economic, military, demographic, cultural — or accelerating irrelevance and instability. He judges Trump "a good thing for America" and potentially a useful shock for Europe if it wakes up, describes himself as unhopeful but not despondent about Britain, and closes with the Thomas Sowell-inspired plea that decades of choosing "what feels good" over "what works" must end: "If we can make that adjustment and get back to reality, the world's our oyster."[^the-man-warning-the-west-trump-is-changing-the-w-nJeU72Rgjh4]
