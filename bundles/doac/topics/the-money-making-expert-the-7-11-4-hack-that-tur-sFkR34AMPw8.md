# Overview

Steven Bartlett hosts serial entrepreneur and author Daniel Priestley, who has built multiple businesses over 20 years and says he has mentored over 3,500 (elsewhere he cites 5,500) companies. Priestley's core thesis is that the industrial age is being replaced by the digital age just as the industrial age replaced the agricultural/feudal system, and that society — schools, governments, careers — is still running on obsolete industrial-era rules. This, he argues, is why a whole generation feels invisible, stuck, and detached from meaning: they were trained for twelve years for a world that no longer exists.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]

His prescription is to build a personal brand based on unique intellectual property and attach it to a scalable, "elegant" business model. He explains the 7-11-4 principle — people need 7 hours of content, 11 interactions, across 4 platforms to feel they know you — as the mechanism for building parasocial relationships at scale, and offers frameworks for standing out (the five things the brain won't delete), introducing yourself (name, same, fame, aim, game), testing demand before building supply (waitlists, Facebook ad tests, discussion groups, assessments), and the founder's five P's: pitch, publish, product, profile, partnerships.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]

The conversation ranges across the 2024 "long-form unscripted era" in politics and marketing, the entrepreneurial pyramid from skilled labor up to financial assets, bell-curve versus power-law opportunities, AI as the new electricity, UK tax policy and millionaire emigration, and Priestley's closing ideas: appreciate "the mountain you're already standing on," fall in love with repetition, and remember that environment dictates performance.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]

# Key points

- The guest advises would-be entrepreneurs (like the 21-year-olds Bartlett met in Cape Town) to start with an "entrepreneur apprenticeship": work in a team of fewer than 12 people with direct contact with an entrepreneur who has a personal brand (5,000–50,000 followers) and an inspiring business model, before attempting 90-day open-and-shut side hustles of their own — as he did with nightclub parties and door-to-door Valentine's roses.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- He stresses the move from consumer to creator via publishing: only about 3% of LinkedIn's billion users publish regularly and less than 1% weekly, and only about 4% of YouTube's 2.7 billion users have accounts — so the creation side is far less crowded than people assume.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- The 7-11-4 rule: because Dunbar's numbers cap how many people we remember (roughly 1,500 total, 150 well), you must clock up 7 hours of watch/read/listen time, 11 interactions, on 4 platforms for someone to feel they know you — the basis of parasocial relationships at scale. His test: could a stranger fill an entire day consuming your on-message content online?[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- The brain deletes almost everything, but five things survive: scary, strange, sexy, free value (genuinely valuable and beautifully packaged), and familiar — with "free and familiar" being the two anyone can apply.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- He argues 2024's US election began the "long-form unscripted era": by his numbers Trump did roughly 40 hours of podcast watch time earning 124 million views versus Kamala Harris's about 7 hours and a few million, and he predicts big-company CEOs will soon clamber onto long-form podcasts because it's the key to talent, customers, and investors.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- Test demand, not supply: productize the demo and a "customer needs analysis," run waitlist campaigns with situational-model questions (where are you now, where do you want to be, what's in the way, what's your path of least resistance), and prototype with Facebook ads — Bartlett ran 70 book-title ads and saw click rates from 0.3% to 15% for about £200. Priestley cites clients who built businesses from a 23,000-member fertility discussion group and a 400-person family-office WhatsApp group representing over $10 billion.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- He claims audience wealth follows a fractal: the top 1% hold 15% of spending budget and the top 10% hold 60%, so the emerging model is giving free value to the bottom 90% while monetizing the top 10% with rare, special, high-priced offerings — and being careful not to let the noisy 90% drag pricing down.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- His business taxonomy: the "lifestyle boutique" (6–12 people, £2–3 million revenue, potentially £1 million profit, maximum fulfillment) versus the "performance business" (~30 people, built to sell for $10–100 million), which requires genuinely geeking out on business and technology.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- He distinguishes bell-curve opportunities (e.g., NHS doctors' salaries) from power-law opportunities enabled by leverage — fame, capital, databases, distribution, partnerships — citing Ali Abdaal's jump from NHS doctor to YouTuber; 70% of the world now has fast internet versus 28% in 2010, so everyone is either competing with the world or selling to it.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- The entrepreneurial pyramid: escape the skilled-labor model (selling time) by climbing through intellectual property and media, then data and software, then financial assets — he cites Stephen Covey turning reflections into a book that sold 20 million copies and a $400 million consulting company, and argues wealth is created "from figments of imagination" (his startup Book Magic raising £400,000 for 10% instantly created £3.6 million of new value).[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- He argues AI is at the "power station, not toasters" stage — like electricity, a 100-year transition — and points to Nvidia's $3,000 supercomputer as the basis for future 10-person, billion-dollar companies; he claims 50% of Western economies are owned by baby boomers (aged 61–79), making buying, disrupting, or selling to boomer businesses the biggest opportunity.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- On tax and geography, he argues low taxes collect more revenue in a mobile digital world, notes 10,000 millionaires left the UK last year, criticizes the cut of entrepreneurs' relief and the 15% employment tax, and says governments defined by geography "miss the point" of a digital economy; wealth inequality, he claims, is really a technology-adoption gap — a runner racing a cyclist.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
- His closing frameworks: 78% of Bartlett's last 117 guests were authors, and writing a book is "relationships on steroids"; marketing is no longer a funnel but a "messy middle" playground of explore-and-evaluate loops (per Google's research); a key person of influence with 5,000–10,000 followers in a high-value niche can out-sell influencers with millions; and "environment dictates performance" — change your environment (courses, accelerators, people a few steps ahead) and hard things become normal.[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]

# Takeaways

The guest's argument is that the rules of economic success have flipped: schools still train "standardized component labor" for an industrial economy while the money now flows to individuals who publish ideas, build parasocial trust through 7-11-4, test demand before supply, and climb from selling time to owning intellectual property, data, software, and equity. His parting counsel is psychological rather than tactical — appreciate the "mountain of value" you already stand on, commit to repetition (get to video 1,000) because exponential growth means each doubling eclipses everything before it, and engineer your environment, since it dictates performance. He closes with a personal health scare that reframed his optimization habits: "a healthy man has many goals, a sick man has only one."[^the-money-making-expert-the-7-11-4-hack-that-tur-sFkR34AMPw8]
