# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, host Steven Bartlett speaks with psychiatrist and brain-imaging specialist Dr. Daniel Amen, who says he has scanned around 260,000 brains — including those of Justin Bieber, Miley Cyrus, Kendall Jenner, and Bartlett himself — using SPECT imaging, which measures blood flow and activity in the brain.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY] Amen's central argument is that the brain is the organ behind every decision, mood, and relationship — "when it works right, you work right" — and that most mental-health struggles reflect the physical functioning of the brain, which psychiatry rarely examines directly.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]

Much of the conversation catalogues what Amen says damages the brain: alcohol (which he says produces a shrivelled, "scalloped" scan appearance and suppresses new stem-cell growth in the hippocampus), marijuana, pornography and other high-dopamine stimuli, contact sports, sugar-heavy diets, chronic negative thinking, and excess weight.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY] He is sharply critical of what he sees as psychiatry's fashion for psychedelics and of prescribing SSRIs without ever looking at the organ being treated, comparing a depression diagnosis to "chest pain" — a symptom with many causes that each need different treatment.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]

The episode closes on parenting and hope: Amen argues the key parenting mistake is doing too much for children — "if you do too much for your kids, you build your self-esteem by stealing theirs" — and prescribes daily "special time," active listening, and never rewarding tantrums.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY] His overarching prescription is a single question applied to every habit: "Is this good for my brain or bad for it?"[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]

# Key points

- The guest says protecting the brain until age 25 is critical because the prefrontal cortex is not fully developed before then; high-dopamine stimuli — pornography, gaming, gambling, cocaine, fame — repeatedly hit the nucleus accumbens and "deaden" the dopamine response, which he says is especially damaging to developing brains.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On alcohol, Amen cites the US Surgeon General's push for cancer warning labels, the American Cancer Society's position that any alcohol raises the risk of seven cancers, and a Spanish study finding even light drinkers had disrupted white matter — "potholes" in the brain's highways.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- He references a 2009 monkey study showing alcohol produced a 58% decline in new brain-cell development and a 63% reduction in new-cell survival, and figures discussed on his podcast that REM sleep falls to roughly an hour after two drinks, 30 minutes after four, and under two minutes after six.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- Amen calls the idea that marijuana is innocuous "a lie," citing his own study of 1,000 users showing lower activity in every brain area and a JAMA study of over 1,000 users showing reduced hippocampal activity; teenage use, he says, predicts higher anxiety, depression, and suicide in the twenties.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- He is skeptical of the psychedelic revival ("the street drugs of the '60s are coming back"), claiming psilocybin-associated psychosis has risen 300% in recent years, and argues clinicians should scan brains to find the cause of depression rather than fly blind — noting he treated Kendall Jenner's post-COVID anxiety as brain inflammation with an anti-inflammatory protocol.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On antidepressants, he claims 85% of US psychiatric drugs are prescribed by non-psychiatrists in 7-minute visits, and that head-to-head studies show saffron (25 randomized controlled trials, with sexual function enhanced rather than impaired), brisk walking 45 minutes four times a week, omega-3s, and learning to challenge negative thoughts are each as effective as SSRIs.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- Amen teaches patients to kill "ANTs" (automatic negative thoughts) with a five-question process adapted from Byron Katie — Is it true? Is it absolutely true? How does the thought make me feel and act? How would I feel without it? Turn it to its opposite — and cites his study linking negativity bias to lower prefrontal activity, plus a claim that people who watch morning news are 27% less happy that afternoon.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On trauma, he describes his study of 21,000 people separating PTSD from traumatic brain injury on scans, and the world's largest childhood-trauma (ACE) study: four or more adverse childhood experiences raise the risk of seven of the top ten causes of death, and six or more predict dying roughly 20 years earlier; yet only about 10% of people develop PTSD after trauma while another 10% show post-traumatic growth, as with kidnapping survivor Elizabeth Smart, whose brain he found healthy.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- His "brain reserve" concept — extra tissue to absorb life's stresses — starts before conception via epigenetics and maternal health (the brain begins developing around day 21 of pregnancy), and explains why two soldiers in the same blast can have completely different outcomes.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On Alzheimer's, he offers the "BRIGHT MINDS" mnemonic of 11 risk factors and cites a Mayo Clinic study: a fat-based diet cut Alzheimer's risk 42%, a protein-based diet 21%, while a simple-carbohydrate diet raised risk 400%; depression doubles a woman's risk and quadruples a man's, and he notes half of people 85 and older have the disease.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On parenting, he prescribes 20 minutes of daily "special time" doing what the child wants with no commands or questions, active listening, and the rule "if you have a tantrum to get your way, the answer's no" — while warning that rescuing children from struggle steals the self-esteem that competence builds.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
- On ADHD, he says it is real and genetic but its expression is amplified by sugar, red dye 40, screens, and indoor life; untreated it carries risks like school failure, incarceration, bankruptcy, and divorce, and he recounts how a tiny dose of Ritalin "normalized" his own daughter's brain and turned her B-and-C record into a decade of straight A's.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]

# Takeaways

The guest's through-line is "brain envy": you have to want a better brain before you will care for it, and he claims even damaged brains — 80% of the NFL players he treated — can measurably improve within months of stopping harmful habits and adopting protective ones (exercise, racket sports like padel, new learning, meditation such as the 12-minute Kirtan Kriya, omega-3s, vitamin D, and purpose).[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY] His parting advice, for individuals and prospective parents alike, is to run every choice through one question — is this good for my brain or bad for it? — because, in his view, optimizing the brain optimizes everything downstream of it.[^the-no-1-brain-doctor-this-parenting-mistake-rui-bOi_Y4ythYY]
