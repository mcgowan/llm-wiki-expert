# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Joseph Allen, a doctor of optometry, fellow of the American Academy of Optometry and diplomate of the American Board of Optometry, who runs a large eye-education presence on social media.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] His central argument inverts the popular anxiety about screens: the blue light emitted by phones and laptops, he says, has consistently failed to show any increase in the risk of ageing eye diseases and no proven effect on eye strain, while the genuinely damaging thing about devices is how close we hold them and how much time we spend indoors doing close work.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] He treats sunlight as the more powerful actor — good for the developing eye when experienced outdoors, dangerous when stared at directly.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]

Much of the episode is anatomical explanation with a model eye: how the crystalline lens gains a layer every year until it stiffens into presbyopia and then clouds into cataract; how the eyeball elongating by a single millimetre shifts a spectacle prescription by roughly three dioptres; how glaucoma kills the irreversible ganglion cells of the optic nerve under pressure; how the vitreous gel's collagen clumps cast the shadows people see as floaters.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] Allen repeatedly frames the eye as an extension of the brain and as a diagnostic window: he says over 270 systemic and vision conditions can be caught in a routine eye exam, and tells the story of a healthy 20-year-old whose Roth spot led to blood work, an immune thrombocytopenic purpura diagnosis and three days in intensive care.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]

He is notably cautious about the wellness products the host raises. He debunks the carrot myth as British wartime propaganda, calls the pineapple-for-floaters study methodologically weak, warns that consumer red light panels are poorly standardised and have caused documented eye damage, and says myopia cannot be reversed — people who claim otherwise are usually selling something or experiencing pseudomyopia from relaxed eye muscles.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] His positive recommendations are unglamorous: an annual eye exam, a Mediterranean-style diet, outdoor time for children, UV protection, and moving the phone further from the face.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]

# Key points

- Citing Holden's 2016 paper in *Ophthalmology*, Allen says about 30% of the world is currently nearsighted and roughly 50% will be by 2050; in the US around 42% of 5-to-19-year-olds are already myopic with 50% expected by 2030, and parts of East Asia such as Japan are already at 80–90%.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- He puts genetics at no more than about 30% of myopia risk, attributing the rest to lifestyle: urbanisation, earlier and heavier education, tablets as "babysitter 2.0", and time spent indoors.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- The physical mechanism is eyeball elongation toward the orbit — 1 mm of growth shifts a prescription by about three dioptres and 2 mm reaches severe myopia, which raises the risk of glaucoma, cataracts, retinal detachment and myopic maculopathy.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- Studies converge on roughly 90 minutes to two hours outdoors a day to offset close work; he cites a Taiwanese policy that has coincided with a decade of falling childhood myopia onset, while noting a recent meta-analysis found the effect on progression (as opposed to onset) not statistically significant, and that survey-based data is now being replaced by light-tracking wearables.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- Myopia cannot be reversed, he says, because there is no way to shorten the eyeball; people who report reversing it usually had pseudomyopia — a ciliary-muscle spasm from prolonged close focus that relaxes with outdoor time — and eye exercises cannot strengthen that muscle, though vision therapy can retrain brain-muscle coordination in binocular disorders like convergence insufficiency.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- On blue light he distinguishes sunlight's high-energy blue, which may drive ageing changes in the eye, from screen blue light, which he says lacks the power and shows no association with ageing eye disease or eye strain; he attributes reported benefits of blue light glasses to placebo or to the anti-glare coatings usually applied to them, while accepting blue light does affect sleep.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- He highlights Apple's 2023 "Screen Distance" feature (under Settings, Screen Time), which warns when a device is held too close and recommends 30 cm, noting people average about 8 inches — and that simply moving a phone twice as far away cuts blue light exposure by 75%.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- On dark circles and under-eye bags, he says studies from the last five years found poor sleep and stress worsened people's *subjective* view of their own appearance while objective measurements of eyelid pigmentation did not change; real contributors are skin pigmentation, sun exposure, allergies causing vascular dilation, orbital shadowing and salt drawing fluid into the tissue.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- Cold compresses and cucumber work by constricting blood vessels but should be limited to 10–15 minutes, since longer can rebound into more inflammation; eye creams that remove pigment take months of two-to-three-times-daily use, and beyond that there are light therapies, hyaluronic acid fillers and blepharoplasty.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- He warns against sun gazing outright, describing a current patient with permanent solar retinopathy — burn holes in the retina — because the cornea and lens focus sunlight onto the macula, the "bullseye" responsible for sharp vision, and can cause thermochemical damage within seconds.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- On red light therapy he reports evidence for dry eye (including a 2022 study of 20 participants treated twice weekly for three weeks) and for macular degeneration, where a combined red, near-infrared and yellow light protocol is approved in Europe, in FDA trials in the US, and has been shown to shrink protein deposits, slow progression and in some cases restore sight — but he cautions that consumer devices are poorly standardised in wavelength and power density and have caused documented eye damage.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- Citing an *American Journal of Nutrition* paper drawing on the AREDS cohort (around 4,000 people over nine years), he says 2.7 or more servings of green leafy vegetables a week cut the risk of progressing to advanced macular degeneration by 25%, two servings of oily fish a week by 21%, and both together by 41%.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- He debunks the carrots-for-night-vision belief as Second World War British propaganda intended to hide radar from Germany, noting vitamin A does matter for photoreceptors but deficiency is rare outside malnourished populations, and names green leafy vegetables — for lutein and zeaxanthin — as his top eye food.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- On omega-3, he notes DHA makes up 50–60% of the fatty acid content of photoreceptors and that oily-fish diets track with less macular degeneration and diabetic retinopathy, but supplements have not replicated the result; early mouse research points to the MFSD2A transporter and lysophospholipid DHA (found at up to 30% in krill and 35–70% in fish roe) as the more bioavailable form. His own 90-day self-experiment took his omega-3 blood level from about 4.7% toward a 9.5% result against an 8–12% target, with improved dry eye symptoms — which he stresses is an n of 1.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- On floaters he says the chance rises roughly 10% per decade of life (about 80% by age 80); the 2019 *Journal of American Science* pineapple study claiming a 75% improvement from three slices a day is not regarded as scientifically robust, while a 2021 placebo-controlled trial of an enzyme and vitamin formulation reduced symptoms after six months. A sudden shower of floaters, a lightning-bolt flash or a descending dark shadow warrants urgent review, since roughly 5% of cases involve a retinal tear that a laser can tack down before detachment.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
- Smaller diagnoses he covers: eyelid twitching (myokymia) as a stress, sleep-deprivation and caffeine threshold — historically nicknamed "medical student's twitching eye"; glaucoma as irreversible ganglion cell death driven by intraocular pressure, likened to a bike tyre bursting at its weakest point; and diabetes as devastating for the heavily vascularised retina, sometimes first detected as an unexplained two-step prescription change from lens swelling.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]

# Takeaways

The guest's closing message is that the eye is an extension of the brain, so what is good for the eyes is also good for the heart and brain — diet, exercise, sleep, hydration and time outdoors all ripple through it, and he notes that poor vision is associated with greater later risk of dementia and Alzheimer's.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] His single strongest recommendation is an annual eye exam even for people who believe they see perfectly, on the grounds that it is one of the least invasive ways to detect the widest range of conditions, and that by the time patients notice something is wrong it is often too late.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4] Throughout, he is careful to separate settled findings from early research — flagging red light therapy, lysophospholipid DHA and floater supplements as promising but immature, and urging listeners not to buy consumer devices for their eyes without a specialist's guidance.[^the-no-1-eye-doctor-theyre-lying-to-you-about-bl-SWjzjClBCO4]
