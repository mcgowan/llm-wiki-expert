# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Will Bulsiewicz ("Dr. B"), a gastroenterologist trained at Georgetown and Northwestern, author of *Fiber Fueled*, and US medical director at Zoe, who argues that the West is living through an epidemic of gut health problems and that the missing piece of the puzzle in almost every chronic condition is the gut microbiome.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] His framing is that a human being is a "superorganism": roughly 38 trillion mostly bacterial microbes, concentrated in the colon, that co-evolved with humans over a billion years and were handed to us at birth through the birth canal, and to whom we have outsourced digestion, immune training, metabolism, hormone regulation and a large share of mood and cognition.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]

The mechanism he keeps returning to is short-chain fatty acids — butyrate, acetate, propionate — which gut microbes produce when they ferment fibre and resistant starch, and which he calls the most healing and most anti-inflammatory molecules he has come across.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] In his account these postbiotics rebuild the single-cell epithelial barrier that separates 38 trillion microbes from 70% of the immune system, calm the immune response, improve insulin sensitivity and fat burning, and cross the blood-brain barrier to influence mood and focus; when the barrier breaks down, bacterial endotoxin leaks into the bloodstream and drives the chronic inflammation he sees behind twenty-first-century disease.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] Alcohol, he argues, is a direct assault on this system — he claims a hangover is microbiome damage rather than dehydration.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]

Much of the episode is spent making gut health observable without a lab: Bulsiewicz walks the host through model stool samples on the Bristol stool scale, explains what colour changes mean, and presents Zoe's "blue poo" work on gut transit time.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] His prescription is additive rather than restrictive — 30 different plants a week, fermented foods, and his "F GOALS" framework — and he is explicit that the biggest misconception in the gut health space is that people restrict quickly and add back slowly.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] Both men disclose commercial interests: the host is an investor in Zoe, and the guest sells a prebiotic powder called 38 Tera launching in mid-January.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]

# Key points

- Bulsiewicz says a single thumb carries as many microbes as the UK has people, and the colon holds about 38 trillion, mostly bacteria plus fungi, viruses and parasites; while human genetic codes are 99.99% identical, identical twins share only about 25% of their microbes.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He argues the gut barrier is a single layer of cells held by tight junctions, with 70% of the immune system on one side and 38 trillion microbes on the other; it regenerates every three to four days and microbes are its "stewards", so a broken barrier means perpetual immune activation and chronic inflammation.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- Citing Warren David's 2014 *Nature* study, which swung participants between fully plant-based and fully animal-based diets for five days, he says dietary changes shift the microbiome within 24 hours — and notes microbes replicate roughly every 20 minutes, so one microbe becomes at least a thousand in a day.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- The "30 plants a week" target comes from the American Gut Project (with the British Gut Project a major component), associated with Tim Spector; he says the Western average is around 10–15, that the number is a spectrum rather than a threshold, and that going from 10 to 15 is worth celebrating.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He cites a Stanford randomized controlled trial in which adding fermented food to the diets of non-consumers increased gut microbiome diversity after eight weeks, and explains fermentation as controlled spoilage that yields probiotics (live microbes), prebiotics (unlocked microbe food) and postbiotics (compounds microbes make) in a single jar of sauerkraut.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- On the fibre deficit he says 95% of Americans fall short, with the average woman eating 15 g against a 25 g target and the average man 18 g against 38 g, and that only about 10% of American calories come from fibre-containing plants.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He points to Andrew Reynolds' compilation of the available fibre data, which he calls one of his favourite studies: higher fibre intake is associated with weight loss, fewer heart attacks and strokes, lower risk of diabetes and multiple cancers, lower blood pressure, better blood sugar and lower cholesterol — with risk falling further for each additional 5 g of fibre, including for colorectal cancer.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- Zoe's "blue poo" study, published in the journal *Gut*, used blue-dyed muffins to measure gut transit time: under 14 hours is very fast, over 58 hours very slow, with most people at 24 or 48 hours; the follow-up "Big Poo Review" surveyed 142,000 people across the UK and found the average person poos 1.7 times a day, one in five is constipated and one in six has diarrhoea.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- On the Bristol stool scale — derived from a 1990s study of fewer than 2,000 people in Bristol — he places the ideal at type four (smooth sausage), calls types one and two constipation and six and seven diarrhoea, and says the Big Poo Review associated type four with higher intake of legumes, whole grains, fruit and vegetables while both extremes were linked to low fibre.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- His stool-colour guidance: brown comes from bile, white suggests a blockage stopping bile reaching the intestine, yellow with an oil slick suggests undigested fat (for example chronic pancreatitis), green can come from giardia or heavy green smoothies, and black can be Pepto-Bismol or a slow upper GI bleed that is tarry and foul-smelling; any blood in the stool, he says, is grounds to see a doctor.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He warns colon cancer is the number two cause of cancer death in the US and UK with 150,000 US diagnoses a year, and is shifting younger — the host's generation, he says, is four times more likely to be diagnosed with rectal cancer and twice as likely with colon cancer than his own parents' generation.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- On transplant research he notes fecal transplants have treated C. diff for over a decade but have largely failed for ulcerative colitis — except in one small study where the patients who reached remission all shared the same donor — and describes mouse work in which human poo transfers body type (even on identical calories), diabetes, colitis and anxiety, depression or PTSD phenotypes.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He is sceptical of calories-in-calories-out and of GLP-1 drugs for non-medical use, arguing restriction slows metabolism and triggers rebound weight gain with muscle permanently lost, and that a high-prebiotic diet stimulates the body's own GLP-1 and peptide YY so people can eat to fullness without restriction; he adds that healthier microbiomes excrete more calories.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- On the gut-brain axis he cites 95% of the body's serotonin and 50% of dopamine produced in the gut, over 30 neurotransmitters, the vagus nerve as a direct "phone line", and short-chain fatty acids crossing into the brain — plus a study of adopted two-year-olds whose early trauma showed up in their microbiome, stress response and fMRI brain activity, and his clinical view that healing trauma is sometimes what finally heals the gut.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- His "F GOALS" framework is fruit and fermented, greens and grains (unrefined), omega-3 super seeds (chia, flax, hemp, walnuts), aromatics (onions, garlic, shallots), legumes — which he calls the number one superfood — and shrooms, seaweed and sprouts, noting broccoli sprouts contain 50 to 100 times more of the cancer-fighting compound sulforaphane than adult broccoli.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
- He describes Justin Sonnenburg's Stanford mouse work showing that a low-fibre diet costs microbial diversity that compounds across generations, and that adding fibre back restores some but never all of what was lost — and cites research that people sharing a home have healthier microbiomes and swap more microbes, with the closest couples sharing the most even after controlling for diet.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]

# Takeaways

The guest's closing prescription is deliberately cheap and additive: eat a wide variety of plants — fruits, vegetables, whole grains, seeds, nuts and legumes, with coffee counting too — add fermented food, exercise outdoors, sleep well, and prioritise human connection, since several of these cost nothing.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] He frames the gut as a trainable muscle: people who cannot tolerate fibre should "start low and go slow" over weeks or months rather than concluding the food is the problem, because microbes only get good at digesting what they are given practice on.[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8] Listeners should note that several of the strongest claims — poo transplants as future daily "super probiotics", the microbiome as a matchmaker behind sexual attraction — are presented by him as speculation or as animal research awaiting human confirmation, and that both host and guest have financial stakes in products discussed (Zoe and the guest's 38 Tera prebiotic).[^the-no-1-poo-gut-scientist-if-your-poo-looks-lik-qqabbfk9wV8]
