# Overview

Dr Tyna Moore — a naturopathic physician and chiropractor who took over her late mentor's musculoskeletal and regenerative-injection practice — tells host Steven Bartlett that she treats people rather than diagnoses, and that metabolic dysfunction is the root-cause driver behind most of the lifestyle diseases she sees[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. Her central claim in this episode is that semaglutide and the wider GLP-1 family are being misunderstood on both sides of the argument: not the evil weight-loss shortcut the 2023 media cycle described, but also not something that should be escalated to the doses at which it is routinely prescribed[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].

Moore's route into the subject was personal. She describes spending most of 2021 flat on her back with undiagnosed psoriatic arthritis, feeling her spine fusing and thinking by year's end that if it did not improve she would kill herself[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. Searching for anything that calmed neuroinflammation, she says she typed "GLP-1 and neural inflammation" into Google in May 2023, found a literature she says contradicted the public narrative, and started herself on a fraction of the standard starting dose — reporting brain clarity within days, anxiety dropping away, and significant pain reduction within two weeks[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. She then reports similar results in her mother, her daughter, her husband and her patients.

The rest of the conversation lays out her dosing philosophy and her wider diagnosis of why people are sick. She treats semaglutide as a hormone to be supplemented at the lowest effective dose, capped, cycled and never used as monotherapy, in contrast to the standard sixteen-week escalation she says throws already-fragile patients over the edge into gallstones, pancreatitis and depression[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. She frames the drug as a "window of opportunity" that quiets dopamine-driven noise long enough for someone to hardwire the six lifestyle pillars she considers non-negotiable — strength training, protein, walking and daylight, sleep, mindset and heat[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].

# Key points

- Moore explains that GLP-1 is a peptide the body makes in the L cells of the small intestine and also in the brain, and that semaglutide is essentially bioidentical to it with lipids added to lengthen the half-life; she argues that people with obesity, type 2 diabetes and fatty liver are GLP-1 deficient, and suspects chronic gut inflammation leaves many others under-producing it too[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].
- She describes the standard protocol as starting at 0.25 mg weekly and roughly doubling every four weeks over sixteen weeks to around 2.5 mg — a tenfold increase — with no breaks, no attention to receptor sensitivity, and an assumption of lifelong use; her own approach gives a fraction of 0.25 mg, drawn with an insulin syringe, often never escalated, and cycled so receptors can resensitise[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].
- Her stated dosing rule is to keep patients "just below symptoms" — no nausea, no vomiting — and she says one obese patient took nine months to reach 1 mg, a level she is reluctant to raise because she wants lifestyle change doing the metabolic healing[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].
- On the cardiovascular evidence she cites the SELECT trial published at the end of 2023 — Novo Nordisk-sponsored but, in her view, well conducted — which found a 20% reduction in severe cardiovascular events in overweight people in their forties without type 2 diabetes, and she stresses the reanalysis showing the benefit held independent of weight loss[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].
- She points to the FLOW chronic kidney disease trial being stopped early because the effect was strong enough that withholding the drug from the control group became an ethical problem, and to data she says shows regeneration of pancreatic beta cells in type 1 diabetics treated early enough — something she says nothing but stem cells otherwise achieves[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].

# Takeaways

Moore's argument is that the public fight over Ozempic has been conducted at the wrong dose. She contends the peptide's anti-inflammatory, neuroregenerative and metabolic effects show up at fractions of the prescribed amount, while most of the horror stories track the sixteen-week escalation, the crushed appetite and the rapid weight loss rather than the molecule[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. Her framing is that the drug buys a window — quieter dopamine noise, clearer cognition, restored agency — that is only worth having if the person spends it on strength training, protein, daylight, sleep and community, and she is candid that much of what she describes is her own clinical observation and early or correlative data rather than settled evidence[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y]. Both she and Bartlett close by telling listeners to check sources, talk to a qualified doctor and not act on a podcast[^the-ozempic-expert-ozempic-transforms-your-gut-m-XDcESvzX58Y].
