# Overview

Gastroenterologist Dr. Will Bulsiewicz returns to The Diary Of A CEO to argue that chronic low-grade inflammation is "the health story of our time" — an always-on immune system that quietly drives fatigue, poor sleep, skin issues, brain fog, and some 130 health conditions. His model has three layers of defense: the 38 trillion microbes of the gut microbiome, the single-cell-thick gut barrier they feed and repair (rebuilt every 3-5 days), and the immune system itself. When the modern lifestyle damages the microbes, the barrier leaks, the immune system attacks what sneaks across, and the body ends up in a "forever war" of chronic inflammation.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]

Bulsiewicz illustrates the microbiome's power with fecal transplants: a patient with life-threatening C. diff colitis who was saved overnight when he delivered a donor's stool by scope, and studies where transplants from immunotherapy responders roughly doubled melanoma treatment success and produced durable improvement in Parkinson's symptoms — a disease he says begins in the gut, with constipation preceding diagnosis. He also dissects everyday complaints — constipation as the hidden number-one cause of bloating and gas, "gluten intolerance" as largely fructan intolerance, and glyphosate-sprayed US wheat as a reason people tolerate pasta in Italy but not at home.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]

The prescriptive core is his list of four things missing from modern diets — fiber, polyphenols, healthy fats, and fermented foods — plus an hour-by-hour "perfect gut day" built on circadian consistency: morning hydration and prebiotic fiber, sunlight and light exercise for a cortisol peak, meals spaced four hours apart, an afternoon workout window, dimmed evening lights, and a consistent bedtime. He closes with the roles of loneliness, trauma, and the brain-gut axis in inflammation, and the personal story of reconciling with his father before his death.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]

# Key points

- Bulsiewicz frames inflammation as the immune system stuck "on" 24/7: gut microbes are the first line of defense, the gut barrier (which regenerates every 3-5 days) the second, and when it leaks — "leaky gut" — immune activation and cytokine cascades follow, with neuroinflammation linked to depression, Alzheimer's, and Parkinson's.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- He notes that 60% of stool weight is microbial, and describes performing an emergency fecal transplant on a delirious patient with antibiotic-induced C. diff colitis — the alternative was removing her colon; she was normal by the next day and home in two.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- He cites melanoma studies where fecal transplants from immunotherapy responders roughly doubled the number of patients beating cancer, and Parkinson's trials showing durable movement benefits a year after transplant — while warning strongly against DIY at-home fecal transplants.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- Constipation is his number-one hidden culprit: many people doing "partial poops" several times a day are still constipated, and stalled stool gives microbes unlimited time to ferment and produce gas; a DOAC audience poll found over 60% of listeners currently struggle with some gut problem.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- On gluten: he cites a Gastroenterology journal study in which self-identified gluten-sensitive people had fewer symptoms on a gluten bar than placebo but were triggered by fructans — suggesting most "gluten intolerance" is fructan intolerance (also found in garlic and onions); sourdough fermentation reduces fructans, which is why many tolerate it.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- He argues glyphosate sprayed on non-organic US wheat depletes beneficial gut bacteria (microbes lack the human workaround to its shikimate-pathway blockade), possibly explaining why some Americans can eat pasta in Italy but not at home.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- Antibiotics double the risk of developing inflammatory bowel disease within a year, and US ulcerative colitis and Crohn's cases rose by up to 55% from 1970 to 2010 — diseases he describes as the immune system rejecting the microbiome, rare in non-industrialized countries.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- Fiber (deficient in 95% of Americans and 90% of Britons) feeds microbes that produce short-chain fatty acids — acetate, propionate, and butyrate — which he calls the most anti-inflammatory compounds he has ever encountered; a Finnish study of ~1,100 children found low butyrate-producer levels at 12 months predicted which kids would develop allergic diseases by age five.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- Early-life factors matter: antibiotics, bottle-feeding, and C-section birth are associated with increased risk of allergic, autoimmune, and metabolic disease, since the microbiome is essentially adult-sized by age three and breast milk contains 200+ human milk oligosaccharides that exist solely to feed the infant microbiome.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- An alcohol study changed his own drinking habits: blood lipopolysaccharide (a marker of leaky gut) tracked blood alcohol levels exactly and did not normalize until alcohol hit zero, leading him to conclude no detectable amount of alcohol is safe for the gut barrier.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- The "cheap spice" is turmeric: its polyphenol curcumin blocks a specific inflammatory cytokine, and he recommends it daily (alongside vitamin D and omega-3s in the morning; zinc and magnesium at night) for people with chronic inflammatory issues — while cautioning against overdosing supplements, and noting 95% of polyphenols require gut microbes for activation.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- His "perfect gut day" is built on circadian consistency: waking at the same time, hydration with prebiotic fiber before coffee, morning sunlight (boosting the cortisol peak ~50%) plus light exercise (another 25-50%), meals spaced 4 hours apart to protect the migrating motor complex, a 3-6pm window for vigorous workouts (never within 2 hours of bed), dimming lights after sunset (casual home lighting cuts melatonin by 30%), a 12-14 hour overnight fast, and no late-night eating.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
- He identifies four things missing from modern diets — fiber, polyphenols, healthy fats (extra virgin olive oil, avocado, nuts, omega-3s), and fermented foods (average US intake: zero) — citing the Stanford study by Gardner and the Sonnenburgs showing 8 weeks of fermented foods increased microbiome diversity and lowered inflammation; loneliness (on par with smoking for longevity) and unresolved trauma also drive inflammation via the brain-gut axis and chronically elevated cortisol.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]

# Takeaways

Bulsiewicz's message is that the gut is remarkably forgiving — microbes reproduce in as little as 20 minutes and the gut barrier rebuilds every 3-5 days — so adding what is missing (fiber, polyphenols, healthy fats, fermented foods) and living in circadian consistency can produce fast, meaningful change without extreme diets, which he dismisses as trend-driven whiplash. He stresses that healing is personal: for some the lever is diet, for others sleep, loneliness, or trauma — and his own transformation required not just changing his diet and training but forgiving and reconnecting with his estranged father before his sudden death.[^the-poo-doctor-this-cheap-spice-fixes-a-damaged-5Tr7AhkOEj4]
