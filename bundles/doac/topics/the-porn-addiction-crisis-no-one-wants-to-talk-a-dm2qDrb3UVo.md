# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett speaks with Dr. Alok Kanojia ("Dr. K"), a Harvard-trained psychiatrist who spent years studying to become a monk, about pornography addiction and the broader rise in addictive behavior. Dr. K's framing is that good diagnosis precedes good treatment: pornography addiction has multiple layers — societal, physiological, neuroscientific, psychological, and spiritual — and the fact that all addictions are rising at once signals that something fundamental has changed in society rather than in any single substance.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]

Neurologically, Dr. K explains, sexual stimulation suppresses the amygdala — the brain's emotional and survival circuitry — so pornography functions as a powerful emotional coping mechanism, which is why many addicts watch for hours without necessarily masturbating. Because dopamine ties pleasure, craving, and motivation together, each use "buys" future craving, and suppressed emotions like guilt grow over time, feeding a self-reinforcing cycle. Societally, he argues men and women are having less sex and marrying less, and porn substitutes a "slice" of what the brain craves — like calorie-dense junk food that kills the appetite for broccoli — leaving connection needs unmet while removing the motivation to pursue real relationships.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]

Dr. K goes further, describing a "mass extinction event": a generation of men aged roughly 15–50 who, after COVID eliminated the third spaces that once forced social interaction, may never form relationships or pass on their genes — a natural-selection dynamic he compares to Darwin's finches. His treatment paradigm rejects white-knuckle resistance in favor of scheduling usage, practicing urge surfing and alternate nostril breathing, understanding one's emotions, and ultimately cultivating an internal "why" — what he calls dharma — since in his clinical experience external motivations, like appeasing a partner, reliably lead to relapse.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]

# Key points

- Dr. K argues that sexual stimulation suppresses the amygdala, the brain's emotional and survival center, making porn act like "aromatherapy for your brain" — which is why many porn addicts watch for hours throughout the day without necessarily masturbating.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- He explains that dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens ties pleasure, craving, and motivation together, so every pleasurable use of porn "buys" future craving — behavioral reinforcement there is no way to escape — while suppressed emotions like guilt and shame grow, creating a neurological cycle.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- "Postnut clarity," in his account, occurs because lust circuitry literally inhibits the risk-assessing, rational parts of the brain — an evolutionary design that made humans ignore red flags in order to procreate — and once the drive is satisfied, motivation to pursue a real relationship disappears.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- The guest claims porn delivers only a slice of what the brain wants from sex — no bond, safety, or emotional connection — comparing it to filling your stomach with processed junk food so that you never crave broccoli; society, he says, is using porn as a substitute for relationships.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- Dr. K describes a "mass extinction event": men between roughly 15 and 50 who will never procreate, citing South Korea's birth rate of about 0.7, the 4B movement, incel anger, and the viral "man or bear" question as symptoms of an existential panic among men whose genes are dying out.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- He frames COVID as a natural-selection event, likening struggling men to Darwin's finches after their food source vanished: third spaces that once forced awkward men into social interaction are gone, so standard advice ("work on yourself, try harder") fails people whose attachment style, neuroticism, or circumstances don't fit the new environment — and he notes about a third of the men he sees do not know how to give or receive love.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- The two strongest variables he says correlate with pornography addiction are a sense of meaninglessness in life and lacking a real reason to stop; people conquer the addiction when purpose leaves them no time for it.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- His practical paradigm: schedule porn use to one hour a day rather than going cold turkey, log out of all devices, anticipate the emotionally hard parts of the day in advance, and practice urge surfing — waiting out cravings, which peak and disappear on their own — plus 5–15 minutes daily of alternate nostril breathing as a trained emotional-regulation alternative.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- Dr. K redefines willpower: the anterior cingulate cortex that exerts willpower is the same part of the brain that monitors internal conflict, so feeling conflicted is willpower working — "the only way an addiction can win is when you leave the ring."[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- Citing Sun Tzu ("he will win who knows when to fight and when not to fight") and lessons from opioid addiction, he warns that resisting and then caving teaches the body to escalate suffering — jumping straight to 80% intensity cravings — so you should pick battles you will definitely win, or give in early rather than cave late.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- On psychedelics, he is only cautiously optimistic: in his practice, patients harmed by psychedelics far outnumber those helped, because the drugs put the brain in neuroplastic "edit mode" — beneficial only with proper set, setting, shepherding, and integration therapy, and dangerous for people self-medicating alone.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
- He predicts AI girlfriends will evolve to be deliberately volatile — using random reinforcement schedules like loot boxes — and, discussing an MIT study Bartlett cites (where roughly 80% of ChatGPT users could not recall a sentence of the essay they had just written), he warns AI reflects users' cognitive biases back at them and atrophies skills like an elevator replacing stairs, though he says you must still use it or be outcompeted.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]

# Takeaways

Dr. K's core message is that pornography addiction is a symptom of deeper starvation — for connection, meaning, and self-understanding — and cannot be beaten by brute-force resistance or externally imposed reasons. His prescribed path runs from diagnosis (understanding your own emotions and patterns) through trained skills (urge surfing, alternate nostril breathing, staying in the ring of internal conflict) to the deeper work of finding a dharma: a why that comes from within, discovered in silence rather than borrowed from what the world tells you to want. He frames addiction itself as a kind of spiritual boot camp — those who conquer it, in his experience, emerge with an unusual capacity to handle adversity.[^the-porn-addiction-crisis-no-one-wants-to-talk-a-dm2qDrb3UVo]
