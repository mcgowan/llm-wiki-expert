# Overview

Dr Natalie Crawford is a double board-certified fertility doctor and co-founder of the Fora Fertility clinic in Austin, which she started in 2020 with her partner Amanda Skiller[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She tells host Steven Bartlett that her mission began when she realised she was meeting patients who were already behind — people without basic knowledge of how their hormones and reproductive systems worked, who repeatedly said they wished they had known earlier because they would have made different decisions[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Her central reframe is that infertility is classified as a disease by the WHO and CDC, so fertility itself should be treated as a health state and approached preventatively, the way we approach cancer or Alzheimer's risk[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].

Crawford speaks openly about her own four pregnancy losses during residency and fellowship, including an ectopic pregnancy diagnosed while her husband was away, and says her mistake was telling nobody she was pregnant — which left her unable to ask for support when the losses came[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She describes the isolation and identity crisis her patients report — "I feel like I'm stuck while everybody else is moving on because I'm broken" — and says she always tries to reframe infertility as a team diagnosis rather than one partner's failing[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].

The bulk of the episode is a mechanical walkthrough: how sperm production differs fundamentally from the fixed egg "vault", how the FSH/LH cycle works, why egg quality rather than egg quantity is the rate-limiting step in older pregnancies, and how PCOS, endometriosis, IVF, egg freezing and embryo banking fit in[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Throughout, Crawford's argument is that the traditional model is reactive — prove you have a problem before we test — and that people with known risk factors should be tested before they start trying[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].

# Key points

- Crawford says US infertility rates have risen from roughly one in eight women to one in five trying to conceive for the first time, and that the increase persists after controlling for age; she attributes it to a combination of later starts (only around 5% began families over 30 in the 1970s versus 25-30% now), rising obesity and diabetes, more autoimmune disease and inflammation, chronic stress and environmental toxins[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She cites sperm counts falling roughly 50% in 50 years, and notes the alarming detail that the decline over the past ten years ran at double the rate of the preceding forty[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Bartlett adds published figures showing the global fertility rate fell from 4.84 live births per woman in 1950 to 2.23 in 2021, projected to reach 1.59 by 2100[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- The asymmetry she opens with: men produce 200-300 million sperm a day, about 1,500 a second, on a 90-day cycle (72 days to grow across the testicle, 18 days to move through the ejaculatory system), meaning sperm can be improved in three months — whereas women are born with every egg they will ever have[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She argues saving up sperm backfires: sperm are fragile and dead sperm act like stalled cars on a freeway, obstructing the healthy ones, so abstinence beyond seven days sharply raises debris; her recommendation is ejaculation every day to every three or four days[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- On male factors she flags cigarettes, marijuana (which she says raises a partner's miscarriage risk through DNA damage in the sperm head even if she is never exposed to the smoke) and probably vaping; on phones she says the association with lower counts came mostly from 2005-2010 handsets with higher radiation, and phone location made no difference — the real risk is scrotal heat from daily saunas, hot tubs, laptops and long-distance cycling[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She says she sees a couple a week where the man was put on TRT at a men's health clinic, which she calls effectively male birth control because exogenous testosterone shuts down the brain's signal and renders men azoospermic, sometimes irreversibly[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Her "vault" analogy: a female fetus has 6-7 million eggs at five months' gestation, 1-2 million at birth, half a million at puberty, about 300,000 entering the reproductive years and fewer than a thousand at menopause (average age 51-52), while only 400-500 are ever ovulated[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Eggs leave the vault each month in proportion to how many remain — roughly 20 per month at 30, 14-15 at 35, 8-10 at 40, 3-4 at 44 — with FSH stimulating just one to ovulate, chosen at random, and the rest dying[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Crawford stresses that quality, not quantity, is the rate-limiting step: eggs sitting in the ovary for decades hold their chromosomes in position "like having kindergarteners stand in a line for 40 years", so more get out of line with age[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She gives monthly conception chances of about 20% at 30, 10-15% at 35, 5-8% at 38 and 3-5% at 40, with miscarriage risk rising from 25% at 35 to 50% at 40[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She publicly disagrees with the American College of OBGYN's recommendation against checking AMH in women who are not trying to conceive and have no infertility, arguing that a low ovarian reserve does not change your monthly odds but does shrink your total opportunity — and that withholding the data leaves women in the regret category, unable to make choices while they still have eggs left[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Her lifestyle prescription: avoid cigarettes, marijuana and cocaine; keep alcohol low, noting moderate drinking is around four drinks a week and is associated with reduced egg quality; avoid cooking or microwaving in plastic, Teflon and thermal-paper receipts, and decant takeaway food out of its container because heat leaches chemicals into it; reduce chronic inflammation; and eat a diet high in fruit and vegetables, limiting processed meat, refined sugar and red meat — she cites an IVF study where more weekly servings of red meat correlated with fewer embryos developing — while favouring whole-fat dairy over skimmed and fish up to three times a week[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Asked for the single most overlooked factor, she names sleep — seven and a half to eight hours — because sleep is when cells repair inflammatory damage; she calls it the simplest possible intervention because it is not a pill or a sacrifice[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She pairs it with daily movement of around 60 minutes and 20 daily screen-free minutes for cortisol reduction, noting both extremes — elite-level training or eating disorders causing hypothalamic shutdown, and excess fat tissue producing estrogen that confuses the brain into under-signalling — impair ovulation[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She explains the fertile window as the five days before ovulation plus the ovulation day itself, since sperm survive up to five days in the female tract while the egg lives 24 hours; because ovulation is roughly cycle length minus 14 days, the window differs sharply between a 28-day and a 35-day cycle, which is why she says tracking apps mislead people[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Her simplest advice is sex every day or every other day, and she says the most common mistake she sees is couples having less sex to "save up"[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- On myths she says sperm reach the cervix within about two minutes and the fallopian tubes within five, so propping hips up is unnecessary and urinating afterwards flushes nothing out while reducing UTI risk; no position raises the odds; penis size is irrelevant; and female orgasm does appear to help, with studies showing uterine contractions speed sperm transit[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She describes PCOS as being born with more eggs in the vault, so the same amount of FSH is diluted across more follicles and none gets a strong enough signal; the bored ovary upregulates testosterone, driving insulin resistance, abdominal weight, acne, facial hair and male-pattern baldness in a self-reinforcing loop[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She puts the official prevalence at 10-13% while noting 70% of cases go undiagnosed, gives the two-of-three diagnostic criteria (polycystic appearance on ultrasound, androgen signs, irregular or absent periods), and warns that absent periods with continuous low-level estrogen and no progesterone create a significant endometrial cancer risk — the reason progesterone or the pill is prescribed[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Endometriosis she describes as an inflammatory autoimmune reaction to retrograde menstrual cells, diagnosable only surgically by laparoscopy through the belly button; hallmarks are severe period pain that cancels plans and deep pain during intercourse in certain positions[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She puts prevalence at about 10% of all women but 30-50% of infertility patients, notes it destroys eggs faster, and describes the cruel bind that treating it requires suppressing ovulation — though she says women on the pill long-term have higher subsequent fertility than peers who were never on it, because ovulatory months were paused[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- On egg freezing she is blunt that it is "not an insurance policy, it's an investment": frozen eggs survive thaw about 90% of the time (versus over 99% for embryos, which are 300-500 cells rather than one), fertilisation runs 75-80%, roughly half of fertilised eggs fail to reach blastocyst, and the normal-embryo rate is 60-70% at 30, about 50% at 35, a third at 38 and 20-25% at 40 — so 20 eggs at 30 might yield around four normal embryos if everything goes perfectly[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She quotes roughly $10,000 for egg freezing, $20,000 for IVF and $500-$1,500 a year in storage[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- On outcomes she says one genetically normal embryo transferred gives about a 65% live birth chance, cumulatively 88% after two and 95% after three, so the main reason IVF fails is not having enough normal embryos; she recommends two to three normal embryos per desired child, single-embryo transfer so the embryo gets the whole uterus, and notes single transfers raise identical twinning from about 0.5% to 2-3%[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She adds that PGT can also screen out recessive conditions such as cystic fibrosis and eliminate autosomal dominant diseases like Huntington's from a family line[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- Her headline number is that most people wanting four children would need to start before age 28, and that studies point to 32 or 33 as the optimal intervention point for the average person not yet ready — though she stresses that if you know you want to freeze eggs, do not wait until 32, and that the point of seeing a fertility doctor is to make an active choice rather than have time make it for you[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. For couples with a diagnosis and a large desired family she recommends embryo banking now rather than IVF at 39, noting PCOS patients are excellent candidates because of their high egg yield[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
- She cautions against informal donor arrangements, recounting an Oklahoma case where a lesbian couple found an unpaid sperm donor through a Facebook group, signed paperwork waiving his rights, and lost when he later sued for custody — they now share custody with him[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].

# Takeaways

Crawford's repeated line to patients is that you cannot control everything but you should control what you can — sleep, movement, stress, diet, toxin exposure — so that those questions are eliminated from your mind regardless of outcome[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. Her structural complaint is that reproductive medicine is reactive, requiring patients to prove a problem before testing, when people with PCOS, endometriosis or a known risk factor should be evaluated before they start trying, and everyone should have an explicit family-planning conversation rather than what Bartlett calls "family reaction"[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She also urges second opinions when cycles keep failing, and warns that red flags include irregular periods with no explanation, period pain that interferes with life, and a doctor you can never reach[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw]. She closes with the words her own OB gave her after a miscarriage suffered mid-shift as chief resident — that meaning is hard to see in the middle of a journey, and your job in it is not to understand the whys but to keep going[^the-pregnancy-doctor-pregnancy-is-halved-every-y-w8h4qksd6Yw].
