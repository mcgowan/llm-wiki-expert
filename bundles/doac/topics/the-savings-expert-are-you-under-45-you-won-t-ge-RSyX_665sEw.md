# Overview

Jaspreet Singh is a licensed attorney who has never worked a day as one, a real estate investor, and the founder of Briefs Media and the Minority Mindset YouTube channel[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He tells host Steven Bartlett that the single difference between people who build wealth and people who do not is that the wealthy understand how money works — a conclusion he reached after checking every box the system told him to check, from high school through college, graduate school and law school, without once being taught anything about money, investing or passive income[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].

His account of his own turning point runs from playing the dhol at Punjabi weddings for $50 a night, through a teen party business that netted four dollars of profit split two ways, to buying a foreclosed condo for $8,000 the day after sitting the MCAT in 2011 and renting it out for $600 a month[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He describes the anger that followed: he had done everything he was told, and nobody had mentioned that wealthy people work to own the corporate ladder rather than climb it[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].

The episode's central provocation is that your own home is a liability rather than an asset, that the generational-wealth story told about it is a lie, and that the system profits from financial illiteracy — banks through perpetual debt, corporations through status spending, and the government through the high tax rates paid by employees[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Singh's counter-programme covers a fixed spending rule, a mindset framework, a five-bucket portfolio, and a redefinition of retirement as the point where investment cash flow exceeds expenses[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].

# Key points

- Singh names three things that have built more wealth than anything else over the last century — starting a business, investing in real estate, and investing in stocks — and notes none of them appeared anywhere in his long formal education, which was oriented entirely around getting a good job[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His definitional frame, learned from books after his party promotion business started making money, is that an asset puts money in your pocket and a liability takes it out; he says he was buying watches, rims and subwoofers to look rich while making everyone else rich[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On homes he is emphatic that he is not against buying one, only against treating it as an asset or buying one you cannot afford: a paid-off million-dollar house left to children who cannot cover property tax, insurance, maintenance and upgrades forces a sale, and a windfall with no financial education gets spent[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. By contrast, a rental property's rent pays the maintenance, taxes and insurance and still produces cash flow you can spend[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- He explains mortgage front-loading: on a typical 30-year US mortgage, for roughly the first 14 years and 8 months the majority of each payment goes to interest, so of a $3,000 monthly payment perhaps $100 reaches equity early on — and refinancing before that point restarts the clock[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On opportunity cost he points out that a $20,000 deposit spent on a home is $20,000 not available for a rental property, the stock market or a business, and warns against the assumption that houses always rise — citing the 2008 crash, where he says values in Michigan fell by as much as 93%[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He dismisses the "renting is throwing money away" objection by noting that every hotel stay, restaurant meal and mug purchase also enriches someone else, and says he rents both his home and his offices[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His affordability test has three parts — the moving costs (movers, furniture, upgrades, beyond closing costs), a minimum 20% down payment, and the monthly payment fitting inside his 75-15-10 plan: of every dollar earned, 75 cents is the maximum you may spend, 15 cents the minimum you invest, and 10 cents the minimum you save[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He acknowledges people dislike the 20% rule, and says he does not say what he says to make friends[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- He cites the statistic that 78% of Americans live paycheck to paycheck, and describes a credit-based economy where earning $50,000 makes you creditworthy enough to spend $60,000, $70,000 or $80,000 — jokingly contrasting a traditional Indian culture of making a dollar to spend twenty cents with an American one of making a dollar to spend two[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He names Bernard Arnault, the LVMH founder and 2023's richest person, as the proof that millions of people pay to look rich while someone else gets rich[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His "financial danger zone" is having no $2,000 emergency fund and carrying credit card debt; the prescribed response is drastic — stop restaurants, vacations and Netflix, sell what you own and cannot afford, and reclaim the two-plus hours a day the average American spends on television to learn and earn instead[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He frames the point of cancelling streaming as recovering time, not the $15[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- He argues the fear of looking broke is precisely what keeps people broke, and that people making emotional spending decisions become the prime marks for get-rich-quick schemes and the "laptop lifestyle" pitch[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. His own counterexample is driving a $500 car with no bumper the first year he made a million dollars, while his employees drove better cars[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His portfolio breaks down as roughly 50% real estate, 30% stocks (split about evenly between individual companies and funds), 18% speculative including startups and crypto, and about 2% physical gold, plus separate cash buckets for personal emergencies, business emergencies and money waiting to be deployed into each asset class[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He says he began buying Bitcoin around 2016-2017 at roughly $3,000 a coin and sold a chunk at record highs to buy more rental property, but insists crypto is speculative and should not carry someone's long-term wealth[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Gold he treats not as an investment but as hard-money saving and doomsday insurance, noting it produces no cash flow and creates no value[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His money mindset has four parts: I will become wealthy; money is a tool that amplifies who you already are; money is abundant; and it is your duty to become wealthy[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Under "money is a tool" he sets out four fitnesses — physical, mental, spiritual (meaning purpose, not religion) and financial — arguing financial problems are a leading cause of suicide and divorce and can undermine all three of the others[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- To illustrate self-imposed limits he recounts guest-teaching in Detroit public schools past metal detectors, where students named a Ford Mustang or Dodge Challenger as their dream car and, asked why not a Bugatti, replied that someone like them could never have that — they could not even dream it[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He makes every new employee do the nine-dots puzzle on day one, since the only solution is to draw outside the implied box[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Bartlett pairs this with stereotype threat research and a Channel 4 documentary in Liverpool where a boy said he wanted to be a millionaire and his mother laughed on camera[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On abundance, Singh's practical point is that squeezing a $50,000 income has a floor while earning has none — so rather than cutting from $30,000 of living costs to $25,000, ask what it would take to earn $500,000, whether through a raise, a career change or a side business[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His real estate criterion is a minimum 7% cash-on-cash return — $7,000 a year in post-expense cash flow per $100,000 invested — favouring single-family houses and multi-family apartments as more innovation-proof than offices or retail, and handing keys to a property manager because he wants to spend his time acquiring rather than managing[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- He tells two costly-mistake stories: on the $8,000 condo he hired a bad contractor and a property manager who never signed contracts, then was sued by tenants claiming a chipped bathtub caused a fall — documentation showed the husband had slipped at a friend's barbecue and the contractor had been turned away three times, but the insurer still settled for $14,000[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Later, a cheap accountant rang him one January to ask for $18,000 wired to Michigan and about $100,000 to the IRS the same day, plus penalties and interest, saying it was "nobody's fault" — Singh's conclusion was that it was his own, and that one of the most expensive things you can do is be cheap[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. Bartlett draws out the internal locus of control on display and adds that his brother traced his own net worth back to six or seven good early hires who then hired well themselves[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On tax he contrasts Coca-Cola CEO James Quincey's roughly $8 million of 2024 cash compensation taxed at a top federal rate of 37% with Warren Buffett's over $700 million in Coca-Cola dividends taxed at a top rate of 20%[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He then explains borrowing against assets, using Elon Musk's Tesla stock options granted around $6 a share — unexercised options generate no taxable income, banks lend against them at low rates, debt is not taxed, and rising collateral value lets new loans repay old ones, with the risk being a margin call if the asset collapses[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On the retirement crisis he says average US retirement savings at 60 is roughly $500,000 against an average death age of 77 and retirement at 67, while USA Today puts the comfortable figure at about $1.8 million[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He argues social security will not literally vanish because the government can print money, but that the 2.5% inflation adjustment for 2025 is both insufficient and applied a year late; pensions are largely gone for anyone under 45 and some funds have gone bankrupt; and good financial advisers generally will not take clients below a few hundred thousand in assets[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- He redefines retirement as wealth, and wealth as the point where investment cash flow exceeds expenses — $4,001 a month of cash flow against $4,000 of expenses qualifies — which decouples it from turning 67[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He also cautions against retirement as an absence of purpose, saying he has watched healthy people retire and deteriorate[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- His case that you must invest rather than earn or save your way to wealth rests on two comparisons: from 2019 to 2024 median household income grew about 18% while the S&P 500 grew nearly 100%; and from 1971 to 2021 median household income grew around 600% — during a period when households went from one earner to two — while the S&P 500 grew around 4,000%[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He argues inflation benefits investors, since rents and dividends adjust upward[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On patience he notes the stock market has averaged about 10% a year over the last century yet most people lose money, because they abandon the boring index for the next Tesla and invest on emotion rather than financials; his illustration is that $100 a month invested from 21 to 65 at that return retires you a millionaire[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- On the 2024 US election he says long-term index investors should ignore election cycles entirely, but notes government spending is about 30% of GDP, so a Trump administration's stated intentions to deregulate oil and gas, deregulate financial services (with crypto rallying on the news) and increase military spending create what he calls government shifts that more sophisticated investors may want to track[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
- Asked for the best investment he ever made, Singh names himself — self-education outside school and the lessons bought by his own failures — and prescribes a free-first curriculum of YouTube and podcasts before books, naming *Rich Dad Poor Dad*, *The Total Money Makeover* and *The Creature from Jekyll Island*, then five books each on money, personal development, starting a business, leadership and scaling as an MBA-level education for a fraction of the cost[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].

# Takeaways

Singh's framing is that everyone should be a business owner — buying a share of Amazon makes you one — but that only people with a genuine entrepreneurial itch should operate a business, since it demands tolerance for uncertainty, no blueprint, no stopping point at five o'clock, and a willingness to be criticised[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He is direct that this requires sacrifice and picking your hard — either life is hard now or hard for the rest of it — while insisting he is not advocating a money-obsessed life, only a bounded season of restraint[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. His two-part account of blame is deliberate: the system genuinely profits from your financial illiteracy, and simultaneously nobody else will fix your spending, so responsibility has to be taken forward from wherever you were dealt in[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw]. He closes by flagging the topic they did not cover — wealth preservation through accounting and tax strategy, legal structuring, insurance and estate planning — which he says every wealthy person invests heavily in and most people do not know exists[^the-savings-expert-are-you-under-45-you-won-t-ge-RSyX_665sEw].
