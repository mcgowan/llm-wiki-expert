# Overview

In this episode, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. Andrew Koutnik, a research scientist who says he has worked on over 100 studies on metabolic health, diabetes, and the ketogenic diet. Koutnik frames his mission as empowering people to take control of their health with science, drawing on his own story: he became obese in childhood despite following standard exercise and diet advice, reaching about 255 lbs, and was later diagnosed with type 1 diabetes — an irreversible disease he manages daily with an insulin pump and a continuous glucose monitor, both of which he demonstrates on camera during the conversation[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].

Koutnik's central argument is that long-term blood glucose control sits at the top of the health hierarchy: HbA1c, an average measure of blood glucose over two to three months, is among the strongest predictors of cardiovascular disease, the leading cause of death, and food — carbohydrates in particular — is the most potent factor driving glucose elevations at every meal. From this he argues that "therapeutic carbohydrate restriction," most notably a well-formulated ketogenic diet built on green leafy vegetables, protein, and fats rather than just "steak and bacon," can prevent and in some cases reverse metabolic disease. He recounts that switching to a ketogenic diet a decade ago dropped his own insulin requirements by over 40% and normalized his glucose to the point that his doctor, then the American Diabetes Association president, said he had never seen normal-range blood sugar in a type 1 diabetic[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].

The episode also features a live self-experiment: Koutnik eats oranges — a fruit he notes is promoted as a "superfood" for diabetics — while his CGM and insulin pump data are shown on screen, illustrating the glucose spike and insulin response in real time. The conversation ranges across the history of carbohydrate restriction in medicine, Koutnik's 10-year ketogenic case study in type 1 diabetes, his athletic performance research, exogenous ketones, food industry engineering of hyper-palatable products, and his simple closing pillars of nutrition, exercise, and sleep[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].

# Key points

- Koutnik claims over 68% of Americans are obese and that over 20% of children have obesity — a rate he says has quadrupled over the last 30 years — with insulin sensitivity dropping around 34-35% in the early stages of obesity, before symptoms or organ damage appear[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- He argues glucose control is the top health priority because HbA1c is one of the strongest predictors of cardiovascular disease, eye disease, and kidney disease; focusing on anything else first is, in his analogy, like choosing rims for a car with no engine[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- Carbohydrate restriction for diabetes is centuries old, he notes: physician John Rollo reported treating diabetes with a reduced-carbohydrate diet in 1796, and Mayo Clinic work in the 1920s showed ketogenic diets mimic fasting physiology and attenuate seizures in children with epilepsy[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- On the standard of care, Koutnik claims 99% of type 1 diabetes patients never regain normal metabolic control, 100% are expected to develop insulin resistance, MRI-visible brain changes appear in children within 3 years of diagnosis, and early signs of atherosclerotic progression (stiffening vessels, impaired nitric oxide response) appear within about 4 years[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- His 10-year case study — which he describes as the longest of its kind — followed a type 1 diabetic on a ketogenic diet whose LDL cholesterol nearly doubled, yet who maintained normal glycemic control, cut insulin load over 40%, and showed cardiovascular health better in almost every category than people without type 1 diabetes[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- A follow-up analysis of over 46,000 type 1 diabetes patients found, he says, that in over 70% of reports of very-low-carbohydrate ketogenic diets, patients completely normalized their glucose control[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- In his 2025 performance study, athletes adapted to a ketogenic diet for four weeks (with calories, body composition, and activity controlled) showed no deterioration in 6x800m sprint performance and recorded the highest fat-oxidation levels ever reported in the literature, even above 85% of VO2 max — challenging earlier 5-day-to-3-week studies that found a roughly 2% performance decline before full keto-adaptation[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- On exogenous ketones, he cites MIT aerospace research on 1,3-butanediol from the 1960s, a $10M DARPA metabolic optimization program from 2016 linking oral ketones to improved physical performance, a six-month study showing ketones attenuated cognitive decline in people at risk of Alzheimer's, and his own graduate work showing exogenous ketones delayed metastatic cancer progression and preserved muscle mass against cachexia[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- Bartlett cites a 2020 study in which brain network stability was 87% greater after ketone consumption than after glucose, and both discuss roughly 11 ongoing clinical trials on ketone-based therapies for serious mental illness, which Koutnik says affects over one in four US adults[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- Koutnik argues "keto-friendly" and "zero sugar" labels are often tricks: manufacturers swap sugar for ingredients like maltitol and maltodextrin that have the same metabolic effect, and add unnoticeable salt and sugar to products like bacon and nut butters to drive overconsumption and "food noise"[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- He singles out liquid calories — soda, orange juice, fruit smoothies — as a "dead giveaway bad move," explaining that blending fruit strips its structure, speeds absorption, and alters GLP-1 and insulin responses; he also flags white rice, potatoes, pasta, refined cereals (which he says spike blood sugar worse than most other foods), and dried fruit as pronounced glucose offenders[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
- His closing advice: be conscious of what you eat because food is as powerful as medicine, restrict carbohydrates if you have metabolic dysfunction (which he claims about 93% of Americans have in some form), exercise daily — he lifts weights six days a week and does jiu-jitsu five — and prioritize sleep as one of the three pillars of health[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].

# Takeaways

The through-line of the episode is Koutnik's contention that chronic disease is largely a story of unregulated blood glucose, and that the most powerful interventions — carbohydrate restriction, whole foods, exercise, and sleep — are behavioral rather than pharmaceutical, and often not what patients hear at the doctor's office. He stops short of prescribing the ketogenic diet universally, arguing instead that individuals vary widely around study averages and "you will never know the potential of its benefit or lack thereof if you don't try." Listeners should note the commercial context: Bartlett discloses during the episode that he is a co-owner of exogenous ketone company Ketone IQ, whose product is discussed and advertised within the conversation[^the-sugar-doctor-the-simple-diet-that-prevents-8-QXUNnXHXeVQ].
