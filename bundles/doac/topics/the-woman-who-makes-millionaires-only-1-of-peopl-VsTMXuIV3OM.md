# Overview

Steven Bartlett interviews Natalie Dawson, who over six years co-founded two nine-figure businesses — Cardone Ventures, a management consulting and investment firm she runs with Grant Cardone that has worked with over 15,000 small-business owners, and 10X Health, a wellness company acquired in 2023.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM] Dawson describes her own transformation from a 20-year-old "crippled with anxiety and fear about what others thought" — including a failed presentation that left her unable to speak to groups for four years — into a confident operator, a change she attributes to deliberately accumulating provable "stats," rebuilding her communication skills through a podcast and constant practice, and choosing to be respected rather than liked.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]

The conversation is built around her frameworks: PPF (personal, professional, financial goals set over one-, three- and five-year horizons), VCE (vision, commitment, execution) for high-stakes communication, a three-step interview process (cultural, technical, core values) for hiring, and the "four M's" (model, mimic, master, multiply) for training employees so founders can escape being the bottleneck in their own businesses.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM] She also makes contrarian arguments — that burnout is a "misconception" ("you're not a candle"), that passive income should be ignored until you have a million dollars, and that she would fire an employee for cheating on their partner — and closes with her thesis on the coming "women's wealth transfer" and on learning as the only durable skill in an AI-transformed economy.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]

# Key points

- The guest argues wealth should come from "hard work on the right set of problems": if your income isn't growing, the problems you spend your days solving are not tied to your goal, and your calendar — not manifestation — is the true representation of your priorities.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- Her PPF goal-setting framework buckets goals into personal, professional, and financial, each set at one-, three-, and five-year horizons; after over a thousand such conversations she says she has never found a goal outside those three buckets, and most people struggle most with the five-year view.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- Success ultimately comes down to character, she claims: the same playbook fails in the hands of someone who is unethical, doesn't want to win, or has surrounded themselves with people — spouses, kids, or employees — who act as "stops" on their growth.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- Her three-step hiring process runs a cultural interview (candidates without five-year goals are rejected outright), a technical interview putting candidates as close to the real work as possible (sales candidates make live calls; designers build assets on the spot), and a core-values interview where candidates present evidence of living the company's values — she personally watched all of the first 100 presentations at Cardone Ventures.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- She rejects burnout as a concept, quoting Grant Cardone — "You're not a candle. You can't burn out" — and argues people only feel burned out when they can't see how their sacrifices connect to a goal they actually want.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- To earn respect, she says you must first decide to be respected rather than liked, then accumulate undeniable "stats" — objective, provable results in the specific domain where you want respect.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- She defends firing two employees who were having an affair (both had outside partners), arguing that leadership means "making other people's success easy" and that someone who cheats on a spouse is a liability who may also cheat the company and its clients — a position the host pushes back on as over-policing private life.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- For founders stuck around $1 million in revenue, she prescribes documenting what they do, then training people through "tell me, show me, let me, coach me" and the four M's — model, mimic, master, multiply — insisting new hires first replicate what already works before innovating, since bringing in outside stars too early causes "slipbacks"; she contends hiring people smarter than you matters only after roughly $30 million in revenue.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- On business opportunities for 2025, she highlights helping the 35 million US small-business owners adopt AI (which she calls "the great leveler"), service businesses like roofing and HVAC that AI won't disrupt "until robots," hybrid wellness clubs, and pet care.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- She describes a "women's wealth transfer": US women hold about $10 trillion today, projected to reach $30 trillion by 2030 within a $124 trillion generational transfer, yet women often inherit assets they were excluded from managing — she cites an Ellevest analysis finding 12 times more manifestation content than investing content from women creators, and says 70% of widows fire their financial advisor within a year of their spouse's death.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- On passive income: "before you have a million dollars, don't even think about passive income" — 8% returns on a $60,000 salary won't create freedom, so time is better spent learning skills that raise income.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
- She frames the real "financial crisis" as how few people earn enough: of 35 million US businesses, fewer than 200,000 make over $1 million a year at an average margin of 8.5%, and the only durable edge in an AI world is the skill of learning and adapting itself.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]

# Takeaways

Dawson's core message is that wealth and respect are engineered, not manifested: set concrete personal, professional and financial goals on explicit time horizons, align your calendar to them, accumulate provable results, and treat every gap in your knowledge — from reading a P&L to understanding AI — as a learnable skill rather than evidence of inadequacy.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM] She tells listeners, especially women whom she observes systematically under-asking relative to less-performant male peers, that being underestimated is "actually your superpower" — fuel for building the stats that force people to take you seriously — and that liking yourself, earned through honest self-assessment and sacrifice, is the foundation the rest is built on.[^the-woman-who-makes-millionaires-only-1-of-peopl-VsTMXuIV3OM]
