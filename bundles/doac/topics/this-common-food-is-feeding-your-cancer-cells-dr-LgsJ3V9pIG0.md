# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett interviews Dr. William Li, a Harvard-trained physician, angiogenesis researcher, and author of Eat to Beat Disease and Eat to Beat Your Diet. Li's central argument is that everyone forms microscopic cancers constantly — he claims the body makes roughly 10,000 uncaught DNA copying mistakes every 24 hours, each the seed of a microscopic cancer — but that clinical cancer is usually prevented by hardwired "health defense systems," chiefly the immune system and angiogenesis, the body's regulation of blood vessel growth.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]

Li recounts the experiment that converted him to "food as medicine": his Angiogenesis Foundation swapped half its candidate cancer drugs out of a drug-screening system and tested food-derived compounds instead, finding that dietary factors such as green tea, onions, garlic, red grapes, and strawberries could cut off the blood supply that would feed a tumor — potency he says made his jaw drop as a former skeptic. He argues the flip side is equally real: excess salt, added sugar, alcohol, chronic stress, poor sleep, visceral fat, processed meats, and a rising load of ingested microplastics all take the body's shields down, and he links these exposures to the worrying global rise in early-onset cancers.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]

The conversation also covers Li's personal stake: his mother went from stage four endometrial cancer to stage zero after three immunotherapy treatments (no chemotherapy) combined with dietary support, and he describes emerging personalized cancer vaccines and the gut bacterium Akkermansia muciniphila as the frontier of cancer treatment. He closes with practical guidance — his top five foods, a low-friction approach to intermittent fasting, and the advice to build a sustainable "MediterrAsian" way of eating around healthy foods you already love.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]

# Key points

- Li claims the body makes about 10,000 uncorrected DNA replication errors every 24 hours, each a "microscopic cancer," and that we avoid clinical cancer only because health defense systems — he names five, including immunity and angiogenesis — continuously find and eliminate them.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- He states a 1 cm breast tumor (the smallest a trained hand can feel) already contains 1 billion cancer cells fed by roughly 100 million blood vessels, and that in the lab a microscopic tumor touched by a single blood vessel grows 16,000-fold in two weeks — which is why anti-angiogenic foods like green tea, coffee, soy, artichokes, and berries matter in his framework.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- His foundation's screening experiment replaced half the drugs under test with food powders and found food-derived compounds could shrink blood vessel growth like candidate cancer drugs; he says he has studied more than 200 foods that can help starve cancers, and that anti-angiogenic foods will not starve healthy tissue because the angiogenesis system is self-regulating ("Goldilocks zone").[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- Shield-lowering factors he names: excess sodium (accelerates cellular aging and damages the vessel lining), sustained added sugar (wears out insulin response), alcohol (a "universal toxin" regardless of the drink — any benefit of red wine comes from grape polyphenols, never the alcohol), and chronic stress, which suppresses immunity, damages DNA, and wrecks sleep.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- On microplastics, Li cites an autopsy study finding roughly a plastic spoon's worth of plastic distributed through the average human brain, an estimate (contested, he notes) that people may consume up to a credit card's worth of plastic weekly, and an Italian study in which men with plastic embedded in carotid artery plaque had a fourfold increase in fatal heart attack or stroke; his easy wins are ditching plastic cups/plates/cutlery, avoiding plastic-packaged food, and avoiding tea bags, which University of Montreal research found can shed around a billion microplastic particles per bag.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- His mother's case: diagnosed with stage four endometrial cancer at 80, she received three immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitor) treatments over nine weeks — plus dietary adjustment and a little radiation, no chemotherapy — and went to stage zero; she remains cancer-free at 91.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- He describes research presented by Dr. Laurence Zitvogel (Institut Gustave Roussy) showing the only factor separating immunotherapy responders from non-responders in about 100 patients was the gut bacterium Akkermansia muciniphila; foods he says grow Akkermansia include pomegranate, cranberries, Concord grapes, chili peppers, and Chinese black vinegar.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- He points to personalized peptide cancer vaccines — sequencing a patient's full tumor and normal genomes, isolating the "smoking gun" mutations, and printing them as a protein to vaccinate the patient against their own cancer — citing colleague Saskia Biskup's trials and a Nature Communications paper on over 100 glioblastoma patients, some kept alive and cancer-free.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- Specific food claims: matcha's polyphenols killed breast cancer stem cells in the lab; purple potato anthocyanins killed colon cancer stem cells in Penn State research; Earl Grey tea surprisingly beat green teas in his foundation's angiogenesis-supporting tests; and men eating two to three half-cup servings of cooked tomatoes weekly had a 29% lower prostate cancer risk, with tomato eaters who did develop the disease showing fewer, less aggressive tumor blood vessels.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- On body fat: he distinguishes subcutaneous, visceral, and brown fat, arguing visceral fat is dangerous because it outgrows its blood supply, becomes hypoxic and inflammatory, and "leaks" inflammation; a Cornell study of about 3,000 normal-weight Swedish women found those who were "skinny fat" had a threefold higher breast cancer risk over 13 years, and he links excess visceral fat to elevated risk of 14 other cancers.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- Brown fat, activated by cold exposure and foods — he says coffee's chlorogenic acid at three to four cups a day triggers thermogenesis — burns down harmful visceral fat.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
- His practical prescriptions: fast naturally by ending eating after dinner and delaying breakfast an hour (about 12 hours without calendarizing), sleep 7-9 hours so the brain's glymphatic system can drain toxins during deep sleep, follow the Japanese hara hachi bu principle of stopping at 80% full, and choose a sustainable "MediterrAsian" diet over rigid trend diets; his five desert-island foods are coffee/tea, tree nuts, tomatoes, berries, and MediterrAsian leafy greens.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]

# Takeaways

Li's message is deliberately empowering rather than restrictive: since everyone encounters food roughly five times a day, each meal is an opportunity to raise the body's shields, and occasional indulgences are harmless if the underlying defenses are strong. He urges listeners not to follow a fixed menu but to circle the foods they already love from the 200-plus he has studied, arguing that "love your food to love your health" is the only sustainable path — while remaining clear that food lowers risk rather than guaranteeing outcomes. These are the guest's claims and interpretations of the research he cites, presented as his views throughout the episode.[^this-common-food-is-feeding-your-cancer-cells-dr-LgsJ3V9pIG0]
