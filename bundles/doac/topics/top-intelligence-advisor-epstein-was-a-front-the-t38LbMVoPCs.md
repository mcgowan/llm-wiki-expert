# Overview

Gavin de Becker, the security consultant behind *The Gift of Fear* and founder of a protective-services firm whose main function he describes as "anti-assassination," joins Steven Bartlett to talk about what decades of working for heads of state, royalty and the world's wealthiest people have taught him about power, deception and personal safety. He declines throughout to name clients, treating confidentiality the way a doctor or psychiatrist would, and confines himself to matters already public — chiefly his role in the Jeff Bezos phone-hacking and *National Enquirer* extortion episode.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]

The first half of the conversation is a bleak account of digital privacy. De Becker's position is that no consumer product reliably protects a phone against a state actor, and that the Bezos case — where he says the Saudi government used the Israeli-made Pegasus 3 zero-click exploit — demonstrates it. From there he moves to Epstein, whom he characterises not as a self-made billionaire but as a "construct": a manufactured persona funded largely by Les Wexner and operated, in his view, as a compromat blackmail apparatus on behalf of a foreign intelligence service. He names Israel as the service he believes was involved, while acknowledging he is assembling public patterns rather than presenting direct evidence he is willing to share. He also sets these claims inside a broader argument that governments and corporations routinely delay the truth by decades, offering asbestos in baby powder, Agent Orange and opioids as his examples.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]

The second half turns personal and philosophical. De Becker argues that intuition — not logic, and not expert authority — is the human nuclear defence system, and that the work is not improving intuition but learning to stop interrogating it. He recounts a violent childhood, a heroin-addicted mother who shot his stepfather in front of him and died by suicide when he was 16, and defines healing as no longer spending energy managing the past. He closes on governance at the smallest possible scale, a belief that everything worth having is "downstream," and a stated conviction that life is predetermined.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]

# Key points

- De Becker describes his firm's core business as anti-assassination — threat assessment, physical protective coverage, armoured vehicles and home modifications — staffed by fit, specifically trained young people rather than retired officers on a second career, and summarises the whole enterprise as "preventing tissue damage."[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- On the Bezos case he says the Saudi government had just obtained Pegasus 3, an Israeli-made no-click exploit that he claims can access everything on a phone from 7,000 miles away — camera and microphone included — and that his job was to reconstruct how it happened.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- His central privacy claim is that there is no reliable protection for phone confidentiality against a determined government: each Apple patch is met by thousands of people worldwide working on the next exploit, so any "secure" handset stops working within about a month.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- He says the *National Enquirer*'s AMI demanded, via his and Bezos's lawyers, that he publicly state the coverage was not politically motivated and that no hacking was involved — a demand he found so strange that he refused, and which he took as confirmation both were false. He characterises the threat as extortion over material they may not have possessed, making it "a double crime, an extortion and fraud."[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- On Epstein, de Becker argues the wealth was manufactured: roughly $500 million came from Les Wexner along with power of attorney, which he reads as the funding mechanism for a construct — private jet, private island, unmarried, young women — rather than evidence of genuine billionaire status.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- He points to the Florida plea deal exempting *unnamed* co-conspirators — rather than the ordinary practice of naming unindicted co-conspirators — as anomalous, notes the US attorney who granted it later became Secretary of Labor and resigned, and repeats that prosecutor's reported explanation that he "was told he belonged to intelligence."[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- He describes the blackmail mechanism as the "rescuer" variant rather than a direct threat: the operator calls with bad news about a recording and an underage participant, then offers to handle it, and thereby owns the target permanently. He says cameras, and later audio, were installed at the New York apartment and the island, corroborated by testimony from girls who worked there about a recording room near the front door.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- He recounts an FBI search of the apartment at which labelled discs were photographed but not seized, with agents saying they would return with a warrant; when they came back six days later, he says, the material was gone.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- Asked directly, he says he believes Epstein was an intelligence asset of Israel, citing Ghislaine Maxwell's father's documented ties — a funeral in Israel attended by the prime minister and every living Mossad head — and notes Bartlett's earlier guest John Kiriakou said the same. He is explicit that this rests on pattern alignment, and suggests asking an AI to "make the best case for" a proposition because a straight question returns the official narrative first.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- His general model of institutions is that all power centres lie and admit the truth roughly 25 to 50 years late: Johnson & Johnson disclosed asbestos in baby powder to the FDA about 52 years before the end-of-2024 ruling; Agent Orange testing killed 38 of 40 lab mice within five days and was classified; he predicts the same arc for mass vaccination. His caution is that knowing you are being lied to does not tell you the truth.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- On geopolitics he calls the US an empire in decline — 760 overseas bases against China's roughly one, a military budget exceeding the rest of the world combined, 40,000 new laws a year and almost none rescinded — and argues tyranny is humanity's historical norm, exercised through fear, with division as the fuel of power ("the king and queen high-five when they see their subjects fighting").[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- His practical advice centres on intuition, which he says is always right in two ways: it always has your interests at heart, and it is always based on something. The training required, he argues, is not to sharpen intuition but to stop prosecuting it — his example is the woman who overrides her fear and steps into a soundproof steel box with a stranger, something no other animal would do.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
- He advocates small-scale governance and "subsidiarity" — government at the most local possible level — drawing on Fijian villages of roughly 300 people, and says he deliberately shrank his own company from about 1,000 employees to 600, using a daily employee survey system called CARE (continuous asking, responding and evaluation) to keep management close to individuals.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]

# Takeaways

De Becker's argument runs from the technical to the personal along a single line: institutions of every kind — states, agencies, corporations — behave in ways that make trusting them naive, and the individual's only durable instrument is the one they were born with. He does not claim his Epstein account is proven, and he explicitly allows that he may himself be naive about a level of power he cannot see; his practical prescription is smaller institutions, no pretence of digital privacy, and a habit of listening to fear before rationalising it away.[^top-intelligence-advisor-epstein-was-a-front-the-t38LbMVoPCs]
