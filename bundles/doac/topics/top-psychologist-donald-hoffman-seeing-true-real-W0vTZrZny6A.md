# Overview

Donald Hoffman, a cognitive scientist who has spent roughly four decades on the problem of consciousness, tells Steven Bartlett that nobody — including the most advanced professionals — understands the nature of reality, and that our best physics already rules out space-time as fundamental.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] His governing metaphor is a virtual-reality headset: the table, the cup and the body are rendered on demand for the purposes of the game, and there is an entire reality outside them, as unlike our experience as a supercomputer's toggling voltages are unlike Grand Theft Auto.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] He is careful to flag which parts are his own view rather than physics.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]

The scientific spine of the argument is evolutionary. Hoffman says he and colleagues have published mathematical results showing that on Darwin's theory the probability is zero that any sensory system was ever shaped to perceive objective reality truly — perception is metabolically expensive, and organisms that spent energy on truth lost to organisms that used cheap tricks.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] From there he draws a metaphysical conclusion he presents openly as speculation: that there is one transcendent consciousness wearing an infinite number of headsets, that "you and I are really the same one reality looking at itself through two different avatars," and that death is simply the headset coming off.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]

The conversation also gets personal and practical. Hoffman describes long COVID, two heart surgeries and a night at 190 beats per minute during which he sent his wife a goodbye message, and uses it to admit that intellectually he is convinced and emotionally he is not — "put a gun to my head and you'll find out."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] He also claims that within months of the recording his theory began yielding space-time structure from a consciousness-first model, which he frames as prising open Pandora's box.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]

# Key points

- Hoffman notes that Einstein's theory combined with quantum theory implies space-time has no operational meaning below 10^-33 cm and 10^-43 seconds, which he takes as evidence that space-time cannot be fundamental — and stresses that calling it a virtual reality is "Hoffman, not physics."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- He says he has no serious quarrel with Darwin — there is "no serious competitor" as a theory of the origin of species — but argues Darwin's own mathematics entails that fitness, not truth, is what senses track: they were shaped "to keep you alive long enough to reproduce successfully. Period."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- His evolutionary simulations, run before the theorems existed, pitted artificial organisms that perceived the true state of the world against organisms with a cheap interface; across a wide range of conditions the truth-seeing organisms went extinct, because "seeing the truth takes too much time and energy."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- The Australian jewel beetle is his favourite illustration: males, cued only by "dimpled, glossy and brown," mate with discarded beer bottles — evolution gave them just enough information to reproduce and no insight into their own species.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- He makes a strong epistemic claim: because every scientific theory rests on assumptions and there is an infinite regress of ever-deeper theories, "all of our theories will comprehend 0% of reality" — while insisting he is a working scientist who encourages others into science.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- On rendering, he pushes the VR metaphor to its conclusion: the cup exists only when he creates it, Bartlett renders his own separate cup, and neither has a brain until someone looks — the correlations between brain activity and experience are real, he says, but run the other way, with consciousness creating the brain as an icon.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- He distinguishes his position sharply from Nick Bostrom's simulation hypothesis: Bostrom's version keeps a physical substrate generating conscious experience, and Hoffman says that programme has "no beef on the table" until someone can specify which causal structure must be the taste of mint, and why.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- His psychological reading of the theory is that ego, competition between people, religions and countries all stem from mistakenly identifying with the avatar: "You're not a little player. You're the inventor of this whole thing. You have nothing to prove."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- He connects this to religious language — the Bible calling people children of God, Jesus saying that what is done to the least is done to him — and concludes that "the reason to love your neighbour as yourself is because your neighbour is yourself." Raised in a fundamentalist church where his father was a pastor, he says he is not a card-carrying believer in any religion, and reads parts of scripture as bogus.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- Hoffman says he now meditates three to four hours a day, has never taken psychedelics, smoked, or drunk alcohol in decades, and that all the creativity in his scientific work "comes from the silence" — while describing meditation as often painful, a death of an illusion rather than uninterrupted peace.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- His technical proposal is "conscious agent network theory," developed with Chetan Prakash: model an observer as a set of experiences plus a Markov matrix of transition probabilities and a counter, then derive physics from it. He says that in the three or four months before the recording he showed he could get the constancy of the speed of light across inertial frames out of that model, and thinks he has light and possibly the electron.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- The technological stakes he claims are extreme: if space-time is a headset that can be reverse-engineered, one could act on the code rather than within it — moving something to Andromeda's 2.4 million light years without traversing them, in a way that would look like instant travel. He compares the prospect to opening Pandora's box and says nuclear bombs "will be like firecrackers" beside it.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- On suffering, he refuses a glib answer, noting that Christianity's central symbol is a crucifixion — about the most drawn-out and horrific death imaginable — and locating the heart of it in forgiveness offered from the cross. He offers only an undefended sense that "all is well" and that nothing can hurt the one.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
- On AI, having studied under Marvin Minsky and done PhD work on Lisp machines at MIT's AI lab from 1979, he says large language models are impressive at correlation but "dumber than cucumbers," praises Karl Friston's active inference and free energy approach to minimising surprise, and says his own logic minimises surprise more directly — a new kind of AI he would build if he lives long enough.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]

# Takeaways

Hoffman's practical summary, when Bartlett presses him for one, is two lines: love your neighbour as yourself because your neighbour is yourself, and never assume you know everything, because "reality is far more interesting and exciting than you could ever imagine."[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] He argues most of his own stress comes from believing he must become or prove something, when on his view the striving is an artefact of identifying with an avatar.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A] He is unusually explicit about the limits of his own position — his theory is "just a theory," destined to be superseded, and his emotions have not caught up with his intellect — while arguing that if a consciousness-first physics ever delivers working technology, the burden of proof between physicalism and spirituality flips entirely.[^top-psychologist-donald-hoffman-seeing-true-real-W0vTZrZny6A]
