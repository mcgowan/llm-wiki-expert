# Overview

Dr Stasha Gominak, a neurologist trained at Harvard and formerly a clinical assistant professor at Stanford, tells Steven Bartlett that the standard behavioral advice for sleep — blackout curtains, fixed bedtimes, cool rooms — fails the patients who end up in her clinic, because their real problem is brain-stem chemistry. Her account begins in 2004 with young, thin women suffering daily headaches whose sleep studies showed missing or delayed REM sleep; she argues that REM sleep, when the body is most paralyzed, depends on the neurotransmitter acetylcholine, and that vitamin D drives production of the enzyme (choline acetyltransferase) that makes it. She claims to have worked with roughly 2,000 patients and another 5,000-6,000 clients through her online sleep-coaching business.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]

Gominak's central claim is that vitamin D was "mistakenly" named a vitamin: it is a hormone made on the skin from sun exposure, and the shift indoors — she dates the convergence of sunscreen, computers, and air conditioning to the 1980s — created a mass deficiency state she connects to epidemics of sleep disorders, obesity, ADHD, fatty liver, and diabetes that emerged around the same era. Building on Walter Stumpf's work locating vitamin D receptors in the brain stem's sleep-controlling nuclei, and on her own testing of about 500 patients in 2009, she developed a hypothesis linking D deficiency to an abnormal gut microbiome that stops producing the eight B vitamins, which she says are bacterial growth factors, not primarily food nutrients.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]

From this she built her "RightSleep" protocol — individualized vitamin D dosing to a blood level above 60, B12 if deficient, and a B50 complex for about three months to "jump-start" the microbiome before tapering off. Bartlett repeatedly presses her on the fact that mainstream literature does not support a vitamin D-to-B-deficiency link; Gominak concedes the claim rests on her own 2016 hypothesis paper and clinical observations, frames all medical consensus as "made-up stories" or best guesses, and insists supplementation is dangerous without tracking blood levels and symptoms.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]

# Key points

- Gominak argues vitamin D is a hormone made on the skin, not a vitamin: eating vitamin D in salmon, she says, is like expecting "salmon estrogen" to run your menstrual cycle, and supplementing is not equivalent to sun exposure because UVB on skin produces sulfated D plus perhaps 15-20 other chemicals a pill lacks.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- She claims that when vitamin D goes very low "our brain actually forgets how to sleep," citing Walter Stumpf's findings of vitamin D receptors in the brain-stem nuclei that paralyze us in REM sleep and in the brain's clock nuclei.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Her origin story: a young, thin female headache patient — nothing like the "fat older male with a fat tongue" apnea stereotype — was cured of five years of daily headaches by CPAP, prompting Gominak to run sleep studies showing her headache patients had roughly one-sixth the normal REM sleep.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Testing about 500 patients between September and December 2009, she found every one had low vitamin D; in 2009 a PubMed search for "vitamin D and sleep" returned zero hits.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- She reports that patients on vitamin D alone for two years developed new problems — burning hands and feet, aching joints — which she attributes to induced B-vitamin deficiency, supported by 1950s prisoner experiments near the Iowa State Prison showing that blocking pantothenic acid (B5) for two weeks caused insomnia, gut complaints, abnormal gait, and burning feet.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Her core hypothesis, published in 2016: vitamin D deficiency changes the intestinal microbiome, reducing gut B-vitamin production; supplementing D plus all eight Bs for about three months returns the microbiome to normal, after which continued high-dose Bs themselves disrupt sleep and cause pain.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- She contends the eight B vitamins were first described as bacterial growth factors and that the human microbiome — about 200 named bacteria of which only 2% are well understood — is the body's natural B-vitamin production system, invoking fecal transplants for C. diff as evidence of the microbiome's power.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Case studies: "Madeline," 100 lb overweight with nine medications, daily headaches, and gestational diabetes, is on 30,000 IU of vitamin D daily (versus a typical 5,000-10,000) with levels holding in the 60s, and after years on the protocol came off three pain drugs and is returning to work; "Natalie," a triathlete with postpartum insomnia, recovered with D plus a three-month B50 course.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Gominak criticizes the Endocrine Society for concluding in its guidance that doctors should avoid vitamin D levels and recommendations altogether rather than call for hormone-style, blood-level-based studies, and argues fixed-dose trials (e.g., everyone gets 4,000 IU) are the wrong way to study a hormone.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- She disputes chronotype dogma from researchers like Matthew Walker, claiming her patients — including lifelong night-shift workers — revert to sleeping 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. once their chemistry is corrected, because humans are "day hunters."[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- Bartlett cites data that 90% of Americans spend close to 22 hours a day inside and about 20% essentially never go outside; Gominak's preferred fix for the mildly affected is more outdoor time and fermented foods, reserving supplements for people with genuine problems — "if there's nothing wrong with you, do not mess with supplements, especially D."[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
- She makes further contrarian claims: narcolepsy and sleep apnea are end-stage versions of the same chemistry problem; one client "cured" narcolepsy with round-the-clock nicotine patches because nicotine is the only drug that duplicates acetylcholine; and smokers with Parkinson's have fared better because Parkinson's is "an acetylcholine deficiency state first."[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]

# Takeaways

The episode presents Gominak's self-described belief system: that a hidden, fixable deficiency chain — indoor living, low vitamin D, a degraded microbiome, missing B vitamins — underlies most modern sleep disorders and many chronic diseases. She is candid that this is a hypothesis built from clinical observation rather than controlled trials, that mainstream medicine and the published literature do not endorse the D-causes-B-deficiency link (her own paper aside), and that vitamins used carelessly are "just as dangerous as any drug." Her claims about doses, disease reversal, and narcolepsy cures are her own assertions and case anecdotes, reported here as the guest's views rather than established medical guidance.[^vitamin-d-expert-the-supplement-world-is-giving-BbGv7GTbRN8]
