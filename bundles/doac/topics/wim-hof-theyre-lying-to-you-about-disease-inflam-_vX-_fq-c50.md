# Overview

Wim Hof, the Dutch extreme athlete known as "the Iceman," tells host Steven Bartlett that his mission is to bring "love and power" — but through science rather than exhortation, because nobody listens to a man shouting about love from a rooftop[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. His diagnosis of modern life is that people are drained by a system that consumes their energy, that we can go to Mars and build AI but "cannot create happiness, strength and health," and that we have optimised discomfort out of existence to our own cost[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. His compressed slogan for this is that if you do not go to the cold, the cold will come to you — and if you do not go to stress, stress will come to you unprepared[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].

Hof traces his own path to two events. He says he was an undiscovered second twin, found almost too late and delivered in a hospital corridor, blue and nearly suffocated — a birth trauma he credits with making him a lifelong "seeker"[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. At seventeen, following intuition, he got into a freezing lake in Amsterdam and felt he had found what the religions and ascetic disciplines had been pointing at all along[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. The second event is the death of his first wife in 1995, who deteriorated through depression, mania and schizophrenia over fifteen years and took her own life by jumping from an eighth storey, leaving him alone with four children aged roughly seven to twelve and almost no money[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. Cold water, he says, was the only thing that interrupted the agonising thinking long enough for him to function as a father[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].

The scientific spine of the episode is a run of studies Bartlett summarises and Hof endorses, from the 2010 Radboud endotoxin experiment to a January 2024 paper Hof calls a landmark. His interpretation of all of it is that interoception — a learned ability to listen to and command the body from within — is the key, and that this capacity is the "pharmacy" in the deepest part of the brain that we have handed over to pills and doctors[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].

# Key points

- Hof says he lived as a squatter through his twenties with no money, living off scraps from the marketplace, and calls it the best time of his life — evidence, he argues, that the propagated need for a house, a mortgage and a nine-to-five turns people into slaves who only survive rather than express themselves[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- He describes getting the phone call about his wife's death from a phone booth at a campsite while guiding a group in the mountains, and says the grief could not arrive at first — the shock allows no words, and he only cried later, standing with her father over her body[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- Asked how he told children aged seven to twelve, Hof says he avoided the word dead — "she is gone, her body is gone, she is no longer with us" — and framed it as the family finding life together while she remained present with them[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- His account of the 2010 Radboud University Medical Center study, as recounted by Bartlett, is that Hof at 51 was injected with a dead strain of E. coli that had produced flu-like responses in 100 previous healthy subjects, but showed no negative immune response beyond a minor headache, with blood results showing cortisol, adrenaline and the anti-inflammatory messenger IL-10[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- The follow-up, they say, gathered 30 male volunteers — 12 controls and 18 trained by Hof over four days in Poland in breathing, meditation and cold exposure — and found the trained group had a significantly smaller immune response and recovered faster, which Bartlett characterises as demonstrating voluntary control of the autonomic nervous system and immune response previously thought impossible[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- On the 2018 Wayne State University "brain over body" study, Bartlett describes a perfusion vest of the kind firefighters use, pumping cold or warm water through tubes while subjects lay in an fMRI scanner; where 74 comparison subjects' skin temperature tracked the water, Hof using his techniques held a skin temperature of 34°C for 25 minutes, with PET imaging showing his intercostal muscles burning glucose and releasing heat[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- The same study, per Bartlett's summary, found Hof's brain did not show the ordinary unconscious reaction to cold — his breathing engaged the sympathetic nervous system and triggered the periaqueductal gray in the brainstem, releasing cannabinoids and opioids that produced well-being and reduced anxiety; Hof frames the finding as choosing to feel good while stress is coming in[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- Hof cites a January 2024 study he calls a landmark, led by a professor in Detroit, finding that cannabinoid receptors form part of the salience network — the system that makes the brain's other networks work together — and that the breathing gives access to areas otherwise unreachable, which he says is the mechanism by which stored trauma is released[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- He also references a study of 540 people in Australia which he says found the method outperformed meditation and mindfulness, raising stress resilience and cognitive awareness while lowering stress experience[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- On cardiologists' heart films, Hof claims that holding the breath after exhalation for a minute and a half sends five times more blood into the brain and the heart, and that the breathing raises blood pH high enough that the CO2-driven urge to breathe simply does not fire[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- He explains trauma as "stored-up chemistry" — experience that could not be processed at the time and was stashed away biochemically in deeper tissue, unreachable by thinking — and says conscious deep breathing breaks the conditioned pattern and lets it surface[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- Hof is emphatic about safety, insisting the breathing is a powerful therapeutic exercise that must never be done while driving, in a swimming pool or anywhere loss of motor control would be dangerous[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- His examples of the "life force" include a 76-year-old with Lyme disease who he says climbed Kilimanjaro with him in 31 hours rather than the usual five to nine days, and footballer Patrice Evra, who Hof says had a childhood trauma around cold water used as punishment and went on to sit in icy water for 16 minutes until the BBC asked him to get out[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- He argues scientific institutions have not pursued this because they are funded by the pharmaceutical industry, that psychiatry's medications left his first wife "like a zombie," and that natural methods matching drug results without side effects deserve serious examination[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
- His daily routine, at 65, is waking around 4:30 to 5, breathing exercises and stretching in bed, coffee with milk and no sugar, no breakfast or lunch, one meal a day — a habit he says he has had since childhood, following intuition rather than the "made up" three-meal rule — plus resistance work taken deliberately to the threshold of pain; he also mentions marking birthdays 60 through 63 with 60, 61, 62 and 63 minutes in ice before deciding it had become boring[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].

# Takeaways

Hof's three pillars are conscious breathing, cold exposure and what he calls the power of the mind, and he insists the cold is never the point — it is a route to interoception and command over one's own emotional state, so that "we can make our bad feelings, emotions, disease go away in our command"[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. He frames this as a move from victimhood to responsibility, defining responsibility literally as the ability to respond[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. Asked how to find one's purpose, his answer is deflationary: "just be happy, strong and healthy — the rest is [nonsense]," and purpose will follow[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50]. Bartlett, after roughly six minutes in an ice bath during the episode, describes the first twenty seconds as a wall of panic he had built himself, and takes from it a lesson about the other self-limiting beliefs he may be living inside — a reading Hof endorses, saying the cold does not show you what you think, it shows you who you are[^wim-hof-theyre-lying-to-you-about-disease-inflam-_vX-_fq-c50].
