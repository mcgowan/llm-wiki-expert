# Overview

This is the second part of a Diary Of A CEO panel with four specialists in women's health: exercise physiologist Dr. Stacy Sims, orthopaedic surgeon Dr. Vonda Wright, menopause physician Dr. Mary Claire Haver and fertility specialist Dr. Natalie Crawford. Where part one covered hormones and menopause, this episode is deliberately practical — exercise, nutrition, fasting, supplements, environmental toxins and sleep — and opens with the panel's shared premise that sport and exercise science has overwhelmingly been done on men, so standard recommendations for training, recovery and nutrition are not generalisable to women.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]

Their central argument is a reframing of the goal. The panel contends that decades of messaging pushed women toward being small at any cost, and that the result is an epidemic of osteoporosis, frailty and dementia risk. They want the target changed from weight to body composition — muscle mass and body fat percentage rather than the number on the scale — because muscle functions as a metabolic and endocrine organ, and because bone, once estrogen departs, declines at a rate men never experience. Alongside this they argue that many active women are not overtraining so much as under-recovering and under-fuelling, with knock-on effects on the menstrual cycle, thyroid function and bone.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]

The prescription that emerges is specific: mobility work, heavy compound lifting two to four days a week, jump training for bone, short true-sprint intervals, and low-intensity movement everywhere else — with the middle-intensity zone that most group fitness classes occupy treated as the trap. They also push back on absolutist cycle-syncing advice on social media, on fasting for women, on juice cleanses, and on GLP-1 medications used without oversight — while explicitly declining to dismiss those medications, which several of them prescribe.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]

# Key points

- The panel rejects rigid cycle-syncing rules. Sims notes that molecular differences exist across the cycle but that without knowing whether a woman ovulates the advice cannot be individualised; Crawford adds that roughly 15% of women are not ovulating. Their compromise: strength training stays constant regardless of phase, and the *other* days flex with how you feel — putting the hardest sessions in the late follicular window, roughly days 6 to 14, when many women feel strongest.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On ovulation they describe wide variation — some women feel bulletproof at the estrogen surge, others experience a transient drop as the granulosa cells rupture, and some experience mittelschmerz severe enough to write off training entirely. Days 24-27, as both hormones fall, are typically the PMS low point.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Wright's clinical illustration of the middle-intensity trap is a class visible from her office window, full of midlife women training five to seven days a week: never intense enough to change physiology, never easy enough to recover, and reappearing in her clinic injured every three weeks. The panel calls "polarisation" the fix — genuinely high intensity plus genuinely low intensity, avoiding zones three and four unless training for an endurance event.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Sims positions Pilates and yoga as complements rather than substitutes: good for isometric control, core strength, balance and proprioception, but single-plane and not a sufficient stimulus for muscle or bone. Wright agrees it will keep you from becoming stiff and hunched but "will never give you strength and power in the way to protect yourself from falling down."[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Their sample week: 10 minutes of mobility first (joint-capsule distraction with heavy bands, which Wright distinguishes from stretching — stretching lengthens muscle, it does not open the capsule), then one compound movement per session rotating knee-dominant squat, upper-body push/pull, and posterior chain, finishing with plyometrics or sprint work; other days low-intensity walking. Wright explains injuries in returning midlife athletes as load outstripping tissue compliance — "they return to their memories of success" and rupture tendons.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Wright's bone numbers: both sexes peak around 25-30; men then lose about 1% a year and typically do not fracture until their 70s or 80s, while women lose 15-20% of bone density through perimenopause from estrogen loss alone, because estrogen restrains the osteoclast and buffers inflammation. She adds that 40-50% of women will have low bone density, 70% of hip fractures occur in women, 30% of hip fractures carry a one-year mortality risk, and 50% of survivors never return to pre-fall function.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- She argues for baseline DEXA or REMS scanning far earlier than the standard age-65 recommendation — around $99 out of pocket in her area — saying she knows no orthopaedic surgeon or exercise physiologist who thinks 65 is sensible. Crawford extends this to reproductive-age women with prolonged low-estrogen states, for whom bone health is among the most defensible reasons to prescribe estrogen.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On whether osteoporotic women can lift, they cite Belinda Beck's LIFTMOR trial — five sets of five reps near failure, supervised — in which none of the osteoporotic participants broke and all built bone. A follow-up study from May 2025 compared doing nothing, resistance plus jump training, and the same exercise plus hormone therapy; the exercise group gained bone and the exercise-plus-HRT group gained more, which they describe as synergistic.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Sims explains that estrogen governs how tightly myosin binds actin, so declining estrogen produces myosin dysfunction — women report losing power and grip strength before body composition visibly changes — and that lifting heavy loads provides an alternative neuromuscular stimulus for that adaptation. Wright adds that jumping matters specifically because osteocytes sense fluid shifts as a biochemical signal to build bone, and that running does not supply the multidirectional ground reaction force required.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On why women don't start, Sims points to gendered gym onboarding: women are shown the class schedule, men are shown the lifting platforms. The panel also addresses the fear of bulking, noting a physique-model build takes five to ten years of deliberate work, and reframes the goal as recomposition — "it's what you're made of that matters, not the size of the shell."[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Sims prefers "under-recovering" to "overtraining," and describes widespread low energy availability among recreationally active women, noting that four days of it measurably affects thyroid function and that bookending calories at either end of the day creates a mid-day starvation signal. Crawford cites 58% of runners having a luteal phase defect, and warns that "I still have my period" is not proof of energy balance.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On protein, the panel calls the RDA of 0.8 g/kg a malnutrition-prevention floor — "survival doses of protein like sitting on a chair like a mushroom" — and recommends roughly 0.86 g to 1 g per pound of ideal body weight. They cite a 12-week study in normal-weight-obese women where raising intake to 1.6 g/kg alone, with no exercise, improved muscle quality and lowered body fat, and Women's Health Initiative data showing the lowest frailty scores above 1.6 g/kg.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On fasting they distinguish extended intermittent fasting — which Sims says men can often tolerate but which for women promotes visceral fat storage, inflammation and hypothalamic suppression — from time-restricted eating aligned to circadian rhythm: food within about half an hour of waking, protein and fibre at every eating occasion, and nothing for two to three hours before bed. Haver describes her own reversal on fasting once she realised patients could not hit protein targets while doing it. They also argue autophagy and telomere benefits attributed to fasting are obtainable through exercise, and advise against fasted training for women, who use more amino acids as fuel.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On GLP-1s the panel is deliberately balanced: Haver starts patients with three to six months of lifestyle plus or minus HRT, and says about 50% resolve there. Crawford notes the drugs can be transformative for women with PCOS and for fertility timelines. Their objection is access without oversight — their protocol involves hour-long visits, mandatory resistance training and protein targets, serial body-composition scans, and cutting the dose if muscle loss exceeds about 10%. Bartlett's summary, which the panel affirms: these drugs stop you eating rather than burning fat, so without lifting and protein you lose muscle and bone.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Wright's arithmetic against out-exercising diet: about 100 calories burned per mile run versus 180 calories in three Thin Mints — and, as she notes, nobody eats three. The panel's answer to "the healthiest way to lose weight" is simply "lift weights and eat," with an 80/20 or 90/10 framing, plant-forward and anti-inflammatory eating, and ditching alcohol first.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Sims links high-intensity work to brain health via lactate: women have fewer glycolytic fibres and lose them with age, and she argues lactate production supports brain metabolism at a point where glucose utilisation shifts through the menopause transition, citing Lisa Mosconi's imaging work. Wright cites research that caregivers of a parent with dementia carry a 60% increased risk themselves once genetics are removed, which she attributes to caregiving stress.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On supplements: Sims argues creatine is not just for bodybuilders — women hold roughly 70-80% of men's stores, 3-5 g daily saturates tissues, and she cites benefits for cognition, concussion recovery, gut symptoms and fatigue. Haver adds vitamin D (safe up to about 4,000 IU, with about 80% of her patients deficient) and fibre; Crawford adds folic acid for anyone trying to conceive, omega-3s, magnesium and CoQ10 for egg quality; Wright adds fisetin for senescent cell load and NMN as an NAD+ precursor, noting IV NAD+ does not work because the coenzyme acts intracellularly. On collagen, Sims says it does not count as dietary protein for muscle but type 2 collagen has evidence for reducing joint pain without regenerating cartilage.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- On environmental toxins Crawford separates endocrine-disrupting chemicals, microplastics that physically deposit and can cause ovarian fibrosis, and "behavioural toxins" like alcohol and tobacco. Practical steps: filter water, remove plastics from the kitchen, decant hot takeaway food immediately, decline thermal receipts (BPA), and audit cosmetics. She cites higher BPA exposure correlating with lower ovarian reserve, soy intake correlating with lower BPA levels, and a study finding nine-years-earlier menopause in a cohort where both mother and child had experienced sexual abuse.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
- Sleep is treated as the foundational pillar: no metabolic or body-composition change is possible without it. Wright stops eating three hours before bed, quit alcohol largely for sleep, and fixes her wake time to anchor circadian rhythm. Haver recommends melatonin only in low doses — around 1 mg, often requiring children's formulations, against the 5-10 mg people typically take — and says a woman still waking at 3am after hormones and sleep hygiene deserves a sleep apnoea workup, noting more than 50% of women with it go undiagnosed because they present differently from men.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]

# Takeaways

The panel's closing message is one of agency rather than optimism about the system: Haver's line, quoting Sims, is that after reproduction ends women are treated as "small men with rust and uteruses," and that no medical specialty owns life after fertility, so women will have to quarterback their own care until they find an informed partner in it. Wright frames the counter-narrative as her book's thesis — that a woman's destiny is not frailty, but only if she is proactive rather than waiting for time to make her its victim. Their practical throughline is preventive and early: measure bone and body composition before guidelines say to, lift heavy, jump, eat enough protein, sleep, and stop treating the scale as the outcome.[^women-s-exercise-debate-the-7-weight-loss-lies-w-P1CeHGJOX5g]
