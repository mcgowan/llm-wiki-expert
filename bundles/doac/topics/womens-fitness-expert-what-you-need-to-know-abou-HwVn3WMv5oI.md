# Overview

Dr Stephanie Estima — a 48-year-old chiropractor with a neuroscience and psychology degree from the University of Toronto, 20 years in practice, and author of The Betty Body and the forthcoming Nothing to Lose — tells Steven Bartlett she is "on a mission to undo the genuinely terrible advice" women receive about health and fitness. Her core message is a reframe: she wants women "to stop being losers" — to stop organizing their entire fitness identity around losing weight and shrinking, and instead pursue gains in muscle, bone density, and connective tissue. She grounds this in her own history: competing in a figure competition at 11% body fat, she lost her period for months, developed lasting hormonal problems, regained all the weight, and "hated" herself — while being showered with compliments on how amazing she looked.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]

Estima organizes women's fitness journeys into four archetypes — Overwhelmed Olivia (paralyzed by contradictory online "infobesity"), Skinny Fat Sophia (thin outside, under-muscled and losing bone, afraid of weights), Exercist Emily (trains hard but under-eats out of fear of gaining weight, as Estima did during her divorce), and Dialed-in Diana (the goal: training, eating, and recovering without punishment). She then debunks a series of myths: that carbs make women fat, that lifting heavy makes women bulky (she claims 97-98% of women lack the hormonal environment to bulk, since men have 10-20x more testosterone), that long fasts are a female fat-loss tool, and that post-workout protein must be consumed within a narrow window.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]

The conversation also covers female-specific biomechanics — the wider female pelvis, the Q angle, knock-knees and ACL risk, pelvic-floor differences and postpartum training — plus her supplement stack, sprinting and VO2 max, plyometrics for bone density, and her caution that GLP-1s and hormone therapy help but "won't go to the gym and lift weights with you."[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]

# Key points

- Estima argues the "pursuit of skinny at all costs" has devastating long-term consequences: women who prize slimness under-eat, avoid challenging weights, and can end up with osteoporosis — "if you can fit into a size-whatever dress at 40 but have osteoporosis at 65, you haven't won, you've been tricked."[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- Her own figure-competition experience: at 11% body fat (near the 10-13% essential minimum for women, versus a healthy 18-25%), she lost her period for months, suffered prolonged hormonal dysregulation, and regained all the weight — concluding the standard "cardio, restrict, earn your recovery" formula failed her.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- On carbs: temporary low-carb or keto phases can help conditions like type 2 diabetes or PCOS, but permanent carb avoidance is like staying on antibiotics forever; chronic restriction can suppress thyroid function, with signs including constant coldness, heavy periods, hair shedding, and thinning of the outer third of the eyebrow.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- On bulking fears: she says 97-98% of women lack the hormones to bulk — comparing the fear to worrying that driving to the store will turn you into Lewis Hamilton — and the early "thick" feeling is muscle swelling under a fat layer, not bulk.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- On fasting: long fasts (20-72 hours) are riskier for women because ovaries — with around 100,000 mitochondria per oocyte — constantly scan nutrient status, and chronic fasting can signal famine and shut down ovulation; she recommends only an overnight ~10-11 hour fast, stopping food 2-3 hours before sleep.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- Her training prescription: three to four strength days weekly, roughly 10 sets per muscle group per week, taken within one to three reps of failure; even two sessions a week can produce "incredible results," and progressive overload can come from load, volume, or density, not only heavy weight.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- You "can't spot reduce but you can spot build": her hourglass-building muscle groups are the lateral delts ("bread buns"), lats ("angel wings"), the three glutes, the adductors, and the pelvic floor/core.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- Female biomechanics differ: the wider, shallower female pelvis creates a larger Q angle and more knock-kneed tracking, raising ACL-injury risk (she cites the reported cluster of ACL tears before the Women's World Cup, typically occurring in fatigued, under-recovered athletes) — so women often squat better with a wider, toes-out stance and need strong hip stabilizers like gluteus medius.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- Her tier-one supplements: magnesium glycinate (~250 mg twice daily, habit-stacked), omega-3s (2-4 g/day), vitamin D3 with K2 (minimum 4,000 IU daily — "they call it a vitamin, it's not really a vitamin," more a prohormone), creatine (3-5 g daily, up to 10 g for cognition after a bad perimenopausal night's sleep), hydrolyzed collagen types 1-3 (10-15 g for joints, tendons, ligaments) with vitamin C, and electrolytes on heavy-sweat days.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- On aging: VO2 max declines about 10% per decade if untrained, but she cites a study of women averaging age 58 who raised VO2 max 10% in 8 weeks of sprint training, with mitochondrial efficiency gains of 69% versus 49% in 18-30-year-olds — "it's never too late"; she recommends sprinting, the Norwegian 4x4 protocol, jumping/plyometrics for bone density, and deceleration training to prevent falls.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- For PCOS, she pushes back on the idea that cortisol spikes from HIIT are inherently harmful — training-induced glucose and cortisol spikes are normal physiology — and says resistance training plus both zone 2 and sprint-interval cardio improve glucose disposal.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
- On Pilates and GLP-1s: Pilates (which she does twice weekly) is great for pelvic floor and posture but insufficient alone for muscle and bone loading; GLP-1s and menopause hormone therapy have real benefits but must be blended with lifestyle medicine — training, nutrition, stress management, and above all sleep, her "S-tier" recovery tool, supplemented by sauna as "lazy cardio."[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]

# Takeaways

Estima's through-line is that women's bodies are not "little men": training advice, nutrition dogma, and cultural ideals built around male physiology or thinness have left many women under-muscled, under-fed, and at risk of osteoporosis and frailty. Her prescription — eat enough, lift progressively, sprint, jump, protect connective tissue, and unhitch self-worth from the scale — is presented as both clinical observation from tens of thousands of patients and hard-won personal experience. These are the guest's recommendations and interpretations of the research, offered in a popular podcast format rather than as individualized medical advice.[^womens-fitness-expert-what-you-need-to-know-abou-HwVn3WMv5oI]
