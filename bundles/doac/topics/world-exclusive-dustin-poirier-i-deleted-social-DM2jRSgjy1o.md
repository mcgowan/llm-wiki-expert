# Overview

In this world-exclusive interview, retired UFC lightweight Dustin Poirier gives Steven Bartlett his first full account of "the incident": on Father's Day, weeks before the interview, Poirier began drinking at the airport in Lafayette and then Atlanta, got into an altercation with gate agents, offered a police officer a fight, and was arrested and charged with public intoxication. He describes the day as the boiling point of a returning depression — "like a cloud in my head that I just can't get out from under" — triggered largely by thoughts of his father, a lifelong alcoholic who is currently homeless and, at around 74-75, sleeping in a truck Poirier's sister gave him. Poirier says he deleted all social media after the arrest, has refused to watch the viral video of himself, went straight back into therapy, and has decided to cut alcohol out of his life entirely.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]

The conversation frames the incident against Poirier's childhood in working-class Lafayette, Louisiana: violent, divorcing parents, an alcoholic father, drinking from age 12-13, expulsion from school for fighting, and juvenile detention at 14. Fighting, he says, became his therapy and his entire organizing purpose for two decades — which is why retirement, on July 30 the previous year in Louisiana at age 36, left a void nothing has filled. He repeatedly quotes a line a friend gave him: "If a man's lucky, he gets to die twice" — the fighter Dustin is dead, and "businessman Dustin" is still trying to learn how to dream again.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]

Bartlett steers the discussion into athlete retirement (Poirier says the UFC offers no post-fighting transition program), the professional fallout of the arrest (canceled gigs, at least one major sponsor lost, uncertainty over his Paramount/CBS desk-analyst contract), CTE and the brain changes a neurologist found in Poirier's scans, and his Good Fight Foundation charity work. Throughout, Poirier refuses to use depression, his father, or head trauma as excuses: "I did it... it's all on me."[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]

- Poirier recounts the Father's Day sequence: a good morning with his kids, then the depressive "cloud" descending as he traveled; two champagnes on the Lafayette flight, more champagne and shots during his Atlanta layover, an argument with the desk agent he cannot remember, police called, arrest, and a night in jail before release on bond — with a three-leg work trip (South Florida, LA commercial shoot, CBS in Vegas) never getting past the first leg.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- He has refused to watch the viral video, piecing events together from his wife and a training partner, saying watching himself "disrespecting police officers... my family" would only drag him down; he wants to contact the arresting officer to thank him for his professionalism, noting "it could have been so much worse."[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- He deleted and uninstalled all social media the day after the arrest and has seen none of the coverage; Bartlett tells him the public reaction was overwhelmingly sympathetic, aided by resurfaced clips from the Theo Von and Joe Rogan shows in which Poirier had said "I'm a danger to myself when I have no goal circled on my calendar."[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- His childhood: parents whose earliest memories together involve "fighting and violence," divorce around kindergarten, a father whose alcoholism "ruined his life" and every relationship, drinking from age 12-13, expulsion for fighting, and juvenile detention at 14 after an assault, truancy, and a failed drug test on probation.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- His father is currently homeless in Louisiana; after the arrest Poirier obtained an order of protective custody to have him picked up, but he was released because he presented as lucid; Poirier says he has been bracing for "the call" for months, while insisting "it's not my weight to carry — I can't babysit my father."[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- On alcohol, Poirier says he has "always had a bad relationship" with it — "if I drink, we're drinking till the bottle's gone" — and, after talking with his wife Jolie (his partner since middle school, who dropped out of nursing school to move to South Florida for his career), he has decided to quit alcohol completely so his children never see him intoxicated.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- He first sought therapy after losing his second Justin Gaethje fight, stopped when he felt better, and restarted the day he returned from Atlanta; his key lesson is that mental health "is not a Eureka moment where you're fixed — it's everyday work for the rest of your life," and that admitting therapy once felt "weak" in a tough-guy sport but he now sees it as strength.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- On retirement: fighting was his outlet, education, and identity for 20 years ("nothing fills that void"), the UFC offers no post-retirement support program, and desk work and podcasting reach "maybe 20%" of what fighting gave him; he oscillates between believing he can still beat the current top lightweights and relief that he no longer has to, putting his odds of a UFC return at about 5% and falling.[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
- The arrest carried real costs: the week's gigs were canceled, at least one big sponsor dropped him, and his one-year Paramount/CBS desk contract is uncertain — though he says his career earnings and investing since age 23 mean he never has to work again (while joking with his wife that quitting gambling is further down the list than quitting drinking).[^world-exclusive-dustin-poirier-i-deleted-social-DM2jRSgjy1o]
