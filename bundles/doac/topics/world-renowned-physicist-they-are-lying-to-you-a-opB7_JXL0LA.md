# Overview

Theoretical physicist Michio Kaku, a co-founder of string field theory who has studied physics for 71 years since Einstein's death inspired him at age eight, joins Steven Bartlett to work through the biggest questions in physics: where the universe came from, whether we are alone, whether reality is a simulation, and how quantum computers, AI, and biotechnology will reshape human destiny. Kaku frames his life's work as completing Einstein's unfinished quest for a "theory of everything" — an equation "perhaps no more than one inch long" that would let us "read the mind of God" — and argues we are close, because the equations of physics keep getting simpler over time.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]

On the newly declassified UFO files, Kaku takes a rigorously agnostic line: he is confident alien life exists statistically, but insists we remain at "close encounters of the first kind" — sightings without tangible hardware — and says the released material shows only "lights dancing in the sky." He refuses to be cornered into a yes-or-no on whether aliens have visited, landing on "maybe," though he speculates that any real craft would be robotic, since the maneuvers observed would crush any organic occupant.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]

The conversation also ranges over Kaku's rejection of simulation theory, his account of reality as an evolutionarily useful illusion, religion as evolutionary "glue," his morality-forming service in the Vietnam-era US Army, and a forecast of the coming decades: quantum computers that threaten every digital code, telomerase-based longevity constrained by cancer, and a civilizational "knife's edge" between world war and abundance.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]

# Key points

- Kaku argues we are not alone: the Milky Way has 100 billion stars, roughly 10% with Earth-like planets, making the probability of life "almost 100%" — but a Saturn V rocket would take 70,000 years to reach the nearest star, so any visitors would need space-warp technology and would be hundreds or thousands of years ahead of us.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- Using the "close encounters" taxonomy, he says humanity is stuck at the first kind (sightings); 95% of sightings are explainable by known physics and the remaining 5% are either optical illusions or evidence of visitation. A piece of recovered UFO hardware — the second kind — "would nail it to the wall," but he found nothing compelling in the roughly 160 declassified encounters released under Trump.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- On government secrecy, Kaku suggests the "public isn't ready" narrative is the traditional excuse; the likelier reason for military lying is that UFO reports serve as cover for classified aircraft like the stealth bomber and experimental aerodynamics.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- If UAP craft are real, Kaku bets they are robotic rather than organic — their zigzags and 70,000-foot dives would "crush the bones of any living creature" — and he reasons aliens are not hostile, because if they wanted to destroy us "they could have done it years ago"; instead we may be observed like animals in a zoo that don't know they're in a zoo.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- He explains string theory as the claim that all subatomic particles — of which hundreds have been found in atom smashers — are vibrations of a single string, with each "note" a different particle; dark matter, he suggests, may be the "next octave" of vibrations that don't interact with light, and a Nobel Prize awaits whoever identifies it.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- On cosmic origins, Kaku says "big bang" is a misnomer disguising ignorance; in string theory the universe bounced rather than banged, and he personally favors a "bubble bath" multiverse in which empty space froths with bubbles and one, ~14 billion years ago, kept expanding — with our four-dimensional universe embedded in 11 dimensions.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- He rejects Nick Bostrom's simulation argument, proposing "option four: there is no simulation at all" — the universe runs on quantum probabilities, not scripts — but agrees perceived reality is a "partial fiction": evolution tuned our senses to a "teeny-weeny fragment" of the electromagnetic spectrum because seeing more wasn't necessary for survival.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- Kaku describes himself as agnostic, framing God and religion as evolutionary "glue" that held intelligent, bickering tribes together, and Genesis as a "compelling fairy tale" best read as metaphor; his own morality was forged in the US Army during Vietnam — including a sergeant maimed by a child's grenade — which taught him to question which side of a war is right.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- On mortality, he says the brain's electricity simply turns off at death and he sees no energy source that could sustain ghosts; but "indefinite lifespan" is physically possible — telomeres act as a cellular clock and telomerase stops it — the catch being that cancer uses the same machinery to become immortal, so the challenge is extending lifespan "without wakening up the cancers."[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- Kaku warns quantum computers, which compute on atoms rather than transistors, will eventually break any digital code — a prospect that worries even the CIA, with Google reportedly setting 2029 as a deadline for the security world; if codes fall, "capitalism would vanish, society would come to a halt."[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- He is skeptical of current AI hype: today's robots have "the intelligence of a bug," LLMs are imitative rather than truly creative (true creativity, like Newton inventing calculus, "comes from almost nothing"), and creative machines are decades away — but as robots climb from mouse to monkey intelligence they become dangerous, and his proposed answer is that humans should merge with them to gain superhuman abilities rather than risk a "civil war" with machines.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
- His decade-by-decade view of history yields both optimism and alarm: enormous progress every decade, but for the first time — only in the last ~80 years — humanity can destroy itself with designer germs, nuclear weapons, and perhaps AI; "we're at a knife's edge: tilt it one way and there's world war, tilt it the other and there's food and luxury for everyone."[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]

# Takeaways

Kaku's closing register is one of disciplined wonder: the mystery he would most like solved is the one he has chased his entire life — the inch-long equation unifying all four forces — and he draws comfort from imagining an alien on the far side of the galaxy writing down the same universal equations, "part of the same club." He argues meaning is not given by the universe but created individually, that the best strategy for failure is to try again with a slight change, and that humanity's future — moon and Mars settlement, AI-assisted cures, possible immortality — depends on choosing which way the knife tilts.[^world-renowned-physicist-they-are-lying-to-you-a-opB7_JXL0LA]
