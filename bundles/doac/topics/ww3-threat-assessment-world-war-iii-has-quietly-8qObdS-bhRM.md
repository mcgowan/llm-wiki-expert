# Overview

In this roundtable episode, host Steven Bartlett convenes three national-security voices — journalist Annie Jacobsen (author of "Nuclear War: A Scenario"), former CIA clandestine officer and ex-ICBM launch officer Andrew Bustamante, and Iranian-born scholar Benjamin Radd — to assess whether the world is sliding into a third world war. Bustamante's central claim is that World War III has already quietly begun, but that it does not resemble the wars of the past: it is being fought through proxy wars, cyber operations, trade and supply-chain pressure, and above all information warfare, where a "server farm and 20 people" can destabilize a society from thousands of miles away.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]

The guests dissect the June 2025 US bombing of Iran's nuclear sites as a case study. Radd traces the US–Iran conflict from the 1979 revolution and the regime's three founding pillars (independence from the West, hostility to Israel, and exporting the revolution), argues diplomacy with the current leadership had been exhausted, and warns that bombing a sovereign state may teach other nations that only a nuclear weapon guarantees their borders — with North Korea as the "shining example" of untouchability. Bustamante contends the strike was "the right thing at the wrong time," suggests Netanyahu likely fed Washington selective intelligence, and highlights how President Trump publicly overrode his own intelligence chiefs.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]

Jacobsen anchors the nuclear stakes: from launch to nuclear winter takes roughly 72 minutes, the US president holds sole launch authority with only minutes to decide, and the Pentagon rehearses telling the president a nuke is inbound three times a day. All three converge on the view that the greatest existential threat is human miscalculation — echoing UN Secretary-General Guterres's warning that we are "one misunderstanding, one miscalculation away," or even one AI-generated viral video away, from nuclear annihilation.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]

# Key points

- Bartlett opens with statistics framing the tension: conflict zones up 66% in three years; in a December 2024 Atlantic Council survey of about 400 strategists, 65% expect China to invade Taiwan by force within 10 years, ~50% expect nuclear weapons to be used, and ~45% expect direct Russia–NATO fighting; 59 states have erupted in war since 2023 — the most logged since 1946 — and world military spending is up about 10% year-over-year, the highest sum SIPRI has ever recorded.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Bustamante argues World War III is already underway in its early stages, shaped by proxy warfare — a wealthy state funding, training, and arming conflict in a poorer state to degrade its primary target while conserving its own troops and weapons — citing Libya, Syria, Yemen, Russia–Ukraine, and controversially framing Israel as a US proxy against Iran.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Radd contends conflict has shifted from "kinetic" warfare to information warfare in a post-truth society: with no monopoly on objective truth, adversaries like Russia and China exploit polarization at scale — "warfare on the cheap" — while Jacobsen adds that America's warring political parties attack each other "at the national security peril."[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Radd describes a war-game simulation he designed (a hypothetical second January 6 arising from within the military, later the documentary "War Game") in which a red cell of two or three young trolls, amplified by the algorithm, overwhelmed an entire experienced White House communications team.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- The guests note Trump publicly rejected his own intelligence apparatus twice in weeks: DNI Tulsi Gabbard's assessment that Iran was not imminently nuclear-capable (she was then marginalized and reversed herself), and the DIA's finding that the strikes may not have achieved "total obliteration."[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- On enrichment, the guests explain the nuclear-threshold concept: the NPT permits enrichment to 20%; Iran reached 60%; weapons-grade is ~90%, but anything between 20% and 90% can fuel a radioactive dirty bomb — and Saudi Arabia's MBS has said that if Iran gets the bomb, Saudi Arabia will too, which the guests call a recipe for regional nuclear cascade.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Bustamante and Jacobsen detail Israel's intelligence penetration of Iran — Mossad-run assets fabricating thousands of AI-controlled drones inside Tehran, and the Hezbollah pager operation in which Israel infiltrated the supply chain to embed explosives at the fabricator — while noting Western agencies have historically misread Iran (the missed 1979 revolution, the 1953 coup).[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Bustamante puts the odds of a nuclear detonation of some kind in our lifetime at 30% — up from his 15–20% estimate less than two years earlier — most likely a non-state or non-attributable event such as a dirty bomb or a small tactical nuke, noting the last known detonation was North Korea's 2017 underground test; Jacobsen counters that tactical use is a cliff, not a slope, since Pentagon war games show any nuclear war ends in total annihilation, which is why the US removed tactical nukes from its arsenal.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- Jacobsen recounts near-miss history: Stanislav Petrov, "the man who saved the world," declining to escalate a false 1983 Soviet radar alert; and Russia's November Oreshnik intermediate-range ballistic missile strike on Ukraine, where Lavrov's 30-minute advance notice through the State Department's nuclear risk-reduction center was all that prevented a launch-on-warning crisis — "nuclear chicken."[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- On command and control: the president has sole launch authority choosing pre-set strike options from the "black book" in the football (once compared to a Denny's menu); missile crews turn keys on encrypted orders they cannot interpret ("a firing squad where you don't know who shoots the fatal bullet"); the decision window is about seven minutes, with missiles flying Pyongyang-to-DC in 33 minutes and Russia-to-DC in under 27; Russia's Cold War "dead hand" system could launch its entire arsenal even if its leadership were dead; and Admiral Connor told Jacobsen the Pentagon bunker rehearses warning the president three times a day — one per shift.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- The guests warn the next trigger may be economic and technological: China can win Taiwan without invading — jamming GPS, blocking shipping lanes, and banning rare-earth exports as the US sits depleted (THAAD interceptors spent defending Israel) — the way it took Hong Kong administratively; global arsenals now total roughly 12,300 warheads (about 10,000 held by the US and Russia), with an average warhead around 300 kilotons and modern ICBMs carrying up to 10 MIRVed warheads.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
- On safe zones, Jacobsen says nuclear-winter modeling leaves only New Zealand and parts of Australia agriculturally viable — she has spoken with billionaires with New Zealand bunkers and G5 jets — while Bustamante disputes full-extinction modeling but is acting personally: he plans to change his appearance and move his family out of the United States by spring 2026 to raise his children as "global citizens who are American."[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]

# Takeaways

The guests close on personal defenses rather than geopolitical fixes. Radd teaches media literacy — including having students build deepfake political content so they recognize manipulation when it targets them — and warns that civic breakdown, not weaponry, is the deeper vulnerability; Jacobsen urges disciplined information diets, time in nature, and points to third-party diplomacy (she briefed the Vatican on nuclear war) and the Reagan–Gorbachev arms-reduction legacy as the hopeful path; Bustamante advises diligence about information sources and living outside one's national bubble. Their shared message is that ordinary citizens cannot defeat the forces of misinformation and escalation, only manage them — through curiosity, media skepticism, and civic engagement — while pressuring leaders to keep the nuclear line uncrossable.[^ww3-threat-assessment-world-war-iii-has-quietly-8qObdS-bhRM]
