# Overview

In this episode of The Diary Of A CEO, Steven Bartlett hosts professor and entrepreneur Scott Galloway and behavioral scientist and dating coach Logan Ury (who spent five years working with data at Hinge) for a conversation framed around the Centre for Social Justice's March 2025 "Lost Boys" report, which found that boys are struggling in education, more likely to take their own lives, finding it harder to secure stable work, and too often caught up in crime.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY] Galloway argues the "first point of failure" in a struggling boy's life is the loss of a male role model: in single-parent homes girls show broadly similar outcomes to their peers, he says, while boys become far more likely to be incarcerated, commit crime, or harm themselves — and this is compounded by schools where few teachers are men and where classroom norms reward stereotypically female behaviors.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]

The guests connect this developmental story to what Ury calls a "dating crisis" and a widening "mating gap." Because women tend toward hypergamy — seeking partners of equal or higher economic and social status — the combination of women out-earning young men in some markets and dominating college enrollment leaves a shrinking pool of men that women consider dateable, concentrating romantic opportunity in a small top tier of men while the rest are effectively shut out.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY] Galloway argues that online dating has created a winner-take-most market that has been "amazing" for tall, attractive, wealthy men and "a disaster" for everyone else, and warns that porn, video games, and coming AI companions are giving young men a frictionless facsimile of life that saps the motivation ("mojo") to develop the social and professional skills that romantic pursuit historically forced them to build.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]

Both guests insist this is not a zero-sum framing: they celebrate women's economic ascent as a collective victory while arguing that society now needs empathy and concrete programs for boys — more male teachers and mentors, vocational paths, men's groups, third spaces, and a positive script for modern masculinity — because, as Ury puts it in closing, "women don't have to do worse when men do better."[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]

# Key points

- Galloway, citing Richard Reeves, argues the loss of a male role model is the single strongest predictor of a boy's struggles; he claims individuals from father-absent homes are 300% more likely to carry drugs or guns, and Bartlett notes that about 25% of US boys now live without a biological, step, or adoptive father, with children living with only their mother roughly doubling from 11% in 1968 to 21% in 2020.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- The guests describe schools as tilted against boys: Galloway says a boy is twice as likely to be suspended for the same infraction as a girl (five times if he is a Black boy), around 70% of US valedictorians are female, and only about a quarter of teachers are men — while shop classes that once led to middle-class jobs have disappeared.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Because girls' prefrontal cortex development runs roughly 18 months ahead of boys', the guests discuss Reeves's proposal to "red-shirt" boys — starting them in school a year later — noting UK research that 70% of girls but many fewer boys are ready for school at age five.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Galloway contends boys are less psychologically resilient than girls ("orchids" versus "dandelions" in Ury's parenting metaphor): the absence of a father raises depression risk for boys but not girls to the same degree, and he cites a claim that a boy who is sexually molested is six to ten times more likely to later kill himself than a girl suffering the same crime.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- The episode highlights a "reverse gender pay gap": young women now out-earn young men in some urban US markets, young men face nearly twice women's unemployment rate, and UK males aged 16-24 not in education or employment have risen by 40% — though Ury cautions that women's earnings advantage disappears once they have children.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Galloway cites data that when the woman in a relationship starts making more money, the couple becomes twice as likely to divorce and the man three times as likely to use ED drugs, which he attributes to men losing a sense of purpose while failing to "step up logistically" at home.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Ury describes dating-app dynamics that shut most men out: many US women set height filters at 6 feet though only 14% of US men are that tall, and Bartlett cites a claim that a man of average attractiveness must swipe right roughly 1,000 times on Tinder to get one coffee date that actually happens — rejection the guests say pushes men toward porn, Reddit, and red-pilled figures like Andrew Tate.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Galloway warns of "an evolving new species of asexual, asocial male": only one in three US men under 30 has a girlfriend while two in three women that age have boyfriends (because women date older), fewer than half of Gen Z men had a teenage relationship versus three-quarters of Boomers and Gen X, and per Pew more than 50% of men aged 18-24 have never asked a woman out in person.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Ury identifies dating paradoxes: women now demand emotional intelligence as "the new currency in dating" from men raised only to be providers, yet men who do open up are often shamed for it; and men are still expected to initiate in a post-MeToo culture where approaching can get them filmed and publicly mocked online.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Galloway's prescription for the young men he coaches is to reclaim 8-12 hours a week from screens and reinvest them in three things: strength training, making money (any money, to "get a taste" for economic agency), and joining groups of strangers in service of something bigger — plus deliberately practicing rejection by asking people out with "no" as the expected, celebrated outcome.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- Ury champions men's groups — monthly councils of six or seven peers who surface and hold each other accountable for their most pressing problems — as a scalable, affordable alternative to therapy, and recommends reading fiction as a cheap way for men to build empathy.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
- The guests link male worthlessness to suicide — about 75% of suicides in the UK and worldwide are men, and suicide is the leading cause of death among young men in some 50 countries — and Galloway argues both political parties have failed young men: the right offers "coarseness and cruelty" as role models while the left treats masculinity itself as toxic, a void he says helped drive under-30 men toward Trump.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]

# Takeaways

The episode's throughline, as the guests present it, is that young men's quiet withdrawal — from school, work, dating, and society — stems from compounding losses: absent fathers and male mentors, an education system misfit to boys, a winner-take-all economy that concentrates opportunity in the "remarkable," and digitized mating and dopamine machines that remove the friction that once forced men to grow.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY] Their proposed remedies run from policy (vocational training, national service, more college seats, more male teachers) to personal practice (fitness, economic discipline, kindness as a "secret weapon," rejection resilience, men's groups), anchored by Galloway's redefinition of masculinity as being a provider, protector, and procreator who adds "surplus value" to the world — and by the shared conviction that helping struggling boys is not a defeat for women but a precondition for everyone thriving.[^young-men-are-quietly-giving-up-heres-why-li70iz1NaDY]
