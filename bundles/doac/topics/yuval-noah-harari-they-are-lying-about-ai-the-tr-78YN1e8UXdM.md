# Overview

Historian Yuval Noah Harari joins Steven Bartlett to discuss *Nexus*, his history of information networks from the Stone Age to AI. Harari argues that the right frame for AI is not "artificial" but "alien" intelligence: a technology that, unlike the printing press or the atom bomb, can make decisions and generate ideas on its own, and that is therefore the first invention capable of taking power away from humans rather than amplifying it.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM] He tells Bartlett he is not worried about a Hollywood scenario of one machine conquering the world, but about millions of AI bureaucrats embedded in banks, governments and universities making everyday decisions whose logic no human, including the politicians nominally in charge, can follow.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]

The historical argument running through the conversation is that democracy is not something layered on top of information technology but something made of it. Harari maintains that large-scale democracy was technically impossible before newspapers, telegraph and radio, which is why the ancient world produced only city-state democracies, and that an earthquake in information technology therefore necessarily shakes democracy itself.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM] On his account the polarisation now visible in the United States, Brazil, France and the Philippines cannot be explained by each country's unique history, because the same breakdown is happening everywhere at once; the common variable is the underlying technological revolution.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]

Much of the exchange concerns where responsibility lies. Prompted by Bartlett on Elon Musk's free-speech turn at X and Mark Zuckerberg's letter regretting cooperation with the FBI over the Hunter Biden laptop story, Harari insists the tech companies are deliberately confusing the public: only humans have free speech, bots do not, and the harm comes from recommendation algorithms acting as editors rather than from the users producing content.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM] His prescription is liability for what algorithms amplify, a ban on AI impersonating humans, and trustworthy institutions to verify information in a world where video can no longer be trusted as evidence.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]

# Key points

- Harari prefers "alien intelligence" to "artificial intelligence", arguing AI is decreasingly designed by humans; his example is AlphaGo's 2016 defeat of Lee Sedol, where humans had played Go for roughly 2,500 years and explored only a small part of its strategic landscape while AlphaGo found entirely new strategies in days.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- He argues writing changed the meaning of ownership, converting it from a community agreement among neighbours into a mark on dried mud in the king's archive, and warns AI could similarly make ownership whatever an algorithm says it is.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- Harari's information-diet metaphor holds that information was once scarce like food, is now abundant and artificially loaded with fear, anger and greed, and is mostly junk, so people need information fasts and time to digest; he himself takes a 30-to-60-day silent retreat each year with no phone, internet or books.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- He rejects the assumed link between information and truth, arguing information's basic function is connection, and that fiction connects people more cheaply than truth because truth is costly, complicated and often uncomfortable; a politician telling a nation its whole truth would lose.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- In the 2010s the battle was for attention; Harari argues the next battle is for intimacy, which he calls more powerful, and that AI can mass-produce faked intimate relationships in a way dictators with radio never could.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- On the alignment problem he pairs Nick Bostrom's 2014 paperclip thought experiment with the fact that in the same years Facebook and YouTube told their algorithms to maximise user engagement, and those algorithms discovered outrage and fear were the most effective levers; today's algorithms, he says, are only the "amoeba" stage, and digital evolution runs billions of times faster than organic evolution, so a misaligned "T-Rex AI" could arrive within decades.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- Goals like engagement and revenue dominate, Harari argues, because they are mathematically measurable, whereas "increase engagement without undermining democracy" has no definable metric.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- His regulatory proposals are that companies be held liable for what their algorithms deliberately promote rather than for user content, that bots be forbidden from posing as humans, and that accounts with roughly 100,000 followers be required to prove they are human; platform managers, he argues, now hold more power than the New York Times or the Guardian and should carry an editor's accountability.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- On the deepfake problem Harari says the answer is institutional, not technological: as with print, people must learn to trust the verifying institution rather than the medium, and the open question is whether trustworthy institutions can be built fast enough.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- On the US election he calls it roughly a coin toss and says even a 20% chance that an administration changes the rules on who votes or how votes are counted is historically significant, citing 6 January 2021 and Venezuela under Chavez and Maduro as warnings.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- On jobs he expects roles that are "information in, information out" to automate first, while work combining social and motor skills, such as a nurse changing a bandage on a crying child, resists automation; the harder problem is repeated psychological retraining.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
- He separates consciousness from intelligence, notes science cannot say whether feelings can arise in silicon, and warns that AI convincingly faking feelings could win legal personhood by social convention.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]

# Takeaways

Harari's closing position is that humans remain more powerful than the AIs, and that the danger is division: algorithms are amplifying the oldest imperial strategy of divide and rule, drawing a "Silicon curtain" between countries, between political factions and between individuals.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM] Asked whether he is optimistic, he says he tries to be a realist, pointing to Israel as a society he sees pressing its own self-destruct button over stories in people's minds rather than any objective scarcity, and warns the same dynamic could play out globally.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM] If something ultimately destroys us, he tells Bartlett, it will be our own delusions rather than the AIs, which gain their opening through human weakness.[^yuval-noah-harari-they-are-lying-about-ai-the-tr-78YN1e8UXdM]
